# Prayer

How Christians are taught to pray: every day, with the words Jesus gave, for others and with thanks, honestly in sorrow as in joy, together with other Christians, and humbly. The churches agree on all of this, while they differ on whether to ask the saints for their prayers and on praying for the dead, which are listed under "Open questions". Reading Scripture in prayer is covered in [Reading Scripture](scripture.md), and the love that prayer serves in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (1648, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Q. 178–196 explain what prayer is, for whom and how to pray, and the Lord's Prayer as "the special rule of direction" for prayer.
- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): Lord's Days 45–52 (Q&A 116–129) explain why Christians pray, what kind of prayer God hears, and the Lord's Prayer petition by petition.
- `8` [The Lord's Prayer](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-lords-prayer) (English Language Liturgical Consultation): the agreed ecumenical English text of the Lord's Prayer, with notes on its wording; the Consultation calls it the prayer that "lies at the heart of Christian devotion".
- `8` [Prayer](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Prayer) (Marcel Sarot, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): what prayer is, its forms (petition, intercession, praise, confession, lament and thanksgiving), its history, and where the churches' practice of prayer differs.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2558–2565](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8Z.HTM): the opening of Part Four, "Christian Prayer", which runs to 2865 and ends with the Lord's Prayer petition by petition.
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism: The Lord's Prayer](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/lords-prayer/) (1529): Luther's teaching that prayer is commanded, his explanation of each petition, and his encouragement to pray daily.
- `7` [The Longer Catechism of the Orthodox, Catholic, Eastern Church, Part Two](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html) (St Philaret of Moscow, 1830, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): Orthodox teaching on prayer (Q. 389–396) and the Lord's Prayer (Q. 397–434).
- `7` [The Orthodox Faith: Prayer](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/prayer1) (Thomas Hopko, Orthodox Church in America): what prayer is in the Orthodox spiritual tradition, from Jesus' teaching to "go into your room".

## Key rules

### A life of prayer

- `9` `PRAY-1` **Pray every day: make prayer a regular part of your life.** Paul writes "pray without ceasing", Jesus taught that "they must always pray and not give up", and Daniel prayed "three times a day". The churches teach that we cannot pray "at all times" if we do not pray at specific times, and give each day a rhythm of prayer: morning and evening prayer and grace at meals in Catholic teaching, morning and evening prayers with the Creed and the Lord's Prayer in Luther's Small Catechism, a personal "rule of prayer" in the Orthodox tradition, and prayer "in private families daily, and in secret, each one by himself" in the Westminster Confession. ([1 Thessalonians 5:16–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TH05.htm#V16), [Luke 18:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK18.htm#V1), [Daniel 6:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DAN06.htm#V10), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2697–2698](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9J.HTM), [Luther's Small Catechism, Daily Prayers](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/daily-prayers/), [The Orthodox Faith: Unceasing Prayer](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/unceasing-prayer), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html))
- `7` `PRAY-7` **Pray humbly and with trust, not to be seen by others.** Jesus warns against praying "that they may be seen by men" and tells his followers to pray to their Father "in secret", and in his parable it is the tax collector, praying "God, be merciful to me, a sinner!", who goes home justified. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "humility is the foundation of prayer", the Heidelberg Catechism that we "humble ourselves in God's majestic presence" and rest on the promise that God will hear us because of Christ, and the Westminster Larger Catechism that we pray "with humble submission to his will". ([Matthew 6:5–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V5), [Luke 18:9–14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK18.htm#V9), [Philippians 4:6–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V6), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2559](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8Z.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 117](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 185](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [The Orthodox Faith: Prayer](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/prayer1))

### The Lord's Prayer

- `9` `PRAY-2` **Pray the Lord's Prayer, and let it shape all your prayer.** Jesus gave it when his disciples asked him to teach them to pray. The Catechism of the Catholic Church calls it "the most perfect of prayers", the Orthodox Longer Catechism the "pattern of all prayers", Luther's Large Catechism says "there is no nobler prayer to be found upon earth", and the Westminster Larger Catechism teaches that it is both a pattern for other prayers and a prayer to be prayed. ([Matthew 6:9–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V9), [Luke 11:1–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK11.htm#V1), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2762–2764](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9X.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 397](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, The Lord's Prayer, 23](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/lords-prayer/), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 186–187](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 118–119](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism))

### Kinds of prayer

- `8` `PRAY-3` **Pray for others: for those in need, for those in authority and for those who wrong you.** Paul asks that prayers be made "for all men, for kings and all who are in high places", and Jesus tells his followers to "pray for those who mistreat you and persecute you". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches intercession "even to the point of praying for those who do him harm", the Westminster Larger Catechism prayer for "magistrates, and ministers; for ourselves, our brethren, yea, our enemies", and Orthodox teaching prayer "on behalf of all and for all", in the words of the Divine Liturgy. ([1 Timothy 2:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI02.htm#V1), [Matthew 5:44](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V44), [James 5:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS05.htm#V16), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2634–2636](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P98.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 183](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [The Orthodox Faith: Intercessory Prayer](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/intercessory-prayer), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 463](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html))
- `8` `PRAY-4` **Give thanks to God in every circumstance.** "In everything give thanks, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus towards you." The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "every event and need can become an offering of thanksgiving", the Heidelberg Catechism that prayer is "the most important part of the thankfulness God requires of us", the Westminster Confession that prayer "with thanksgiving" is required of all, and the Orthodox Longer Catechism names praise, thanksgiving and petition as the three chief forms of prayer. ([1 Thessalonians 5:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TH05.htm#V18), [Philippians 4:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V6), [Psalm 100](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA100.htm), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2637–2638](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P99.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 116](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 391](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html))
- `7` `PRAY-5` **Bring your sorrow, anger and questions to God honestly: lament is prayer too.** The psalms of lament cry "How long, LORD? Will you forget me forever?" and "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?", and Lamentations finds hope in God's mercies, "new every morning", in the middle of grief. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the Psalms, "prayers of lamentation or thanksgiving" alike, can be "prayed in truth by men of all times and conditions", the Book of Common Prayer orders that the whole Psalter, laments included, "shall be read through once every Month" at Morning and Evening Prayer, and John Calvin called the Psalms "An Anatomy of all the Parts of the Soul", in which all "griefs, sorrows, fears, doubts" are brought before God. ([Psalm 13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA013.htm), [Psalm 22:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA022.htm#V1), [Lamentations 3:19–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LAM03.htm#V19), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2588](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P91.HTM), [Book of Common Prayer, The Order how the Psalter is appointed to be read](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/order-how-psalter-appointed-be-read), [Calvin, Commentary on the Psalms, Preface](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom08.vi.html), [St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Prayer, 4.4](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Prayer#section4.4))

### Praying together

- `8` `PRAY-6` **Pray together with other Christians.** Jesus promised that "where two or three are gathered together in my name, there I am in the middle of them", and the first disciples "with one accord continued steadfastly in prayer". The Catechism of the Catholic Church calls the family the "domestic church" where children learn to pray, and the parish church the place of the community's liturgical prayer; Orthodox teaching asks believers to pray in church "with the Church"; and the Westminster Confession calls public worship something "not carelessly or willfully to be neglected". Every year, Christians of many churches pray together in the Week of Prayer for Christian Unity. ([Matthew 18:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V19), [Acts 1:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT01.htm#V14), [Acts 2:42](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT02.htm#V42), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2685 and 2691](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9H.HTM), [The Orthodox Faith: Liturgical Prayer](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/liturgical-prayer), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [World Council of Churches, Week of Prayer for Christian Unity](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/week-of-prayer-for-christian-unity))

## How to apply

- `8` [The Liturgy of the Hours](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, Sacrosanctum Concilium, 83–101, 1963): the daily prayer of the Church, by which "the whole course of the day and night is made holy by the praises of God"; the Council encourages lay people to pray it "with the priests, or among themselves, or even individually" (100). Supports `PRAY-1`, `PRAY-6` and `PRAY-4`.
- `7` [The Order for Morning Prayer](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/order-morning-prayer) (Church of England, Book of Common Prayer): the Anglican daily office, "daily throughout the year": confession, the Lord's Prayer, the psalms "in order as they be appointed", Bible readings, and prayers for the King and for others. Supports `PRAY-1`, `PRAY-2`, `PRAY-3` and `PRAY-5`.
- `7` [Luther's Small Catechism: Daily Prayers](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/daily-prayers/) (1529): short morning and evening prayers for households, said with the Creed and the Lord's Prayer, with a prayer of thanks for being kept through the night or the day. Supports `PRAY-1`, `PRAY-2` and `PRAY-4`.
- `7` [The Jesus Prayer](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/the-jesus-prayer) (Thomas Hopko, Orthodox Church in America): the short prayer "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me a sinner", repeated as a form of unceasing prayer in the Orthodox tradition. Supports `PRAY-1` and `PRAY-7`.
- `7` [Week of Prayer for Christian Unity](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/week-of-prayer-for-christian-unity) (World Council of Churches): an annual week of prayer, traditionally 18–25 January, with material prepared jointly by the World Council of Churches and the Catholic Church's Dicastery for Promoting Christian Unity, for congregations to pray together across the churches. Supports `PRAY-6` and `PRAY-3`.
- `6` [The Daily Examen](https://www.ignatianspirituality.com/ignatian-prayer/the-examen/) (IgnatianSpirituality.com): a short daily prayer from St Ignatius of Loyola that reviews the day "with gratitude". Supports `PRAY-4` and `PRAY-1`.

## Open questions

- `7` [Prayer, 8.1: Prayer within ecumenical relationships](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Prayer#section8.1) (Marcel Sarot, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): may Christians ask the saints in heaven to pray for them? All the churches ask fellow Christians on earth for their prayers. The Catholic Church teaches that "we can and should ask them to intercede for us" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2683](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9H.HTM)), and the Orthodox churches call on Christ "by the prayers of his most pure Mother and all his saints" ([Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 263–264](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html)). The Augsburg Confession holds up the saints as examples, but teaches that Scripture does not teach "the invocation of saints", because it sets before us Christ as the one mediator ([Augsburg Confession, XXI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-worship-of-saints/)), and the Westminster Confession directs worship to God alone, "not to angels, saints, or any other creature" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [Prayer, 8.1: Prayer within ecumenical relationships](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Prayer#section8.1) (Marcel Sarot, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): should Christians pray for the dead? The Catholic Church prays for the dead, a practice it finds already in Scripture ([2 Maccabees 12:43–45](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2MA12.htm#V43)), and above all offers the Eucharist for them ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 958](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2B.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1032](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2N.HTM)). The Orthodox churches teach that the departed may be helped "by prayers offered in their behalf" ([Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 376](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html)). Protestant churches hold, in the encyclopaedia's summary, that a person's final destiny is decided at death, so prayer can no longer change it ([Hebrews 9:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB09.htm#V27)), and the Westminster Confession teaches that prayer is to be made for the living, "but not for the dead" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.4](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 183](https://www.opc.org/lc.html)).
