# Reading Scripture

How Christians are taught to read the Bible: regularly, in context, as a whole, with other Christians, in prayer, and so as to live by it. The churches agree on much of this, while they differ on how Scripture relates to the church's tradition and teaching office, and on some questions of interpretation, which are listed under "Open questions". Which books count as Scripture, and which translations to read, is set out in [The Bible](../bible/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Dei Verbum](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1965): the Catholic Church's constitution on divine revelation: Scripture and Tradition (9–10), the inspiration and interpretation of Scripture (11–12), and Scripture in the life of the Church (21–25).
- `9` [Westminster Confession of Faith, chapter 1](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html) (1646, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): the Reformed confession's chapter on Holy Scripture: which books it contains, its authority, its clarity in what is necessary for salvation (1.7), and Scripture as the interpreter of Scripture (1.9).
- `8` [A Treasure in Earthen Vessels: An Instrument for an Ecumenical Reflection on Hermeneutics](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/a-treasure-in-earthen-vessels-an-instrument-for-an-ecumenical-reflection-on-hermeneutics) (World Council of Churches, Faith and Order Paper No. 182, 1998): how churches of different traditions interpret Scripture and tradition, the place of scholarly methods, and the church as a community that interprets together.
- `8` [Biblical Hermeneutics](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/BiblicalHermeneutics) (Richard S. Briggs, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): the study of biblical interpretation: meaning, method, history, readers, and reading Scripture in the life of the church.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 109–119](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PQ.HTM): Catholic teaching on interpreting Scripture: the human authors' intention and literary genres, the unity of Scripture, the living Tradition, and the senses of Scripture.
- `7` [The Longer Catechism of the Orthodox, Catholic, Eastern Church, Introduction](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.i.html) (St Philaret of Moscow, 1830, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): Orthodox teaching on holy tradition and holy Scripture (Q. 16–55), and rules for reading Scripture (Q. 56).

## Key rules

### Reading Scripture

- `9` `SCRIP-1` **Read the Bible regularly, and let it shape your faith and life.** Scripture calls itself "profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness", and "a lamp to my feet". The churches urge every Christian to read it often, and agree that it "holds a unique place in the shaping of Christian faith and practice". ([2 Timothy 3:14–17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TI03.htm#V14), [Psalm 1:1–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA001.htm#V1), [Psalm 119:105](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA119.htm#V105), [A Treasure in Earthen Vessels, 27](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/a-treasure-in-earthen-vessels-an-instrument-for-an-ecumenical-reflection-on-hermeneutics), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 131–133](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PS.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 156](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Third Commandment, 89–91](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 56](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.i.html))

### Understanding what you read

- `8` `SCRIP-2` **Read each passage in its context: the book it belongs to, its kind of writing and its setting.** When the Law was read to the returned exiles, the teachers "gave the sense, so that they understood the reading". The churches teach that God speaks through human authors, so readers should ask what kind of text they are reading, and what its writer meant in their own time and culture. ([Nehemiah 8:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NEH08.htm#V8), [Acts 8:30–31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT08.htm#V30), [A Treasure in Earthen Vessels, 21](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/a-treasure-in-earthen-vessels-an-instrument-for-an-ecumenical-reflection-on-hermeneutics), [Dei Verbum, 12](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 109–110](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PQ.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 157](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Orthodox Church in America, The Bible: Interpretation](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/the-bible/interpretation))
- `8` `SCRIP-3` **Read Scripture as a whole: let clearer passages shed light on harder ones, and read the Old and New Testaments in the light of each other and of Christ.** The risen Jesus explained "in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself", beginning from Moses and the prophets. Catholic teaching asks readers to attend to "the content and unity of the whole Scripture", the Westminster Confession says that a hard passage "must be searched and known by other places that speak more clearly", and Orthodox teaching interprets all parts of the Bible "in the light of Christ". ([Luke 24:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK24.htm#V27), [Luke 24:44–45](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK24.htm#V44), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 112](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PQ.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 128–130](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PR.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.9](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Orthodox Church in America, Sources of Christian Doctrine: The Bible](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/sources-of-christian-doctrine/bible1))

### Reading with the church

- `7` `SCRIP-4` **Read Scripture with other Christians: hear it in worship, study it together and learn from those who teach it.** The first Christians "continued steadfastly in the apostles' teaching", and Paul asks them to let the word of Christ dwell in them richly, "teaching and admonishing one another". The churches teach that Scripture is received above all in worship, and that it is understood within the community of faith, not by each reader alone. ([Colossians 3:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V16), [Acts 2:42](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT02.htm#V42), [Acts 8:30–35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT08.htm#V30), [A Treasure in Earthen Vessels, 7 and 27](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/a-treasure-in-earthen-vessels-an-instrument-for-an-ecumenical-reflection-on-hermeneutics), [Dei Verbum, 21](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 160](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Small Catechism, Third Commandment](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Orthodox Church in America, The Bible: Interpretation](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/the-bible/interpretation))

### Praying and living the word

- `8` `SCRIP-6` **Put what you read into practice.** "Be doers of the word, and not only hearers", writes James, and Jesus calls blessed "those who hear the word of God, and keep it". The Westminster Larger Catechism asks hearers to "bring forth the fruit of it in their lives", the Orthodox Longer Catechism to read "with a pure desire of instruction in faith, and incitement to good works", and the Catechism of the Catholic Church to pass in meditation "from thoughts to reality". ([James 1:22–25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V22), [Matthew 7:24–27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT07.htm#V24), [Luke 11:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK11.htm#V28), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 160](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 56](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.i.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2706](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9L.HTM))
- `7` `SCRIP-5` **Pray with Scripture: read slowly, meditate on it and answer God in prayer.** The psalmist meditates on God's law "day and night", and Mary kept what she heard, "pondering" it "in her heart". Catholic teaching asks that "prayer should accompany the reading of Sacred Scripture", the Westminster Larger Catechism that Scripture be read "with meditation, application, self-denial, and prayer", and the Orthodox Longer Catechism that it be read "with prayer to understand it aright". ([Psalm 1:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA001.htm#V2), [Psalm 119:15–16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA119.htm#V15), [Luke 2:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK02.htm#V19), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2653–2654](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9D.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2705–2708](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9L.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 157](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Third Commandment, 91](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 56](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.i.html))

## How to apply

- `8` [Lectio divina](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_ben-xvi_exh_20100930_verbum-domini.html) (Benedict XVI, Verbum Domini, 87, 2010): the ancient way of praying with Scripture, in steps: reading (what does the text say in itself?), meditation (what does it say to us?), prayer (what do we say to the Lord in response?), contemplation, and action, which "moves the believer to make his or her life a gift for others". Supports `SCRIP-5`, `SCRIP-6` and `SCRIP-2`.
- `6` [The Revised Common Lectionary](https://lectionary.library.vanderbilt.edu/faq/) (Vanderbilt Divinity Library): a three-year cycle of Sunday readings, an Old Testament reading, a psalm, a reading from the letters and a Gospel, "used to varying degrees by the vast majority of mainline Protestant churches in Canada and the United States", and compiled with Catholic participation. Following a lectionary means reading the Bible through the year, with the church, and hearing the Testaments side by side. Supports `SCRIP-1`, `SCRIP-3` and `SCRIP-4`.

## Open questions

- `8` [A Treasure in Earthen Vessels, 16 and 27](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/a-treasure-in-earthen-vessels-an-instrument-for-an-ecumenical-reflection-on-hermeneutics) (World Council of Churches): how do Scripture and the church's tradition relate? Catholic teaching holds that Scripture and Tradition "form one sacred deposit of the word of God", and that authentically interpreting it is entrusted to the Church's living teaching office ([Dei Verbum, 9–10](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 80–83](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PL.HTM)). Orthodox teaching sees Holy Tradition as the whole life of the Church, in which "the Bible holds the first place" ([Orthodox Church in America, Tradition](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/sources-of-christian-doctrine/tradition)). The Lutheran Formula of Concord holds the Scriptures to be "the sole rule and standard" by which all teaching and teachers are judged ([Formula of Concord, Epitome, Rule and Norm, 1](https://bookofconcord.org/epitome/)), and the Westminster Confession names "the Holy Spirit speaking in the Scripture" as the supreme judge of all controversies of religion ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.6 and 1.10](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). The World Council of Churches' Faith and Order Commission notes that its 1963 Montreal conference "helped to overcome the old contrast between 'sola Scriptura' and 'Scripture and tradition'", while noting that interpreters from different churches have not yet reached enough agreement for visible unity.
- `8` [Biblical Hermeneutics](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/BiblicalHermeneutics) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): in what sense is Scripture without error? Catholic teaching holds that the books of Scripture teach "without error that truth which God wanted put into sacred writings for the sake of salvation" ([Dei Verbum, 11](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html)). The evangelical Lausanne Covenant affirms Scripture as "without error in all that it affirms" ([Lausanne Covenant, 2](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant)), and the Chicago Statement denies that inerrancy is limited to spiritual themes, "exclusive of assertions in the fields of history and science" ([Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy, Article XII](https://alliancenet.org/icbi/the-chicago-statement-on-biblical-inerrancy/)). The encyclopaedia entry describes a modern debate in which some defenders insist that the Bible must be historically accurate "always and everywhere", while some critics argue that it is not.
- `8` [A Treasure in Earthen Vessels, 21–22](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/a-treasure-in-earthen-vessels-an-instrument-for-an-ecumenical-reflection-on-hermeneutics) (World Council of Churches): how far should historical and critical scholarship guide the reading of Scripture? Faith and Order holds that the tools of modern scholarship are important, and should not be the only approach, while noting that "some churches and individual Christians reject historical-critical interpretation". Catholic teaching asks interpreters to study the writers' literary forms, time and culture ([Dei Verbum, 12](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html)). Orthodox teaching values scholars' historical and literary studies, but holds that scholarship alone cannot give the right interpretation outside the life of the Church ([Orthodox Church in America, The Bible: Interpretation](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/the-bible/interpretation)).
