# Suffering, death and hope

"God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble" ([Psalm 46:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA046.htm#V1)), and Jesus himself wept at the grave of his friend ([John 11:35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN11.htm#V35)). This file covers what the churches teach together about suffering, illness, dying and grief: bringing suffering to God, staying with those who suffer, caring for the sick and the dying, grieving with hope, preparing for death, and helping anyone who thinks of taking their own life. If you or someone near you is in danger now, call your local emergency number or a crisis line. Lament as prayer is `PRAY-5` in [Prayer](prayer.md); prayer for the dead and purgatory are open questions in [Prayer](prayer.md) and [Repentance and forgiveness](repentance.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): Q&A 1 asks "What is your only comfort in life and in death?", Q&A 26–28 teach God's providence in "health and sickness", and Q&A 57–58 the comfort of the resurrection of the body and life everlasting.
- `8` [Salvifici doloris](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_letters/1984/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_11021984_salvifici-doloris.html) (John Paul II, 1984): the apostolic letter "on the Christian meaning of human suffering", from the Book of Job to the cross, with a chapter on the Good Samaritan, who shows that a neighbour "cannot indifferently pass by the suffering of another".
- `8` [Samaritanus bonus](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20200714_samaritanus-bonus_en.html) (Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, 2020): Catholic teaching on the care of people in the critical and final stages of life: palliative care, the family and hospices, accompaniment and the sacraments, and the rejection of euthanasia, summed up as "to cure if possible, always to care".
- `8` [Suffering](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Suffering) (Kelly M. Kapic, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): suffering in the Old and New Testaments, lament and praise, the suffering of Christ, and the theological questions suffering raises.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1005–1014](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2I.HTM): Catholic teaching on dying in Christ, the meaning of death, and preparing for the hour of death.
- `7` [Sickness, Suffering, and Death](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sickness-suffering-and-death/suffering) (Thomas Hopko, *The Orthodox Faith*, Orthodox Church in America): Orthodox teaching on suffering, with companion pages on [sickness](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sickness-suffering-and-death/sickness) and [death](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sickness-suffering-and-death/death).
- `7` [The Order for the Visitation of the Sick](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/visitation-sick) (Church of England, Book of Common Prayer): the Anglican service for a minister visiting someone who is sick, with prayers, the Creed, and an exhortation to be at peace with God and with others.

## Key rules

### In suffering

- `9` `HOPE-1` **Bring your suffering to God, and trust him in it.** The psalm calls God "a very present help in trouble"; Peter writes "casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you"; and Paul calls God the "God of all comfort" and trusts that "all things work together for good for those who love God". The Catechism of the Catholic Church acknowledges that "experiences of evil and suffering, injustice and death" can shake faith, and points to the witnesses of faith who "believed against hope", and it notes that in the Old Testament the sick person "laments his illness" before God; the Heidelberg Catechism's "only comfort in life and in death" is belonging to Christ, and knowing God's providence means "We can be patient when things go against us"; and Orthodox teaching holds that "There is no greater witness to the love of God and faith in Christ than sickness endured with faith and love". ([Psalm 46:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA046.htm#V1), [2 Corinthians 1:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO01.htm#V3), [1 Peter 5:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE05.htm#V7), [Romans 8:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM08.htm#V28), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 164–165](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PX.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1502](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4K.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1 and 28](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Orthodox Church in America, Sickness](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sickness-suffering-and-death/sickness))

### Caring for those who suffer

- `9` `HOPE-2` **Stay with people who suffer and grieve, and comfort them.** "Weep with those who weep", and "Bear one another's burdens"; when Job's friends heard of his trouble they came "to sympathise with him and to comfort him", and sat with him on the ground "seven days and seven nights". John Paul II writes that the person who is a neighbour "cannot indifferently pass by the suffering of another" but must "stop" and "sympathize", like the Good Samaritan; the Catechism of the Catholic Church counts "consoling, comforting" among the works of mercy; the Orthodox Longer Catechism lists "To comfort the afflicted"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "comforting and succoring the distressed" among the duties of the sixth commandment; and the Southern Baptist Convention calls churches and families to give "compassionate, faithful presence to those who are suffering both physically and mentally". ([Romans 12:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V15), [Job 2:11–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JOB02.htm#V11), [Galatians 6:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL06.htm#V2), [Salvifici doloris, 28–29](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_letters/1984/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_11021984_salvifici-doloris.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2447](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8F.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 463](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 135](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Southern Baptist Convention, On Assisted Suicide and the Sanctity of Life (2026)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-assisted-suicide-and-the-sanctity-of-life/))
- `9` `HOPE-3` **Care for the sick and the dying, and never abandon them.** Jesus says "I was sick and you visited me"; James asks the sick to call the elders, who are to "pray over him, anointing him with oil"; and the Samaritan bound up the wounded man's wounds and "took care of him". The Catholic Church teaches "to cure if possible, always to care", that an illness being incurable "cannot mean that care has come at an end", and that "The dying should be given attention and care to help them live their last moments in dignity and peace"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism counts it a work of mercy "To visit the sick, minister to them, and forward their recovery", and the Russian Orthodox Church teaches that when intensive care becomes impossible "its place should be taken by palliative aid … and pastoral care"; Luther's Large Catechism warns those who leave the sick and imprisoned to "perish in distress"; and the Book of Common Prayer asks that the minister be told "When any person is sick". ([Matthew 25:36](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT25.htm#V36), [James 5:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS05.htm#V14), [Luke 10:34](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK10.htm#V34), [Samaritanus bonus](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20200714_samaritanus-bonus_en.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2299](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P80.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 462](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XII](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xii/), [Luther's Large Catechism, Fifth Commandment, 189–191](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Book of Common Prayer, The Visitation of the Sick](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/visitation-sick))

### Death and hope

- `8` `HOPE-4` **Grieve with hope in the resurrection.** Paul writes "so that you don't grieve like the rest, who have no hope", and Jesus says "I am the resurrection and the life", yet he too wept at his friend's grave. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the Christian meaning of death is revealed in Christ's death and resurrection, "in whom resides our only hope"; Orthodox teaching holds that Christians "can only mourn and weep" at death, but that in Christ "the tragedy of death can be transformed into victory"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks how "the resurrection of the body" comforts the believer; Luther's Small Catechism confesses that God "will raise up me and all the dead"; and the Book of Common Prayer buries the dead "in sure and certain hope of the Resurrection to eternal life". ([1 Thessalonians 4:13–14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TH04.htm#V13), [John 11:25–26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN11.htm#V25), [John 11:35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN11.htm#V35), [1 Corinthians 15:20–22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO15.htm#V20), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1681](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P5B.HTM), [Orthodox Church in America, Death](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sickness-suffering-and-death/death), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 57](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Creed](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-creed/), [Book of Common Prayer, The Burial of the Dead](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/burial-dead))
- `7` `HOPE-5` **Prepare for your own death: make peace with God and with others.** "So teach us to count our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom"; and Paul, near his death, writes "I have finished the course. I have kept the faith." The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "The Church encourages us to prepare ourselves for the hour of our death"; Orthodox teaching holds that "The preparation for death is at the center of the spiritual life", and the Orthodox Longer Catechism speaks of helping the sick to "a Christian preparation for death"; Luther's Small Catechism prays that God would "grant us a blessed end"; and in the Book of Common Prayer the minister urges the sick person "to forgive, from the bottom of his heart, all persons that have offended him", to ask forgiveness of others and "make amends", and to make a will. ([Psalm 90:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA090.htm#V12), [Luke 12:20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK12.htm#V20), [2 Timothy 4:6–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TI04.htm#V6), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1014](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2I.HTM), [Orthodox Church in America, Death](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sickness-suffering-and-death/death), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 462](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Lord's Prayer](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-lords-prayer/), [Book of Common Prayer, The Visitation of the Sick](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/visitation-sick))

### When life seems unbearable

- `9` `HOPE-6` **Take thoughts of suicide seriously: get help, and don't leave someone who is at risk alone.** When the prophet Elijah asked God to take away his life, an angel touched him and told him to eat and rest; "The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart"; and Christians are to "Bear one another's burdens". The Evangelical Lutheran Church in America's message on suicide prevention asks Christians to take threats of suicide seriously, says that a person with a plan "should not be left alone but should be helped to get treatment immediately", advises calling a crisis line, and proclaims that "God's boundless love in Jesus Christ will leave no one alone and abandoned"; the Southern Baptist Convention supports "the wise use of medical intervention for mental health concerns" and opposes their stigmatisation; the Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "Grave psychological disturbances, anguish, or grave fear" can diminish a person's responsibility, and that "We should not despair of the eternal salvation of persons who have taken their own lives"; the Russian Orthodox Church reminds the people around those at risk of their call to "effective compassion and mercy"; and the Westminster Larger Catechism counts it a duty to preserve "the life of ourselves and others". If someone is in danger now, call your local emergency number or a crisis line. ([1 Kings 19:4–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1KI19.htm#V4), [Psalm 34:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA034.htm#V18), [Galatians 6:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL06.htm#V2), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Suicide Prevention](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Suicide_PreventionSM.pdf), [Southern Baptist Convention, On Mental Health Concerns and the Heart of God (2013)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-mental-health-concerns-and-the-heart-of-god/), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2280–2283](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7Z.HTM), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XII](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xii/), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 135](https://www.opc.org/lc.html))

## How to apply

- `8` [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Suicide Prevention](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Suicide_PreventionSM.pdf) (1999, updated 2021): false beliefs about suicide, and a helpcard with signs to watch for, such as someone talking about having no reason to live, and what anyone can do: stay calm and listen, ask directly, take threats seriously, "Don't swear secrecy", and go with the person to get help; with how congregations can care for the bereaved. Supports `HOPE-6` and `HOPE-2`.
- `8` [Samaritanus bonus, V.4–5](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20200714_samaritanus-bonus_en.html): palliative care, the family and hospices, which the Catholic Church asks to accompany the sick "until the very end". Supports `HOPE-3`.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1514–1516](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4L.HTM): the anointing of the sick, which the Catholic Church gives "as soon as anyone of the faithful begins to be in danger of death from sickness or old age"; the Orthodox churches celebrate holy unction for the sick ([Orthodox Church in America, Holy Unction](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/holy-unction)). Supports `HOPE-3`.
- `7` [Book of Common Prayer, The Visitation of the Sick](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/visitation-sick): an order for visiting someone who is sick, with prayers for them, the Creed, and an invitation to forgive, seek forgiveness and set affairs in order. Supports `HOPE-3` and `HOPE-5`.
- `6` [Church of England, Facing death and mortality](https://www.churchofengland.org/life-events/funerals/after-funeral/facing-death-and-mortality): practical steps for someone approaching death: "Patch up a relationship", tell people you love them, talk about fears and hopes, write a will, and "Ask someone to pray with you". Supports `HOPE-5`.
- `6` [Church of England, Journey through grief](https://www.churchofengland.org/life-events/funerals/after-funeral/journey-through-grief): what grief can feel like over time, and where bereaved people can find support, from family and friends to trained listeners and church bereavement counsellors. Supports `HOPE-2` and `HOPE-4`.

## Open questions

- `7` [St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Suffering, 3.1](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Suffering#section3.1): why does God allow suffering? Christian thinkers have given different answers: that this is the best of all possible worlds (Leibniz), that suffering can foster growth (Hick), that evil follows from creatures' freedom (Plantinga), or, among process theologians and open theists, that God's attributes must be reimagined; the entry adds that the church has more often responded "by proclaiming the gospel and engaging in practices that seek to alleviate pain". The churches also speak differently: the Heidelberg Catechism holds that "health and sickness" come to us "not by chance but by his fatherly hand" ([Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 27](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism)), while Orthodox teaching holds that "Sickness exists in the world only because of sin" and that sickness is not necessarily personal punishment ([Orthodox Church in America, Sickness](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sickness-suffering-and-death/sickness)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2276–2279](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7Z.HTM): where does refusing treatment end and ending a life begin? The churches cited here all reject euthanasia and assisted suicide, and differ on the details. The Catholic Church teaches that stopping treatment that is "burdensome, dangerous, extraordinary, or disproportionate" can be legitimate and that painkillers may be used even at the risk of shortening life, while nutrition and hydration remain basic care "provided that it does not cause harm or intolerable suffering" ([Samaritanus bonus](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20200714_samaritanus-bonus_en.html)). The Russian Orthodox Church holds that prolonging life by artificial means "cannot be viewed as obligatory" ([Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XII](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xii/)). The Southern Baptist Convention opposes euthanasia and assisted suicide "in all its forms, including practices now described as 'medical aid in dying'" ([Southern Baptist Convention, On Assisted Suicide and the Sanctity of Life (2026)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-assisted-suicide-and-the-sanctity-of-life/)), and the Archbishop of Canterbury, with other faith leaders, urged instead "compassionate, high quality palliative care" ([Church of England, Faith leaders join to oppose Assisted Dying Bill (2015)](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/faith-leaders-join-oppose-assisted-dying-bill)).
- `7` [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XII](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xii/): how should the church bury and pray for someone who has died by suicide? The Russian Orthodox Church does not grant Christian burial to someone who took their life deliberately, but allows church prayer, after the bishop has looked into the case, when the suicide was committed "in a fit of a mental disease". The Catholic Church teaches that "the Church prays for persons who have taken their own lives" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2283](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7Z.HTM)). The Evangelical Lutheran Church in America holds that funerals proclaim "that suicide and death itself do not place one beyond the communion of saints" ([Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Suicide Prevention](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Suicide_PreventionSM.pdf)), and the Southern Baptist Convention asks churches to assure bereaved families "that those in Christ cannot be separated from the eternal love of God" ([Southern Baptist Convention, On Mental Health Concerns and the Heart of God (2013)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-mental-health-concerns-and-the-heart-of-god/)).
- `7` [St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Hell, 3.1](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Hell#section3.1): will everyone be saved in the end? The Catholic Church teaches the existence of hell and its eternity, that "God predestines no one to go to hell", and prays that none be lost ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1033–1037](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2O.HTM)); the Westminster Confession holds that the wicked "shall be cast into eternal torments" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 33.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). The entry describes other views held by Christian theologians: that the wicked will cease to exist rather than suffer for ever, a view taken by several theologians and, more recently, by evangelicals such as John Stott; and the hope of Karl Barth who, though he denied believing in universalism, wrote that "we can only say there is full salvation for all men in Christ".
