# Public life and citizenship

Jesus said "Give therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's" ([Matthew 22:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT22.htm#V21)), and Paul asks for prayers "for kings and all who are in high places" ([1 Timothy 2:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI02.htm#V1)). This file covers how Christians relate to those in authority and to the society they live in: respect and prayer, obeying just laws, the limits of obedience, peace, religious freedom, and taking part in public life. It takes no side between political parties, countries or leaders. Working for just laws and structures is `JUST-5` in [Justice and the poor](justice.md). Whether Christians may use force or serve as soldiers is an open question in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md) and [Work and rest](work.md); this file links to those questions rather than repeating them.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

- `8` [The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world) (Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church, 2016): freedom and responsibility (B), peace and justice (C), and "Peace and the Aversion of War".
- `8` [Statement on the Way of Just Peace](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/statement-on-the-way-of-just-peace) (World Council of Churches, 10th Assembly, 2013): what the member churches believe and call for together on peace in the community, with the earth, in the economy and among the nations.

## Key rules

### Respect for those in authority

- `8` `CIV-1` **Respect and pray for those in authority.** Paul asks that prayers be made "for kings and all who are in high places", and Peter writes "Fear God. Honour the king." The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that citizens should regard those in authority "as representatives of God" and recalls the call to offer prayers "for kings and all who exercise authority", adding that citizens have "the right, and at times the duty, to voice their just criticisms"; the Russian Orthodox Church teaches that the Church "prays for it", the state, "that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life"; the Westminster Confession calls it "the duty of people to pray for magistrates, to honor their persons"; the Mennonite Confession asks Christians "to respect those in authority and to pray for all people, including those in government". ([1 Timothy 2:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI02.htm#V1), [Romans 13:1–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM13.htm#V1), [1 Peter 2:13–17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE02.htm#V13), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2238 and 2240](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7W.HTM), [Bases of the Social Concept, III.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/iii/), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 23.4](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 127](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 23](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/churchs-relation-to-government-and-society/))

### Obeying the law, and its limits

- `9` `CIV-3` **Obey God rather than people when those in authority command what is wrong.** When the council commanded them not to teach in Jesus' name, Peter and the apostles answered "We must obey God rather than men", and Daniel's three friends told the king "we will not serve your gods". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "the citizen is obliged in conscience not to follow the directives of civil authorities when they are contrary to the demands of the moral order, to the fundamental rights of persons or the teachings of the Gospel"; the Russian Orthodox Church teaches that "the Christian, following the will of his conscience, can refuse to fulfil the commands of state forcing him into a grave sin"; the Augsburg Confession teaches that Christians must obey their magistrates and laws "save only when commanded to sin; for then they ought to obey God rather than men"; the Baptist Faith and Message asks for "loyal obedience" to government "in all things not contrary to the revealed will of God". ([Acts 5:29](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT05.htm#V29), [Acts 4:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT04.htm#V19), [Daniel 3:16–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DAN03.htm#V16), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2242](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7W.HTM), [Bases of the Social Concept, III.5](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/iii/), [Augsburg Confession, XVI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-civil-affairs/), [Baptist Faith and Message 2000, XVII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xvii), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 20.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html))
- `8` `CIV-2` **Obey just laws and pay your taxes.** Paul writes "if you owe taxes, pay taxes", and Jesus says "Give therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that it is "morally obligatory to pay taxes"; the Russian Orthodox Church teaches that the Church prescribes "to obey state power regardless of the convictions and faith of its bearers"; the Westminster Confession calls on people "to pay them tribute or other dues, to obey their lawful commands"; the Augsburg Confession teaches that "Christians are necessarily bound to obey their own magistrates". ([Romans 13:6–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM13.htm#V6), [Matthew 22:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT22.htm#V21), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2240](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7W.HTM), [Bases of the Social Concept, III.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/iii/), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 23.4](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Augsburg Confession, XVI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-civil-affairs/))

### Peace and freedom

- `8` `CIV-4` **Work for peace, and settle conflicts without violence wherever you can.** Jesus says "Blessed are the peacemakers", Paul writes "If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men", and Isaiah foresees a day when nations "beat their swords into ploughshares". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "all citizens and all governments are obliged to work for the avoidance of war"; the Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church "supports all initiatives and efforts to prevent or avert it through dialogue and every other viable means"; the World Council of Churches holds that "churches can help build cultures of peace by learning to prevent and transform conflicts"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks Christians to be "patient, peace-loving, gentle". Whether force may ever be used is an [open question](love.md#open-questions). ([Matthew 5:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V9), [Romans 12:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V18), [Isaiah 2:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ISA02.htm#V4), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2308](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P81.HTM), [The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World, Peace and the Aversion of War](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world), [WCC, Statement on the Way of Just Peace](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/statement-on-the-way-of-just-peace), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 107](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism))
- `8` `CIV-5` **Defend religious freedom for everyone, not only for Christians.** Jesus distinguished what belongs to Caesar from what belongs to God, and rebuked James and John when they wanted to call down fire on a Samaritan village that had not welcomed him. The Second Vatican Council declares that "the human person has a right to religious freedom", so that "all men are to be immune from coercion"; the Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church counts "the restriction of human rights in the field of freedom of conscience—in particular religious freedom" among the consequences of evil in today's world; the Baptist Faith and Message holds that "the state has no right to impose penalties for religious opinions of any kind"; the Lausanne Covenant calls on governments "to guarantee freedom of thought and conscience, and freedom to practise and propagate religion"; the Westminster Confession, in the text the Orthodox Presbyterian Church uses, asks that no one, "either upon pretense of religion or of infidelity", be allowed to harm another. ([Matthew 22:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT22.htm#V21), [Luke 9:52–56](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK09.htm#V52), [Dignitatis humanae, 2](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651207_dignitatis-humanae_en.html), [The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World, B](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world), [Baptist Faith and Message 2000, XVII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xvii), [Lausanne Covenant, 13](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 23.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html))

### Taking part in public life

- `7` `CIV-6` **Take part in public life for the common good: vote, speak up and serve.** Jeremiah told the exiles to "seek the peace of the city" and to pray for it, and Proverbs says "Open your mouth for the mute". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that it is "the duty of citizens to contribute along with the civil authorities to the good of society" and that it is "morally obligatory" to "exercise the right to vote", and the Second Vatican Council praises "those who for the good of men devote themselves to the service of the state"; the Augsburg Confession teaches that "it is right for Christians to bear civil office", and the Westminster Confession that "it is lawful for Christians to accept and execute the office of a magistrate"; the Lausanne Covenant affirms that "evangelism and socio-political involvement are both part of our Christian duty". ([Jeremiah 29:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JER29.htm#V7), [Proverbs 31:8–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO31.htm#V8), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2239–2240](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7W.HTM), [Gaudium et spes, 75](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html), [Augsburg Confession, XVI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-civil-affairs/), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 23.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Lausanne Covenant, 5](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant))

## How to apply

- `8` [The Lausanne Covenant, 13: Freedom and Persecution](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant): a pattern for intercession: "we therefore pray for the leaders of nations", and for "all who have been unjustly imprisoned", whose freedom the signatories "promise to pray and work for". Supports `CIV-1` and `CIV-5`.
- `7` [Church and State](https://www.churchofengland.org/about/church-and-state) (Church of England): the Church of England's liturgy "includes prayer for the Monarch, the Royal Family and Members of Parliament, as part of daily worship", an example of praying for those in authority in public worship, whatever one's view of an established church. Supports `CIV-1`.
- `5` [Lombard Mennonite Peace Center](https://www.lmpeacecenter.org/): a Mennonite ministry that offers churches "of all denominations" education "on biblical reconciliation, as well as conflict transformation skills training". Supports `CIV-4`.

## Open questions

- `8` [Gaudium et spes, 76](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html): how should church and state relate? The Second Vatican Council teaches that "the Church and the political community in their own fields are autonomous and independent from each other", yet should cooperate for the good of all, and the Russian Orthodox Church that "the Church can cooperate with the state in affairs which benefit the Church herself, as well as the individual and society" ([Bases of the Social Concept, III.8](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/iii/)). The Church of England "is the Established Church in England, with the Monarch as its Supreme Governor", and its senior bishops sit in the House of Lords ([Church of England, Church and State](https://www.churchofengland.org/about/church-and-state)). The Baptist Faith and Message holds that "church and state should be separate" and that "a free church in a free state is the Christian ideal" ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, XVII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xvii)). The Barmen Declaration of 1934 rejected both a state that becomes "the single and totalitarian order of human life" and a church that becomes "an organ of the state" ([The Barmen Declaration, 5](https://www.ekd.de/en/The-Barmen-Declaration-303.htm)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2243](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7W.HTM): when is resistance to authority justified, and how far may it go? The Catechism of the Catholic Church holds that armed resistance is not legitimate unless five conditions are all met, among them a "certain, grave, and prolonged violation of fundamental rights" and that "all other means of redress have been exhausted". The Russian Orthodox Church names dialogue, democratic means, appeals to international bodies and "peaceful civil disobedience" ([Bases of the Social Concept, III.5](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/iii/)). The Westminster Confession warns that those who "upon pretense of Christian liberty, shall oppose any lawful power" resist God's ordinance ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 20.4](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)), and the Mennonite Confession, which holds that the church "needs no violence for its protection", recognises "no other authority's claims as ultimate" than Christ's ([Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 23](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/churchs-relation-to-government-and-society/)).
- `7` [Augsburg Confession, XVI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-civil-affairs/): may Christians hold public office, serve as soldiers and swear oaths? The Augsburg Confession teaches that it is right for Christians "to bear civil office, to sit as judges", "to serve as soldiers" and "to make oath when required by the magistrates", and the Westminster Confession that they may hold office and, "upon just and necessary occasion", wage war ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 23.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). The Mennonite Confession holds that Christians "may participate in government or other institutions of society only in ways that do not violate the love and holiness taught by Christ" ([Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 23](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/churchs-relation-to-government-and-society/)). See also the open questions on war in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md#open-questions) and on oaths in [Honest and kind speech](speech.md#open-questions), and [The Anabaptist churches](../traditions/anabaptist.md).
