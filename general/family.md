# Marriage, family and singleness

"Honour your father and your mother", says the commandment ([Exodus 20:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO20.htm#V12)), and Jesus says of husband and wife: "What therefore God has joined together, don't let man tear apart" ([Matthew 19:4–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT19.htm#V4)). This file covers what the churches teach together about family life: faithfulness in marriage, love between husband and wife, honouring and caring for parents, bringing up children, singleness as a way to serve God, and keeping everyone in the family safe from abuse. The churches differ on several questions about marriage, such as divorce and remarriage, contraception, same-sex marriage, the roles of husbands and wives, and the celibacy of clergy; those are set out under "Open questions" without taking a side. If you or someone else is in danger at home, contact the emergency services first. Protecting children and vulnerable adults in church life is covered in [Belonging to a church](church.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): Q&A 104 explains the commandment to honour father and mother, and Q&A 108–109 the commandment against adultery, asking for "decent and chaste lives, within or outside of the holy state of marriage".
- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (1648, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Q. 123–133 set out the duties of parents and children and of all who are responsible for others, and Q. 137–139 the duties of the seventh commandment, among them "conjugal love, and cohabitation".
- `8` [Amoris laetitia](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf) (Pope Francis, 2016, PDF): the apostolic exhortation "on love in the family": love in marriage, preparing for marriage, bringing up children, the elderly, the unmarried, and caring for families in difficulty, including where there is abuse.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1643–1654](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P55.HTM): Catholic teaching on the goods of marriage: unity, indissolubility, "inviolable fidelity" and openness to children.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2214–2231](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM): Catholic teaching on the duties of children towards their parents, and of parents towards their children.
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, The Ten Commandments](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/) (1529): the fourth commandment (paragraphs 103–178), on honouring parents and the duty of parents to bring up their children, and the sixth (199–221), on chastity and love between husband and wife.
- `7` [The Longer Catechism of the Orthodox, Catholic, Eastern Church, Part Three](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html) (St Philaret of Moscow, 1830, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): Q. 554–571 on honouring parents and on duties towards children, and Q. 584–591 on the commandment against adultery and the duties of husband and wife.
- `7` [Marriage](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/marriage) (Thomas Hopko, *The Orthodox Faith*, Orthodox Church in America): Orthodox teaching on the sacrament of marriage, which Christ gives to make the love of husband and wife "unique, indestructible, unending and divine", and on second marriages.
- `7` [The Form of Solemnization of Matrimony](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/form-solemnization-matrimony) (Church of England, Book of Common Prayer): the Anglican marriage service, with the purposes of marriage and the vows the couple make to each other.
- `7` [Baptist Faith and Message, XVIII: The Family](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xviii) (Southern Baptist Convention, 2000): the family, marriage, and the duties of husbands, wives, parents and children in a Baptist confession.

## Key rules

### Marriage

- `9` `FAM-2` **Be faithful to your husband or wife.** The commandment says "You shall not commit adultery", Jesus says of marriage "What therefore God has joined together, don't let man tear apart", and the letter to the Hebrews asks: "Let marriage be held in honour amongst all". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that conjugal love "requires the inviolable fidelity of the spouses"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism names "conjugal love and fidelity" as the virtues the commandment asks for; Luther's Small Catechism asks that "each love and honor his spouse"; and in the Book of Common Prayer each spouse promises, "forsaking all other", to keep only to the other "so long as ye both shall live". ([Exodus 20:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO20.htm#V14), [Matthew 19:4–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT19.htm#V4), [Hebrews 13:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB13.htm#V4), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1646](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P55.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 589](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Luther's Small Catechism, Sixth Commandment](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 108](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Book of Common Prayer, The Form of Solemnization of Matrimony](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/form-solemnization-matrimony))
- `8` `FAM-4` **Love and honour your husband or wife with self-giving love, as Christ loved the church.** The letter to the Ephesians asks Christians to be "subjecting yourselves to one another in the fear of Christ" and husbands to love their wives "even as Christ also loved the assembly and gave himself up for her"; Peter calls husbands and wives "joint heirs of the grace of life". The Catechism of the Catholic Church speaks of the spouses' "day-to-day fidelity to their marriage promise of total mutual self-giving"; Luther's Large Catechism asks that husband and wife "live together in love and harmony, that one may cherish the other from the heart and with entire fidelity"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "conjugal love" among the duties of the seventh commandment; and the Book of Common Prayer names "the mutual society, help, and comfort, that the one ought to have of the other, both in prosperity and adversity" as one of the purposes of marriage. How the roles of husbands and wives are ordered is an open question below. ([Ephesians 5:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH05.htm#V21), [Ephesians 5:25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH05.htm#V25), [Colossians 3:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V19), [1 Peter 3:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE03.htm#V7), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1644](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P55.HTM), [Luther's Large Catechism, Sixth Commandment, 219](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 138](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Book of Common Prayer, The Form of Solemnization of Matrimony](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/form-solemnization-matrimony))

### Parents and children

- `9` `FAM-1` **Honour your father and mother, and care for them when they are old.** The commandment says "Honour your father and your mother"; Paul calls it "the first commandment with a promise", and asks children and grandchildren "to repay their parents". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that grown children "must give them material and moral support in old age and in times of illness, loneliness, or distress"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism lists the duty "To support and comfort them in sickness and age"; Luther's Large Catechism asks Christians to "provide for them when they are old, sick, infirm, or poor"; and the Heidelberg Catechism asks believers to "honor, love, and be loyal to my father and mother" and to "be patient with their failings". ([Exodus 20:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO20.htm#V12), [Ephesians 6:1–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V1), [1 Timothy 5:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI05.htm#V4), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2218](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 554](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Fourth Commandment, 111](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 104](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism))
- `9` `FAM-3` **Love and care for your children, bring them up in the faith, and don't provoke them.** "You fathers, don't provoke your children to wrath, but nurture them in the discipline and instruction of the Lord", and the Law asks parents to teach God's words "diligently to your children". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that parents have "the first responsibility for the education of their children", shown first "by creating a home where tenderness, forgiveness, respect, fidelity, and disinterested service are the rule"; Luther's Large Catechism asks every parent "to bring up his children above all things in the fear and knowledge of God"; the Westminster Larger Catechism asks those responsible for others, parents first, to "love, pray for, and bless" them and to provide "all things necessary for soul and body", and counts "provoking them to wrath" a sin; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism answers that Scripture prescribes duties towards children, quoting the same verse. ([Ephesians 6:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V4), [Deuteronomy 6:6–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU06.htm#V6), [Colossians 3:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V21), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2221–2228](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM), [Luther's Large Catechism, Fourth Commandment, 168–176](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 129–130](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 569](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))

### Singleness

- `8` `FAM-5` **Honour singleness, as well as marriage, as a way to love and serve God.** Jesus speaks of those who remain unmarried "for the Kingdom of Heaven's sake", and Paul, himself unmarried, writes that "each man has his own gift from God" and that the unmarried can be "concerned for the things of the Lord". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "Both the sacrament of Matrimony and virginity for the Kingdom of God come from the Lord himself", and Pope Francis writes that many unmarried people "render great service in their group of friends, in the Church community and in their professional lives"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism commends, "for such as can receive it, perfect purity and chastity"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks for chaste lives "within or outside of the holy state of marriage"; and the Augsburg Confession holds that "not all men are fit to lead a single life", which needs "a singular gift and work of God". How the churches order vows of celibacy and the celibacy of clergy is an open question below. ([Matthew 19:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT19.htm#V12), [1 Corinthians 7:7–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO07.htm#V7), [1 Corinthians 7:32–35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO07.htm#V32), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1618–1620](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P51.HTM), [Amoris laetitia, 158](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 589](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 108](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Augsburg Confession, XXIII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-marriage-of-priests/))

### Safety at home

- `10` `FAM-6` **Never abuse or neglect anyone in your family; protect those at risk, and help them find safety.** Scripture asks husbands not to be "bitter against" their wives and fathers not to "provoke" their children, and calls God "a father of the fatherless, and a defender of the widows". The US Catholic bishops state "that violence against women, inside or outside the home, is never justified", that violence in any form "is sinful; often, it is a crime as well", and that "no person is expected to stay in an abusive marriage"; Pope Francis writes that separation can become "morally necessary" to remove "the more vulnerable spouse or young children from serious injury due to abuse and violence"; the Church of England's guidance for those who hear of domestic abuse says "Prioritise safety", warns against pressing someone to "reconcile or forgive", and says to contact the emergency services when someone is in immediate danger; and the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "leaving them to wrong, temptation, and danger" among the sins of those responsible for others. ([Colossians 3:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V19), [Ephesians 6:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V4), [Psalm 68:5–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA068.htm#V5), [USCCB, When I Call for Help](https://www.usccb.org/topics/marriage-and-family-life-ministries/when-i-call-help-pastoral-response-domestic-violence), [Amoris laetitia, 241](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf), [Church of England, Domestic abuse](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/supporting-victims-and-survivors/domestic-abuse), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 130](https://www.opc.org/lc.html))

## How to apply

- `8` [Amoris laetitia, 205–216](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf): marriage preparation, which Pope Francis asks Christian communities to offer to engaged couples, with individual meetings, since "the primary objective is to help each to learn how to love this very real person" they will marry. Supports `FAM-2` and `FAM-4`.
- `7` [Church of England, Domestic abuse](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/supporting-victims-and-survivors/domestic-abuse): practical guidance for anyone in a church who hears a disclosure of domestic abuse: listen without judgement, prioritise safety, respect the person's choices, pass concerns to the safeguarding officer, and don't promise confidentiality, investigate alone or offer couples counselling where abuse is suspected. Supports `FAM-6`.
- `7` [Luther's Small Catechism, Daily Prayers](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/daily-prayers/): "How the head of the family should teach his household to pray morning and evening", with the Creed and the Lord's Prayer: a simple pattern of family prayer. Supports `FAM-3`.

## Open questions

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2380–2391](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P87.HTM): may a divorced person marry again? The Catholic Church teaches that a ratified and consummated marriage between the baptised "cannot be dissolved by any human power or for any reason other than death", though separation, and even civil divorce, can be tolerated in some cases ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1649–1650](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P55.HTM)). The Orthodox churches regard divorce as contrary to Christ's teaching, yet "through penance" have "a service of second marriage" ([Orthodox Church in America, Marriage](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/marriage)). The Westminster Confession allows divorce, and remarriage of the innocent party, for adultery or "such willful desertion as can no way be remedied" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 24.5–24.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Anglican Theology, 5.2](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/AnglicanTheology#section5.2): may married couples use contraception? The Catholic Church excludes "any action" specifically intended to prevent procreation, while accepting periodic continence ([Humanae vitae, 14 and 16](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_p-vi_enc_25071968_humanae-vitae.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2370](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P86.HTM)). The Russian Orthodox Church teaches that means which do not end a conceived life "cannot be equated with abortion in the least", while warning against refusing children "on egoistic grounds" ([Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XII.3](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xii/)). The Lambeth Conference of 1930 recognised that contraception "may sometimes be right", and in 1958 called family planning "a positive choice before God", as this encyclopaedia entry reports.
- `7` [St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Queer Theology, 6.1](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/QueerTheology#section6.1): may the church marry or bless same-sex couples? The Catholic Church teaches that homosexual acts are "intrinsically disordered", and that homosexual persons "must be accepted with respect, compassion, and sensitivity" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2357–2359](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P85.HTM)). Orthodox teaching counts sexual relations between members of the same sex as "contrary to the human perfection revealed by God in Christ" ([Orthodox Church in America, Marriage](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/marriage)), and the Baptist Faith and Message defines marriage as "the uniting of one man and one woman in covenant commitment for a lifetime" ([Baptist Faith and Message, XVIII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xviii)). The Episcopal Church amended its canons in 2015 "to permit any couple the rite of holy matrimony" ([The Episcopal Church, LGBTQ+ in the Church](https://www.episcopalchurch.org/who-we-are/ministries/gender-justice/lgbtq-in-the-church/)), and in the Church of England ministers may offer same-sex couples the Prayers of Love and Faith, while the church says "There are still deep divisions" ([Church of England, Relationships, sexuality and marriage](https://www.churchofengland.org/about/our-views/relationships-sexuality-and-marriage)).
- `7` [Baptist Faith and Message, XVIII: The Family](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xviii): how are the roles of husband and wife ordered? The Baptist Faith and Message holds that husband and wife "are of equal worth before God", that a husband is "to provide for, to protect, and to lead his family", and that a wife "is to submit herself graciously to the servant leadership of her husband". Pope John Paul II taught that the "subjection" of the wife must be understood as a "mutual subjection" of both "out of reverence for Christ" ([Mulieris dignitatem, 24](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_letters/1988/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_19880815_mulieris-dignitatem.html)). The Orthodox Longer Catechism quotes Ephesians on the duties of both husbands and wives ([Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 590](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1577–1580](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4X.HTM): must clergy be celibate? In the Latin Catholic Church, ordained ministers other than permanent deacons are normally chosen from men "who live a celibate life", while in the Eastern Churches "married men can be ordained as deacons and priests" and bishops are chosen from among celibates. The Augsburg Confession teaches that it is lawful for priests to marry, since "not all men are fit to lead a single life" ([Augsburg Confession, XXIII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-marriage-of-priests/)).
