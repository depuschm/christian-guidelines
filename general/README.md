# General guidelines

Guidance for every Christian, whatever their church, and for anyone who wants to understand how Christians are taught to live. Each rule states what the churches broadly agree on, and cites Scripture and the teaching of more than one church family ([ADR-0006](../docs/decisions/0006-write-rules-only-where-the-churches-agree.md)). The [Bible book pages](../bible/README.md) show where each teaching comes from in Scripture, the [areas of life](../specific/README.md) build on these files, and the [church pages](../traditions/README.md) show what each church teaches in its own terms.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Start here

If you only read three sources beyond the Bible, read these:

- `9` [The Nicene Creed](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-nicene-creed) (English Language Liturgical Consultation): the creed of the councils of Nicaea and Constantinople (325 and 381), the statement of faith shared most widely across the churches, in the agreed ecumenical English text. Its notes explain why the words "and the Son" are in brackets: they were added in the West, and each church decides whether to say them.
- `9` [The Apostles' Creed](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-apostles-creed) (English Language Liturgical Consultation): a creed that grew out of the questions asked at baptism, in the agreed ecumenical English text, with notes on its origin.
- `8` [Christian Ethics](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianEthics) (Esther D. Reed, Dion Forster, Rudolf von Sinner and others, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): how Christians reason about how to live, the place of the Bible, and Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Pentecostal and liberation perspectives.
