# Repentance and forgiveness

Jesus began his preaching with a call to repent ([Mark 1:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK01.htm#V15)), and taught his disciples to pray "Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors" ([Matthew 6:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V12)). This file covers turning back to God, confessing sin, receiving and giving forgiveness, putting things right with others, not taking revenge, and helping those who fall. Love of enemies is in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md). The churches differ on how the church takes part in God's forgiveness, for example through confession to a priest; see "Open questions" and the [church pages](../traditions/README.md).

Forgiving someone doesn't mean excusing abuse, forgetting it, or staying in danger. The Catholic bishops of the United States write that forgiveness "is not permission to repeat the abuse", and that "no person is expected to stay in an abusive marriage" ([USCCB, When I Call for Help](https://www.usccb.org/topics/marriage-and-family-life-ministries/when-i-call-help-pastoral-response-domestic-violence)). The Church of England's safeguarding guidance warns those who hear a disclosure of abuse not to pressure the person to "reconcile or forgive", because this can increase the risk ([Church of England, Domestic abuse](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/supporting-victims-and-survivors/domestic-abuse)). If you or someone else is in danger, contact the emergency services or a domestic abuse helpline first.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Westminster Confession of Faith, chapter 15: Of Repentance unto Life](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html) (1646, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): what repentance is, why every sinner needs it, the duty to repent of particular sins, and confession to God and to those we have offended.
- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): Q&A 88–90 describe genuine repentance as "the dying-away of the old self, and the rising-to-life of the new", and Q&A 126 explains the prayer for forgiveness.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1430–1433](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4A.HTM): Catholic teaching on interior repentance, "a radical reorientation of our whole life", and on conversion as first of all a work of God's grace. The paragraphs that follow set out the forms of penance in Christian life and the sacrament of Penance and Reconciliation.
- `7` [Augsburg Confession, Article XII: Of Repentance](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-repentance/) (1530): the Lutheran teaching that repentance has two parts, contrition and faith in the forgiveness of sins for Christ's sake, and that "good works are bound to follow".
- `7` [Penance](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/penance) (Thomas Hopko, The Orthodox Faith, Orthodox Church in America): the Orthodox sacrament of penance, with its three elements of sorrow for sin, confession and the prayer of absolution, fulfilled in Holy Communion and reconciliation "with God and all men".

## Key rules

### Turning back to God

- `9` `FORG-2` **Repent: admit your sins, turn away from them and turn back to God.** Jesus began his preaching with "Repent, and believe in the Good News", and Peter called his hearers to "repent therefore, and turn again". The churches teach that repentance is sorrow for sin and a turning to God with the resolve to change, made possible by his grace and trusting in his mercy. ([Mark 1:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK01.htm#V15), [Acts 3:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT03.htm#V19), [Psalm 51](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA051.htm), [1 John 1:8–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1JN01.htm#V8), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1430–1433](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4A.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 15.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 88–90](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Augsburg Confession, XII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-repentance/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 353](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html))
- `8` `FORG-3` **Confess your sins to God, trusting his forgiveness.** "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and righteous to forgive us the sins", and in Jesus' parable the tax collector prays "God, be merciful to me, a sinner!" The Catechism of the Catholic Church calls asking forgiveness "the first movement of the prayer of petition", the Westminster Confession teaches that everyone "is bound to make private confession of his sins to God", and Luther's Small Catechism teaches that "before God we should plead guilty of all sins". ([1 John 1:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1JN01.htm#V9), [Psalm 32:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA032.htm#V5), [Luke 15:11–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK15.htm#V11), [Luke 18:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK18.htm#V13), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2631](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P97.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 15.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Luther's Small Catechism, How Christians Should Be Taught to Confess](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/how-christians-confess/))

### Forgiving others

- `9` `FORG-1` **Forgive those who wrong you, as God has forgiven you.** Jesus taught his disciples to pray "Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors", told Peter to forgive "until seventy times seven", and Paul asks Christians to forgive "just as God also in Christ forgave you". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that this forgiveness has "no limit or measure", and that although we cannot stop feeling an offence, the heart open to the Holy Spirit can turn injury into compassion. The Heidelberg and Westminster catechisms, Luther's Small Catechism and the Orthodox Longer Catechism all teach that those who pray to be forgiven must forgive others from the heart. ([Matthew 6:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V12), [Matthew 6:14–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V14), [Matthew 18:21–35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V21), [Ephesians 4:32](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V32), [Colossians 3:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V13), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2838–2845](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PAB.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 126](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 194](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Lord's Prayer, fifth petition](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-lords-prayer/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 424–426](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html))
- `8` `FORG-5` **Don't take revenge: leave judgement to God and to just authorities.** Paul writes "Repay no one evil for evil" and "Vengeance belongs to me; I will repay, says the Lord", and Jesus tells his followers to turn the other cheek. The Heidelberg Catechism asks believers to "put away all desire for revenge" and notes that "government is armed with the sword" to prevent murder; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "desire of revenge" among the sins against the sixth commandment; and the Catechism of the Catholic Church calls the desire for vengeance in order to do evil to someone "illicit". ([Romans 12:17–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V17), [Matthew 5:38–39](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V38), [Leviticus 19:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV19.htm#V18), [Romans 13:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM13.htm#V4), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 105](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 136](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2302](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P81.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2262](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7Z.HTM))

### Putting things right

- `8` `FORG-4` **Seek to be reconciled with those you have wronged, and put right what you can.** Jesus tells anyone who remembers at the altar that a brother has something against them to "first be reconciled to your brother", and Zacchaeus promises "I restore four times as much" to those he cheated. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that one "must do what is possible in order to repair the harm", such as returning stolen goods; the Westminster Confession asks those who have given offence to declare their repentance to those they offended, who are then to be reconciled to them; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism asks those who pray to be "in peace and charity with all men". ([Matthew 5:23–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V23), [Luke 19:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK19.htm#V8), [2 Corinthians 5:18–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO05.htm#V18), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1459](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4D.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 15.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 135](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 426](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iii.html), [Orthodox Church in America, Penance](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/penance))
- `7` `FORG-6` **Help those who fall to find their way back, gently.** Paul asks believers to "restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness", James praises whoever turns a sinner back, and Jesus teaches to speak to a brother who sins first "between you and him alone". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that charity "demands beneficence and fraternal correction", and Luther's Large Catechism asks Christians not to spread a neighbour's sin to others but to "admonish him privately that he may amend". ([Galatians 6:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL06.htm#V1), [James 5:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS05.htm#V19), [Matthew 18:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V15), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1829](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P66.HTM), [Luther's Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment, 276](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/))

## How to apply

- `7` [The General Confession at Morning Prayer](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/order-morning-prayer) (Church of England, Book of Common Prayer): a confession the congregation says together ("We have left undone those things which we ought to have done, And we have done those things which we ought not to have done"), followed by the declaration of God's forgiveness. A short form for confessing sin to God, alone or together. Supports `FORG-2` and `FORG-3`.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1480–1484](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4H.HTM): how the **Catholic Church** celebrates the sacrament of Penance: the word of God and an exhortation to repentance, confession to the priest, the acceptance of a penance, absolution, and thanksgiving. Supports `FORG-2` and `FORG-3`.
- `7` [Luther's Small Catechism, How Christians Should Be Taught to Confess](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/how-christians-confess/): the **Lutheran** form of private confession and absolution: before God, plead guilty of all sins; before the confessor, confess those you know and feel in your heart; then receive absolution "as from God Himself". Supports `FORG-2` and `FORG-3`.
- `7` [Penance](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/penance) (Orthodox Church in America): in the **Orthodox churches**, Christians are expected to make use of this sacrament periodically, to humble themselves before God and receive guidance from their pastor, and its fulfilment includes genuine reconciliation with others. Supports `FORG-2` and `FORG-4`.

## Open questions

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1450–1460](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4D.HTM): is confession to a priest needed for the forgiveness of grave sins? The **Catholic Church** teaches that "confession to a priest is an essential part of the sacrament of Penance", in which all mortal sins must be confessed. The **Orthodox churches** hold penance to be a sacrament in which the priest's prayer of absolution bestows God's forgiveness, and which is necessary before Holy Communion after grave sins ([Orthodox Church in America, Penance](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/penance), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 351](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html)). **Lutheran** churches retain private absolution, but do not require a list of all sins ([Augsburg Confession, XI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-confession/)). The **Westminster Confession** teaches private confession to God, and confession to those one has offended ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 15.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1030–1032](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2N.HTM): is there a purification after death, and can the church grant indulgences? The **Catholic Church** teaches a final purification, purgatory, for those who die in God's grace but still imperfectly purified, and grants indulgences, a remission of the temporal punishment due to sins already forgiven ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1471–1479](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4G.HTM)). Article XXII of the **Church of England** rejects the Roman doctrine of purgatory and pardons as without warrant in Scripture ([Articles of Religion, XXII](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#XXII)), and the **Westminster Confession** holds that Scripture acknowledges no place for souls after death besides heaven and hell ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 32.1](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
