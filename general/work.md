# Work and rest

God put the first human in the garden "to cultivate and keep it" ([Genesis 2:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN02.htm#V15)), and the churches teach that work, paid or unpaid, is part of a good human life and a way to serve God and others. This file covers work itself: working well, paying and treating workers justly, the dignity of every kind of honest work, the rest owed to workers, work as a calling, and providing for yourself and others. Keeping a day of rest and worship for yourself is `WOR-5` in [Worship and the sacraments](worship.md). When the New Testament letters speak to slaves and masters, they address the slavery of the Roman world. The churches today condemn slavery: the Catechism of the Catholic Church calls it "a sin against the dignity of persons and their fundamental rights to reduce them by violence to their productive value or to a source of profit", recalling Paul's plea that a master receive his slave "no longer as a slave, but more than a slave, a beloved brother", and the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "man-stealing" among the sins against the eighth commandment ([Philemon 15–16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHM01.htm#V15), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2414](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html)). Luther's Small Catechism already applied the letters' teaching to "Hired Men, and Laborers" and their masters ([Small Catechism, Table of Duties](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/table-of-duties/)), and this file applies it to employees and employers today.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (1648, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Q. 141–142 list the duties and sins of the eighth commandment, from "a lawful calling, and diligence in it" to "oppression, extortion" and withholding from a neighbour what belongs to him; Q. 118 and 129–130 set out what those in charge owe to the people under them.
- `8` [Laborem exercens](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_14091981_laborem-exercens.html) (John Paul II, 1981): the encyclical on human work: why work is "for man" and not man "for work" (6), just wages and the right to rest (19), and a spirituality of work as sharing in God's work (24–27).
- `8` [Rerum novarum](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_15051891_rerum-novarum.html) (Leo XIII, 1891): the first modern papal encyclical on the condition of workers: the duties of employers and workers (20), rest and working hours (41–42) and a living wage (45).
- `8` [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html) (Pontifical Council for Justice and Peace, 2004): chapter six, on human work (255–322), sets out the Bible's teaching on work, the dignity of work and rest from work, the right to work, and the rights of workers, including fair pay.
- `8` [The Cape Town Commitment](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment) (Lausanne Movement, 2010): the evangelical statement whose part IIA, section 3, "Truth and the workplace", brings "the whole of our working lives within the sphere of ministry".
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, The Ten Commandments](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/) (1529): paragraph 83 on rest for those who work all week, 145 on the worth of ordinary daily work, and 222–253 on the seventh commandment, which counts lazy, unfaithful work and overcharging as theft.
- `7` [The Longer Catechism of the Orthodox, Catholic, Eastern Church, Part Three](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html) (St Philaret of Moscow, 1830, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): Q. 553 on idleness and "works befitting their calling", and Q. 592–595 on the sins against the eighth commandment, including neglected work and exploiting the labour of others.

## Key rules

### Workers and employers

- `9` `WORK-2` **Pay workers fairly and on time, and treat them justly.** The Law commands "In his day you shall give him his wages, neither shall the sun go down on it", and James warns that "the wages of the labourers who mowed your fields, which you have kept back by fraud, cry out". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "a just wage is the legitimate fruit of work" and that to refuse or withhold it "can be a grave injustice", and Leo XIII's *Rerum novarum* that wages "ought not to be insufficient to support a frugal and well-behaved wage-earner"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "oppression, extortion" and "withholding from our neighbor what belongs to him" among the sins against the eighth commandment; the Orthodox Longer Catechism condemns those who "make their own advantage of the property, the labors, or even the misfortunes of others"; and the Church of England's General Synod in 2024 encouraged all its parishes, dioceses and other bodies to pay "at a minimum, the living wage". ([Leviticus 19:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV19.htm#V13), [Deuteronomy 24:14–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU24.htm#V14), [James 5:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS05.htm#V4), [Colossians 4:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V1), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2434](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Rerum novarum, 45](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_15051891_rerum-novarum.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Church of England, General Synod on work and human dignity, 2024](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/synod-affirms-work-key-human-dignity-and-purpose-face-ai-revolution))
- `8` `WORK-3` **Respect the dignity of every worker and every kind of honest work.** Paul reminds masters that their own Master is in heaven, "and there is no partiality with him". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "work is for man, not man for work", and *Rerum novarum* asks employers "not to look upon their work people as their bondsmen, but to respect in every man his dignity as a person"; Luther's Large Catechism says that to "perform your daily domestic task" is better "than all the sanctity and ascetic life of monks"; and the Church of England's General Synod affirmed "the dignity and value of purposeful work, whether paid or unpaid". ([Genesis 2:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN02.htm#V15), [Colossians 3:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V23), [Ephesians 6:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V9), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2428](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Laborem exercens, 6](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_14091981_laborem-exercens.html), [Rerum novarum, 20](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_15051891_rerum-novarum.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Fourth Commandment, 145](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Church of England, General Synod on work and human dignity, 2024](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/synod-affirms-work-key-human-dignity-and-purpose-face-ai-revolution))
- `8` `WORK-4` **Don't overwork yourself or others: give those who work for you their rest.** The commandment gives the Sabbath rest to servants and strangers too, "that your male servant and your female servant may rest as well as you", and Jesus told his busy disciples "Come away into a deserted place, and rest awhile". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "human life has a rhythm of work and rest" and that employers have an obligation toward their employees to give them time for rest, and *Rerum novarum* that daily work should not be "protracted over longer hours than strength admits"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism condemns masters who "wear out their dependents by excessive imposts or tasks"; Luther's Large Catechism explains that holy days are kept first of all so that those "who have been attending to their work and trade the whole week" may "rest and be refreshed"; and the Church of England's General Synod asked its bodies "to have a regard for work/life balance and dignity at work". ([Deuteronomy 5:12–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU05.htm#V12), [Exodus 23:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO23.htm#V12), [Mark 6:31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK06.htm#V31), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2184–2187](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7O.HTM), [Rerum novarum, 42](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_15051891_rerum-novarum.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Third Commandment, 83](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Church of England, General Synod on work and human dignity, 2024](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/synod-affirms-work-key-human-dignity-and-purpose-face-ai-revolution))

### Working well

- `8` `WORK-1` **Work honestly and well, as serving God.** Paul writes "whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men", and asks those who were not working "that they work with quietness and eat their own bread". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "work is a duty" and that it "honors the Creator's gifts"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "a lawful calling, and diligence in it" among the duties of the eighth commandment; Luther's Large Catechism counts it as theft when workers are "lazy and unfaithful in their work"; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism calls it "eating the bread of idleness" when people take "pay for work, which they neglect". ([Colossians 3:23–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V23), [Ephesians 4:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V28), [2 Thessalonians 3:10–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH03.htm#V10), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2427](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 141](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Seventh Commandment, 225–226](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))
- `7` `WORK-5` **See your work as a calling in which you serve God and your neighbour.** Paul tells believers "as God has called each, so let him walk", and "whatever you do, in word or in deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that work "can be a means of sanctification"; the Augsburg Confession places "evangelical perfection" in the fear of God and faith, not "in forsaking civil offices", and teaches that the gospel requires "that charity be practiced in such ordinances"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism speaks of "works befitting their calling"; and the Lausanne Movement's Cape Town Commitment rejects "the falsehood of a 'sacred-secular divide'" and encourages believers to see their "daily ministry and mission as being wherever God has called them to work". ([1 Corinthians 7:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO07.htm#V17), [Colossians 3:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V17), [1 Peter 4:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE04.htm#V10), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2427](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Augsburg Confession, XVI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-civil-affairs/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 553](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Cape Town Commitment, IIA.3](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment))
- `7` `WORK-6` **Provide for yourself and your family by your work where you can, and help those who can't.** Paul asks believers "to work with your own hands" so that they "may have need of nothing", and to labour "that he may have something to give to him who has need"; whoever "doesn't provide for his own" has "denied the faith". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "everyone should be able to draw from work the means of providing for his life and that of his family"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks believers to "work faithfully so that I may share with those in need"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and the necessities of others" among the duties of the eighth commandment; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism counts it as theft when those "who are able to support themselves by work" live on alms instead, and enjoins "mercy to the poor". ([1 Thessalonians 4:11–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TH04.htm#V11), [1 Timothy 5:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI05.htm#V8), [Ephesians 4:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V28), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2428](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 111](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 141](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593–595](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))

## How to apply

- `8` [Cape Town Commitment, IIA.3: Truth and the workplace](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment) (Lausanne Movement): a call to churches to "equip the saints for works of service" in every part of their lives: to challenge "the tendency to see ministry and mission" as mainly the work of church-paid ministers, to support people "in the community and in the workplace", and to train all believers "in whole-life discipleship". Supports `WORK-5` and `WORK-1`.
- `7` [Church of England, General Synod on work and human dignity, 2024](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/synod-affirms-work-key-human-dignity-and-purpose-face-ai-revolution): a church as an employer: the motion, agreed with its amendments, encourages parishes, dioceses, cathedrals and other church bodies "to implement, at a minimum, the living wage for employees and to have a regard for work/life balance and dignity at work". Supports `WORK-2`, `WORK-3` and `WORK-4`.
- `6` [What is the real Living Wage?](https://www.livingwage.org.uk/what-real-living-wage) (Living Wage Foundation): in the United Kingdom, a wage rate "independently calculated and rooted in the cost of living", which employers pay voluntarily and for which they can be accredited, as the Church of England's General Synod encouraged church bodies to do. Supports `WORK-2`.

## Open questions

- `7` [Christian Ethics, 7.2: Just war reasoning?](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianEthics#section7.2) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): may a Christian serve as a soldier? The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that those who serve in the armed forces "truly contribute to the common good" if they do their duty honourably, and asks that conscientious objectors be provided for ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2310–2311](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P81.HTM)); the Augsburg Confession holds that it is right for Christians "to engage in just wars, to serve as soldiers" ([Augsburg Confession, XVI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-civil-affairs/)), and the Westminster Confession that rulers may "wage war, upon just and necessary occasion" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 23.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). The Mennonite Confession of Faith says "we do not prepare for war, or participate in war or military service" ([Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 22](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/peace-justice-and-nonresistance/)), and Quakers deny "all outward wars, and strife, and fightings with outward weapons" ([Quaker faith & practice, 24.04](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/24-04/)). See also the open question on love of enemies and force in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md).
