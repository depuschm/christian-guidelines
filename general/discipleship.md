# Growing as a disciple

Jesus called people to follow him, and sent his disciples to "make disciples of all nations, baptising them", "teaching them to observe all things that I commanded you" ([Mark 1:16–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK01.htm#V16), [Matthew 28:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT28.htm#V19)). This file covers following Jesus, growing in the fruit of the Spirit, the disciplines of the Christian life, using your gifts to serve others, companions on the way, and keeping going when it is costly. Reading the Bible, prayer and worship each have their own file: [Reading Scripture](scripture.md), [Prayer](prayer.md) and [Worship and the sacraments](worship.md). The churches agree that growth in holiness is the work of God's grace; they differ on how far it can go in this life and on whether good works can be called meritorious, which are listed under "Open questions".

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Lumen gentium, chapter V: The universal call to holiness in the Church](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1964): the Catholic teaching that "all the faithful of Christ of whatever rank or status, are called to the fullness of the Christian life and to the perfection of charity" (40), and the ways of holiness in each state of life.
- `9` [Westminster Confession of Faith, chapters 13 and 16](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html) (1646, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): sanctification, by which believers are "more and more quickened and strengthened in all saving graces", and good works as "the fruits and evidences of a true and lively faith".
- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism, Part III: Gratitude](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): Q&A 86–91 explain why the redeemed do good works, as Christ "is also restoring us by his Spirit into his image", and what genuine conversion is.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1691–1698](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P5D.HTM): the introduction to Catholic moral teaching, "Life in Christ": Christians following Christ, united with him and led by the Holy Spirit.
- `7` [The Virtues](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-virtues/the-virtues) (Thomas Hopko, The Orthodox Faith, Orthodox Church in America): the fruits of the Holy Spirit as the Christian virtues, with a page on each, such as patience, courage, humility and self-control.
- `7` [The Importance of Fasting and Its Observance Today](https://www.holycouncil.org/fasting) (Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church, 2016): fasting and spiritual struggle in Orthodox life, and the goal of "deification by grace".

## Key rules

### Following Jesus

- `9` `DISC-1` **Follow Jesus: put him first, and learn from his life and teaching.** Jesus said "If anyone desires to come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me", invited the weary to "learn from me", and told those who believed him "If you remain in my word, then you are truly my disciples". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that Christians, "following Christ and united with him", strive to follow his example, and the Second Vatican Council that the faithful "must follow in His footsteps". The Heidelberg Catechism teaches that Christ makes believers "wholeheartedly willing and ready from now on to live for him"; the Orthodox Faith calls following Christ's way and doing what he does "the essence of spiritual life"; and the Lausanne Covenant that "Jesus still calls all who would follow him to deny themselves, take up their cross". ([Mark 1:16–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK01.htm#V16), [Luke 9:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK09.htm#V23), [Matthew 11:28–30](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT11.htm#V28), [John 8:31–32](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN08.htm#V31), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1694](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P5D.HTM), [Lumen gentium, 40](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 1](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Hopko, The Orthodox Faith: Christ](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/orthodox-spirituality/christ), [Lausanne Covenant, 4](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant))
- `8` `DISC-6` **Keep following Jesus when it is costly.** Hebrews urges believers to "run with perseverance the race that is set before us, looking to Jesus", James calls blessed "a person who endures temptation", and Jesus blesses those persecuted "for righteousness' sake". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that all "must be prepared to confess Christ before men and to follow him along the way of the Cross", the Lausanne Covenant that "we have no liberty to conceal the cost of discipleship", the Heidelberg Catechism that believers are "to strive with a free conscience against sin and the devil in this life", and the Orthodox Faith that patience means to "wait on the Lord through all tribulations and trials with courage and hope". ([Hebrews 12:1–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB12.htm#V1), [James 1:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V12), [Matthew 5:10–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V10), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1816](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P66.HTM), [Lausanne Covenant, 4](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 32](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Hopko, The Orthodox Faith: Patience](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-virtues/patience))

### Growing in grace

- `8` `DISC-2` **Grow in the fruit of the Spirit, such as love, joy, peace, patience and kindness.** Paul writes that "the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, and self-control", and 2 Peter asks believers to add moral excellence, knowledge, self-control and perseverance to their faith. In the Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification, Catholics and Lutherans confess together that good works "follow justification and are its fruits", and both hold that there is growth in grace. The Catechism of the Catholic Church calls the fruits of the Spirit "perfections that the Holy Spirit forms in us"; the Westminster Confession teaches that believers are "more and more quickened and strengthened in all saving graces" and "grow in grace"; and the Orthodox Faith teaches that "these fruits of the Spirit are often called the Christian virtues". ([Galatians 5:22–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL05.htm#V22), [2 Peter 1:5–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2PE01.htm#V5), [Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification, 37–39](https://www.christianunity.va/content/dam/unitacristiani/Relazioni%20ecumeniche/Sezione%20occidentale/Dialogo%20multilaterale/Edizione_20_Anniversario_DCDJ/EN%2020th%20Anniversary%20Edition%20joint_declaration_2019.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1832](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P67.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 13.1–13.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Hopko, The Orthodox Faith: The Virtues](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-virtues/the-virtues))
- `7` `DISC-3` **Practise the disciplines of the Christian life, such as Scripture, prayer, fasting, worship and service, as ways to grow in grace, not as achievements.** Jesus expects his followers to fast, in secret and not "to be seen by men to be fasting", Paul writes "Exercise yourself towards godliness", and the church at Antioch "fasted and prayed" before sending out Barnabas and Saul. The Catechism of the Catholic Church names fasting, prayer and almsgiving as expressing "conversion in relation to oneself, to God, and to others"; the Augsburg Confession teaches that every Christian ought to train himself with "bodily exercises and labors", "but not that we may merit grace" by them; the Westminster Confession counts "solemn fastings" among the parts of worship for special occasions; the General Rules of the Methodist societies ask members to attend on worship, the Lord's Supper, prayer, "searching the Scriptures" and "fasting or abstinence"; and the Orthodox Faith teaches that Christians fast not as a "reparation" for sin but so that "the free gift of salvation in Christ might produce great fruit in their lives". ([Matthew 6:16–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V16), [1 Timothy 4:7–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI04.htm#V7), [Acts 13:2–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT13.htm#V2), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1434](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4B.HTM), [Augsburg Confession, XXVI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-the-distinction-of-meats/), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.5](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [The General Rules of the Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/the-general-rules-of-the-methodist-church), [Hopko, The Orthodox Faith: Fasting](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/fasting))

### Serving and growing together

- `8` `DISC-4` **Use the gifts God has given you to serve others.** "As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another", and Jesus "came not to be served but to serve". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that charisms, graces of the Holy Spirit "whether extraordinary or simple and humble", are "ordered as they are to her building up, to the good of men, and to the needs of the world"; the Heidelberg Catechism that "each member should consider it a duty to use these gifts readily and joyfully for the service and enrichment of the other members"; and the Westminster Confession that believers "have communion in each other's gifts and graces" and are bound to the duties that serve "their mutual good". ([1 Peter 4:10–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE04.htm#V10), [Romans 12:4–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V4), [Mark 10:43–45](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK10.htm#V43), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 799–800](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P28.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 55](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 26.1](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html))
- `7` `DISC-5` **Find friends and mentors in the faith who help you grow, and help others to grow.** "Iron sharpens iron; so a man sharpens his friend's countenance", Hebrews asks believers to "exhort one another day by day", and Paul asks Timothy to pass on what he learned "to faithful men who will be able to teach others also". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the Holy Spirit gives some of the faithful the gifts of "wisdom, faith and discernment" for spiritual direction; the Westminster Confession that believers are bound to "maintain an holy fellowship and communion" in "such other spiritual services as tend to their mutual edification"; and the General Rules describe the early Methodist societies as people "united in order to pray together, to receive the word of exhortation, and to watch over one another in love". ([Proverbs 27:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO27.htm#V17), [Hebrews 3:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB03.htm#V13), [2 Timothy 2:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TI02.htm#V2), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2690](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9H.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 26.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [The General Rules of the Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/the-general-rules-of-the-methodist-church))

## How to apply

- `7` [The General Rules of the Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/the-general-rules-of-the-methodist-church) (The United Methodist Church): John Wesley's rule for the early Methodist societies: do no harm, do good, and attend on "all the ordinances of God", including worship, the Lord's Supper, prayer, "searching the Scriptures" and fasting. The societies met in "classes" of about twelve people who watched over one another in love. A simple rule of life, and a model for small groups. Supports `DISC-3` and `DISC-5`.
- `7` [Great Lent](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-church-year/great-lent) (The Orthodox Faith, Orthodox Church in America): in the **Orthodox churches**, the season before Pascha is "a time of renewed devotion: of prayer, fasting, and almsgiving". The **Catholic Church** keeps Lent and each Friday as "intense moments of the Church's penitential practice", for spiritual exercises, fasting, almsgiving and works of charity ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1438](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4B.HTM)). A yearly season for taking up the disciplines together. Supports `DISC-3`.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2690](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9H.HTM): spiritual direction, in which an experienced believer gifted with "wisdom, faith and discernment" helps another to grow in prayer and the Christian life. Supports `DISC-5`.

## Open questions

- `10` [Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification, 37–39](https://www.christianunity.va/content/dam/unitacristiani/Relazioni%20ecumeniche/Sezione%20occidentale/Dialogo%20multilaterale/Edizione_20_Anniversario_DCDJ/EN%2020th%20Anniversary%20Edition%20joint_declaration_2019.pdf) (Lutheran World Federation and Catholic Church, 1999): can good works be called meritorious? Lutherans and Catholics confess together that good works follow justification as its fruits. The **Catholic Church** affirms the "meritorious" character of good works, meaning that a reward in heaven is promised to them, while holding that justification "always remains the unmerited gift of grace" and that merit "is to be attributed in the first place to the grace of God" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2006–2011](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P70.HTM)). **Lutherans** see good works as "the fruits and signs of justification and not as one's own 'merits'", and teach that we should not rely on them "to merit justification before God" ([Augsburg Confession, VI](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-new-obedience/)). The **Westminster Confession** holds that "we cannot by our best works merit pardon of sin, or eternal life" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 16.5](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [Distinctive Wesleyan Emphases](https://www.umc.org/en/content/distinctive-wesleyan-emphases) (The Book of Discipline of The United Methodist Church, 2016): how far can Christians grow in holiness in this life? **Methodist** teaching holds that sanctifying grace "draws us toward the gift of Christian perfection", which Wesley described as a heart "habitually filled with the love of God and neighbor", and which is "the hope and expectation of the faithful"; Wesley defined it as the fullest possible love of God and neighbour ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Methodist Theology, 1.3](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/MethodistTheology#section1.3)). The **Westminster Confession** teaches that sanctification is "imperfect in this life, there abiding still some remnants of corruption in every part", although "the saints grow in grace" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 13.2–13.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). The **Catholic Church** teaches that all are called "to the fullness of Christian life and to the perfection of charity", by a way that "passes by way of the Cross" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2013–2015](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P71.HTM)). The **Orthodox churches** see the goal as "deification by grace", and teach that "ascesis and spiritual struggle, like the refinement of the perfect, are endless in this life" ([Holy and Great Council, The Importance of Fasting](https://www.holycouncil.org/fasting)).
