# Money, possessions and generosity

Jesus warned "You can't serve both God and Mammon", and taught "where your treasure is, there your heart will be also" ([Matthew 6:24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V24), [Matthew 6:19–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V19)). This file covers how Christians earn, owe, keep and give money: honesty in all dealings, generosity, freedom from greed, contentment, paying debts, and using possessions as stewards of what belongs to God. Fair wages and the treatment of workers will be covered in a planned topic on work and rest, and justice for the poor in a planned topic on justice. Love shown in deeds is in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (1648, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Q. 141–142 set out the duties and sins of the commandment against stealing, from "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce" and "giving and lending freely" to "covetousness" and "prodigality", and Q. 147–148 ask for "full contentment with our own condition".
- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): Q&A 110–111 explain that theft includes "all scheming and swindling", that God "forbids all greed and pointless squandering of his gifts", and that believers should "work faithfully so that I may share with those in need".
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2407–2418](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM): Catholic teaching on respect for other people's goods: theft and fraud, keeping contracts, paying debts and making restitution. The pages around it cover the universal destination of goods (2402–2406), love for the poor (2443–2449) and the tenth commandment against greed (2534–2557).
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, The Ten Commandments](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/) (1529): paragraphs 222–253 explain the seventh commandment as forbidding every kind of cheating "wherever there is trading or taking and giving of money", and commanding that we help those in want and "lend both to friends and foes".
- `7` [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 21: Christian Stewardship](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/christian-stewardship/) (Mennonite Church USA): "everything belongs to God", and Christians, as stewards of money and possessions, are "to live simply, practice mutual aid within the church, uphold economic justice, and give generously and cheerfully".
- `7` [Almsgiving](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/almsgiving) (Thomas Hopko, *The Orthodox Faith*, Orthodox Church in America): Orthodox teaching that "almsgiving goes together with fasting and prayer", that giving must be done in secret, and that people "are but stewards of what belongs to the Lord".
- `7` [The Longer Catechism of the Orthodox, Catholic, Eastern Church, Part Three](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html) (St Philaret of Moscow, 1830, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): Q. 592–596 on the commandment against stealing, including fraud, unpaid debts and mercy to the poor, and Q. 602–607 on the commandment against covetousness, which asks us "to be content with our lot".

## Key rules

### Honesty in money matters

- `9` `MONEY-1` **Don't steal or cheat: be honest in all your dealings.** The commandment says "You shall not steal", the Law asks for "just balances, just weights", and Paul writes "Let him who stole steal no more; but rather let him labour". The Catechism of the Catholic Church counts "business fraud", "work poorly done" and "tax evasion" as forms of theft; the Westminster Larger Catechism asks for "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce" and forbids "fraudulent dealing, false weights and measures"; the Heidelberg Catechism counts as theft "all scheming and swindling", "fraudulent merchandising" and "excessive interest"; Luther's Small Catechism forbids getting a neighbour's money or property "by false ware or dealing"; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism names as fraud passing off "bad wares for good" and using "false weights and measures". ([Exodus 20:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO20.htm#V15), [Leviticus 19:35–36](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV19.htm#V35), [Ephesians 4:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V28), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2408–2409](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 141–142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 110](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Luther's Small Catechism, Seventh Commandment](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))
- `7` `MONEY-5` **Repay what you owe, and don't take on debts you can't repay.** Paul writes "Owe no one anything, except to love one another"; the psalm says "The wicked borrow, and don't pay back, but the righteous give generously"; and Proverbs warns that "the borrower is servant to the lender". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that justice in exchanges "requires safeguarding property rights, paying debts, and fulfilling obligations freely contracted"; the Westminster Larger Catechism asks for "rendering to every one his due" and "avoiding unnecessary lawsuits, and suretiship, or other like engagements", and counts "prodigality" as a sin; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism counts it as fraud when people "conceal their effects to avoid paying their debts". ([Romans 13:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM13.htm#V8), [Psalm 37:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA037.htm#V21), [Proverbs 22:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO22.htm#V7), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2410–2411](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 141–142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))

### Generosity

- `9` `MONEY-2` **Give generously to people in need and to the work of the church.** Paul writes that "God loves a cheerful giver" and recalls the words of Jesus, "It is more blessed to give than to receive"; Proverbs says "He who has pity on the poor lends to the LORD". The Catechism of the Catholic Church calls giving alms to the poor "one of the chief witnesses to fraternal charity" and "a work of justice pleasing to God", and names among the precepts of the Church "the duty of providing for the material needs of the Church, each according to his abilities"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism teaches that "our abundance is given us by his Providence for the assistance of the poor"; the Westminster Larger Catechism asks for "giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and the necessities of others"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks believers to "work faithfully so that I may share with those in need"; and the Baptist Faith and Message asks Christians to "contribute of their means cheerfully, regularly, systematically, proportionately, and liberally". ([2 Corinthians 9:6–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO09.htm#V6), [Acts 20:35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT20.htm#V35), [Proverbs 19:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO19.htm#V17), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2443–2449](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8F.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2043](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P75.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 595](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 141](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 111](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Baptist Faith and Message, XIII: Stewardship](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xiii))

### Freedom from greed

- `8` `MONEY-3` **Don't let money rule you: guard against greed and the love of money.** Paul warns that "the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil", Jesus says "Beware! Keep yourselves from covetousness, for a man's life doesn't consist of the abundance of the things which he possesses", and "You can't serve both God and Mammon". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the tenth commandment "forbids greed and the desire to amass earthly goods without limit"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "covetousness; inordinate prizing and affecting worldly goods" as sins; the Heidelberg Catechism says that God "forbids all greed"; Luther's Large Catechism says the last commandments are "especially directed against envy and miserable avarice"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism forbids "all thoughts of avarice and ambition"; and the Mennonite Confession of Faith says "We cannot be true servants of God and let our lives be ruled by desire for wealth." ([1 Timothy 6:9–10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI06.htm#V9), [Luke 12:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK12.htm#V15), [Matthew 6:24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V24), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2535–2540](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8U.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 110](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Luther's Large Catechism, Ninth and Tenth Commandments, 310](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 606](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 21](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/christian-stewardship/))
- `8` `MONEY-4` **Be content with what you have.** Paul writes "I have learnt in whatever state I am, to be content in it" and "godliness with contentment is great gain", and Hebrews says "Be free from the love of money, content with such things as you have". The Westminster Larger Catechism asks for "such a full contentment with our own condition" and counts "discontentment with our own estate" as a sin; the Orthodox Longer Catechism names as a duty of the tenth commandment "to be content with our lot"; the Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "abandonment to the providence of the Father in heaven frees us from anxiety about tomorrow"; and the Mennonite Confession of Faith says that "we are not to be anxious about the necessities of life, but to seek first the kingdom of God". ([Philippians 4:11–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V11), [1 Timothy 6:6–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI06.htm#V6), [Hebrews 13:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB13.htm#V5), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 147–148](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 607](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2544–2547](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8W.HTM), [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 21](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/christian-stewardship/))

### Stewardship

- `7` `MONEY-6` **Use what you have as a steward who will give account to God.** In the parable of the talents the servants must give account of what they were entrusted with; Jesus says "He who is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much"; and Peter asks Christians to serve one another "as good managers of the grace of God". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "the ownership of any property makes its holder a steward of Providence, with the task of making it fruitful and communicating its benefits to others, first of all his family"; Orthodox teaching holds that people "are but stewards of what belongs to the Lord and should share the gifts of His creation with one another as much as they can"; the Heidelberg Catechism forbids "pointless squandering of his gifts"; the Baptist Faith and Message speaks of "a binding stewardship in their possessions"; and the Mennonite Confession of Faith says that "everything belongs to God, who calls us as the church to live as faithful stewards". ([Matthew 25:14–30](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT25.htm#V14), [Luke 16:10–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK16.htm#V10), [1 Peter 4:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE04.htm#V10), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2402–2406](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8A.HTM), [Orthodox Church in America, Almsgiving](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/almsgiving), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 110](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Baptist Faith and Message, XIII: Stewardship](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xiii), [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 21](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/christian-stewardship/))

## How to apply

- `8` [The Lausanne Covenant, 9](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant) (Lausanne Movement, 1974): a simple lifestyle for the sake of others. The evangelical signatories wrote that "those of us who live in affluent circumstances accept our duty to develop a simple life-style in order to contribute more generously to both relief and evangelism". Supports `MONEY-2`, `MONEY-3` and `MONEY-4`.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1434–1439](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4B.HTM): fasting, prayer and almsgiving together. The Catechism names "fasting, prayer, and almsgiving" as the three chief forms of penance, and Lent as a time for "fasting and almsgiving, and fraternal sharing"; Orthodox teaching likewise holds that "almsgiving goes together with fasting and prayer" and that alms should be given in secret ([Orthodox Church in America, Almsgiving](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/prayer-fasting-and-almsgiving/almsgiving), [Matthew 6:1–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V1)). Supports `MONEY-2` and `MONEY-3`.
- `7` [John Wesley, Sermon 50: The Use of Money](https://wesley.nnu.edu/john-wesley/the-sermons-of-john-wesley-1872-edition/sermon-50-the-use-of-money/) (Wesley Center Online): Wesley's three rules, "Gain all you can" by honest work, "Save all you can" by cutting needless expense, and "give all you can", since God placed us in the world "not as a proprietor, but a steward". He advises providing first what is needful for yourself and your household, then doing good to "the household of faith", then to all. Supports `MONEY-2` and `MONEY-6`.
- `7` [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 21: Christian Stewardship](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/christian-stewardship/): mutual aid within the church, "so that no one in the family of faith will be without the necessities of life", through a community of goods or other forms of financial sharing. Supports `MONEY-2`.

## Open questions

- `8` [Pentecostal Assemblies of Canada, Statement of Essential Truths and Positions and Practices, 6.5](https://paoc.org/docs/default-source/fellowship-services-documents/constitutions/2026/2026-statement-of-essential-truths-and-positions-and-practices.pdf?sfvrsn=9e0cc06a_4): must Christians give a tenth (a tithe) of their income? The Pentecostal Assemblies of Canada teach that "all Christians should conscientiously and systematically tithe their income to God", taking the Old Testament tithe as the measure of the New Testament's "grace of giving" ([Malachi 3:8–10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAL03.htm#V8), [Matthew 23:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT23.htm#V23)). The Baptist Faith and Message asks for giving "cheerfully, regularly, systematically, proportionately, and liberally" and cites the tithe passages, without naming a share ([Baptist Faith and Message, XIII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xiii)). The Catechism of the Catholic Church speaks of providing for the Church's needs "each according to his abilities", without a fixed share ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2043](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P75.HTM)), and Paul asks each to give "as he has determined in his heart, not grudgingly or under compulsion" ([2 Corinthians 9:6–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO09.htm#V6)).
- `7` [Lausanne Theology Working Group, A Statement on the Prosperity Gospel](https://lausanne.org/content/a-statement-on-the-prosperity-gospel) (Lausanne Movement): does God promise wealth and health to those who have enough faith and give? The working group describes the prosperity gospel as "the teaching that believers have a right to the blessings of health and wealth" which they can obtain through "positive confessions of faith" and "the faithful payments of tithes and offerings", and notes that it "cuts across denominational barriers". It affirms "a biblical vision of human prospering", but asks the Lausanne Movement to make "a very clear statement rejecting the excesses of prosperity teaching as incompatible with evangelical biblical Christianity". The St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology describes the growing place of prosperity theology among Pentecostals, driven by the Word of Faith movement, and Pentecostal responses that turn "away from theological principles of individual prosperity" towards questions of justice ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Pentecostal Theology, 4.3](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/PentecostalTheology#section4.3)).
