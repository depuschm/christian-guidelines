# Belonging to a church

The first Christians "continued steadfastly in the apostles' teaching and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and prayer" ([Acts 2:42](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT02.htm#V42)), and Paul calls the church the body of Christ, in which each member belongs to the others ([1 Corinthians 12:12–27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO12.htm#V12)). This file covers belonging to a local church, protecting children and vulnerable adults, working for unity, sharing in the church's life, the church's leaders, and handling disagreements. What the churches confess together about the Church is in [What the churches confess together](beliefs.md), worship and the sacraments are in [Worship and the sacraments](worship.md), and what each church teaches about the Church is on the [church pages](../traditions/README.md). A fuller area on church leadership and safeguarding is planned.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [The Church: Towards a Common Vision](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/The_Church_Towards_a_common_vision.pdf) (World Council of Churches, Faith and Order Paper No. 214, 2013): the Faith and Order Commission's convergence text on what the churches can say together about the Church: its unity and diversity, the local church, faith, sacraments and ministry, authority and primacy, and its service to the world, with the questions on which they still differ.
- `9` [Westminster Confession of Faith, chapters 25–26](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html) (1646, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Reformed teaching on the Church, visible and invisible, and on the communion of saints, which binds believers to worship together and to help one another.
- `9` [Lumen gentium](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1964): the Catholic Church's constitution on the Church: the Church as the people of God and the body of Christ, its bishops and the Pope, the calling of all the faithful, and its unity.
- `8` [Unitatis redintegratio](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_unitatis-redintegratio_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1964): the Catholic decree on ecumenism, which calls the restoration of unity among all Christians "one of the principal concerns" of the Council.
- `7` [Augsburg Confession, Article VII: Of the Church](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-the-church/) (1530): the Lutheran teaching that the Church is "the congregation of saints, in which the Gospel is rightly taught and the Sacraments are rightly administered".
- `7` [The Orthodox Faith: Church](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/the-symbol-of-faith/church) (Thomas Hopko, Orthodox Church in America): Orthodox teaching on the Church as one, holy, catholic and apostolic, and on each local church as the whole Church in its place.

## Key rules

### Belonging

- `9` `CHURCH-1` **Belong to a local church: worship, learn and live your faith with other Christians.** The first Christians met daily for teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread and prayer, and Hebrews warns against "forsaking our own assembling together". The World Council of Churches' Faith and Order Commission writes that most Christians could agree that the local church is "a community of baptized believers in which the word of God is preached, the apostolic faith confessed, the sacraments are celebrated". The Catechism of the Catholic Church calls the parish the place where "all the faithful can be gathered together for the Sunday celebration of the Eucharist", the Heidelberg Catechism confesses "of this community I am and always will be a living member", the Westminster Confession binds believers "to maintain an holy fellowship and communion in the worship of God", and Philaret's Orthodox catechism calls the Church "a divinely instituted community of men". ([Acts 2:42–47](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT02.htm#V42), [Hebrews 10:24–25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB10.htm#V24), [1 Corinthians 12:12–27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO12.htm#V12), [The Church: Towards a Common Vision, 31](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/The_Church_Towards_a_common_vision.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2179](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7O.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 54](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 26.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 252–253](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html))

### Protecting children and vulnerable adults

- `10` `CHURCH-6` **Protect children and vulnerable adults in the church, and report abuse to the church and to the authorities.** Jesus said that it would be better to be thrown into the sea with a millstone around one's neck than to cause "one of these little ones" to stumble. The Catholic Church's law *Vos estis lux mundi* says that the crimes of sexual abuse "cause physical, psychological and spiritual damage to the victims and harm the community of the faithful", obliges clergy and religious to report them promptly, and applies without prejudice to "any reporting obligations to the competent civil authorities". The Orthodox Church in America commits itself to "report allegations in accordance with the civil laws" and to cooperate with civil investigations. The Church of England holds that the care and protection of children and vulnerable adults "is the responsibility of the whole Church", and tells anyone worried about a child to contact "the local authority or the police". The Methodist Church in Britain's policy holds that "Everyone has the right to protection from abuse". ([Mark 9:42](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK09.htm#V42), [Matthew 18:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V10), [Luke 17:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK17.htm#V1), [Vos estis lux mundi, 2023](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 3.02](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf), [Church of England, Promoting a Safer Church](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0), [Church of England, Report a concern or abuse](https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/report-concern-or-abuse), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, Procedures and Guidance, 2](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf))

### Unity

- `9` `CHURCH-2` **Work for unity, in your own church and between the churches.** Jesus prayed for all who would believe "that they may all be one", and Paul asks Christians to be "eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the Church "must always pray and work to maintain, reinforce, and perfect the unity that Christ wills for her", and the Second Vatican Council asks Catholics to "take an active and intelligent part in the work of ecumenism". The Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church (2016) says that the Orthodox Church "has always worked for the restoration of Christian unity", and the Augsburg Confession teaches that for "the true unity of the Church it is enough to agree concerning the doctrine of the Gospel and the administration of the Sacraments". ([John 17:20–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN17.htm#V20), [Ephesians 4:1–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V1), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 820–821](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P29.HTM), [Unitatis redintegratio, 4](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_unitatis-redintegratio_en.html), [Holy and Great Council, Relations of the Orthodox Church with the Rest of the Christian World](https://www.holycouncil.org/rest-of-christian-world), [Augsburg Confession, VII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-the-church/), [The Church: Towards a Common Vision, 37](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/The_Church_Towards_a_common_vision.pdf))

### Sharing the church's life

- `8` `CHURCH-3` **Share in the church's life: serve, give and care for one another.** Paul compares the church to a body whose members serve one another with different gifts, and writes "Bear one another's burdens" and "God loves a cheerful giver". The Heidelberg Catechism asks each member to use their gifts "readily and joyfully for the service and enrichment of the other members", the Westminster Confession speaks of "relieving each other in outward things, according to their several abilities and necessities", and the Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "the faithful also have the duty of providing for the material needs of the Church, each according to his abilities". ([Romans 12:4–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V4), [Galatians 6:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL06.htm#V2), [2 Corinthians 9:6–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO09.htm#V6), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 55](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 26.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2043](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P75.HTM))

### Leaders

- `8` `CHURCH-4` **Respect and pray for those who lead the church, and hold them to account.** Hebrews asks Christians to remember their leaders, who "watch on behalf of your souls, as those who will give account", and 1 Timothy sets out how to weigh an accusation against an elder fairly. The Catechism of the Catholic Church asks the baptised to hold the Church's leaders "in respect and affection", the Westminster Larger Catechism counts those over us "in family, church, or commonwealth" among the superiors owed "prayer and thanksgiving for them", and Philaret's Orthodox catechism names "our spiritual pastors and teachers" among those to be honoured as parents. The churches also hold leaders to account: the Augsburg Confession teaches that when bishops "teach or ordain anything against the Gospel, then the congregations have a commandment of God prohibiting obedience", and the Catholic Church's *Vos estis lux mundi* sets out how reports of abuse, or of covering it up, by bishops and other leaders are to be investigated. ([Hebrews 13:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB13.htm#V7), [Hebrews 13:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB13.htm#V17), [1 Thessalonians 5:12–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TH05.htm#V12), [1 Timothy 5:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI05.htm#V19), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1269](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3N.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 124 and 127](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 560 and 563](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Augsburg Confession, XXVIII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-ecclesiastical-power/), [Vos estis lux mundi, 2023, art. 1 and 6](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html))

### Disagreements

- `8` `CHURCH-5` **Settle disagreements in the church directly and gently, not through gossip or division.** Jesus tells his disciples to go to someone who sins against them and "show him his fault between you and him alone", and only then to involve others; Paul pleads "that there be no divisions amongst you". Luther's Large Catechism calls this order "a precious and excellent teaching for governing well the tongue", the Heidelberg Catechism asks believers not to "gossip or slander", and the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "talebearing, whispering" among the sins against the ninth commandment. The Catechism of the Catholic Church warns against rash judgement and detraction and asks Christians to interpret others' words "in a favorable way", and Philaret's Orthodox catechism counts it as false witness to "slander any one behind his back". ([Matthew 18:15–17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V15), [Philippians 4:2–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V2), [1 Corinthians 1:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO01.htm#V10), [Luther's Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment, 276](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 112](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 145](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2477–2478](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8K.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 598](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))

## How to apply

- `7` [Report a concern or abuse](https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/report-concern-or-abuse) (Church of England): who to contact when someone may be at risk: emergency services if someone is in immediate danger, the safeguarding officers that every diocese and parish has, and the local authority or the police. Find out who your own church's safeguarding contact is before you need them. Supports `CHURCH-6`.
- `7` [Safeguarding Learning and Development Framework](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/safeguarding-learning-and-development-framework) (Church of England): the Church of England's safeguarding requirements and learning pathways for those who serve in the church. Supports `CHURCH-6`.
- `7` [Prevention of Sexual Misconduct](https://www.oca.org/about/sexual-misconduct) (Orthodox Church in America): background checks for those who work in the church, a child safety training programme, and a confidential line for reporting misconduct. Supports `CHURCH-6` and `CHURCH-4`.
- `7` [Week of Prayer for Christian Unity](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/week-of-prayer-for-christian-unity) (World Council of Churches): prayer for unity, traditionally from 18 to 25 January, with material published jointly by the WCC and the Catholic Dicastery for Promoting Christian Unity for local churches to pray together. Supports `CHURCH-2`.
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment, 276](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/): a simple rule for disagreements: speak to the person privately first, and if someone tells you about another's fault, send them to speak to that person rather than passing it on. Supports `CHURCH-5`.

## Open questions

- `9` [The Church: Towards a Common Vision, 31](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/The_Church_Towards_a_common_vision.pdf) (World Council of Churches): what is the Church, and where is it found? The Second Vatican Council teaches that the Church of Christ "subsists in the Catholic Church", while "many elements of sanctification and of truth are found outside of its visible structure" ([Lumen gentium, 8](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html)). The Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church speaks of "the Orthodox Church, as the One, Holy, Catholic, and Apostolic Church" ([Relations of the Orthodox Church with the Rest of the Christian World](https://www.holycouncil.org/rest-of-christian-world)). The Augsburg Confession finds the Church wherever the gospel is rightly taught and the sacraments rightly administered ([Augsburg Confession, VII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-the-church/)), and the Baptist Faith and Message describes a New Testament church as "an autonomous local congregation of baptized believers" ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VI](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/)).
- `9` [The Church: Towards a Common Vision, 52 and notes](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/The_Church_Towards_a_common_vision.pdf) (World Council of Churches): how should the church be ordered and governed? Faith and Order recalls that the churches have been described since 1927 as "episcopal", "presbyteral" and "congregational", and asks that oversight be exercised "in personal, collegial and communal ways". The Catholic Church teaches that the bishops, in communion with the Pope, are successors of the apostles ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 880–882](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2A.HTM)); the Presbyterian Church in America holds that the scriptural form of church government is "representative or presbyterian", through elders ([Book of Church Order, 1-1](https://www.pcaac.org/book-of-church-order/part-1-the-form-of-government/)); and the Baptist Faith and Message holds that each congregation "operates under the Lordship of Christ through democratic processes" ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VI](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/)).
- `8` [Ravenna Document, 41 and 43](https://www.christianunity.va/content/unitacristiani/en/dialoghi/sezione-orientale/chiese-ortodosse-di-tradizione-bizantina/commissione-mista-internazionale-per-il-dialogo-teologico-tra-la/documenti-di-dialogo/testo-in-inglese.html) (Joint International Commission for Theological Dialogue between the Catholic Church and the Orthodox Church, 2007): what is the place of the Bishop of Rome? The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the Pope has "full, supreme, and universal power over the whole Church" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 882](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2A.HTM)). In the Ravenna Document, Catholics and Orthodox agree that in the era of the undivided Church, Rome "occupied the first place in the taxis", the order of the churches, but "disagree, however, on the interpretation of the historical evidence" about the bishop of Rome's prerogatives. The Westminster Confession holds that "There is no other head of the church but the Lord Jesus Christ" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 25.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)), and Pope John Paul II invited other churches to "a patient and fraternal dialogue" on how the primacy is exercised ([Ut unum sint, 95–96](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_25051995_ut-unum-sint.html)).
