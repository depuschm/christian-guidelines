# Discernment and decisions

How Christians make choices, large and small: asking God for wisdom, testing a choice against Scripture and the church's teaching, following a well-formed conscience, taking advice, looking at the fruit, and thinking of those the choice affects. Paul asks Christians to be transformed "by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what is the good, well-pleasing, and perfect will of God" ([Romans 12:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V2)). The planned discernment-record skill will turn this file into steps for working through a decision and recording it.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Westminster Confession of Faith](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html) (1646, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): chapter 1 on Scripture as the rule for "faith and life", with "Christian prudence" for what it doesn't set down (1.6), and chapter 20 on Christian liberty and liberty of conscience, "God alone is Lord of the conscience" (20.2).
- `8` [Book of Discipline, ¶105: Our Theological Task](https://www.umc.org/en/content/book-of-discipline-105-our-theological-task) (The United Methodist Church): how United Methodists reason about faith and life, with Scripture as "the primary source and criterion" and tradition, experience and reason "all finally working together".
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1783–1785](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P60.HTM): Catholic teaching on the formation of conscience, "a lifelong task", with the Word of God as "the light for our path".
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1786–1789](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P61.HTM): how to choose in accord with conscience, and the rules that "apply in every case", such as never doing evil so that good may come of it.
- `7` [The Orthodox Faith: Wisdom](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-virtues/wisdom1) (Orthodox Church in America): wisdom as "the practical understanding and grasping of what is true and right", and the wise person as one "who can give concrete advice in the everyday affairs of life".

## Key rules

### Seeking God's will

- `8` `DISCERN-1` **Pray for wisdom, and ask for God's guidance before important decisions.** James tells anyone who lacks wisdom to "ask of God, who gives to all liberally", and Proverbs says: "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and don't lean on your own understanding." The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "by prayer we can discern 'what is the will of God'"; the Westminster Larger Catechism explains the petition "Thy will be done" as a prayer that God would make us "able and willing to know, do, and submit to his will in all things", and the Heidelberg Catechism as "Help us… to obey your will"; the Orthodox Faith recalls that Scripture calls the Spirit of the Lord "the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel", the Spirit given to those who believe; and Quaker faith & practice asks: "Do you encourage in yourself and in others a habit of dependence on God's guidance for each day?" ([James 1:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V5), [Proverbs 3:5–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO03.htm#V5), [Psalm 25:4–5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA025.htm#V4), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2826](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PA9.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 192](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 124](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [The Orthodox Faith: Wisdom](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-virtues/wisdom1), [Quaker faith & practice, 1.02](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/1-02/))
- `8` `DISCERN-2` **Test your choices against Scripture and the teaching of the church.** The people of Berea examined "the Scriptures daily to see whether these things were so", and John asks believers to "test the spirits, whether they are of God". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that in forming conscience "the Word of God is the light for our path", and that we are "guided by the authoritative teaching of the Church"; the Westminster Confession holds that what Scripture doesn't set down is to be ordered "by the light of nature, and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word"; the Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church teaches that good works are known from bad by "the witness of our conscience" and "God's commandments"; and the United Methodist Book of Discipline names Scripture "the primary source and criterion", with tradition, experience and reason. ([Psalm 119:105](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA119.htm#V105), [Acts 17:11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT17.htm#V11), [1 John 4:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1JN04.htm#V1), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1785](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P60.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 485](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Book of Discipline, ¶105](https://www.umc.org/en/content/book-of-discipline-105-our-theological-task))

### Conscience

- `8` `DISCERN-3` **Follow your conscience, and take care to form it well.** Paul speaks of the law "written in their hearts, their conscience testifying with them", and of keeping "a conscience void of offence towards God and men". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that a person "must always obey the certain judgment of his conscience", that "the education of the conscience is a lifelong task", and that conscience can err through ignorance for which a person may be responsible; the Westminster Confession holds that "God alone is Lord of the conscience", which he has left free from human commandments "contrary to his Word"; and the Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church calls conscience "the inward law of God". ([Romans 2:14–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM02.htm#V14), [Romans 14:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM14.htm#V23), [Acts 24:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT24.htm#V16), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1783–1785](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P60.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1790–1794](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P62.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 20.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 485–486](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))

### Counsel and fruit

- `7` `DISCERN-4` **Seek the advice of wise, mature Christians.** "Where there is no counsel, plans fail; but in a multitude of counsellors they are established." The Catechism of the Catholic Church names "the advice of competent people" among the helps for a hard moral choice, and commends spiritual direction by those with "the gifts of wisdom, faith and discernment"; Orthodox teaching describes the wise person as "the one who can point out the will of God to man who is confronted by actual problems and decisions"; the Methodist General Rules describe societies united "to watch over one another in love"; and Quaker faith & practice offers meetings for clearness to people "confronted by difficult choices at turning points in their lives". ([Proverbs 15:22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO15.htm#V22), [Proverbs 11:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO11.htm#V14), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1788](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P61.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2690](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P9H.HTM), [The Orthodox Faith: Wisdom](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-virtues/wisdom1), [The General Rules of the Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/the-general-rules-of-the-methodist-church), [Quaker faith & practice, 12.23](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/12-23/))
- `7` `DISCERN-5` **Look at the fruit: what leads towards love, joy and peace, and what leads away from God.** Jesus says "By their fruits you will know them", Paul names the fruit of the Spirit, "love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, and self-control", and asks the church to "test all things, and hold firmly that which is good". The Catechism of the Catholic Church applies Paul's words to the "discernment of charisms" and lists the fruits of the Spirit; and Quaker faith & practice asks Friends to "take heed… to the promptings of love and truth in your hearts". ([Matthew 7:16–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT07.htm#V16), [Galatians 5:22–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL05.htm#V22), [1 Thessalonians 5:19–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TH05.htm#V19), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 801](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P28.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1832](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P67.HTM), [Quaker faith & practice, 1.02](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/1-02/))

### Others

- `7` `DISCERN-6` **Consider how your decision affects others, especially the weakest.** Paul warns: "be careful that by no means does this liberty of yours become a stumbling block to the weak", and asks each to look "not just… to his own things, but… also to the things of others". The Catechism of the Catholic Church counts among the rules that "apply in every case" that charity respects the neighbour and his conscience, so that "it is right not to… do anything that makes your brother stumble"; and the Formula of Concord asks that "special care should be taken to exercise forbearance towards the weak in faith". ([1 Corinthians 8:9–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO08.htm#V9), [Philippians 2:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP02.htm#V3), [Romans 14:13–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM14.htm#V13), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1789](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P61.HTM), [Formula of Concord, Epitome, X: Church Rites](https://bookofconcord.org/epitome/church-rites/))

## How to apply

- `8` [Book of Discipline, ¶105: Our Theological Task](https://www.umc.org/en/content/book-of-discipline-105-our-theological-task) (The United Methodist Church): the four sources, Scripture first with tradition, experience and reason, "all finally working together". Used as a checklist for a decision, they ask what the Bible says, what the church has taught, what experience shows and what careful thought concludes. Supports `DISCERN-2` and `DISCERN-3`.
- `7` [Quaker faith & practice, 12.23](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/12-23/) (Britain Yearly Meeting): meetings for clearness, in which a small group of trusted Friends meets in worship with someone facing a hard decision; four or five are invited who "are likely to be able to contribute constructively in the process of discernment" ([12.24](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/12-24/)). Supports `DISCERN-4` and `DISCERN-1`.
- `6` [An Approach to Good Choices](https://www.ignatianspirituality.com/making-good-decisions/an-approach-to-good-choices/) (IgnatianSpirituality.com): the Ignatian way of making a decision, which asks for freedom from our own preferences and from pressure, prayer and self-knowledge, and gathering the facts and weighing "all the circumstances and likely outcomes"; it "involves both the heart and the mind". Supports `DISCERN-1` and `DISCERN-6`.
- `6` [Rules for Discernment](https://www.ignatianspirituality.com/making-good-decisions/discernment-of-spirits/rules-for-discernment/) (IgnatianSpirituality.com): Ignatius of Loyola's two sets of rules in the Spiritual Exercises (313–336) for interpreting consolation ("joy, peace, gratitude") and desolation, ideally with an experienced spiritual director. Supports `DISCERN-5` and `DISCERN-4`.
- `6` [The Daily Examen](https://www.ignatianspirituality.com/ignatian-prayer/the-examen/) (IgnatianSpirituality.com): a short daily review, in God's presence, of the day and of the feelings it brought, which "can help us see God's hand at work in our whole experience". Supports `DISCERN-5`.

## Open questions

- `8` [Elim's Foundational Truths](https://www.elim.org.uk/Articles/417857/Our_Beliefs.aspx) (Elim Pentecostal Church): does God still guide through prophecy, dreams or a direct sense of leading today? Elim, a Pentecostal church, believes in the ministry of prophets and "the present operation of the manifold Gifts of the Holy Spirit"; the Catechism of the Catholic Church accepts "private" revelations that help people live Christ's revelation more fully, discerned under the Magisterium, while none can "surpass or correct" it ([67](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PH.HTM)); and the Westminster Confession holds that God's "former ways" of revealing his will have "now ceased", and that nothing is to be added to Scripture "by new revelations of the Spirit" ([1.1 and 1.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). Paul tells the church to "discern" what prophets say ([1 Corinthians 14:29](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO14.htm#V29)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1790–1794](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P62.HTM): when conscience, Scripture and the church's teaching seem to point different ways, where does final authority lie? The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that conscience is formed "guided by the authoritative teaching of the Church" ([1785](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P60.HTM)), and names "rejection of the Church's authority and her teaching" among the sources of error (1792); Orthodox teaching counts obedience "to the Church… to the leaders of the Church" as a basic virtue ([The Orthodox Faith: Obedience](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/the-virtues/obedience)); the Westminster Confession holds that "God alone is Lord of the conscience" and that conscience is free from commandments of men "contrary to his Word" ([20.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)); and United Methodists weigh tradition, experience and reason with Scripture as "primary" ([¶105](https://www.umc.org/en/content/book-of-discipline-105-our-theological-task)). The related question of Scripture and Tradition is set out in [Reading Scripture](scripture.md#open-questions).
