# What the churches confess together

The creeds of the early church are the shared ground of the churches' faith. The Nicene Creed, the creed of the councils of Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381), is used in worship in both the Eastern and the Western churches, and the Apostles' Creed grew out of the questions asked at baptism in Rome ([English Language Liturgical Consultation, The Nicene Creed](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-nicene-creed), [The Apostles' Creed](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-apostles-creed)). The fellowship of the World Council of Churches is built on the same faith: its member churches "confess the Lord Jesus Christ as God and Saviour according to the scriptures" and seek their common calling "to the glory of the one God, Father, Son and Holy Spirit" ([World Council of Churches, What is the World Council of Churches?](https://www.oikoumene.org/about-the-wcc)).

This page describes that shared faith, article by article, in the creeds' own words and with the churches' own explanations. It has no rules: the rules about how Christians live are in the other topic files ([ADR-0006](../docs/decisions/0006-write-rules-only-where-the-churches-agree.md)), and what each church teaches beyond the creeds is on the [church pages](../traditions/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [The Nicene Creed](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-nicene-creed) (English Language Liturgical Consultation): the creed of Nicaea and Constantinople in the agreed ecumenical English text, with notes on its history and on each line, including why the words "and the Son" are printed in brackets.
- `9` [The Apostles' Creed](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-apostles-creed) (English Language Liturgical Consultation): the baptismal creed that took shape in Rome, in the agreed ecumenical English text, with notes on its origin in the threefold questions put to those being baptised.
- `8` [Confessing the One Faith](https://archive.org/details/wccfops2.160) (World Council of Churches, Faith and Order Paper 153, 1991, in the WCC's digital edition of the Faith and Order papers): an ecumenical explanation of the Nicene Creed, article by article, prepared by the WCC's Faith and Order Commission and sent to the churches for study.
- `7` [The Creed: The Symbol of Faith](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/prayers/symbol-of-faith) (Orthodox Church in America): the Nicene Creed as the Orthodox churches pray it, in the first person and without the words "and the Son".
- `7` [The Symbol of Chalcedon](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.iv.i.iii.html) (Council of Chalcedon, 451, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): the council's definition that Christ is one person, "truly God and truly man", in two natures "inconfusedly, unchangeably, indivisibly, inseparably".
- `7` [The Athanasian Creed](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.iv.i.iv.html) (in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): a Western creed, in Latin and English, that sets out the faith in "one God in Trinity, and Trinity in Unity" and in Christ as God and man.

## Where the churches differ

Questions about the creeds on which the churches differ. These guidelines don't pick a side. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `9` [The Nicene Creed, note on line 26](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-nicene-creed) (English Language Liturgical Consultation): **"and the Son"** (in Latin, *filioque*). The Western churches added these words to the article on the Holy Spirit, first at Toledo in 589, and Rome accepted them after the year 1000; the agreed ecumenical text prints them in brackets, so that each church decides whether to say them. The Orthodox churches confess that the Spirit "proceeds from the Father" ([Orthodox Church in America, The Creed](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/prayers/symbol-of-faith)), and Philaret's catechism teaches that this doctrine admits no "change or supplement" ([Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 241–242](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html)). The World Council of Churches' study of the Creed records that Western and Eastern Christians both wish to be faithful to the Creed's words that the Spirit proceeds from the Father, and that an increasing number of Western churches consider using the Creed in its original form ([Confessing the One Faith, 210](https://archive.org/details/wccfops2.160)).
- `8` [Conciliar Christology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ConciliarChristology) (Timothy Pawl, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): **how much authority do the creeds and councils have?** The Catholic Church teaches that the bishops, with the Pope, teach infallibly "above all in an Ecumenical Council" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 884 and 891](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2A.HTM)), and Philaret's catechism names seven ecumenical councils whose decisions carry the Church's authority ([Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 69–71](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html)). The Lutheran Book of Concord begins its confessions with the three ecumenical creeds, the Apostles', Nicene and Athanasian ([Book of Concord, The Ecumenical Creeds](https://bookofconcord.org/ecumenical-creeds/)), and Pawl notes that Protestant reformers such as Calvin received the early councils' teaching on Christ. The Southern Baptist Convention holds that "the sole authority for faith and practice among Baptists is the Scriptures", and that confessions of faith "are only guides in interpretation, having no authority over the conscience" ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, Preamble](https://bfm.sbc.net/preamble/)).
- `7` [Orthodox churches (Oriental)](https://www.oikoumene.org/church-families/orthodox-churches-oriental) (World Council of Churches): **the Council of Chalcedon (451).** The Oriental Orthodox churches (the Coptic, Ethiopian, Eritrean, Armenian, Syrian and Indian churches) base their teaching on the first three ecumenical councils and confess Christ with St Cyril of Alexandria as "One nature of the Word Incarnate". They are not in communion with the Eastern Orthodox churches, which, like the Catholic Church, receive Chalcedon's definition of Christ "in two natures" ([The Symbol of Chalcedon](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.iv.i.iii.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 467](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1J.HTM)); an official dialogue since 1985 has produced agreements on Christ, whose reception is the main remaining question. The Assyrian Church of the East accepts only the first two councils ([Pawl, Conciliar Christology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ConciliarChristology)), and in 1994 it and the Catholic Church declared that they confess the same faith in Christ ([Common Christological Declaration](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/speeches/1994/november/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_19941111_dichiarazione-cristologica.html)).
