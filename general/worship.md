# Worship and the sacraments

How Christians are taught to worship: gathering on the Lord's Day, baptism, the Lord's Supper, also called the Eucharist or Holy Communion, keeping a day of rest, and worship that goes on in daily life. The churches agree that Christ commanded baptism and the Lord's Supper and that his people should gather to worship. They differ on whom to baptise, on how Christ is present in the Supper, on how many sacraments there are and on who may receive communion, which are listed under "Open questions". Each church's own practice is described on the [church pages](../traditions/README.md).

## Key rules

### Gathering for worship

- `9` `WOR-1` **Worship God together with the church on the Lord's Day.** The first Christians gathered "on the first day of the week" to break bread, and the Letter to the Hebrews urges them not to forsake "our own assembling together". The churches teach that Sunday, the day of Christ's resurrection, is the day on which Christians gather to hear God's word, pray and share the Lord's Supper. The Faith and Order text calls it "appropriate" that the Eucharist "should take place at least every Sunday". ([Acts 20:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT20.htm#V7), [Hebrews 10:24–25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB10.htm#V24), [Revelation 1:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/REV01.htm#V10), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Eucharist 31](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1166–1167](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P39.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2177–2182](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7O.HTM), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.6–21.7](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 103](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Luther's Large Catechism, Third Commandment, 84–85](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 538–540](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))
- `7` `WOR-5` **Keep a day of rest and worship, free from unnecessary work.** The Ten Commandments set apart one day in seven for rest, and Jesus teaches that "the Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath". The churches teach Christians to keep Sunday for worship, rest and works of mercy, and to avoid work that is not needed, although they differ on how strictly to rest and on which day to keep (see "Open questions"). ([Exodus 20:8–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO20.htm#V8), [Mark 2:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK02.htm#V27), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2184–2185](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7O.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 117](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Third Commandment, 83–86](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 548–550](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))

### Baptism

- `9` `WOR-2` **Be baptised in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, as Christ commanded.** The risen Jesus sent his disciples to make disciples of all nations, "baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit", and Paul teaches that the baptised are buried with Christ so that they "might walk in newness of life". The churches agree that baptism with water in the name of the Trinity is Christ's command, that it unites the baptised with Christ and his people, and that it is given once: "Baptism is an unrepeatable act". They differ on whether infants should be baptised (see "Open questions"). ([Matthew 28:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT28.htm#V19), [Acts 2:38](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT02.htm#V38), [Romans 6:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM06.htm#V3), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Baptism 1–2, 13 and 17](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1223](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3I.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 288](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 28.1–28.2 and 28.7](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Sacrament of Holy Baptism](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-sacrament-of-holy-baptism/), [Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/))

### The Lord's Supper

- `9` `WOR-3` **Share in the Lord's Supper, as Christ commanded: "Do this in memory of me".** At his last supper Jesus took bread and the cup and told his disciples to do this in memory of him, and Paul writes that whenever Christians eat this bread and drink this cup, they "proclaim the Lord's death until he comes". The churches receive the Supper as Christ's gift and command, to be kept until his return, and the Faith and Order text urges that it "should be celebrated frequently". ([Luke 22:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK22.htm#V19), [1 Corinthians 11:23–26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO11.htm#V23), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Eucharist 1 and 30–31](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1337 and 1341](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3Z.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 315–317](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 29.1](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 75](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Sacrament of the Altar](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-sacrament-of-the-altar/))
- `8` `WOR-4` **Prepare to receive the Lord's Supper: examine yourself, and be reconciled with others first.** Paul writes "let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of the bread and drink of the cup", and Jesus tells those who bring a gift to the altar: "First be reconciled to your brother". The churches ask those who come to the Supper to examine their lives, repent, forgive and trust in Christ, though they teach different forms of preparation. The Faith and Order text adds that the Eucharist "demands reconciliation and sharing" among all who share it. ([1 Corinthians 11:27–29](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO11.htm#V27), [Matthew 5:23–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V23), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Eucharist 20](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1385](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P42.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 341](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 171](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 81](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Sacrament of the Altar](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-sacrament-of-the-altar/))

### Worship in all of life

- `7` `WOR-6` **Worship God with your whole life, not only in church.** Paul urges Christians to present their bodies "a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God", and to do everything, "in word or in deed", in the name of the Lord Jesus. The churches teach that worship goes on in daily life: the Faith and Order text says that "we offer ourselves as a living and holy sacrifice in our daily lives", the Catechism of the Catholic Church that "the moral life is spiritual worship", and the Heidelberg Catechism that "with our whole lives" Christians show that they are thankful to God. ([Romans 12:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V1), [Colossians 3:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V17), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Eucharist 10 and 24–25](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2031](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P73.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 32 and 86](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism))

## How to apply

- `7` [Keeping Sunday](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7O.HTM) (Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2184–2188): practical counsel for the Lord's Day: time for worship, for family and relatives, for rest, reflection and silence, and for "good works and humble service of the sick, the infirm, and the elderly"; and not making "unnecessary demands on others" that keep them from their own day of rest. The Westminster Larger Catechism similarly asks Christians to arrange their week so that they are "free and fit" for the day ([Q. 117](https://www.opc.org/lc.html)). Supports `WOR-5` and `WOR-1`.
- `7` [Preparing for communion](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P42.HTM) (Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1385–1389): examining one's conscience, the fast before communion and receiving often. The Orthodox Longer Catechism names fasting and prayer as helps for preparation ([Q. 341](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.ii.html)), and the Westminster Larger Catechism self-examination, "serious meditation, and fervent prayer" ([Q. 171](https://www.opc.org/lc.html)). Supports `WOR-4` and `WOR-3`.
- `7` [The Church Year](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-church-year/church-year) (Thomas Hopko, Orthodox Church in America): the Orthodox year of worship, centred on the feast of the Resurrection, with every Sunday "dedicated to the Resurrection of Christ". Western churches keep a similar liturgical year ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1168–1171](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P39.HTM)), and many follow a shared cycle of Sunday readings ([Revised Common Lectionary](https://lectionary.library.vanderbilt.edu/faq/)). Following the church year gives each Sunday its place in the story of Christ. Supports `WOR-1`.

## Open questions

- `9` [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Eucharist 8 and 13–15, with commentary](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf) (World Council of Churches): how is Christ present in the Lord's Supper, and is it a sacrifice? The Catholic Church teaches that Christ is "truly, really, and substantially contained" in the sacrament, and that the Eucharist is a sacrifice because it "re-presents (makes present) the sacrifice of the cross" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1366 and 1374](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P41.HTM)). Orthodox teaching holds that the gifts are Christ's "true Body and Blood mystically present in the bread and wine", and calls the liturgy a "spiritual and bloodless sacrifice" ([The Orthodox Faith: Holy Eucharist](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/holy-eucharist)). Lutherans confess that Christ's body and blood "are truly present", "under the bread and wine" ([Augsburg Confession, X](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-the-lords-supper/), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Sacrament of the Altar](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-sacrament-of-the-altar/)). The Westminster Confession holds that believers receive Christ "really, but spiritually" by faith, and that the Supper is "only a commemoration" of his one offering on the cross ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 29.2 and 29.7](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)), and the Anglican Articles that Christ's body is received "only after an heavenly and spiritual manner", by faith ([Articles of Religion, XXVIII](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#XXVIII)). Many Baptist churches call it "a symbolic act of obedience" that memorialises Christ's death ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/)). The Faith and Order text asks all the churches to "review the old controversies about 'sacrifice'".
- `7` [Dagmar Heller, Baptism, 4.2: Infant baptism versus adult baptism](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Baptism#section4.2) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): should infants be baptised, or only those who can confess their faith? The Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed and Anglican churches baptise infants as well as adults ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1250](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3K.HTM), [The Orthodox Faith: Baptism](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/baptism), [Augsburg Confession, IX](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-baptism/), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 74](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Articles of Religion, XXVII](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#XXVII)). Baptist and Anabaptist churches baptise only believers who confess their faith ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/), [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 11](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/baptism/)). The Faith and Order text notes that baptism on profession of faith is "the most clearly attested pattern" in the New Testament, while infant baptism in the apostolic age "cannot be excluded", and asks the churches to recognise one another's baptism ([Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Baptism 11–16](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1400–1401](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P42.HTM): who may receive communion? The Catholic Church teaches that intercommunion with the churches of the Reformation "is not possible", apart from grave necessity, and the Orthodox churches give communion to members of the Orthodox Church, including baptised infants ([The Orthodox Faith: Holy Eucharist](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/holy-eucharist)). The Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod practises "close(d) Communion" among churches that share its confession ([Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod, Beliefs](https://www.lcms.org/what-we-believe/beliefs)), and the Southern Baptist confession makes baptism a prerequisite to the Supper ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/)). The United Methodist Church invites "all who love him, who earnestly repent of their sin, and seek to live in peace with one another", including the baptised of other churches ([The United Methodist Church, An open table](https://www.umc.org/en/content/an-open-table-how-united-methodists-understand-communion)).
- `7` [Dagmar Heller, Baptism, 5.1: Sacrament or ordinance](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Baptism#section5.1) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): how many sacraments are there, and are they sacraments or ordinances? The Catholic Church counts seven sacraments ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1113](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2Z.HTM)), and the Orthodox Church usually counts seven holy mysteries, while holding that everything in the Church's life is sacramental ([The Orthodox Faith: The Sacraments](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/the-sacraments)). The Lutheran, Reformed and Anglican confessions count two sacraments ordained by Christ, baptism and the Lord's Supper ([Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 68](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 27.4](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Articles of Religion, XXV](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#XXV)). Churches that baptise only believers usually call the two "ordinances", acts that Christ ordered.
- `7` [Philip Alexander, Sabbath in the Christian Tradition, 4.1: Are Christians obliged to observe Sabbath?](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Sabbath#section4.1) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): which day should Christians keep, and how strictly should they rest? Most churches keep Sunday, the Lord's Day, whose observance, in the words of the Catechism of the Catholic Church, "replaces that of the sabbath" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2175](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7O.HTM)). The Westminster Confession calls Sunday "the Christian Sabbath" and asks for "an holy rest, all the day" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.7–21.8](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)), while Luther taught that the outward Sabbath law does not bind Christians, who keep Sunday for rest and for hearing God's word ([Luther's Large Catechism, Third Commandment, 82–86](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/)). Seventh Day Baptists and Seventh-day Adventists keep the seventh day, Saturday, as the Sabbath ([Seventh Day Baptist General Conference, Statement of Belief](https://www.seventhdaybaptist.org/statement-of-belief/)).
