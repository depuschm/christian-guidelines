# Honest and kind speech

"Death and life are in the power of the tongue", says Proverbs ([Proverbs 18:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO18.htm#V21)), and James calls the tongue "a fire" that can set a whole forest alight ([James 3:1–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS03.htm#V1)). This file covers what Christians say and write about and to others: telling the truth, not gossiping or slandering, speaking to build others up, controlling the tongue and anger, keeping your word, and keeping what you are told in confidence. It applies to speech online as much as face to face: posts, comments, messages and what you share are speech too. Not taking revenge is in [Repentance and forgiveness](repentance.md), and love of enemies is in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): Q&A 112 explains the commandment against false witness: no lying or deceit, no gossip or slander, love of the truth and care for your neighbour's good name. Q&A 105–107 treat insulting words and anger under the commandment against murder.
- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (1648, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Q. 144–145 list the duties and sins of the ninth commandment, from "speaking the truth, and only the truth" to slandering, backbiting and talebearing, and Q. 135–136 the "peaceable, mild and courteous speeches" that the sixth commandment asks for.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2475–2487](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8K.HTM): Catholic teaching on offences against truth: false witness, rash judgement, detraction and calumny, flattery, boasting and lying, and the duty to repair the harm done. The pages before and after it cover living in the truth (2464–2470) and respect for the truth, including secrets (2488–2492).
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, The Ten Commandments](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/) (1529): paragraphs 255–291 explain the eighth commandment as forbidding "all sins of the tongue" against a neighbour, and teach how to speak to someone about their fault before speaking about them to others.
- `7` [The Longer Catechism of the Orthodox, Catholic, Eastern Church, Part Three](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html) (St Philaret of Moscow, 1830, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): Q. 597–601 on the commandment against false witness, which forbids slander and "all lying", and ends: "We must bridle our tongue".

## Key rules

### Truthfulness

- `9` `SPEECH-1` **Tell the truth; don't lie or deceive.** The commandment forbids false testimony against a neighbour, and Paul writes "putting away falsehood, speak truth each one with his neighbour, for we are members of one another". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "a lie consists in speaking a falsehood with the intention of deceiving" and that lying "undermines trust among men"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks believers to avoid "lying and deceit of every kind" and to "love the truth"; the Orthodox Longer Catechism teaches that even lies meant to hurt no one "are inconsistent with love and respect for our neighbor"; and the Mennonite Confession of Faith says "We commit ourselves to tell the truth". ([Exodus 20:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO20.htm#V16), [Ephesians 4:25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V25), [Colossians 3:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V9), [Proverbs 12:22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO12.htm#V22), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2482–2486](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8K.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 112](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 144–145](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 597–600](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 20](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/truth/))
- `7` `SPEECH-5` **Keep your word: let your "yes" mean yes and your "no" mean no.** Jesus taught "let your 'Yes' be 'Yes' and your 'No' be 'No'", and the psalm praises the one "who keeps an oath even when it hurts". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that God's truth "must be honored in all speech", the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "keeping of lawful promises" among the duties of the ninth commandment and their breach among its sins, the Orthodox Longer Catechism names the breaking of "just and lawful oaths" as a sin, and the Mennonite Confession of Faith commits its members to "keeping one's word". ([Matthew 5:37](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V37), [James 5:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS05.htm#V12), [Psalm 15:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA015.htm#V4), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2152–2153](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7J.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 144–145](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 533](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 20](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/truth/))

### Speaking about others

- `9` `SPEECH-2` **Don't gossip about or slander others; defend their good name.** The Law forbids going around "as a slanderer amongst your people", and the letters ask Christians not to "speak against one another" and "to speak evil of no one". The Catechism of the Catholic Church condemns calumny and detraction, disclosing another's faults "without objectively valid reason", and asks everyone to interpret others' words and deeds "in a favorable way"; Luther's Small Catechism asks Christians to "defend" their neighbour and "put the best construction on everything"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks believers not to "gossip or slander" but to "guard and advance my neighbor's good name"; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism forbids slandering anyone "behind his back". ([Leviticus 19:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV19.htm#V16), [James 4:11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS04.htm#V11), [Titus 3:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TIT03.htm#V2), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2477–2479](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8K.HTM), [Luther's Small Catechism, Eighth Commandment](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 112](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 145](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 598](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))
- `7` `SPEECH-6` **Keep what you are told in confidence, unless keeping it would leave someone in danger.** Proverbs says that a gossip "betrays a confidence", but "one who is of a trustworthy spirit is one who keeps a secret". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that confidential information "must be kept, save in exceptional cases where keeping the secret is bound to cause very grave harm" to someone; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts both the "unnecessary discovering of infirmities" and "undue silence in a just cause" as sins; and the Church of England's guidelines for its clergy say that confidential information is not passed on without consent or lawful authority, except where disclosure is required, and that a minister who learns of a risk to children or vulnerable adults should consult the diocesan safeguarding adviser. Where a child or a vulnerable adult may be at risk, follow your church's safeguarding procedures. The seal of sacramental confession is governed by each church's own, stricter rules. ([Proverbs 11:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO11.htm#V13), [Proverbs 25:9–10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO25.htm#V9), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2488–2492](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8L.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 145](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 3.5–3.8 and 12.5](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines))

### Words that build up

- `8` `SPEECH-3` **Speak to build others up, not to wound them.** Paul writes "Let no corrupt speech proceed out of your mouth, but only what is good for building others up", and "Let your speech always be with grace"; Proverbs adds that "a gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger". The Westminster Larger Catechism asks for "peaceable, mild and courteous speeches and behavior", Luther's Large Catechism asks everyone to make their tongue "serve for the best of every one else", and the Orthodox Longer Catechism asks Christians to behave "in a gentle, affectionate, and edifying manner to all". ([Ephesians 4:29](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V29), [Colossians 4:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V6), [Proverbs 15:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO15.htm#V1), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 135](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment, 285](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 583](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))
- `8` `SPEECH-4` **Control your tongue and your anger.** James asks believers to be "swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger", and warns that "out of the same mouth comes blessing and cursing"; Paul writes "'Be angry, and don't sin.' Don't let the sun go down on your wrath". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that Jesus "denounced murderous anger and hatred"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks believers not to "belittle, hate, insult" their neighbour by thoughts, words, look or gesture; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "sinful anger" and "provoking words" as sins; Luther's Large Catechism says that nothing in a person can do greater good or harm "than the tongue"; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism teaches "We must bridle our tongue". ([James 1:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V19), [James 3:1–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS03.htm#V1), [Ephesians 4:26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V26), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2302](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P81.HTM), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 105–106](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 136](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Luther's Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment, 290](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 601](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))

## How to apply

- `7` [Social media community guidelines](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/social-media-community-guidelines) (Church of England): guidelines for conversations online, which the church encourages dioceses and local churches to adopt: be safe, respectful, kind and honest, "assume the best in people", "If you're not sure, don't post it", don't release confidential information, and "always question the source of any content you are considering amplifying". Supports `SPEECH-1`, `SPEECH-3` and `SPEECH-6`.
- `7` [Digital Charter](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/digital-charter) (Church of England): a voluntary pledge for individual Christians and churches to make the web a more positive place, built on truth ("checking that what we post online is fair and factual"), kindness, welcome, inspiration and togetherness, with a pointer to safeguarding advisers. Supports `SPEECH-1` and `SPEECH-3`.
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, Eighth Commandment, 276–280](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/): before speaking about someone's fault to others, follow "the order according to the Gospel": go to the person alone, then with one or two others, and only then before the community or a court, so that the person "is always to be treated with personally, and not to be spoken of without his knowledge" ([Matthew 18:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V15)). Supports `SPEECH-2`.

## Open questions

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2150–2155](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7J.HTM): may a Christian swear an oath, for example in court? The Catholic Church understands Jesus' words "Do not swear at all" as "not excluding oaths made for grave and right reasons (for example, in court)". The Westminster Confession teaches that a lawful oath "imposed by lawful authority" in weighty matters "ought to be taken", the Heidelberg Catechism allows an oath "when the government demands it, or when necessity requires it", and the Orthodox Longer Catechism allows oaths "on grave and necessary occasions" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 22.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 101](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 535](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html)). Mennonites commit themselves "to avoid swearing of oaths" and to affirm instead in legal matters, and Quakers "have refused to take oaths" throughout their history ([Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 20](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/truth/), [Quaker faith & practice, 20.48](https://qfp.quaker.org.uk/passage/20-48/)). All of them read the same words of Jesus ([Matthew 5:33–37](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V33)).
