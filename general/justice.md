# Justice and the poor

The Law and the Prophets return again and again to the poor, the widow, the orphan and the stranger, and in the parable of the judgement Jesus identifies himself with the hungry, the stranger and the prisoner ([Matthew 25:31–46](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT25.htm#V31)). `LOVE-4` in [Love of God and neighbour](love.md) already asks Christians to treat everyone as their neighbour and to help those in need whom they can reach. This file adds justice: defending the poor as a group, the equal dignity of every person and the rejection of racism, honest judgement, welcome for strangers and refugees, just laws and structures, and care for prisoners. Money, work and care for creation will have their own topic files.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

- `8` [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html) (Pontifical Council for Justice and Peace, 2004): Catholic social teaching in one volume: the equal dignity of all people (144–145), the preferential option for the poor (182), solidarity and "structures of sin" (192–196), migrants (297–298), and punishment, prisoners and corruption (402–405, 411).
- `8` [The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world) (Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church, 2016): the Council's document on "peace, justice, freedom, fraternity and love between peoples, and in the removal of racial and other discriminations", including the Church's rejection of discrimination (section E) and its service to people in need (section F).
- `8` [The Belhar Confession](https://www.rca.org/about/theology/creeds-and-confessions/the-belhar-confession/) (Dutch Reformed Mission Church in South Africa, 1986, on the site of the Reformed Church in America): a Reformed confession with its roots in the struggle against apartheid, on the unity of the church across races, reconciliation, and God as "in a special way the God of the destitute, the poor and the wronged".
- `8` [The Lausanne Covenant](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant) (Lausanne Movement, 1974): the evangelical statement whose section 5, "Christian Social Responsibility", affirms God's "concern for justice and reconciliation throughout human society" and the "intrinsic dignity" of every person.
- `7` [Message from the conference "Xenophobia, Racism and Populist Nationalism in the Context of Global Migration"](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/message-from-the-conference-xenophobia-racism-and-populist-nationalism-in-the-context-of-global-migration) (World Council of Churches and the Vatican's Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development, 2018): a joint statement on racism, migrants and the churches' public role.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1934–1938](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P6P.HTM): Catholic teaching on the equal dignity of all people, the differences between them, and "sinful inequalities".

## Key rules

### The poor and the stranger

- `9` `JUST-1` **Defend the poor, the widow, the orphan and the stranger.** The Law says that God "executes justice for the fatherless and widow and loves the foreigner", the prophets call God's people to "Seek justice. Relieve the oppressed. Defend the fatherless. Plead for the widow", and James calls caring for orphans and widows "pure religion". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "those who are oppressed by poverty are the object of a preferential love on the part of the Church"; the Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church teaches that the Church's efforts "to confront destitution and social injustice are an expression of her faith", and the Orthodox Longer Catechism that not to share our abundance with the poor is to "rob and defraud them of their right"; the Belhar Confession confesses a God who "supports the downtrodden, protects the stranger, helps orphans and widows". ([Deuteronomy 10:18–19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU10.htm#V18), [Isaiah 1:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ISA01.htm#V17), [Psalm 82:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA082.htm#V3), [James 1:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V27), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2448](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8F.HTM), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 182](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Holy and Great Council, The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World, F](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 594–595](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Belhar Confession, 4](https://www.rca.org/about/theology/creeds-and-confessions/the-belhar-confession/))
- `8` `JUST-4` **Welcome strangers, refugees and migrants.** The Law says that the stranger "shall be to you as the native-born amongst you, and you shall love him as yourself", Jesus says "I was a stranger and you took me in", and Hebrews adds "Don't forget to show hospitality to strangers". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "the more prosperous nations are obliged, to the extent they are able, to welcome the foreigner in search of the security and the means of livelihood which he cannot find in his country of origin"; the Southern Baptist Convention calls its churches "to be the presence of Christ, in both proclamation and ministry, to all persons, regardless of country of origin or immigration status", and declares "any form of nativism, mistreatment, or exploitation" inconsistent with the gospel; and the joint message of the World Council of Churches and the Vatican says that Jesus "calls all believers to welcome the stranger as an act of love inspired by faith". ([Leviticus 19:33–34](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV19.htm#V33), [Matthew 25:35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT25.htm#V35), [Hebrews 13:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB13.htm#V2), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2241](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7W.HTM), [Southern Baptist Convention, On Immigration and the Gospel (2011)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-immigration-and-the-gospel/), [WCC and Vatican, Message on Xenophobia, Racism and Populist Nationalism, 8–9](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/message-from-the-conference-xenophobia-racism-and-populist-nationalism-in-the-context-of-global-migration))

### Equal dignity

- `9` `JUST-2` **Treat every person with equal dignity, as made in God's image: reject racism and every kind of unjust discrimination.** God created humankind "in his own image" and "made from one blood every nation of men"; in Christ "there is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free man, there is neither male nor female"; and James condemns showing partiality to the rich. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "every form of social or cultural discrimination in fundamental personal rights on the grounds of sex, race, color, social conditions, language, or religion must be curbed and eradicated"; the Holy and Great Council confesses that every human being, "regardless of skin color, religion, race, sex, ethnicity, and language, is created in the image and likeness of God"; the Southern Baptist Convention denounced "racism, in all its forms, as deplorable sin"; the Lausanne Covenant affirms that every person, "regardless of race, religion, colour, culture, class, sex or age, has an intrinsic dignity"; and the joint message of the World Council of Churches and the Vatican says that racism "defaces the image of God in every human being". ([Genesis 1:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V27), [Acts 17:26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT17.htm#V26), [Galatians 3:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL03.htm#V28), [James 2:1–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS02.htm#V1), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1934–1935](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P6P.HTM), [Holy and Great Council, The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World, E](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world), [Southern Baptist Convention, Resolution on Racial Reconciliation (1995)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/resolution-on-racial-reconciliation-on-the-150th-anniversary-of-the-southern-baptist-convention/), [Lausanne Covenant, 5](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant), [WCC and Vatican, Message on Xenophobia, Racism and Populist Nationalism, 6](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/message-from-the-conference-xenophobia-racism-and-populist-nationalism-in-the-context-of-global-migration))

### Justice in judgement and in society

- `9` `JUST-3` **Act justly: don't take bribes, and don't twist justice for anyone.** Micah asks God's people "to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God", and the Law commands: "You shall not pervert justice. You shall not show partiality. You shall not take a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and perverts the words of the righteous." The Catechism of the Catholic Church counts as morally illicit "corruption in which one influences the judgment of those who must make decisions according to law"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "bribery" among the sins against the eighth commandment and "passing unjust sentence" among the sins against the ninth; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism condemns bribery, by which men in office "for gain promote the unworthy, acquit the guilty, or oppress the innocent". ([Micah 6:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MIC06.htm#V8), [Exodus 23:6–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO23.htm#V6), [Deuteronomy 16:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU16.htm#V19), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2409](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 142 and Q. 145](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html))
- `8` `JUST-5` **Work for just laws and structures, not only for charity.** The prophets pronounce woe on "those who decree unrighteous decrees" to "deprive the needy of justice", and on the man who "uses his neighbour's service without wages"; Amos cries "let justice roll on like rivers". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "the demands of justice must be satisfied first of all; that which is already due in justice is not to be offered as a gift of charity", and the Compendium of the Social Doctrine asks that "structures of sin" be "transformed into structures of solidarity through the creation or appropriate modification of laws, market regulations, and juridical systems"; the Belhar Confession holds that the church "must witness against and strive against any form of injustice"; the Lausanne Covenant affirms that "evangelism and socio-political involvement are both part of our Christian duty"; and in their joint message the World Council of Churches and the Vatican commit themselves "to work together for the transformation of unjust structures and systems". ([Isaiah 10:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ISA10.htm#V1), [Jeremiah 22:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JER22.htm#V13), [Amos 5:24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/AMO05.htm#V24), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2446](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8F.HTM), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 193](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Belhar Confession, 4](https://www.rca.org/about/theology/creeds-and-confessions/the-belhar-confession/), [Lausanne Covenant, 5](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant), [WCC and Vatican, Message on Xenophobia, Racism and Populist Nationalism, 13](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/message-from-the-conference-xenophobia-racism-and-populist-nationalism-in-the-context-of-global-migration))

### Prisoners

- `8` `JUST-6` **Visit and care for prisoners, and treat offenders with dignity.** Jesus says "I was in prison and you came to me", and Hebrews asks Christians to "Remember those who are in bonds, as bound with them". The Catechism of the Catholic Church counts "visiting the sick and imprisoned" among the corporal works of mercy, and the Compendium of the Social Doctrine teaches that prison chaplains work "in defence of the dignity of those detained"; the Holy and Great Council names "those in captivity and prison" among the people the Orthodox Church cares for, and the Orthodox Longer Catechism counts visiting "the sick and prisoners" among the works of holy days; and the Methodist General Rules ask Methodists to do good "by visiting or helping them that are sick or in prison". ([Matthew 25:36](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT25.htm#V36), [Hebrews 13:3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB13.htm#V3), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2447](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8F.HTM), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 403](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Holy and Great Council, The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World, F](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 550](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [The General Rules of the Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/the-general-rules-of-the-methodist-church))

## How to apply

- `8` [Fratelli tutti, 129](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20201003_enciclica-fratelli-tutti.html) (Pope Francis, 2020): sums up the response to migrants in "four words: welcome, protect, promote and integrate", a checklist for a parish or church group that wants to help newcomers. Supports `JUST-4`.
- `7` [The General Rules of the Methodist Church](https://www.umc.org/en/content/the-general-rules-of-the-methodist-church) (The United Methodist Church): the rule of life of John Wesley's societies, which asks members to do good "of every possible sort", "by giving food to the hungry, by clothing the naked, by visiting or helping them that are sick or in prison". Supports `JUST-1` and `JUST-6`.
- `5` [Global Refuge](https://www.globalrefuge.org/) (formerly Lutheran Immigration and Refugee Service): an agency that has helped refugees and immigrants settle in the United States since 1939, with ways for volunteers and congregations to take part ([Volunteer](https://www.globalrefuge.org/get-involved/volunteer/)). Supports `JUST-4`.
- `5` [Kairos Prison Ministry International](https://kairosprisonministry.org/about-us/): a "lay-led, interdenominational Christian ministry in which men and women volunteers bring Christ's love and forgiveness to prisoners and their families". Supports `JUST-6`.

## Open questions

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2267](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7Z.HTM): may the state put offenders to death? Since 2018 the Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "the death penalty is inadmissible because it is an attack on the inviolability and dignity of the person", and Pope Francis writes that the Church "is firmly committed to calling for its abolition worldwide" ([Fratelli tutti, 263](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20201003_enciclica-fratelli-tutti.html)). The Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, while recognising that the Lutheran tradition holds that God entrusts the state with the power to take life, opposes capital punishment in its own society ([ELCA, The Death Penalty (1991)](https://www.elca.org/social-teachings/1991-the-death-penalty-theological-discernment-social-statements)). The Westminster Larger Catechism excepts "public justice" from its prohibition of taking life ([Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 136](https://www.opc.org/lc.html)), and the Southern Baptist Convention supports "the fair and equitable use of capital punishment by civil magistrates" for those guilty of murder, citing [Genesis 9:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN09.htm#V6) and [Romans 13:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM13.htm#V4) ([Southern Baptist Convention, On Capital Punishment (2000)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-capital-punishment/)); the same resolution holds that those guilty of capital crimes "should be treated with dignity".
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2241](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7W.HTM): how should welcome for migrants be balanced with a state's control of its borders? The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches both the duty to welcome and that political authorities "may make the exercise of the right to immigrate subject to various juridical conditions", and that immigrants are obliged to obey the laws of the country that receives them. The Southern Baptist Convention asks governments "to prioritize efforts to secure the borders" and to provide, "with the borders secured, a just and compassionate path to legal status" ([Southern Baptist Convention, On Immigration and the Gospel (2011)](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-immigration-and-the-gospel/)). The joint message of the World Council of Churches and the Vatican holds that the concerns of those who feel threatened by migrants "have to be acknowledged and examined", while setting "a narrative of love and of hope" against "the populist narrative of hate and of fear" ([WCC and Vatican, Message on Xenophobia, Racism and Populist Nationalism, 8](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/message-from-the-conference-xenophobia-racism-and-populist-nationalism-in-the-context-of-global-migration)). The churches agree on welcome and on the dignity of migrants, and differ on the policies that follow.
