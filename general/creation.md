# Care for creation

The Bible opens with God making the world and seeing "everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good" ([Genesis 1:31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V31)), and Psalm 24 says that "the earth is the LORD's" ([Psalm 24:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA024.htm#V1)). This file covers how Christians are taught to treat God's creation: caring for the earth, acting against pollution and damage to the natural world, living simply, sharing the earth's goods fairly, treating animals kindly, and thanking God for creation. In 2021 the Pope, the Ecumenical Patriarch and the Archbishop of Canterbury wrote together about the environment for the first time ([A Joint Message for the Protection of Creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/pont-messages/2021/documents/20210901-messaggio-protezionedelcreato.html)). Justice for the poor, and money and possessions, will each have their own topic file. How far humans cause climate change, and what should be done about it, is listed under "Open questions", because the churches' statements differ.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `8` [Laudato si'](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html) (Pope Francis, 2015): the encyclical "on care for our common home": pollution and climate change, the loss of biodiversity, the "common destination of goods", an "integral ecology" that joins care for nature and justice for the poor, and the simple, grateful way of life it asks for.
- `8` [A Joint Message for the Protection of Creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/pont-messages/2021/documents/20210901-messaggio-protezionedelcreato.html) (Pope Francis, Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew and Archbishop Justin Welby, 2021): the first time the three leaders addressed the environment together: stewardship of "our God-given endowment", the impact on people living in poverty, future generations, and a call to "choose to eat, travel, spend, invest and live differently".
- `8` [Hope for All Creation: A Theological Response to the Environmental Crises](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/hope-for-all-creation-for-web-new.pdf) (Church of England, 2026): a teaching document by the Faith and Order Commission, adopted by the House of Bishops: creation in the Bible, Anglican teaching since the Lambeth Conference of 1968, the reading of "dominion", and what churches and Christians can do.
- `8` [The Cape Town Commitment](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment) (Lausanne Movement, 2010): the evangelical commitment's section I.7.A, "We love the world of God's creation", and section II.B.6, "Christ's peace for his suffering creation", on stewardship, climate change and lifestyles.
- `8` [Encyclical of the Holy and Great Council](https://www.holycouncil.org/encyclical-holy-council) (Orthodox Church, 2016): paragraph 14 teaches that "the roots of the ecological crisis are spiritual and ethical", and calls for repentance, "asceticism as an antidote to consumerism" and responsibility to future generations.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2407–2418](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM): Catholic teaching on "respect for the integrity of creation" under the seventh commandment, and on how animals may and may not be treated.

## Key rules

### Caring for the earth

- `9` `CREAT-1` **Care for the earth as God's creation, entrusted to people to tend and keep.** Genesis says that God put the man in the garden "to cultivate and keep it", and the churches read the human calling to "dominion" as responsibility, not licence. Pope Francis, Patriarch Bartholomew and Archbishop Welby call stewardship "individual and collective responsibility for our God-given endowment"; Laudato si' explains "keeping" as "caring, protecting, overseeing and preserving"; Patriarch Bartholomew speaks of "the God-given duty and mandate, to work and preserve"; and the Cape Town Commitment teaches that Christians care for the earth "because it belongs to the one whom we call Lord". ([Genesis 2:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN02.htm#V15), [Psalm 24:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA024.htm#V1), [Genesis 1:31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V31), [A Joint Message for the Protection of Creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/pont-messages/2021/documents/20210901-messaggio-protezionedelcreato.html), [Laudato si', 67](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2415](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew, Message for 1 September 2010](https://www.oca.org/News/archived/ecumenical-patriarch-marks-the-ecclesiastical-new-year-with-a-statement-on-), [Cape Town Commitment, I.7.A](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment), [Church of England, Environment and climate change](https://www.churchofengland.org/about/our-views/environment-and-climate-change))
- `8` `CREAT-3` **Act against pollution and the destruction of the natural world, which harm the poor most.** The prophet Jeremiah hears God say to Israel, "you defiled my land". The three church leaders write that "the people bearing the most catastrophic consequences of these abuses are the poorest on the planet and have been the least responsible for causing them"; Laudato si' that air pollution brings health hazards "especially for the poor"; the Church of England's bishops that the ecological crisis "disproportionately affects" the poorest and most vulnerable; and the Cape Town Commitment asks Christians to renounce "habits of consumption that are destructive or polluting". ([Jeremiah 2:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JER02.htm#V7), [Genesis 2:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN02.htm#V15), [A Joint Message for the Protection of Creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/pont-messages/2021/documents/20210901-messaggio-protezionedelcreato.html), [Laudato si', 20](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html), [Hope for All Creation](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/hope-for-all-creation-for-web-new.pdf), [Cape Town Commitment, II.B.6](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment))

### Living simply and sharing fairly

- `8` `CREAT-2` **Live simply, and avoid waste.** Jesus warns that "a man's life doesn't consist of the abundance of the things which he possesses", and after feeding the crowd he tells his disciples to gather the pieces left over, "that nothing be lost". Laudato si' commends the conviction that "less is more"; the Orthodox Council calls for "asceticism as an antidote to consumerism"; the Lausanne Movement's Jamaica Call to Action renews its "commitment to a simple lifestyle"; and the Church of England's bishops recall that in Lent Christians "practise restraint, fasting and simplicity". ([Luke 12:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK12.htm#V15), [1 Timothy 6:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI06.htm#V8), [John 6:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN06.htm#V12), [Laudato si', 222](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html), [Encyclical of the Holy and Great Council, 14](https://www.holycouncil.org/encyclical-holy-council), [Creation Care and the Gospel: Jamaica Call to Action](https://lausanne.org/statement/creation-care-call-to-action), [Hope for All Creation](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/hope-for-all-creation-for-web-new.pdf))
- `7` `CREAT-6` **Share the earth's goods fairly, with people now and with generations to come.** God tells Israel, "the land is mine", and the first believers "had all things in common". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "the goods of creation are destined for the whole human race", and Laudato si' that "intergenerational solidarity is not optional, but rather a basic question of justice"; the Orthodox Council speaks of "our greatest responsibility to hand down a viable natural environment to future generations"; the three church leaders write, "We must decide what kind of world we want to leave to future generations"; and the Jamaica Call to Action asks Christians "to equitably share its bounty with each other". ([Leviticus 25:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV25.htm#V23), [Acts 4:32](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT04.htm#V32), [Psalm 24:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA024.htm#V1), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2402](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8A.HTM), [Laudato si', 159](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html), [Encyclical of the Holy and Great Council, 14](https://www.holycouncil.org/encyclical-holy-council), [A Joint Message for the Protection of Creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/pont-messages/2021/documents/20210901-messaggio-protezionedelcreato.html), [Creation Care and the Gospel: Jamaica Call to Action](https://lausanne.org/statement/creation-care-call-to-action))

### Animals

- `8` `CREAT-4` **Treat animals kindly, and don't make them suffer needlessly.** "A righteous man respects the life of his animal", says Proverbs; the Law forbids muzzling the ox while it treads out the grain; and Jesus says of the birds, "Your heavenly Father feeds them". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that people "owe them kindness" and that "it is contrary to human dignity to cause animals to suffer or die needlessly", while allowing their use for food, clothing and work, and for medical research "within reasonable limits"; Laudato si' adds that "every act of cruelty towards any creature is 'contrary to human dignity'". The Church of England's bishops recall that the 1968 Lambeth Conference urged responsible stewardship over nature, "in particular in his relationship with animals", and state that "both human and non-human creatures have worth to God"; and Patriarch Bartholomew calls Christians to live so that "every kind of fauna and flora" may "live and thrive". ([Proverbs 12:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO12.htm#V10), [Deuteronomy 25:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU25.htm#V4), [Matthew 6:26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT06.htm#V26), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2416–2418](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Laudato si', 92](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html), [Hope for All Creation](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/hope-for-all-creation-for-web-new.pdf), [Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew, Message for 1 September 2010](https://www.oca.org/News/archived/ecumenical-patriarch-marks-the-ecclesiastical-new-year-with-a-statement-on-))

### Thanksgiving for creation

- `7` `CREAT-5` **Thank and praise God for creation.** "LORD, how many are your works!", sings Psalm 104, and Psalm 148 calls the heavens, the earth and every creature to "Praise the LORD!" Luther's Small Catechism confesses that God "has made me and all creatures", for which "I owe it to Him to thank, praise, serve, and obey Him"; the Heidelberg Catechism asks God to help us "praise you for all your works"; the Westminster Confession teaches that God created the world "for the manifestation of the glory of his eternal power, wisdom, and goodness"; the Catechism of the Catholic Church quotes St Francis's Canticle of the Creatures, "May you be praised, O Lord, in all your creatures"; and the Orthodox Council teaches that people offer creation "by way of doxology to the Creator". ([Psalm 104](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA104.htm), [Psalm 148](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA148.htm), [Genesis 1:31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V31), [Luther's Small Catechism, The Creed, First Article](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-creed/), [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 122](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism), [Westminster Confession of Faith, 4.1](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 344](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1A.HTM), [Encyclical of the Holy and Great Council, 14](https://www.holycouncil.org/encyclical-holy-council))

## How to apply

- `8` [The Season of Creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/pont-messages/2021/documents/20210901-messaggio-protezionedelcreato.html) (A Joint Message for the Protection of Creation): "September is celebrated by many Christians as the Season of Creation, an opportunity to pray and care for God's creation". It begins on 1 September, which the Ecumenical Patriarchate set as a day of prayer for the protection of the environment ([Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew, Message for 1 September 2010](https://www.oca.org/News/archived/ecumenical-patriarch-marks-the-ecclesiastical-new-year-with-a-statement-on-)), and the Church of England keeps a Festival of Creation in Christ on the first Sunday in September, to "give thanks for God's creation" ([Church of England, Hope for All Creation and the Festival of Creation in Christ](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/church-england-publishes-major-teaching-document-care-creation-synod-approves-new-annual)). Supports `CREAT-5` and `CREAT-1`.
- `8` [Everyday care for creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html) (Laudato si', 211): "avoiding the use of plastic and paper, reducing water consumption, separating refuse, cooking only what can reasonably be consumed, showing care for other living beings, using public transport or car-pooling, planting trees, turning off unnecessary lights". Supports `CREAT-2`, `CREAT-3` and `CREAT-4`.
- `7` [Net zero carbon for churches](https://www.churchofengland.org/about/our-views/environment-and-climate-change) (Church of England): the Church of England sees responding to the climate and nature crises as part of its responsibility "to protect God's creation and achieve a just world", and its General Synod voted in 2020 to aim for net zero carbon by 2030, with resources to help local churches change "their buildings and systems" ([Hope for All Creation](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/hope-for-all-creation-for-web-new.pdf)). Supports `CREAT-3` and `CREAT-1`.
- `6` [Eco Church](https://ecochurch.arocha.org.uk/) (A Rocha UK): a scheme "open to all denominations" that helps a church assess and improve its care for creation through a survey of five areas: worship and teaching, buildings and energy, land and nature, community and global engagement, and lifestyle. Supports `CREAT-1`, `CREAT-2` and `CREAT-3`.

## Open questions

- `8` [Laudato si', 23–25](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html): how far is climate change caused by human activity, and what should be done about it? Pope Francis writes that "a very solid scientific consensus" shows a "disturbing warming of the climatic system", and with Patriarch Bartholomew and Archbishop Welby calls climate change "an immediate and urgent matter of survival" ([A Joint Message for the Protection of Creation](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/pont-messages/2021/documents/20210901-messaggio-protezionedelcreato.html)); the Cape Town Commitment calls it "probably the most serious and urgent challenge faced by the physical world now" ([Cape Town Commitment, II.B.6](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment)). The Southern Baptist Convention, while affirming "caring stewardship and dominion over the earth", urged Southern Baptists in 2007 "to proceed cautiously in the human-induced global warming debate in light of conflicting scientific research", and called proposals to cap emissions by a temperature goal "very dangerous" ([Southern Baptist Convention, On Global Warming, 2007](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-global-warming/)).
- `8` [Creation](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Creation) (Ian A. McFarland, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): how do the creation accounts of Genesis relate to the sciences of the origins of the universe and of life? McFarland warns against treating the doctrine of creation as "a scientific hypothesis or historical claim about the origin of the world". The Catechism of the Catholic Church welcomes scientific studies that have "splendidly enriched our knowledge of the age and dimensions of the cosmos, the development of life-forms and the appearance of man" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 283–284](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P19.HTM)), and Pope John Paul II recognised evolution as "more than a hypothesis", while holding that "the spiritual soul is immediately created by God" ([John Paul II, Address to the Pontifical Academy of Sciences, 1996](https://www.vaticanobservatory.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/JPII-Truth-Cannot-Contradict-Truth.pdf)). The Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod restated in 2019 its historic position that God created the world in "six natural days", defining "natural" by the "evening and morning" of Genesis 1 ([Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod, Reporter, 24 July 2019](https://reporter.lcms.org/2019/convention-confesses-god-created-the-world-in-six-natural-days/)).
- `8` [Laudato si', 67](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20150524_enciclica-laudato-si.html): what does the command to "have dominion" ([Genesis 1:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V28)) mean, and has it encouraged the exploitation of nature? Some critics hold that Christianity's reading of human dominion helped to cause the environmental crisis ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Ecotheology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Ecotheology)). Pope Francis answers that the claim that dominion "justifies absolute domination over other creatures" is "not a correct interpretation of the Bible as understood by the Church", and the Church of England's bishops that the verse "is better understood in its context as promoting human responsibility to nurture the world" ([Hope for All Creation](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/hope-for-all-creation-for-web-new.pdf)).

- `8` [Ecotheology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Ecotheology) (Ernst Conradie, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): an overview of Christian thinking on ecological destruction across traditions and continents, including critiques of Christianity and Christian responses, stewardship and "creation care".
