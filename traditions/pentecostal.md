# The Pentecostal and charismatic churches

Pentecostal and charismatic Christians stress the present work of the Holy Spirit: the baptism in the Spirit, and gifts such as speaking in tongues, divine healing and prophecy. In the words of the Pew Research Center, Pentecostals belong "either to one of the historical denominations, such as the Assemblies of God and the Church of God in Christ, that originated in the religious revivals of the early 20th century, or to newer, largely independent churches", while charismatics practise the gifts of the Spirit as members of other churches, most of them "Catholic, Orthodox, mainline Protestant or evangelical Protestant" ([Pew Research Center, Spirit and Power: Glossary of Terms](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2006/10/05/spirit-and-power-a-10-country-survey-of-pentecostals2/)). In 2006 Pew reported, from the World Christian Database, that "at least a quarter of the world's 2 billion Christians" were thought to belong to these movements ([Pew Research Center, Spirit and Power](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2006/10/05/spirit-and-power/)).

The movement grew from several revivals around 1900. The Azusa Street Revival in Los Angeles, from April 1906, is the best known, but Pew notes that influential revivals in Asia came before it ([Pew Research Center, Overview: Pentecostalism in Asia](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2006/10/05/overview-pentecostalism-in-asia/)). The Church of God (Cleveland, Tennessee) traces itself to a Christian union formed in 1886, whose Holiness believers later "experienced an outpouring of the Holy Spirit that included speaking in tongues and divine healing" ([Church of God, A Brief History](https://churchofgod.org/about/a-brief-history-of-the-church-of-god/)). Many Pentecostal churches work together in the Pentecostal World Fellowship ([Pentecostal World Fellowship](https://www.pwfellowship.org/)), and in the Catholic Church the charismatic renewal is served by CHARIS, a body established by the Holy See ([CHARIS, About CHARIS](https://www.charis.international/en/about-charis/)).

Pentecostal churches hold the Bible to be the inspired Word of God and the final authority for faith and life, and most set out their teaching in a short statement of faith rather than a long confession. The theologian Wolfgang Vondey describes Pentecostal doctrine as reflection that "emerges from and aims at the experience of worship" ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Pentecostal Theology, 1.2](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/PentecostalTheology#section1.2)).

## Adopted references

- `8` [Declaration of Faith](https://churchofgod.org/beliefs/declaration-of-faith/) (Church of God, Cleveland, Tennessee): the church's "standard and official expression of its doctrine", fourteen short articles from the inspiration of the Bible to the second coming, including the baptism in the Holy Spirit, tongues as its initial evidence, and divine healing.
- `8` [Elim's Foundational Truths](https://www.elim.org.uk/Articles/417857/Our_Beliefs.aspx) (Elim Pentecostal Church): the twelve-article statement of faith of a Pentecostal church in the United Kingdom and Ireland, on the Bible, the Trinity, the Holy Spirit, salvation, the church, its ministries and ordinances, and its mission.
- `8` [Statement of Essential Truths and Positions and Practices](https://paoc.org/docs/default-source/fellowship-services-documents/constitutions/2026/2026-statement-of-essential-truths-and-positions-and-practices.pdf?sfvrsn=9e0cc06a_4) (Pentecostal Assemblies of Canada, 2026, PDF): the beliefs of a large Pentecostal fellowship, with Bible references for each statement, including Spirit baptism, the church and its practices.
- `8` [Pentecostal Theology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/PentecostalTheology) (Wolfgang Vondey, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): Pentecostal spirituality and the "full gospel", the classical doctrines of salvation, sanctification, Spirit baptism, divine healing and the end times, and current debates.
- `8` [About CHARIS](https://www.charis.international/en/about-charis/) (CHARIS, Catholic Charismatic Renewal International Service): the body "established by the Holy See, through the Dicastery for the Laity, Family and Life" to serve the charismatic renewal in the Catholic Church, with its founding documents.

## Differences within the tradition

- `7` [Pentecostal Theology, 3.3: Spirit baptism](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/PentecostalTheology#section3.3) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): **speaking in tongues**. "Classical Pentecostals, in particular, insist that Spirit baptism is manifested by speaking with other tongues as its initial (physical) evidence." The Church of God believes in speaking with tongues "as the Spirit gives utterance and that it is the initial evidence of the baptism in the Holy Ghost" ([Church of God, Declaration of Faith](https://churchofgod.org/beliefs/declaration-of-faith/)). Elim speaks instead of "the baptism in the Holy Spirit with signs following" ([Elim's Foundational Truths](https://www.elim.org.uk/Articles/417857/Our_Beliefs.aspx)). The doctrinal commission of the Catholic charismatic renewal teaches that tongues as a prayer language "is available to all", and advises those who struggle with it "not [to] concern themselves about tongues" ([CHARIS, Is the Gift of Tongues for Everyone?](https://www.charis.international/en/is-the-gift-of-tongues-for-everyone/)).
- `7` [Our Beliefs](https://upci.org/our-beliefs/) (United Pentecostal Church International): **Oneness Pentecostals**. Most Pentecostal churches confess the Trinity, but the United Pentecostal Church International teaches that "There is one God, who has revealed Himself as Father; through His Son, in redemption; and as the Holy Spirit, by emanation", and baptises "in the name of Jesus Christ". Vondey describes this as the "'Oneness' theology of the Jesus Name group or the Apostolic Pentecostal movement", set against trinitarian Pentecostals ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Pentecostal Theology, 3](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/PentecostalTheology#section3)).
- `7` [A Statement on the Prosperity Gospel](https://lausanne.org/content/a-statement-on-the-prosperity-gospel) (Lausanne Theology Working Group, Africa Chapter, 2008–2009): **the prosperity gospel**, the teaching that believers have a right to "the blessings of health and wealth", obtained through faith and "the 'sowing of seeds'" in tithes and offerings. The statement notes that it is found "in varying degrees in mainstream Protestant, Pentecostal as well as Charismatic Churches", and judges that the teachings of its strongest promoters are "false and gravely distorting of the Bible". Vondey describes the growth of prosperity theology among Pentecostals, driven by the Word of Faith movement, and Pentecostal responses that turn "to ethical questions of renewal shaped by justice, leadership, and education" ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Pentecostal Theology, 4.3](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/PentecostalTheology#section4.3)).
- `6` [Spirit and Power: Glossary of Terms](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2006/10/05/spirit-and-power-a-10-country-survey-of-pentecostals2/) (Pew Research Center, 2006): **Pentecostals, charismatics and independent churches**. Pew distinguishes Pentecostals in historical denominations from "newer, largely independent churches, sometimes labeled as neo-pentecostal churches", and from charismatics who belong to other churches. The Catholic charismatic renewal sees baptism in the Spirit as "a simple and powerful means to renew the life of millions of believers in almost all Christian Churches", in the words of the Preacher to the Papal Household, Raniero Cantalamessa ([CHARIS, Baptism in the Holy Spirit](https://www.charis.international/en/baptism-holy-spirit/)).
