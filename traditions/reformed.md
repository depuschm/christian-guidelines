# The Reformed and Presbyterian churches

The Reformed churches come from the Reformation led by Huldrych Zwingli, John Calvin, John Knox and others. They include Continental Reformed, Presbyterian and Congregational churches, and some evangelical Anglicans also count themselves Reformed ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Christian Theology, 5.1.4](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianTheology#section5.1.4)). Many Reformed churches belong to the World Communion of Reformed Churches, formed in 2010 when the World Alliance of Reformed Churches and the Reformed Ecumenical Council merged. It brings together 80 million Reformed Christians in 230 Congregational, Presbyterian, Reformed and United member churches in 108 countries ([World Council of Churches, World Communion of Reformed Churches](https://www.oikoumene.org/organization/world-communion-of-reformed-churches)). The World Reformed Fellowship, formed in 2000 from fellowships begun by the Presbyterian Church in America and churches in Latin America and Asia, exists "to encourage understanding and cooperation among evangelical Presbyterian and Reformed denominations and institutions" ([World Reformed Fellowship, About](https://wrfnet.org/about)).

In Reformed teaching, Scripture holds the highest authority. The St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology describes the tradition as governed "primarily by the biblical canon and secondarily by the creeds; the first four ecumenical councils; and by various confessional documents, especially the Three Forms of Unity and the Westminster Confession" ([Christian Theology, 5.1.4](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianTheology#section5.1.4)). The Three Forms of Unity are the Belgic Confession (1561), the Heidelberg Catechism (1563) and the Canons of Dort (1618–1619), which continental Reformed churches affirm as "historic Reformed expressions of the Christian faith" ([Christian Reformed Church in North America, Confessions](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions), [The Canons of Dort](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort)). Presbyterian churches hold the Westminster Confession of Faith and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms (1647) as "secondary standards", "the Bible itself being the only infallible rule of faith and practice" ([Orthodox Presbyterian Church, Confession and Catechisms](https://www.opc.org/confessions.html)). Some churches also confess more recent statements, such as the Theological Declaration of Barmen (1934) and the Belhar Confession (1986) ([Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Confessions](https://pcusa.org/confessions), [Reformed Church in America, Creeds and Confessions](https://www.rca.org/about/theology/creeds-and-confessions/)).

## Adopted references

- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): 129 questions and answers in three parts, misery, deliverance and gratitude, explaining the Apostles' Creed, the sacraments, the Ten Commandments and the Lord's Prayer.
- `9` [Westminster Confession of Faith](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html) (on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): the chief Presbyterian confession, in 33 chapters, from Scripture and God's decree to the sacraments, church government and the last judgement.
- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): 196 questions and answers on the faith, the Ten Commandments, the means of grace and prayer.
- `8` [Belgic Confession](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/belgic-confession) (1561, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): the oldest of the Three Forms of Unity, written by Guido de Brès for the Reformed churches of the Low Countries in the time of the Spanish Inquisition, in 37 articles.
- `8` [The Canons of Dort](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/canons-dort) (1618–1619, on the site of the Christian Reformed Church in North America): the decisions of the Synod of Dort on election, Christ's death, human corruption and conversion, and the perseverance of the saints, with an introduction to the Arminian dispute they answered.
- `8` [The Barmen Declaration](https://www.ekd.de/en/The-Barmen-Declaration-303.htm) (1934, Evangelical Church in Germany): the six theses of the Confessing Church, with the Bible passage and the false teaching rejected in each.
- `8` [The Belhar Confession](https://www.rca.org/about/theology/creeds-and-confessions/the-belhar-confession/) (1986, on the site of the Reformed Church in America): a confession on the unity of the church, reconciliation and justice, written in the struggle against apartheid in southern Africa.
- `7` [Christian Theology, 5.1.4: Reformed churches](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianTheology#section5.1.4) (Judith Wolfe, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): the Reformed churches, their sources of authority, worship, government and recurring theological themes.

## Differences within the tradition

- `7` [Christian Theology, 5.1.4: Reformed churches](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianTheology#section5.1.4) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): **families of churches**. Continental Reformed churches confess the Three Forms of Unity, which the Christian Reformed Church in North America and the Reformed Church in America share in a common translation; the Reformed Church in America adds the Belhar Confession to its Standards of Unity ([Christian Reformed Church in North America, Introduction to the Reformed Confessions Translation](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/introduction-reformed-confessions-translation-2011), [Reformed Church in America, Creeds and Confessions](https://www.rca.org/about/theology/creeds-and-confessions/)). Presbyterian churches confess the Westminster Standards ([Orthodox Presbyterian Church, Confession and Catechisms](https://www.opc.org/confessions.html)). United churches bring the families together: the United Reformed Church in the United Kingdom was formed in 1972 from the Presbyterian Church of England and the Congregational Church in England and Wales ([United Reformed Church, What is the URC?](https://urc.org.uk/who-we-are/what-is-the-urc/)).
- `7` [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Confessions](https://pcusa.org/confessions): **how strictly the confessions bind**. The Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) claims "Scripture as the primary rule of faith and life", and its Book of Confessions gathers twelve creeds, confessions and catechisms from different centuries, from the Nicene Creed to Barmen and Belhar, which its elders and candidates for ministry study. The Presbyterian Church in America and the Orthodox Presbyterian Church hold the Westminster Confession and Catechisms as their doctrinal standards, and their officers vow to "sincerely receive and adopt" them "as containing the system of doctrine taught in the Holy Scriptures". The Presbyterian Church in America places its standards under Scripture as "the inerrant Word Of God", and asks candidates for ordination to state any point where they differ from them ([Presbyterian Church in America, Book of Church Order, Preface](https://www.pcaac.org/book-of-church-order/preface/), [Presbyterian Church in America, Book of Church Order, Form of Government](https://www.pcaac.org/book-of-church-order/part-1-the-form-of-government/), [Orthodox Presbyterian Church, Confession and Catechisms](https://www.opc.org/confessions.html)).
- `7` [Presbyterian Mission Agency, Women in the church](https://pma.pcusa.org/ministries/101/women/): **the ordination of women**. Predecessor churches of the Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) began ordaining women as elders in 1930 and as ministers in 1956. The Presbyterian Church in America's Book of Church Order holds that the offices of elder and deacon, "in accord with Scripture", are "open to men only" ([Presbyterian Church in America, Book of Church Order, 7-2](https://www.pcaac.org/book-of-church-order/part-1-the-form-of-government/)).
- `7` [Communion of Protestant Churches in Europe, History](https://www.leuenberg.eu/about-us/history/): **fellowship with Lutheran churches**. In the Leuenberg Agreement of 1973, Reformed and Lutheran churches in Europe recognised each other, so that "a Lutheran pastor has been able to preach from a Reformed pulpit and a Reformed believer can receive communion at a Lutheran service". Differences in certain doctrines were judged to be "bridgeable", with "reconciliation in united diversity as the way forward".
