# Church traditions

What each church, or family of churches, teaches and how it lives, each in its own terms. The [general guidelines](../general/README.md) state what the churches teach together, and that agreement is their evidence ([ADR-0006](../docs/decisions/0006-write-rules-only-where-the-churches-agree.md)). These pages show the rest: how each church reads Scripture, what it holds about the sacraments and ministry, and how it answers the questions on which the churches differ.

Each page sets out a tradition's teaching, cites its own catechisms, confessions and teaching documents and scholarly reference works, shows where churches within it differ, and maps each teaching to the general rules it matches. The pages describe; they don't judge any church's teaching, rank the churches against each other, or adopt one as the standard for the others ([ADR-0007](../docs/decisions/0007-describe-churches-on-their-own-terms.md)). A short page can't do justice to a living church, so each page links to fuller treatments.

## Church traditions

- [The Catholic Church](catholic.md): Scripture and Tradition with the teaching office, the seven sacraments, Mary and the saints, grace and justification, and Catholic social teaching.
- [The Eastern Orthodox churches](eastern-orthodox.md): Holy Tradition, the seven ecumenical councils, deification (theosis), the Divine Liturgy and the holy mysteries, icons, prayer and fasting.
- [The Oriental Orthodox churches](oriental-orthodox.md): the Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian, Eritrean and Malankara churches, the first three councils, the one incarnate nature of Christ, the liturgy, fasting and monasticism.
- [The Lutheran churches](lutheran.md): Scripture as rule and norm, justification by grace through faith, Word and sacraments, the Book of Concord and Luther's catechisms.
- [The Reformed and Presbyterian churches](reformed.md): Scripture as the only rule of faith and life, the Reformed confessions and catechisms, God's sovereignty and grace, government by elders, and the church's public witness.
- [The Anglican churches](anglican.md): the Church of England and the Anglican Communion, Scripture, the creeds, the sacraments and the historic episcopate, the Book of Common Prayer and the Thirty-Nine Articles.

- [The Methodist churches](methodist.md): the Wesleys' movement, Scripture with tradition, reason and experience, grace for all, holiness and Christian perfection, the General Rules and social holiness.
- [The Baptist churches](baptist.md): Scripture as final authority, believers' baptism, the gathered local church and its freedom, the priesthood of all believers, and religious liberty.
- [The Pentecostal and charismatic churches](pentecostal.md): the experience of the Holy Spirit, baptism in the Spirit and its gifts, divine healing, holiness and mission, and the charismatic renewal in older churches.
- [The Anabaptist churches](anabaptist.md): the Mennonite, Amish, Hutterite and Brethren churches, believers' baptism, following Jesus, peace and nonresistance, truth without oaths, and mutual aid.

Many other churches still need pages, including the many independent and evangelical churches, the Seventh-day Adventists, the Quakers and the Churches of Christ.
