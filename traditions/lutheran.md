# The Lutheran churches

The Lutheran churches trace their teaching to Martin Luther (1483–1546) and the Reformation in Wittenberg, which began when Luther disputed the sale of indulgences in his ninety-five theses of 1517 ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Christian Ethics, 4.3](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianEthics#section4.3)). Most Lutheran churches belong to the Lutheran World Federation, founded at Lund in 1947 ([Lutheran World Federation, History](https://lutheranworld.org/who-we-are/history-lwf)). It has 153 member churches, representing over 78 million Christians in 99 countries, united in "pulpit and altar fellowship" ([Lutheran World Federation, Who we are](https://lutheranworld.org/who-we-are), [Our Lutheran Identity](https://lutheranworld.org/who-we-are/our-lutheran-identity)). Other Lutheran churches, such as the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod, belong to the International Lutheran Council, an association of confessional Lutheran churches with an "unconditional commitment" to Scripture and to the Lutheran Confessions ([International Lutheran Council, About us](https://ilcouncil.org/about-us/), [Members](https://ilcouncil.org/members/)).

In Lutheran teaching, Scripture holds the highest authority. The Formula of Concord holds that the prophetic and apostolic Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments alone are "the sole rule and standard" by which all teaching is judged, and other writings are witnesses to how that teaching was kept. It accepts the three ecumenical creeds and, as the confession of its own time, the Augsburg Confession, its Apology, the Smalcald Articles and Luther's Small and Large Catechisms ([Formula of Concord, Epitome, Rule and Norm](https://bookofconcord.org/epitome/rule-and-norm/)). These texts, with the Formula of Concord itself, were collected in the Book of Concord of 1580 ([St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, Lutheran Confessionalism, 3](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/LutheranConfessionalism#section3)).

## Adopted references

- `9` [The Book of Concord](https://bookofconcord.org/): the Lutheran Confessions in English, from the public domain *Triglot Concordia* of 1921 ([About the translation](https://bookofconcord.org/about-the-translation/)): the three ecumenical creeds, the Augsburg Confession and its Apology, the Smalcald Articles, the Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope, Luther's catechisms and the Formula of Concord.
- `8` [The Augsburg Confession](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/) (1530): the chief Lutheran confession, presented to Emperor Charles V. Its first 21 articles set out Lutheran teaching, from God to the worship of the saints.
- `8` [Luther's Small Catechism](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/) (1529): the Ten Commandments, the Creed, the Lord's Prayer, baptism, confession and the Lord's Supper, explained question by question for teaching at home.
- `8` [Lutheran Confessionalism](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/LutheranConfessionalism) (Werner Klän, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): the Lutheran Confessions and their authority, their history, and how confessional Lutheran churches and other Lutheran churches differ today.

## Differences within the tradition

- `7` [Lutheran Confessionalism, 1: Lutheran Confessionalism – Confessional Lutheranism](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/LutheranConfessionalism#section1) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): **how binding the confessions are**. Confessional Lutheran churches accept the Book of Concord without qualification, "because" (*quia*) it agrees with Scripture; the article contrasts this with holding the confessions binding only "insofar as" (*quatenus*) they agree with it. The [Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod](https://www.lcms.org/what-we-believe/beliefs) says that its congregations believe the confessions are "a correct interpretation and presentation of biblical doctrine". The [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America](https://www.elca.org/about-the-elca/who-we-are/beliefs/theology), a member of the Lutheran World Federation, affirms them in its constitution and draws on them "alongside Scripture" in discerning God's revelation today.
- `7` [Lutheran Confessionalism, 9: New profiles in the twenty-first century](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/LutheranConfessionalism#section9) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): **ordination and marriage**. Most confessional Lutheran churches, including those of the International Lutheran Council, hold that Scripture doesn't allow the ordination of women, while valuing other ministries of women, such as deaconesses in the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod. They also hold that the church may not bless same-sex couples. The Lutheran World Federation supports women in the ordained ministry and works "to remove barriers" for them ([Lutheran World Federation, Women and Gender studies](https://lutheranworld.org/what-we-do/theology/women-and-gender-studies), [Facts and Figures](https://lutheranworld.org/who-we-are/history-lwf/facts-and-figures)), and the article notes that the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America supports both women's ordination and same-sex marriage.
- `7` [Lutheran Confessionalism, 10.3: Ecumenical endeavours](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/LutheranConfessionalism#section10.3) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): **fellowship with other churches**. Nearly all the large Lutheran churches of Europe signed the Leuenberg Agreement of 1973 with Reformed and other Protestant churches, so that "a Lutheran pastor has been able to preach from a Reformed pulpit" ([Communion of Protestant Churches in Europe, History](https://www.leuenberg.eu/about-us/history/)). The Porvoo Communion unites the Lutheran churches of the Nordic and Baltic countries and Great Britain with the Anglican churches of Great Britain, Ireland and the Iberian Peninsula ([Porvoo Communion, What is the Porvoo Communion?](https://porvoocommunion.org/what-is-the-porvoo-communion/), [The Porvoo Common Statement](https://porvoocommunion.org/what-is-the-porvoo-communion/statement/the-statement-in-english/)). Confessional Lutheran churches hold that fellowship at the altar and in the pulpit needs agreement in "faith, doctrine, and confession", and don't consider these agreements compatible with the Book of Concord.
- `7` [Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod, Beliefs](https://www.lcms.org/what-we-believe/beliefs): **Scripture**. The Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod teaches that the Bible is "the inspired and inerrant Word of God". The [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America](https://www.elca.org/about-the-elca/who-we-are/beliefs/theology) describes the Scriptures as holding and presenting "the living Christ", who is "the lens through which we read Scripture".
