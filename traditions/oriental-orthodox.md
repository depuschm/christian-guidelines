# The Oriental Orthodox churches

The Oriental Orthodox churches are a family of six churches: the Coptic Orthodox Church of Egypt, the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the Armenian Apostolic Church, the Syriac Orthodox Church of Antioch and the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church of India. The World Council of Churches notes that they have been called "non- or anti- or pre-Chalcedonian, erroneously as Monophysite", and that "the generally accepted name is Oriental Orthodox". Most of their members live in Ethiopia, Egypt, Eritrea, Armenia, India, Syria and Lebanon, with large communities abroad ([World Council of Churches, Orthodox churches (Oriental)](https://www.oikoumene.org/church-families/orthodox-churches-oriental)). The Pew Research Center describes them as separate self-governing churches that together make up roughly a fifth of the world's Orthodox Christians. The largest is the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, with an estimated 36 million members ([Pew Research Center, Orthodox Christianity in the 21st Century](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/11/08/orthodox-christianity-in-the-21st-century/)).

The family's teaching rests on the first three ecumenical councils, Nicaea (325), Constantinople (381) and Ephesus (431), and on the Christology of St Cyril of Alexandria, summed up in his formula "one nature of the Word Incarnate". The churches did not accept the Christological teaching of the Council of Chalcedon (451), and the break over it marked, in the World Council of Churches' words, "the first ecclesial division in church history". The Eastern and Oriental Orthodox are still not in communion with each other. Since 1985 they have held an official theological dialogue, which reached agreements on Christology whose reception is the main remaining question ([World Council of Churches, Orthodox churches (Oriental)](https://www.oikoumene.org/church-families/orthodox-churches-oriental)). The Ethiopian Church writes that it belongs to the group of churches "wrongly termed 'Monophysites'", which prefer to be called non-Chalcedonian ([Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, The Issue between Monophysitism and Dyophysitism](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/dogma/monodyo.html)).

The World Council of Churches describes their theology as "biblical, liturgical and patristic" ([World Council of Churches, Orthodox churches (Oriental)](https://www.oikoumene.org/church-families/orthodox-churches-oriental)). The Armenian Church, for example, "bases its faith on the Bible, holy Tradition, and on the decisions of the first three ecumenical councils" ([World Council of Churches, Armenian Apostolic Church (Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin)](https://www.oikoumene.org/member-churches/armenian-apostolic-church-mother-see-of-holy-etchmiadzin)). The Armenian Church describes itself as sharing "the same confession of faith" with the Coptic, Ethiopian and Indian Malankara churches ([Armenian Apostolic Holy Church, Overview](https://www.armenianchurch.org/en/Overview)). Each church governs itself. The Ethiopian Church, for example, received its bishops from the Coptic Church of Alexandria for centuries, and its independence was sealed when the Patriarch of Alexandria installed its first patriarch in 1959 ([Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, The Hierarchy](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/hierarchy.html)).

## Adopted references

- `8` [Joint Declaration of Pope Paul VI and Pope Shenouda III](https://www.vatican.va/content/paul-vi/en/speeches/1973/may/documents/hf_p-vi_spe_19730510_dichiarazione-comune.html) (Vatican, 10 May 1973): the Catholic and Coptic Orthodox popes confess one faith in Christ, "perfect God with respect to His divinity, perfect man with respect to His humanity", the same seven sacraments and the veneration of the Virgin Mary.
- `8` [Second Agreed Statement](https://dss-syriacpatriarchate.org/second-agreed-statement-1990/?lang=en) (Joint Commission of the Theological Dialogue between the Orthodox Church and the Oriental Orthodox Churches, Chambésy, 1990, published by the Syriac Orthodox Patriarchate's Department of Syriac Studies): the Eastern and Oriental Orthodox families' common statement on Christ, and how each may keep its own terminology.
- `8` [Common Declaration of Pope John Paul II and Patriarch Ignatius Zakka I Iwas](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/speeches/1984/june/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_19840623_jp-ii-zakka-i.html) (Vatican, 23 June 1984): the Catholic and Syriac Orthodox churches find "no real basis" for their division over the Incarnation, and allow their faithful to receive some sacraments in either church when their own priest cannot be reached.
- `8` [The Faith of the Church](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/dogma/faith.html) (Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church): the Ethiopian Church's own introduction to its faith: the Trinity, the Incarnation, salvation, baptism, the Eucharist and the resurrection, drawing on its liturgy.
- `7` [Orthodox churches (Oriental)](https://www.oikoumene.org/church-families/orthodox-churches-oriental) (World Council of Churches): an overview of the family: its churches, its Christology, its relation to the Eastern Orthodox churches and its life today.
- `7` [Pope Shenouda III, The Nature of Christ: The Orthodox Concept regarding the Nature of Christ](https://st-takla.org/books/en/pope-shenouda-iii/nature-of-christ/orthodox-concept.html) (St-Takla.org): the Coptic Orthodox pope's explanation of the Church's teaching on the one nature of the incarnate Word, and of how the Church came to be called "Monophysite".

## Differences within the tradition

- `7` [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, The Hierarchy](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/ethiopian/hierarchy.html): the **Ethiopian and Eritrean churches** both call themselves Tewahedo. The Ethiopian Church received its head from the Coptic Church of Alexandria for centuries and has had its own patriarch since 1959. The Eritrean Church is governed by its own holy synod under its own patriarch ([World Council of Churches, Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church](https://www.oikoumene.org/member-churches/eritrean-orthodox-tewahedo-church)), and in 2025 enthroned its sixth patriarch, Abune Basliyos ([World Council of Churches, 2025](https://www.oikoumene.org/news/wcc-extends-congratulations-to-he-abune-basliyos-upon-enthronement-as-6th-patriarch-of-the-eritrean-orthodox-tewahedo-church)).
- `7` [World Council of Churches, Armenian Apostolic Church (Holy See of Cilicia)](https://www.oikoumene.org/member-churches/armenian-apostolic-church-holy-see-of-cilicia): the **Armenian Church** has had two catholicosates since 1441, the Catholicosate of All Armenians at Holy Etchmiadzin and the Catholicosate of the Great House of Cilicia, now at Antelias in Lebanon, "with equal rights and privileges, and with their respective jurisdictions".
- `7` [World Council of Churches, Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church](https://www.oikoumene.org/member-churches/malankara-orthodox-syrian-church): in **India**, the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church established its own Catholicos in 1912, while, in its words, "a group continued to maintain allegiance" to the claims of the Syriac Orthodox Patriarch of Antioch, and the dispute went to the courts. Today the two sides are the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church and the Jacobite Syriac Orthodox Church, which describes itself as "under the Holy Apostolic See of Antioch" ([Jacobite Syriac Orthodox Church](https://jacobitesyrianchurch.org/)). In December 2024 India's Supreme Court made clear that its ruling of 2017, which requires certain churches in Kerala to be handed over from the Jacobite to the Orthodox side, must be followed ([Bar and Bench, 3 December 2024](https://www.barandbench.com/news/church-dispute-supreme-court-jacobites-2017-verdict-hand-over-churches-orthodox)).
- `6` [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, Religious Holidays and Calendar](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/calendar.html): the churches differ in **calendar and canon**. The Ethiopian calendar came from Egypt and agrees with the Coptic one in its methods and dates, though not in its saints' days, while the Armenian Church mainly uses the Gregorian calendar ([World Council of Churches, Armenian Apostolic Church (Mother See of Holy Etchmiadzin)](https://www.oikoumene.org/member-churches/armenian-apostolic-church-mother-see-of-holy-etchmiadzin)). The Ethiopian Church counts 81 books in its Bible ([Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, The Canonical Books](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html)).
