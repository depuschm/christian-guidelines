# The Catholic Church

The Catholic Church is the Church in communion with the Bishop of Rome, the Pope, whom it holds to be the successor of the apostle Peter ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 880–882](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2A.HTM)). It is made up of the Latin Church and the Eastern Catholic churches, which keep their own liturgy, church law and spiritual heritage. The Second Vatican Council called these churches "of equal dignity" ([Orientalium Ecclesiarum, 2–3](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_orientalium-ecclesiarum_en.html), [Vatican News, Dicastery for the Eastern Churches](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2025-08/dicastery-for-the-eastern-churches.html)). The Church's own statistics counted about 1.42 billion Catholics in mid-2024, 17.8 per cent of the world's population ([Vatican News, Fides 2026 Church statistics](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2026-10/fides-agency-2026-annual-church-statistics.html)).

Catholic teaching holds that God's revelation is handed on in Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition, which "form one sacred deposit of the word of God", and that the task of authentically interpreting it belongs to the Church's living teaching office, the Magisterium: the bishops in communion with the Pope ([Dei Verbum, 10](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 85](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PM.HTM)). The Catechism adds that the Magisterium "is not superior to the Word of God, but is its servant" (86). The ecumenical councils, most recently the Second Vatican Council (1962–1965), and the Catechism of the Catholic Church set out the Church's teaching today ([Bosschaert, History of the Second Vatican Council](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/VaticanII)).

## Adopted references

- `9` [Lumen gentium](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19641121_lumen-gentium_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1964): the Dogmatic Constitution on the Church: the Church as mystery and people of God, the bishops and the Pope, the laity, the call of everyone to holiness, and Mary.
- `9` [Dei Verbum](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1965): the Dogmatic Constitution on Divine Revelation: Scripture and Tradition, the inspiration and interpretation of Scripture, and Scripture in the life of the Church.
- `8` [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html) (Pontifical Council for Justice and Peace, 2004): Catholic social teaching in one volume: human dignity, the common good, subsidiarity, solidarity, work, the economy, the environment and peace.

## Teaching

In the order of the Catechism of the Catholic Church: the faith the Church professes, the sacraments, life in Christ and prayer. *Related rules* are the general rules the teaching matches; the cell is empty where a teaching is the Catholic Church's own.

## Differences within the tradition

- `8` [Orientalium Ecclesiarum, 2–3](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_orientalium-ecclesiarum_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1964): the **Eastern Catholic churches**, whose territories extend from Ethiopia to the Middle East and from Europe to India, share the faith and the communion with the Pope of the Latin Church, but differ from it "in liturgy, ecclesiastical discipline, and spiritual heritage", and the council taught that they are "of equal dignity". They have their own Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches, promulgated in 1990 ([Vatican News, Dicastery for the Eastern Churches](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2025-08/dicastery-for-the-eastern-churches.html)).
- `7` [Bosschaert, History of the Second Vatican Council, 6: Receptions of Vatican II](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/VaticanII#section6) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): Catholics have **received the Second Vatican Council** in different ways. Many welcomed the renewal of the liturgy and the opening to ecumenism, others viewed them with concern; Bishop Marcel Lefebvre questioned the council's legitimacy, which led to the schism of the Society of Saint Pius X in 1988; and the years after the council were marked by debates on how it should be interpreted.
- `7` [Christian Ethics, 4.1: A Roman Catholic perspective](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/ChristianEthics#section4.1) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): in Catholic teaching, questions of **sexual ethics, contraception, abortion, and marriage and divorce** "remain mostly closed to discussion, although they are highly disputed among the faithful themselves". The same encyclopaedia describes Paul VI's encyclical *Humanae vitae* (1968), which rejected artificial contraception, as a catalyst of "catholic dissent", with many lay Catholics appealing to freedom of conscience ([Bosschaert, Receptions of Vatican II](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/VaticanII#section6)).
