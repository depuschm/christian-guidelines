# Mission abroad and development

Scope: missionaries and their families, mission agencies, short-term mission teams, churches with partner churches abroad, and Christian relief and development organisations: everyone who serves across borders in the name of Christ. The general rules on [Sharing the faith](../../general/mission.md) come first: share the good news in word and deed (`MISS-1`), never coerce or manipulate (`MISS-2`), live what you say (`MISS-3`), respect people of other faiths (`MISS-4`), serve people in need without making help depend on their faith (`MISS-5`), and pray for and support mission everywhere (`MISS-6`). So do the rules on unity between the churches (`CHURCH-2`), on defending the poor and the dignity of every person (`JUST-1`, `JUST-2`), and on honesty with money (`MONEY-1`, `LEAD-5`). Work with children and adults at risk abroad follows the same standards as at home: [Safeguarding children and vulnerable adults](../safeguarding/README.md), above all reporting every concern (`SAFE-1`) and safe recruitment (`SAFE-4`). This page adds what is particular to working across borders. It is moral and pastoral guidance, not legal advice: the laws on charities, aid, visas and child protection differ from country to country.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Christian Witness in a Multi-Religious World: Recommendations for Conduct](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/ChristianWitness_recommendations.pdf) (World Council of Churches, Pontifical Council for Interreligious Dialogue and World Evangelical Alliance, 2011): the shared basis for Christian witness agreed with "the largest Christian families of faith (Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant, Evangelical and Pentecostal)", which holds that "the exploitation of situations of poverty and need has no place in Christian outreach" (principle 4). **Follow it as the baseline for witness and service across borders.**
- `8` [Ad gentes](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19651207_ad-gentes_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1965): the Catholic decree on the Church's missionary activity, including how Christians should know and esteem the peoples among whom they live (11) and the training of missionaries in the languages, history and customs of those peoples (26).
- `8` [Redemptoris missio](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_07121990_redemptoris-missio.html) (John Paul II, 1990): the encyclical on the Church's missionary mandate: missionaries working "in communion with their local counterparts" (49), inculturation (52–53), and development, in which "Man is the principal agent of development, not money or technology" (58).
- `8` [The Mission of the Orthodox Church in Today's World](https://www.holycouncil.org/mission-orthodox-church-todays-world) (Holy and Great Council of the Orthodox Church, 2016): the Council's document on mission, including the witness of love through service, in which "the Orthodox Church actively cares for all people in need, including the hungry, the poor, the sick", "the orphans, the victims of destruction and military conflict" (F).
- `8` [The Lausanne Covenant](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant) (Lausanne Movement, 1974): the evangelical covenant on churches in evangelistic partnership (8), evangelism and culture (10), and the training of national leaders (11).
- `8` [The Cape Town Commitment](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment) (Lausanne Movement, 2010): the evangelical commitment, including its call for "a new global partnership within the body of Christ" (I.9) and its section on "Partnership in global mission" between churches in every part of the world (IIF.2).
- `7` [ACT Code of Good Practice](https://actalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/ACT-Code-of-Good-Practice-2024.pdf) (ACT Alliance, Action by Churches Together, 2024, PDF): the standards that the members of this alliance of churches and church-related agencies commit to in humanitarian and development work, with its appendix, the "non-proselytism statement" adopted in 2016.

## Key rules

### Working with local churches and people

- `8` `ABROAD-1` **Work with the local church and the local people as partners: follow their lead, and help their own leaders take responsibility.** Paul thanks the Philippians for their "partnership in furtherance of the Good News", and on his journeys "appointed elders for them in every assembly" before moving on. John Paul II teaches that "Missionary personnel coming from other churches and countries must work in communion with their local counterparts for the development of the Christian community". The Lausanne Covenant confesses that "some of our missions have been too slow to equip and encourage national leaders to assume their rightful responsibilities", and longs "that every church will have national leaders who manifest a Christian style of leadership in terms not of domination but of service"; the Cape Town Commitment asks churches to "no longer impose our own preferred names, slogans, programmes, systems and methods on other parts of the Church". The ACT Alliance asks its members to "Support formal and informal local agency and capacity and locally-led efforts" and to "Engage where possible with local churches and/or other faith-based actors". See also `CHURCH-2` and `LEAD-6`. ([Philippians 1:3–5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V3), [Acts 14:21–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT14.htm#V21), [Redemptoris missio, 49](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_07121990_redemptoris-missio.html), [The Lausanne Covenant, 11](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant), [The Cape Town Commitment, IIF.2](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment), [ACT Code of Good Practice, overarching principle 4 and organisational principle 7](https://actalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/ACT-Code-of-Good-Practice-2024.pdf))
- `8` `ABROAD-2` **Learn the language and the culture of the people you serve, honour what is good in it, and don't take your own culture abroad as if it were the gospel.** Paul writes: "I have become all things to all men, that I may by all means save some", and in Athens he begins from what he has seen of the city's own worship. The Second Vatican Council asks Christians to "be familiar with their national and religious traditions" and to "gladly and reverently lay bare the seeds of the Word which lie hidden among their fellows", and teaches that "anyone who is going to encounter another people should have a great esteem for their patrimony and their language and their customs"; John Paul II, that missionaries "must learn the language of the place in which they work, become familiar with the most important expressions of the local culture, and discover its values through direct experience". The Lausanne Covenant confesses that "Missions have, all too frequently, exported with the gospel an alien culture", and holds that "The gospel does not presuppose the superiority of any culture to another". See also `MISS-4`. ([1 Corinthians 9:19–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO09.htm#V19), [Acts 17:22–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT17.htm#V22), [Ad gentes, 11 and 26](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19651207_ad-gentes_en.html), [Redemptoris missio, 53](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_07121990_redemptoris-missio.html), [The Lausanne Covenant, 10](https://lausanne.org/statement/lausanne-covenant))

### Children

- `8` `ABROAD-3` **Help children grow up in families: support their own families, relatives, foster care and adoption rather than orphanages.** God is "A father of the fatherless" who "sets the lonely in families", and "Pure religion" is "to visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction". Catholic Care for Children International, the initiative of the International Union of Superiors General, supports Catholic sisters' efforts "to reduce recourse to institutional care of children and encourage family-based care". The World Evangelical Alliance holds that "the global Church must urgently shift from institutional child care toward family-based solutions", and that "family-based care—such as family reunification, kinship care, foster care, and adoption—is the most appropriate and life-giving option for children whenever possible"; a Lausanne paper asks churches to "find ways to avoid institutional care, favoring stable, healthy, nurturing relationships for children at risk". The Methodist Church in Ireland, which endorses a campaign that includes "guidance not to visit or volunteer in orphanages", writes that "Even well-run and resourced orphanages, with staff that care about the children, cannot replace the love and stability of family-based care". See also `CHURCH-6` and `PAR-5`. ([Psalm 68:5–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA068.htm#V5), [James 1:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V27), [International Union of Superiors General, Journeying Together to Transform Care for Children](https://www.uisg.org/en/news/Journeying-Together-to-Transform-Care-for-Children), [World Evangelical Alliance, A Call to Embrace Family-Based Care](https://children.worldea.org/global-issues/responding-to-the-orphan-crisis/family-based-care-statement/), [Lausanne Movement, Mission with Children at Risk](https://lausanne.org/occasional-paper/mission-children-risk-lop-66), [Methodist Church in Ireland, Putting Children First](https://irishmethodist.org/news/putting-children-first))

### Money and stories

- `8` `ABROAD-4` **Give and spend accountably, and in ways that build up people's own work and leadership, not dependence or control.** Paul's collection for Jerusalem is "for equality", and he takes care that no one "should blame us concerning this abundance which is administered by us", "Having regard for honourable things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men". John Paul II teaches that "a people's development does not derive primarily from money, material assistance or technological means" and that "Man is the principal agent of development, not money or technology". The Cape Town Commitment warns that "unwise injection of money frequently corrupts and divides the Church", asks that "those who have the most money" not have "all the decision-making power", and seeks "economic sharing without paternalism or unhealthy dependency". The ACT Alliance asks its members to "Manage financial resources in an efficient, transparent and accountable manner". See also `MONEY-1` and `LEAD-5`. ([2 Corinthians 8:13–14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO08.htm#V13), [2 Corinthians 8:20–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO08.htm#V20), [Redemptoris missio, 58](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_07121990_redemptoris-missio.html), [The Cape Town Commitment, I.9 and IIF.2](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment), [ACT Code of Good Practice, organisational principle 8](https://actalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/ACT-Code-of-Good-Practice-2024.pdf))
- `7` `ABROAD-5` **Tell the stories of the people you serve truthfully and with dignity: ask their consent, and never use pictures or words that shame them.** Every person is made "in God's image", and Jesus' rule holds here too: "whatever you desire for men to do to you, you shall also do to them". Caritas Australia, the Catholic agency for international aid and development in Australia, requires that "Informed, freely given and prior consent must be obtained and documented before any photo or video is taken", and rejects "stereotypes and white saviour language that infringe the dignity of participants". The relief and development agency Tearfund recognises "that each person has been created in the image of God, and as such is of inherent value, and worthy of dignity", so that participants' "best interests, dignity and safety must take priority over any editorial or organisational requirement". The ACT Alliance asks its members to "Reflect in communication and fundraising materials the dignity, resilience and initiative of affected communities". See also `JUST-2`. ([Genesis 1:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V27), [Matthew 7:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT07.htm#V12), [Caritas Australia, Ethical Storytelling Guidelines](https://caritas.org.au/media/bwdnpvcv/ethical-storytelling-guidelines_final.pdf), [Tearfund, Content Gathering, Storage and Use Policy](https://learn.tearfund.org/-/media/learn/policies/2025-tearfund-policy-content-gathering-en.pdf), [ACT Code of Good Practice, organisational principle 9](https://actalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/ACT-Code-of-Good-Practice-2024.pdf))

## How to apply

- `7` [ACT Code of Good Practice, Appendix: non-proselytism statement](https://actalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/ACT-Code-of-Good-Practice-2024.pdf) (ACT Alliance, adopted 2016, PDF): a short statement that member agencies can adopt as their own, which rejects "the use of any aid, either to an individual or to a community, to advance religious or partisan political standpoints". Supports `MISS-5` in relief and development work.
- `7` [Caritas Australia, Ethical Storytelling Guidelines](https://caritas.org.au/media/bwdnpvcv/ethical-storytelling-guidelines_final.pdf) (PDF): a practical guide to gathering stories, photos and video, with how to document "free, prior and informed consent", for which "A fingerprint or cross is acceptable in lieu of a signature". Supports `ABROAD-5`.
- `7` [UISG: Catholic sisters advocate for 'a family for every child'](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2023-11/uisg-catholic-sisters-advocate-family-for-evey-child-in-world.html) (Vatican News, 2023): how Catholic Care for Children International, "a global network of Catholic Sisters advocating for family- and community-based care for children", works with others "to facilitate the successful transition of a child back to their family and community, and also prevent the separation of children from their families". Supports `ABROAD-3`.
- `6` [7 Standards of Excellence](https://missionexcellence.global/7-standards/) (MissionExcellence): "A code of best practice for short-term mission practitioners", which asks for "healthy, interdependent, ongoing relationships between sending and receiving partners", planning together with the hosts, training before, during and after the trip, and "Truthfulness in promotion, finances, and reporting results". Supports `ABROAD-1`, `ABROAD-2` and `ABROAD-4`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side. Whether Christians may evangelise members of other churches, and whether people who aren't Christians can be saved, are set out in the [open questions of Sharing the faith](../../general/mission.md#open-questions).

- `7` [The Gifts and the Calling of God are Irrevocable, 40](https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/gifts-of-god-are-irrevocable-30005) (Commission for Religious Relations with the Jews, 2015, on the site of EWTN): **should Christians seek to bring Jewish people to faith in Jesus?** The Catholic Commission, in a reflection that it says "is not a magisterial document or doctrinal teaching of the Catholic Church", writes that "the Catholic Church neither conducts nor supports any specific institutional mission work directed towards Jews", while "Christians are nonetheless called to bear witness to their faith in Jesus Christ also to Jews, although they should do so in a humble and sensitive manner". The Synod of the Evangelical Church in Germany declared in 2016 that "Christians – notwithstanding their mission to the world – are not called to show Israel the way to God and his salvation", and that "All efforts to move Jews to change their religion contradict the confession of God's faithfulness and of Israel's election" (this page's translation of „Christen sind – ungeachtet ihrer Sendung in die Welt – nicht berufen, Israel den Weg zu Gott und seinem Heil zu weisen. Alle Bemühungen, Juden zum Religionswechsel zu bewegen, widersprechen dem Bekenntnis zur Treue Gottes und der Erwählung Israels.“) ([Evangelical Church in Germany, Christen und Juden als Zeugen der Treue Gottes](https://www.ekd.de/pm170_2016_christen_und_juden_als_zeugen_der_treue_gottes.htm)). The Cape Town Commitment, by contrast, holds that the Jewish people "still stand in need of reconciliation to God through the Messiah Jesus", and continues "strongly to affirm the need for the whole Church to share the good news of Jesus as Messiah, Lord and Saviour with Jewish people" ([The Cape Town Commitment, IIB.1](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment)). How Paul's words that "all Israel will be saved" should be read is an open question on the [Romans](../../bible/new-testament/romans.md#open-questions) page ([Romans 11:25–29](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM11.htm#V25)).
- `6` [7 Standards of Excellence](https://missionexcellence.global/7-standards/) (MissionExcellence): **do short-term mission trips do more good than harm?** Those who organise them by these standards hold that a trip planned with and for its hosts, with training and follow-up, can serve both, and a Lausanne paper reports that "Organising short-term teams of business professionals to use their education and expertise in a cross-cultural mission setting has proven highly effective" ([Lausanne Movement, Business as Mission](https://lausanne.org/occasional-paper/business-mission-lop-59)). Other Lausanne papers are more cautious: one says short-term missions "are likely not as effective as is often assumed" ([Lausanne Movement, Mission with Children at Risk](https://lausanne.org/occasional-paper/mission-children-risk-lop-66)), and another warns against "dehumanizing the poor through exposure trips" ([Lausanne Movement, Holistic Mission](https://lausanne.org/occasional-paper/holistic-mission-lop-33)). The Methodist Church in Ireland warns that "the regular turnover of volunteers and visitors to orphanages is harmful to children's development and wellbeing" ([Methodist Church in Ireland, Putting Children First](https://irishmethodist.org/news/putting-children-first)).

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Missiology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Missiology) (James Butler, Harvey Kwiyani and Cathy Ross, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, 2024): the study of mission, how ecumenical councils have joined evangelism and social action, and questions about the colonial assumptions of mission.
- `7` [Holistic Mission](https://lausanne.org/occasional-paper/holistic-mission-lop-33) (Lausanne Occasional Paper 33): an evangelical paper on mission that serves the whole person, which warns that "Relief done poorly can create dependency" and draws on the "Do No Harm" approach to aid in conflict.
