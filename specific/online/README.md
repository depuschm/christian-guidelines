# Online life and technology

Scope: everyone who uses the internet, social media, phones and artificial intelligence (AI) tools; churches that stream their services and run groups and communities online; and Christians who design and build technology. The general rules apply online as they do face to face: tell the truth (`SPEECH-1`), don't gossip or slander (`SPEECH-2`), speak to build others up (`SPEECH-3`), control your tongue and your anger (`SPEECH-4`), keep your word (`SPEECH-5`) and keep confidences (`SPEECH-6`), all in [Honest and kind speech](../../general/speech.md); keep a day of rest (`WORK-4`), treat every person with equal dignity (`JUST-2`), and never pressure or manipulate the people you share your faith with (`MISS-2`). Online contact with children and adults at risk is covered by [Safeguarding children and vulnerable adults](../safeguarding/README.md), above all `CHURCH-6` and `SAFE-5`, and bringing up children in a digital world by [Parents and children](../parents-and-children/README.md). This page adds what the churches teach about life online and about artificial intelligence. If you're worried that a child or an adult at risk is being harmed or groomed online, tell the police and your church's safeguarding lead (`SAFE-1`). This is moral and pastoral guidance, not legal or technical advice.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `8` [Antiqua et nova](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html) (Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and Dicastery for Culture and Education, 2025): the Catholic Church's note on the relationship between artificial intelligence and human intelligence, on what AI can and can't do, on moral responsibility, and on AI in work, healthcare, education, relationships, truth, privacy and warfare. It holds that "Between a machine and a human being, only the latter is truly a moral agent". **Follow it as the baseline for artificial intelligence.**
- `7` [Towards Full Presence: A Pastoral Reflection on Engagement with Social Media](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/dpc/documents/20230528_dpc-verso-piena-presenza_en.html) (Dicastery for Communication, 2023): a Catholic reflection, built on the parable of the Good Samaritan, on how Christians can "live in the digital world as “loving neighbours” who are genuinely present and attentive to each other": listening, silence, community and witness online.
- `7` [Artificial Intelligence and the Wisdom of the Heart: Towards a Fully Human Communication](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/communications/documents/20240124-messaggio-comunicazioni-sociali.html) (Pope Francis, Message for World Communications Day, 2024): on artificial intelligence in information and communication, on "deepfakes", and on the wisdom that "cannot be sought from machines".
- `7` [Bases of the Social Concept, XV: Church and mass media](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xv/) (Russian Orthodox Church, Department for External Church Relations, 2000): Orthodox teaching on the responsibility of the media and of Christians who work in them, which holds that information "should be based not only on the firm commitment to the truth, but also concern for the moral state of the individual and society".
- `7` [Digital Charter](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/digital-charter) (Church of England): a voluntary pledge for individual Christians and churches to make the digital world "as loving and generous as we would when speaking face to face", built on truth, kindness, welcome, inspiration and togetherness, with a point on safeguarding.
- `7` [On Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-artificial-intelligence-and-emerging-technologies/) (Southern Baptist Convention, resolution of 2023): a Baptist resolution which holds that "human dignity must be central to any ethical principles, guidelines, or regulations" for AI, and asks all who use it to do so "in honest, transparent, and Christlike ways".
- `7` [The Seoul Statement, VII: Technology](https://lausanne.org/statement/the-seoul-statement) (Lausanne Movement, 2024): an evangelical statement on technology as an expression of the image of God marred by sin, on deception and addiction in digital media, on artificial intelligence and genetic technologies, and on the faithful stewardship of technology in the church.
- `6` [The Rome Call for AI Ethics](https://www.romecall.org/the-call/) (2020): a call "signed by the Pontifical Academy for Life, Microsoft, IBM, FAO and the Ministry of Innovation, a part of the Italian Government", for an artificial intelligence that "serves every person and humanity as a whole", with six principles: transparency, inclusion, responsibility, impartiality, reliability, and security and privacy.
- `6` [Manifesto of the symposium "Communication for Social Justice in a Digital Age"](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/manifesto-of-the-symposium-communication-for-social-justice-in-a-digital-age) (World Council of Churches and World Association for Christian Communication, 2021): an ecumenical manifesto on disinformation, online hate, privacy and the digital divide, with "Principles to promote communication for social justice in a digital age" and a call for "a transformative movement of individuals, communities, educational institutions, media agencies, and civil society – including communities of faith".

## Key rules

### Online life

- `8` `TECH-1` **Online, speak as you would face to face: truthfully, kindly and with respect, also to those you disagree with.** "Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt", and "let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger". The Catholic Church's reflection on social media asks Christians to be "loving neighbours" online, and to be "known for our availability to listen, to discern before acting, to treat all people with respect, to respond with a question rather than a judgment, to remain silent rather than trigger a controversy". The Russian Orthodox Church holds that "The propaganda of violence, enmity and hatred and ethnic, social and religious discord" is "inadmissible". The Church of England's Digital Charter asks Christians to make the digital world "as loving and generous as we would when speaking face to face" and to "Think the best of people, whether they share our views or are speaking against them", and its guidelines say: "consider not just whether you would say it in person, but the tone you would use". See also `SPEECH-3` and `SPEECH-4`. ([Colossians 4:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V6), [James 1:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V19), [Towards Full Presence, 1 and 77](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/dpc/documents/20230528_dpc-verso-piena-presenza_en.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XV.1](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xv/), [Church of England, Digital Charter](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/digital-charter), [Church of England, Social media community guidelines](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/social-media-community-guidelines))
- `8` `TECH-2` **Check before you post or share: don't pass on rumours, unchecked claims, or false or manipulated pictures and videos.** "You shall not spread a false report", and "A simple man believes everything, but the prudent man carefully considers his ways." Pope Francis calls for "stemming the spread of fake news", and writes that "The best antidotes to falsehoods are not strategies, but people… who are attracted by goodness and take responsibility for how they use language". The Catholic note on artificial intelligence warns that with "deepfake" images, videos and audio, "While the images or videos themselves may be artificial, the damage they cause is real", and asks for "ongoing prudence and careful discernment of all users regarding their activity online". The Church of England's Digital Charter asks Christians to "hold ourselves to high ideals of checking that what we post online is fair and factual", and the Lausanne Movement notes "that media technologies have increased the ease with which people may be deceived". See also `SPEECH-1` and `SPEECH-2`. ([Exodus 23:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO23.htm#V1), [Proverbs 14:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO14.htm#V15), [Pope Francis, Message for World Communications Day 2018](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/communications/documents/papa-francesco_20180124_messaggio-comunicazioni-sociali.html), [Antiqua et nova, 87 and 89](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html), [Church of England, Digital Charter](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/digital-charter), [Lausanne Movement, The Seoul Statement, VII](https://lausanne.org/statement/the-seoul-statement))
- `7` `TECH-3` **Respect other people's privacy: ask before you post about someone or share their picture, and take special care with children.** "Whatever you desire for men to do to you, you shall also do to them", and "don't betray the confidence of another". The Catholic note on artificial intelligence teaches that "privacy plays an essential role in protecting the boundaries of a person’s inner life", and recalls that the Second Vatican Council counted the right "to safeguard privacy" among the fundamental human rights. The Methodist Church in Britain teaches that "we must respect the rights of everyone to choose whether or not to be photographed or recorded", and asks for consent from parents, and from young people aged 12 and over, before photographs of children are taken or shown; the Church of England reminds users that "Text and images shared can be public and permanent, even with privacy settings in place. If you’re not sure, don’t post it."; and the World Council of Churches' manifesto holds that "Everyone is entitled to privacy and control of their information". See also `SPEECH-6` and `SAFE-5`. ([Matthew 7:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT07.htm#V12), [Proverbs 25:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO25.htm#V9), [Antiqua et nova, 90–91](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, Procedures and Guidance, 3.1 and 6.8.1](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [Church of England, Social media community guidelines](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/social-media-community-guidelines), [World Council of Churches, Manifesto "Communication for Social Justice in a Digital Age"](https://www.oikoumene.org/resources/documents/manifesto-of-the-symposium-communication-for-social-justice-in-a-digital-age))
- `7` `TECH-4` **Keep technology in its place: make time away from screens for God, for the people around you and for rest.** Jesus told his disciples, "Come away into a deserted place, and rest awhile", and the psalm says, "Be still, and know that I am God." The Catholic Church's reflection on social media warns that "Without silence and the space to think slowly, deeply, and purposefully, we risk losing not only cognitive capacities but also the depth of our interactions, both human and divine", and speaks of "a growing need to detach ourselves from digital devices", a "digital detox" that "is not simply a withdrawal but rather a way to engage more deeply with God and with others". The Lausanne Movement recognises "that many Christians, especially the young, are addicted to social and digital media and are in effect being “discipled” by them", and calls for "faithful practice of hospitality for forming healthy usage habits"; and the Church of England's reflections on following Jesus in a digital world commend simple choices such as "A prayer before posting" and "Closing a reel instead of slipping into a long scroll". See also `WORK-4` and `PRAY-1`. ([Mark 6:31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK06.htm#V31), [Psalm 46:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA046.htm#V10), [Towards Full Presence, 34–35](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/dpc/documents/20230528_dpc-verso-piena-presenza_en.html), [Lausanne Movement, The Seoul Statement, VII](https://lausanne.org/statement/the-seoul-statement), [Church of England, Following Jesus in a Digital World](https://www.churchofengland.org/prayer-and-reflection/themed/following-jesus-digital-world))

### Artificial intelligence

- `8` `TECH-5` **Use artificial intelligence honestly: be open about using it where honesty requires it, and never use it to deceive or to pass off something false as real.** "Putting away falsehood, speak truth each one with his neighbour", and Paul writes, "we have renounced the hidden things of shame, not walking in craftiness". The Catholic note on artificial intelligence teaches that "misrepresenting AI as a person should always be avoided", that "using AI to deceive in other contexts… is also to be considered immoral", and, for education and research, that "the use of AI should always be transparent and never misrepresented". The Southern Baptist Convention encourages "all who employ these tools to do so in honest, transparent, and Christlike ways", "never seeking to willfully deceive others or take advantage of them for unjust gain or the accumulation of power"; and the Lausanne Movement laments that Christians have not always "resisted the temptation to deceive their audience". See also `SPEECH-1`. ([Ephesians 4:25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V25), [2 Corinthians 4:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO04.htm#V2), [Antiqua et nova, 62 and 84](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html), [Southern Baptist Convention, On Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-artificial-intelligence-and-emerging-technologies/), [Lausanne Movement, The Seoul Statement, VII](https://lausanne.org/statement/the-seoul-statement))
- `8` `TECH-6` **Keep people at the heart of care and of decisions about people: don't let a machine take the place of human presence where it matters most, or decide on its own about people's lives.** John writes, "I don't want to do so with paper and ink, but I hope to come to you and to speak face to face", and Paul asks us to "Weep with those who weep". The Catholic note on artificial intelligence teaches that "Between a machine and a human being, only the latter is truly a moral agent"; that while people are meant for "authentic relationships, AI can only simulate them"; that care should not leave "patients to interact with a machine rather than a human being", and that decisions about treatment "should never be delegated to AI"; and that "those using AI should be careful not to become overly dependent on it for their decision-making". Pope Francis writes that wisdom "cannot be sought from machines". The Southern Baptist Convention holds that "we alone, as distinct moral agents created by God, bear the moral responsibility" for the development and use of these tools, and the Lausanne Movement warns that technology can lead "to misplaced dependence". See also `HOPE-2` and `HOPE-3`. ([2 John 12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2JN01.htm#V12), [Romans 12:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V15), [Antiqua et nova, 39, 46, 63 and 73–74](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html), [Pope Francis, Message for World Communications Day 2024](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/communications/documents/20240124-messaggio-comunicazioni-sociali.html), [Southern Baptist Convention, On Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-artificial-intelligence-and-emerging-technologies/), [Lausanne Movement, The Seoul Statement, VII](https://lausanne.org/statement/the-seoul-statement))
- `7` `TECH-7` **If you design, build or deploy technology, put human dignity, fairness and safety first.** God "created man in his own image", and asks of us "to act justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God". The Catholic note on artificial intelligence repeats Pope Francis's teaching that "the intrinsic dignity of every man and every woman" must be "the key criterion in evaluating emerging technologies", "a criterion of discernment for developers, owners, operators, and regulators of AI, as well as to its users"; Pope Francis writes that human dignity must "serve as indisputable criteria for evaluating them before they are employed"; and the Rome Call asks for AI that does "not create or act according to bias, thus safeguarding fairness and human dignity". The Southern Baptist Convention holds that "human dignity must be central to any ethical principles, guidelines, or regulations for any and all uses of these powerful emerging technologies", and calls on industry leaders to develop them "with the utmost care and discernment"; and the Lausanne Movement calls Christians "who work in this industry" to promote "safe, equitable, and dignifying applications". See also `JUST-2`. ([Genesis 1:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V27), [Micah 6:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MIC06.htm#V8), [Antiqua et nova, 42–43](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20250128_antiqua-et-nova_en.html), [Pope Francis, Message for the World Day of Peace 2024, 2](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/messages/peace/documents/20231208-messaggio-57giornatamondiale-pace2024.html), [Rome Call for AI Ethics](https://www.romecall.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/RomeCall_Paper_web.pdf), [Southern Baptist Convention, On Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-artificial-intelligence-and-emerging-technologies/), [Lausanne Movement, The Seoul Statement, VII](https://lausanne.org/statement/the-seoul-statement))

## How to apply

- `8` [Safeguarding Policy, Procedures and Guidance, 6.8](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf) (Methodist Church in Britain, 2023, PDF): practical guidance for safeguarding children and young people online and for using photographs and recordings, such as making sure "that parents or carers are aware of what their children or young people are doing and have given their written permission in advance", and not using children's names in photograph captions. Supports `TECH-3`, `SAFE-5` and `CHURCH-6`.
- `7` [Social media community guidelines](https://www.churchofengland.org/national-bodies/central-services/communications-team/social-media-community-guidelines) (Church of England): short guidelines that churches can adopt for their own online spaces: "Be safe", "Be respectful", "Be kind", "Be honest", "Take responsibility", "Be a good ambassador" and "Disagree well". Supports `TECH-1`, `TECH-2` and `TECH-3`.
- `6` [Following Jesus in a Digital World](https://www.churchofengland.org/prayer-and-reflection/themed/following-jesus-digital-world) (Church of England): a week of short reflections on the Letter to the Colossians, on "purpose, hope, identity, daily choices, presence, and humility" online, each with a reading, a response and a prayer. Supports `TECH-1` and `TECH-4`.
- `6` [Parental controls offered by your home internet provider](https://saferinternet.org.uk/guide-and-resource/parental-controls-offered-by-your-home-internet-provider) (UK Safer Internet Centre): video guides to setting up the free filters that the main UK internet providers offer, since "filters can be a helpful tool in reducing the chances of coming across something upsetting". Similar tools exist in other countries and on phones and tablets. Supports `FAM-3` and `TECH-4`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Online Celebrations of Holy Communion and Predominantly Online Churches](https://www.methodist.org.uk/about/news/online-celebrations-of-holy-communion-and-predominantly-online-churches/) (Methodist Church in Britain, 2026): **can the church gather and celebrate Holy Communion online?** The Methodist Conference, "following a period of discernment since 2021, has decided that online celebrations of Holy Communion should be generally permitted in the Methodist Church", but has referred the decision to its synods, so that it "will not come into effect yet", while the period of discernment continues. For the Catholic Church, Cardinal Robert Sarah wrote that broadcast Masses have "performed a great service", but that "no broadcast is comparable to personal participation or can replace it" ([Congregation for Divine Worship, Let us return to the Eucharist with joy!, 2020](https://www.cbcew.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2020/09/card-sarah-return-to-eucharist-with-joy.pdf)). The Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod's Commission on Theology and Church Relations has stated that "LCMS churches should refrain from online Communion services" ([Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod, CTCR opinion and addendum: Communion and COVID-19](https://resources.lcms.org/reading-study/ctcr-opinion-communion-covid-19/)).
