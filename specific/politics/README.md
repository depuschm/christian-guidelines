# Politics and public service

Scope: Christians who hold or seek public office, such as members of parliament, ministers, councillors and mayors; judges and civil servants; those who work for political parties and campaigns; and churches when they speak into politics. What every Christian owes to those in authority is in [Public life and citizenship](../../general/public-life.md): respect and pray for those in authority (`CIV-1`), obey just laws and pay taxes (`CIV-2`), obey God rather than people when those in authority command what is wrong (`CIV-3`), work for peace (`CIV-4`), defend religious freedom for everyone (`CIV-5`), and take part in public life for the common good (`CIV-6`). Those who hold power carry the general rules with them into office: act justly and take no bribes (`JUST-3`), work for just laws and structures (`JUST-5`), tell the truth (`SPEECH-1`), don't slander others (`SPEECH-2`), keep your word (`SPEECH-5`), and follow a well-formed conscience (`DISCERN-3`). Campaigning online also falls under [Online life and technology](../online/README.md), above all `TECH-1` and `TECH-2`. This page adds what the churches teach for those who serve in public life, and for churches that speak into politics. It takes no side between political parties, countries or leaders. This is moral guidance, not legal advice: election law and the law on charities and political activity differ from country to country.

## Key rules

### Serving the public

- `9` `POL-1` **Hold office with integrity: take no bribes or favours, and never use public power or public money for yourself, your family or your friends.** Moses chose as rulers "men of truth, hating unjust gain"; Samuel, at the end of his years as judge, could ask the people "Whose ox have I taken?… Of whose hand have I taken a bribe to make me blind my eyes?"; and when his rivals looked for a charge against Daniel, a royal official, "they could find no occasion or fault, because he was faithful." The Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church counts political corruption "one of the most serious" deformities of democracy, which "betrays at one and the same time both moral principles and the norms of social justice", and the Second Vatican Council asks those in politics to act "without regard for their own interests or for material advantages". The Russian Orthodox Church warns the Christian in public life to watch "so that his public or political work may not turn from service into an end in itself that nourishes pride, greed and other vices". Calvin teaches that rulers' revenues are "not so much private chests as treasuries of the whole people"; the United Methodist Church calls "all government officials to aspire to the highest standards of professionalism, honesty and integrity"; and the Cape Town Commitment of the Lausanne Movement holds that "Corruption is condemned in the Bible. It undermines economic development, distorts fair decision-making and destroys social cohesion." See also `JUST-3`. ([Exodus 18:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO18.htm#V21), [1 Samuel 12:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1SA12.htm#V3), [Daniel 6:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DAN06.htm#V4), [Deuteronomy 16:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU16.htm#V19), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 411](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Gaudium et spes, 75](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.3](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/), [Calvin, Institutes, IV.20.13](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.vi.xxi.html), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Political Community, A](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-political-community), [Lausanne Movement, The Cape Town Commitment, IIA.7](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment))
- `8` `POL-2` **Serve the common good, and above all the poor and the weak, rather than your own interest, your party or special interests.** Paul calls the ruler "a servant of God to you for good"; the psalm prays for a king who "will judge your people with righteousness, and your poor with justice" and "will deliver the needy when he cries"; and the prophet tells the king of Judah, "Execute justice and righteousness, and deliver him who is robbed out of the hand of the oppressor." The Second Vatican Council teaches that the political community "exists, consequently, for the sake of the common good", that it is "never allowable" for political parties "to give their interests priority over the common good", and that politicians "should dedicate themselves to the service of all with sincerity and fairness"; the Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church asks for authority "exercised by persons who are able to accept the common good, and not prestige or the gaining of personal advantages, as the true goal of their work". The Russian Orthodox Church calls Orthodox lay people in government to "the unity of justice and mercy" and "the concern for the spiritual and material welfare of people". Among the "sins of superiors", the Westminster Larger Catechism names "an inordinate seeking of themselves, their own glory, ease, profit, or pleasure", and it counts those "over us in place of authority, whether in family, church, or commonwealth" among those superiors; and the Cape Town Commitment teaches that "God holds responsible especially those who are appointed to political or judicial leadership in society" for justice to the needy. See also `JUST-1` and `JUST-5`. ([Romans 13:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM13.htm#V4), [Psalm 72:1–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA072.htm#V1), [Psalm 72:12–14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA072.htm#V12), [Jeremiah 22:1–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JER22.htm#V1), [Gaudium et spes, 74–75](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 410](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.3](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 124 and 130](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Lausanne Movement, The Cape Town Commitment, I.7.C](https://lausanne.org/statement/ctcommitment))

### Truth and respect

- `8` `POL-3` **Tell the truth in politics: don't lie, mislead or smear, and never stir up hatred against any group.** "You shall not spread a false report", and "putting away falsehood, speak truth each one with his neighbour". Pope Francis laments a political life reduced to "slick marketing techniques primarily aimed at discrediting others", and the Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church teaches that "Society has a right to information based on truth, freedom, justice and solidarity". The Archbishops of Canterbury and York wrote before a general election that "each of us is called to honour the gift of truth, both to speak it and to seek it", that "We all have a responsibility to speak accurately, to challenge falsehoods when we hear them, and to be careful to separate facts from opinion", and called on "all standing for election to reject the language of prejudice and not to stoke stigma or hatred towards people on the grounds of their religion, their culture, their origin, their identity or their belief". The Baptist, Methodist and United Reformed churches' Joint Public Issues Team hopes for "A politics characterised by listening, kindness and truthfulness"; the United Methodist Church condemns "the dissemination of stereotypes and untruths about religious minorities"; and the National Association of Evangelicals holds that "differing opinions can be handled without demonizing, misrepresenting or shaming". See also `SPEECH-1`, `SPEECH-2` and `TECH-2`. ([Exodus 23:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO23.htm#V1), [Ephesians 4:25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V25), [Fratelli tutti, 15](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20201003_enciclica-fratelli-tutti.html), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 415](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Church of England, General Election 2019: A message from the Archbishops of Canterbury and York](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/time-leave-our-echo-chambers-and-listen-others-say-archbishops-general-election-message), [Joint Public Issues Team, Politics and Elections](https://jpit.uk/issues/politics-and-elections), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Political Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-political-community), [National Association of Evangelicals, For the Health of the Nation: Introduction](https://www.nae.org/for-the-health-of-a-nation/introduction/))
- `7` `POL-4` **Treat your opponents with respect, and look for agreement where you can.** "If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men"; "Honour all men"; and Paul asks Christians "to speak evil of no one, not to be contentious, to be gentle, showing all humility towards all men". The Second Vatican Council asks Christians in politics to "recognize the legitimacy of different opinions with regard to temporal solutions, and respect citizens, who, even as a group, defend their points of view by honest methods", and Pope Francis warns that "It becomes easier to discredit and insult opponents from the outset than to open a respectful dialogue aimed at achieving agreement on a deeper level." The Russian Orthodox Church holds that "In face of political differences, contradictions and struggle, the Church preaches peace and co-operation among people holding various political views". The Archbishops of Canterbury and York write that "People who hold different political views are not our enemies. Two people can look at the same facts and in good faith interpret them very differently", and the National Association of Evangelicals that "We should not echo the rage and disrespect that typifies much of today's political debates." See also `CIV-4`. ([Romans 12:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ROM12.htm#V18), [1 Peter 2:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE02.htm#V17), [Titus 3:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TIT03.htm#V1), [Gaudium et spes, 75](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html), [Fratelli tutti, 201](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20201003_enciclica-fratelli-tutti.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/), [Church of England, General Election 2019: A message from the Archbishops of Canterbury and York](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/time-leave-our-echo-chambers-and-listen-others-say-archbishops-general-election-message), [National Association of Evangelicals, For the Health of the Nation: Introduction](https://www.nae.org/for-the-health-of-a-nation/introduction/))

### Faith, conscience and the churches

- `8` `POL-5` **Let your faith and a well-formed conscience guide your political decisions, and don't set them aside for the sake of your party or your career.** Daniel, a young man at the king's court, "purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself", and Paul strove "always having a conscience void of offence towards God and men". The Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church teaches that "Living and acting in conformity with one's own conscience on questions of politics is not slavish acceptance of positions alien to politics or some kind of confessionalism", but the way Christians help society "become more just and more consistent with the dignity of the human person"; the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith asks lay Catholics to fulfil their civic duties "guided by a Christian conscience", and holds up Thomas More, who witnessed to "the inalienable dignity of the human conscience". The Russian Orthodox Church calls Orthodox lay people in politics "to base their work on the norms of the gospel's morality", and the National Association of Evangelicals teaches that "When we as Christians engage in political activity, we must maintain our integrity and keep our biblical values intact." How far a Christian in office may accept compromise is an open question below. See also `DISCERN-3` and `CIV-3`. ([Daniel 1:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DAN01.htm#V8), [Acts 24:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT24.htm#V16), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 566](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, Doctrinal Note on the participation of Catholics in political life, 1](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20021124_politica_en.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.3](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/), [National Association of Evangelicals, For the Health of the Nation: The Method for Christian Civic Engagement](https://www.nae.org/for-the-health-of-a-nation/the-method-for-christian-civic-engagement/))
- `8` `POL-6` **As a church, speak up for justice and for the poor, but don't become a party or tie the gospel to one side.** God sent Jeremiah to "the house of the king of Judah" to say "Execute justice and righteousness"; Proverbs says "Open your mouth for the mute"; and Jesus told Pilate, "My Kingdom is not of this world." The Second Vatican Council teaches that "The Church, by reason of her role and competence, is not identified in any way with the political community nor bound to any political system", yet should be free "to pass moral judgment in those matters which regard public order when the fundamental rights of a person or the salvation of souls require it"; the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith adds that "It is not the Church's task to set forth specific political solutions – and even less to propose a single solution as the acceptable one". The Russian Orthodox Church holds that its not taking part "in political struggle, in the work of political parties and in election processes does not mean her refusal to express publicly her stand on socially significant issues". The Barmen Declaration teaches that the church "calls to mind the kingdom of God, God's commandment and righteousness, and thereby the responsibility both of rulers and of the ruled"; the United Methodist Church says, "While we do not believe churches should affiliate with particular political parties, we do encourage churches to speak out boldly on social issues from a Gospel perspective"; the National Association of Evangelicals that "The Church's collective voice should be loving, reasoned, truthful and nonpartisan"; and the Church of England's guidelines for its clergy that "It is appropriate for the clergy to play a positive part in civic society and politics, promoting the kingdom values of justice, integrity and peace in public life, calling attention to the needs of the poor". See also `CIV-6` and `JUST-5`. ([Jeremiah 22:1–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JER22.htm#V1), [Proverbs 31:8–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO31.htm#V8), [John 18:36](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN18.htm#V36), [Gaudium et spes, 76](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651207_gaudium-et-spes_en.html), [Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, Doctrinal Note on the participation of Catholics in political life, 3](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20021124_politica_en.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/), [The Barmen Declaration, 5](https://www.ekd.de/en/The-Barmen-Declaration-303.htm), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Political Community, A](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-political-community), [National Association of Evangelicals, For the Health of the Nation: The Method for Christian Civic Engagement](https://www.nae.org/for-the-health-of-a-nation/the-method-for-christian-civic-engagement/), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 9.5](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines))

## How to apply

- `7` [Elections](https://jpit.uk/elections) (Joint Public Issues Team of the Baptist Union of Great Britain, the Methodist Church and the United Reformed Church): guidance for churches on holding election hustings, which "are run by – but not for – the churches"; tips for avoiding "fake news" and for having "constructive and genuine political conversations with people who might have different views to you"; and the reminder that "As charities, churches are always covered by charity law which requires impartiality towards political parties". Supports `POL-3`, `POL-4`, `POL-6` and `CIV-6`.
- `6` [Politics and Elections](https://jpit.uk/issues/politics-and-elections) (Joint Public Issues Team): its Constituency Action Network, which helps churches build "a meaningful relationship" with their member of parliament, "based on listening and sharing". Supports `CIV-1`, `CIV-6` and `POL-4`.
- `6` [A prayer for the 2024 General Election](https://www.churchofengland.org/prayer-and-reflection/topical-prayers/prayer-2024-general-election) (Church of England): a short prayer that asks God to "bless with your spirit of humility and charity all who will be elected to public office", which can be adapted for any election. Supports `CIV-1` and `POL-2`.
- `6` [Christians in Parliament](https://www.christiansinparliament.org.uk/): an All-Party Parliamentary Group in the UK Parliament which supports members and staff "through: Bible study and prayer groups, one-to-one contact and chapel services". Similar groups exist in other parliaments. Supports `POL-5`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side. Wider questions about public life, such as how church and state should relate, when resistance to authority is justified, and whether Christians may hold public office, serve as soldiers and swear oaths, are in [Public life and citizenship](../../general/public-life.md#open-questions).

- `8` [Doctrinal Note on the participation of Catholics in political life, 4](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20021124_politica_en.html) (Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, 2002): **how far may a Christian in office accept compromises, or vote for laws that fall short of Christian moral teaching?** The Congregation holds that lawmakers have a "grave and clear obligation to oppose" any law that attacks human life, so that "it is impossible to promote such laws or to vote for them"; but, quoting John Paul II, that where such a law cannot be overturned, "an elected official, whose absolute personal opposition to procured abortion was well known, could licitly support proposals aimed at limiting the harm done by such a law and at lessening its negative consequences". The Russian Orthodox Church recognises that "most decisions adopted and political actions taken tend to benefit only a part of society", that "Many such decisions and actions are stained with sin or connivance with sin", and that "Precisely for this reason the Orthodox politician or statesman is required to be very sensitive spiritually and morally" ([Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.3](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/)). The National Association of Evangelicals holds that "While we may sometimes settle for partial solutions, we should not compromise core principles" ([National Association of Evangelicals, For the Health of the Nation: The Method for Christian Civic Engagement](https://www.nae.org/for-the-health-of-a-nation/the-method-for-christian-civic-engagement/)).
- `8` [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 573](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html) (Pontifical Council for Justice and Peace, 2004): **should Christians form Christian parties, and how closely should they bind themselves to any party?** The Compendium teaches that "Christians cannot find one party that fully corresponds to the ethical demands arising from faith and from membership in the Church", so that "Their adherence to a political alliance will never be ideological but always critical". The Russian Orthodox Church perceives "Christian (Orthodox) political organisations and Christian (Orthodox) units in larger political associations" as "positive", while its Bishops' Council of 1997 resolved that such organisations "cannot enjoy the blessing of the church authorities and speak for the Church" ([Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.4](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/)). The National Association of Evangelicals holds that "Individual evangelicals rightly engage in supporting legislation, candidates and political parties", but that "biblical faith is vastly larger and richer than every limited, inevitably imperfect political agenda" ([National Association of Evangelicals, For the Health of the Nation: The Method for Christian Civic Engagement](https://www.nae.org/for-the-health-of-a-nation/the-method-for-christian-civic-engagement/)).
- `7` [Code of Canon Law, Book II, cann. 273–289: The Obligations and Rights of Clerics](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib2-cann208-329_en.html) (Catholic Church, 1983): **may the clergy stand for public office or join political parties?** Catholic canon law holds that "Clerics are forbidden to assume public offices which entail a participation in the exercise of civil power" (285 §3), and that they "are not to have an active part in political parties and in governing labor unions unless, in the judgment of competent ecclesiastical authority, the protection of the rights of the Church or the promotion of the common good requires it" (287 §2). In the Russian Orthodox Church, "The clergy are not allowed to be nominated for elections to any body of representative power at any level", though "nothing should prevent bishops, clergy and laity from participation in the expression of the popular will by voting along with other citizens" ([Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, V.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/v/)). The Church of England's guidelines say "It is appropriate for the clergy to play a positive part in civic society and politics", and bar them only from membership or active support of parties or organisations whose aims are "incompatible with the teaching of the Church of England, as defined by the House of Bishops, in relation to the equality of persons or groups of different races" ([Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 9.5–9.6](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines)).

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Fratelli tutti, chapter five: A better kind of politics](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/encyclicals/documents/papa-francesco_20201003_enciclica-fratelli-tutti.html) (Pope Francis, 2020): on politics as "a lofty vocation and one of the highest forms of charity, inasmuch as it seeks the common good" (180), on forms of populism and liberalism, and on "political love".

- `6` [Responding to Christian Nationalism](https://jpit.uk/responding-to-christian-nationalism) (Joint Public Issues Team): a collection of articles and resources on "Christian Nationalism", "a worldview which brings together religious and national identities, and promotes the political idea of 'a Christian country'", chosen "to encourage careful listening, theological reflection, and gracious engagement with this issue".
