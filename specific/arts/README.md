# The arts and culture

Scope: artists, musicians, writers, actors, designers and architects; church musicians, choirs and organists, and those who commission or look after the art and buildings of a church; and everyone who makes or enjoys art and culture. The general rules come first: use the gifts God has given you to serve others (`DISC-4`), work honestly and well, as serving God (`WORK-1`), and see your work as a calling (`WORK-5`); pay workers fairly (`WORK-2`) and don't steal (`MONEY-1`); treat every person with equal dignity (`JUST-2`); tell the truth (`SPEECH-1`); thank and praise God for creation (`CREAT-5`); and worship God with your whole life (`WOR-6`). For art shared online, check before you share (`TECH-2`) and ask before you share someone's picture (`TECH-3`), as set out in [Online life and technology](../online/README.md). Choirs and music groups with children follow the rules on safer recruitment and open working (`SAFE-4`, `SAFE-5`) in [Safeguarding children and vulnerable adults](../safeguarding/README.md). This page adds what the churches teach together about making art, about art and music in worship, and about the church's inheritance of art. This is moral guidance, not legal advice: copyright law, and the law on changing church buildings, differ from country to country.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `8` [Letter to Artists](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/letters/1999/documents/hf_jp-ii_let_23041999_artists.html) (John Paul II, 1999): the Catholic Church's letter to "all who are passionately dedicated to the search for new 'epiphanies' of beauty": the artist as an image of God the Creator, the artistic vocation "in the service of beauty", "an ethic, even a 'spirituality' of artistic service", and why "the Church needs art".
- `8` [Sacrosanctum concilium](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1963): chapter VI on sacred music (112–121) and chapter VII on sacred art and sacred furnishings (122–130), the Catholic Church's teaching on music, art and buildings in worship.
- `8` [Second Council of Nicaea](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3819.htm) (787, on New Advent): the seventh ecumenical council of the Orthodox and Catholic churches, which defined that "the venerable and holy images" of Christ, the Mother of God, the angels and the saints should be set forth in churches and honoured, because "the honour which is paid to the image passes on to that which the image represents".
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2500–2503](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8N.HTM): Catholic teaching on truth, beauty and sacred art: art "arising from talent given by the Creator and from man's own effort", which is "not an absolute end in itself".
- `7` [Bases of the Social Concept, XIV: Secular science, culture and education](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xiv/) (Russian Orthodox Church, 2000): section XIV.2 on the religious roots of culture, on "the Orthodox icon-painter, poet, philosopher, musician, architect, artist and writer", and on when the Church blesses a creative work and when it opposes it.
- `7` [Icons](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-church-building/icons) (The Orthodox Faith, Orthodox Church in America): the Orthodox teaching that icons "are not only permissible, but are spiritually necessary", and the arrangement of icons in an Orthodox church.
- `7` [Principles for Worship](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf) (Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, 2002, PDF): the parts on music ("Music and the Christian Assembly") and on worship space ("Worship Space and the Christian Assembly"), with principles on the assembly's song, church musicians, the arts in worship and the beauty of church buildings.
- `7` [Institutes of the Christian Religion, Book I, Chapter 11](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xii.html) (John Calvin, on CCEL): the Reformed teaching on images: "sculpture and painting are gifts of God", to be "used purely and lawfully", but it is unlawful "to give a visible shape to God".
- `7` [Commissioning New Art for Churches: A Guide for Parishes and Artists](https://www.artandchristianity.org/s/commissioningnewart-2.pdf) (Church of England, Archbishops' Council, Cathedral and Church Buildings Division, 2017, PDF, published with Art and Christianity): how a parish commissions a work of art, from the brief and the choice of artist to fees, contracts and permission.

## Key rules

### Making art

- `8` **Use your artistic gifts to serve God and your neighbour, and develop them with care.** God filled Bezalel "with the Spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all kinds of workmanship", and put it in his heart "that he may teach". John Paul II writes that artists feel "the obligation not to waste this talent but to develop it, in order to put it at the service of their neighbour and of humanity as a whole", and that "they must labour without allowing themselves to be driven by the search for empty glory or the craving for cheap popularity"; the Russian Orthodox Church teaches that "the Orthodox icon-painter, poet, philosopher, musician, architect, artist and writer — all use the means of art to express the experience of spiritual renewal they have found in themselves and wish to offer to others". Calvin calls sculpture and painting "gifts which the Lord has bestowed upon us, for his glory and our good"; and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America quotes Luther, who wanted to "see all the arts, especially music, used in the service of [God] who gave and made them", and calls on those who make music in the church "to offer their very best to God and the assembly". This applies `DISC-4` and `WORK-5` to art. ([Exodus 31:1–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO31.htm#V1), [Exodus 35:30–35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO35.htm#V30), [Letter to Artists, 3–4](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/letters/1999/documents/hf_jp-ii_let_23041999_artists.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XIV.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xiv/), [Calvin, Institutes, I.11.12](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xii.html), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Principles for Worship, M-1 and M-13](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf))
- `8` **Seek what is true and good in what you make, and never make art that degrades people.** Paul writes: "whatever things are true, whatever things are honourable, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report… think about these things." The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "To the extent that it is inspired by truth and love of beings, art bears a certain likeness to God's activity", that art "is not an absolute end in itself", and that pornography "does grave injury to the dignity of its participants (actors, vendors, the public), since each one becomes an object of base pleasure and illicit profit for others". The Russian Orthodox Church gives its blessing to a creative work that "contributes to the moral and spiritual transformation of the personality", and opposes culture that becomes "anti-religious and anti-humane"; and the United Methodist Church asks media to "protect the dignity and worth of all people, including society's most vulnerable", and "strongly" opposes pornography. This applies `JUST-2` to art. ([Philippians 4:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V8), [Genesis 1:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GEN01.htm#V27), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2501](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8N.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2354](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P85.HTM), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XIV.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xiv/), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Social Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-social-community))
- `7` **Give other people's creative work its due: credit it, get permission to use it, and pay artists and church musicians fairly.** "The labourer is worthy of his wages", and Jeremiah condemns the one "who uses his neighbour's service without wages". The Liturgy Office of the Catholic Bishops' Conference of England and Wales reminds parishes that "Most of the texts used in liturgy are copyright; it is against the law to copy them without permission", that many "are the work of individual authors and composers", and that whatever text or music is used "should be acknowledged". The Methodist Church in Britain explains that "Copyright protects the creative work of songwriters, composers, authors and publishers", so that when a church sings a hymn or displays its words, "you are using someone's creative work". The Evangelical Lutheran Church in America asks congregations to "provide fair compensation and just treatment for church musicians"; and the Church of England's guide to commissioning art tells parishes to "Agree an adequate fee" for an artist's initial designs, and to set out contracts "formally… in writing". This applies `WORK-2` and `MONEY-1` to creative work. ([Luke 10:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK10.htm#V7), [Jeremiah 22:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JER22.htm#V13), [Liturgy Office, Resources for Copyright](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Resources/Copyright/index.html), [Methodist Church in Britain, Copyright Guide for Streamed Worship](https://www.methodist.org.uk/for-churches/digital-church/online-worship-toolkit/copyright-guide-for-streamed-worship/), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Principles for Worship, M-10F](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf), [Church of England, Commissioning New Art for Churches](https://www.artandchristianity.org/s/commissioningnewart-2.pdf))

### Art and music in worship

- `8` **Let the art and music of worship serve the prayer of the whole congregation, so that everyone can take part.** Paul will "sing with the spirit, and… with the understanding also", and asks that when the church comes together "all things be done to build each other up". The Second Vatican Council asks pastors to ensure that "the whole body of the faithful may be able to contribute that active participation which is rightly theirs" when the liturgy is sung, asks composers to provide "for the active participation of the entire assembly of the faithful", and prefers "noble beauty rather than mere sumptuous display". The Evangelical Lutheran Church in America teaches that "The assembly is the primary musical ensemble, and its song is the core of all music in worship"; and Calvin taught that in public worship "we glorify together as it were with one voice and one mouth", and warned "lest our ears be more intent on the music than our minds on the spiritual meaning of the words". ([1 Corinthians 14:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO14.htm#V15), [1 Corinthians 14:26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO14.htm#V26), [Colossians 3:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V16), [Sacrosanctum concilium, 114, 121 and 124](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Principles for Worship, M-3](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf), [Calvin, Institutes, III.20.31–32](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.v.xxi.html))
- `7` **Welcome the art and music of every culture into the church's worship.** John sees "a great multitude… out of every nation and of all tribes, peoples, and languages" praising God before the throne. The Second Vatican Council teaches that the Church "has not adopted any particular style of art as her very own", and that where peoples have "their own musical traditions", "a suitable place is to be given to it" in worship; the Russian Orthodox Church teaches that "No culture can be regarded as the only one acceptable for the expression of the Christian spiritual message"; and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America holds that "Music of many cultures, revealing the wonder of creation in its great variety, is available for use in worship". ([Revelation 7:9–10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/REV07.htm#V9), [Psalm 96:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA096.htm#V1), [Sacrosanctum concilium, 119 and 123](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XIV.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xiv/), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Principles for Worship, M-15](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf))
- `7` **Look after the church's inheritance of art, music and buildings, and add to it with care.** "LORD, I love the habitation of your house, the place where your glory dwells", sings the psalmist, and Israel brought its gifts freely for the making of the tabernacle. The Second Vatican Council calls the Church's art "a treasury of art which must be very carefully preserved", asks that "works of value are not disposed of or dispersed", and gives "free scope" to "The art of our own days". The Church of England's guide to commissioning art holds that "new art should not be at the expense of existing art of quality", and aims for "the legacy of the 21st century" to be "as eloquent and enduring as the best of the past"; and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America teaches that "The creation of a beautiful place need not be determined by how much it costs". ([Psalm 26:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA026.htm#V8), [Exodus 35:20–22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO35.htm#V20), [Sacrosanctum concilium, 123 and 126](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html), [Church of England, Commissioning New Art for Churches](https://www.artandchristianity.org/s/commissioningnewart-2.pdf), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Principles for Worship, S-6](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf))

## How to apply

- `7` [Commissioning New Art for Churches: A Guide for Parishes and Artists](https://www.artandchristianity.org/s/commissioningnewart-2.pdf) (Church of England, 2017, PDF): a step-by-step guide to setting up a commissioning group, writing a brief "with an adequate budget", choosing an artist, agreeing fees and contracts, and getting permission. Supports `ARTS-3` and `ARTS-6`.
- `7` [Resources for Copyright](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Resources/Copyright/index.html) (Liturgy Office, Catholic Bishops' Conference of England and Wales): who holds the copyright of liturgical texts, Scripture translations and hymns, and the music copyright licence schemes that let churches reproduce words and music "for an annual fee". Supports `ARTS-3`.
- `7` [Copyright Guide for Streamed Worship](https://www.methodist.org.uk/for-churches/digital-church/online-worship-toolkit/copyright-guide-for-streamed-worship/) (Methodist Church in Britain): which licences a church needs to sing, display, record and stream music in worship, and why "what is allowed in person is not automatically allowed online". Supports `ARTS-3` and `TECH-2`.
- `6` [Wesleys Take the Web: Directions for Singing](https://www.umc.org/content/wesleys-take-the-web-directions-for-singing) (United Methodist Church): a short video on John Wesley's directions for congregational singing, with tips like "Sing with good courage. Sing modestly. Stay in time", which the United Methodist Hymnal prints in full. Supports `ARTS-4`.
- `6` [The Royal School of Church Music](https://www.rscm.org.uk/) (RSCM): a charity whose "mission to resource, nurture and sustain church music" includes courses, choir training and music for churches and schools. Supports `ARTS-1` and `ARTS-4`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side. How the churches read the commandment against images is set out in [How the churches read Exodus](../../bible/old-testament/exodus.md#how-the-churches-read-it). Whether churches should sing only the psalms is an [open question of the Psalms](../../bible/old-testament/psalms.md#open-questions), and whether David's ordering of temple music, with its instruments, is a pattern for the church's music is an [open question of 1 Chronicles](../../bible/old-testament/1-chronicles.md#open-questions).

- `8` [Second Council of Nicaea](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3819.htm) (787): **should churches have images, and may they be venerated?** The council defined that holy images "should be set forth in the holy churches of God" and receive "due salutation and honourable reverence", though not "that true worship of faith (λατρείαν) which pertains alone to the divine nature". The **Orthodox** Church holds that icons "are not only permissible, but are spiritually necessary because 'the Word became flesh and dwelt among us'" ([Orthodox Church in America, Icons](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-church-building/icons)), and the **Catholic** Church that "The practice of placing sacred images in churches so that they may be venerated by the faithful is to be maintained", though "their number should be moderate" ([Sacrosanctum concilium, 125](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html)). The **Reformed** Heidelberg Catechism teaches that "God can not and may not be visibly portrayed in any way", and that images should not be permitted in churches "in place of books for the unlearned", because God wants his people "instructed by the living preaching of his Word" ([Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 96–98](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism)); Calvin doubted that it is "expedient that churches should contain representations of any kind", because for five hundred years "Christian churches were completely free from visible representations" ([Calvin, Institutes, I.11.13](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xii.html)). **Luther** "was not opposed to images" ([The Theology of the Icon, 10.3: The Reformation and iconoclasm](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/TheologyoftheIcon#section10.3)), and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America teaches that "Art and architecture proclaim the gospel" ([Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Principles for Worship, S-5](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf)).
- `7` [Sacrosanctum concilium, 120](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19631204_sacrosanctum-concilium_en.html): **which instruments belong in worship?** In the Latin Church, the Second Vatican Council teaches that "the pipe organ is to be held in high esteem", and that other instruments may be admitted with the consent of the bishops "only on condition that the instruments are suitable, or can be made suitable, for sacred use". The Evangelical Lutheran Church in America teaches that "the primary musical instrument is the human voice", and that "Choices concerning the use of technology and instrumentation in worship are informed by the priority that is placed on congregational song" ([Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Principles for Worship, M-2 and M-12](https://elcamediaresources.blob.core.windows.net/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Principles_for_Worship.pdf)). Calvin held that the instruments of the temple belonged to "the puerile instruction of the law", as set out in the open question of 1 Chronicles linked above.
- `6` [Pope Francis, In-flight press conference from Sri Lanka to the Philippines](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/speeches/2015/january/documents/papa-francesco_20150115_srilanka-filippine-incontro-giornalisti.html) (15 January 2015): **how far may art and satire go in mocking what others hold sacred?** Asked how far freedom of expression can go, Pope Francis said that "freedom of religion and freedom of speech are both fundamental human rights", that "It is true one cannot respond violently", but that "We cannot provoke others, we cannot insult their faith, we cannot mock their faith". The United Methodist Church "support[s] freedom of public expression, which encompasses freedom of the press and the right of all societal members to share their perspectives and opinions", while asking media to "operate with civility and respect" ([United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Social Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-social-community)). The Russian Orthodox Church reserves the right to give "a moral assessment to cultural developments", "Without insisting that the church evaluation system should be the only one accepted in secular society and state" ([Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, XIV.2](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/xiv/)).

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [The Theology of the Icon](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/TheologyoftheIcon) (Aidan Hart, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): the theology of icons from the Second Commandment to the Second Council of Nicaea, the Reformation and today.
- `7` [Music in the Western Theological Tradition](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/MusicintheWesternTheologicalTradition) (Jeremy Begbie, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, 2024): how Christian theology has shaped the making and hearing of music, from Augustine and Boethius to Luther, Calvin and today.
- `7` [Music and Orthodox Theology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/MusicandOrthodoxTheology) (Ivan Moody, St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology, 2024): music in Scripture and the church fathers, Orthodox chant and hymnography, and the theology of chant and of the icon.
