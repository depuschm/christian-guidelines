# Work and business

Scope: employers, managers, business owners, investors, employees, the self-employed and trade union members, and the decisions they make at work and in business. The general rules come first, in [Work and rest](../../general/work.md): work honestly and well (`WORK-1`), pay workers fairly and on time, including a living wage (`WORK-2`), respect the dignity of every worker (`WORK-3`), give those who work for you their rest (`WORK-4`), see work as a calling (`WORK-5`) and provide for your family (`WORK-6`). So do the rules on honesty and money: don't steal or cheat (`MONEY-1`), don't let money rule you (`MONEY-3`), act justly and refuse bribes (`JUST-3`), work for just structures (`JUST-5`), act against the destruction of the natural world (`CREAT-3`) and tell the truth (`SPEECH-1`). At work, Christians also keep to the rules on witness: never pressure the people you speak with (`MISS-2`), and respect people of other faiths and none (`MISS-4`). This page adds what the churches teach together about honest dealing, the people a business employs, and the purpose of business and investment. This is moral guidance, not legal or financial advice: employment, trade union and company law differs from country to country.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (1648, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Q. 141–142 on the eighth commandment, which requires "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce between man and man", and forbids "fraudulent dealing, false weights and measures", "bribery" and "engrossing commodities to enhance the price".
- `8` [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html) (Pontifical Council for Justice and Peace, 2004): chapter seven, on economic life (323–376), on private initiative and business (336–345), the place of profit (340) and the moral weight of choices about investment (358); and, in chapter six, on labour unions (305–309).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2426–2436](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM): Catholic teaching on economic activity and social justice: the duties of those who run businesses, access to work, just wages, and strikes. The paragraphs before them, on the seventh commandment, deal with fraud, contracts and slavery ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2407–2414](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM)).
- `7` [Social Principles: The Economic Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-economic-community) (United Methodist Church, Book of Discipline 2020/2024): the Methodist teaching on economic life, including human trafficking and slavery, "Graft, Bribery and Corruption", the dignity of work, and "Corporate Responsibility".
- `7` [Sufficient, Sustainable Livelihood for All](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Economic_LifeSS.pdf) (Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, social statement, 1999, PDF): the Lutheran statement on economic life, on vocation, work and human dignity, the rights of employees, and the church's own commitments as an employer.
- `7` [Bases of the Social Concept, VI: Labour and its fruits](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/vi/) (Russian Orthodox Church, 2000): an Orthodox account of work, the rhythm of work and rest, the moral motives of work, and the worker's right to "use the fruits of his labour".
- `7` [The Longer Catechism of the Orthodox, Catholic, Eastern Church, Part Three](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html) (St Philaret of Moscow, 1830, in Philip Schaff's *Creeds of Christendom*): Q. 593 on the sins against the eighth commandment, among them fraud in trade, contracts not "honestly" fulfilled, and bribery.
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, The Ten Commandments](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/) (1529): paragraphs 224–228 on the seventh commandment, which counts as stealing "all kinds of advantage in all sorts of trade to the disadvantage of our neighbor".

## Key rules

### Honest dealing

- `9` **Keep your business honest: true accounts, honest goods and advertising, contracts kept, and no bribes, given or taken.** "A false balance is an abomination to the LORD, but accurate weights are his delight", and "You shall take no bribe, for a bribe blinds those who have sight". The Catechism of the Catholic Church counts "business fraud", "corruption in which one influences the judgment of those who must make decisions according to law", "tax evasion" and "forgery of checks and invoices" as sins against the seventh commandment, and teaches that "Promises must be kept and contracts strictly observed to the extent that the commitments made in them are morally just". The Orthodox Longer Catechism condemns those who pass off "bad wares for good; or use false weights and measures" or "do not honestly fulfill contracts", and counts bribery among the sins against the eighth commandment; the Westminster Larger Catechism requires "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce between man and man", and forbids "fraudulent dealing" and "bribery"; and the United Methodist Church opposes "graft, bribery, and other forms of corruption", deploring the involvement of "business executives" in them. This applies `MONEY-1` and `JUST-3` to business. ([Proverbs 11:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO11.htm#V1), [Leviticus 19:35–36](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV19.htm#V35), [Exodus 23:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO23.htm#V8), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2409–2410](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 593](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.iv.html), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 141–142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Economic Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-economic-community))
- `8` **Charge fair prices and deal fairly with customers and suppliers, and never take advantage of their ignorance or need.** "If you sell anything to your neighbour, or buy from your neighbour, you shall not wrong one another", and Amos condemns traders who make "the ephah small, and the shekel large" and "buy the poor for silver". The Catechism of the Catholic Church counts as theft "forcing up prices by taking advantage of the ignorance or hardship of another", and "speculation in which one contrives to manipulate the price of goods artificially in order to gain an advantage to the detriment of others"; Luther's Large Catechism condemns the trader who "overcharges a person in a trade and wantonly drives a hard bargain, skins and distresses him"; and the Westminster Larger Catechism forbids "engrossing commodities to enhance the price". ([Leviticus 25:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LEV25.htm#V14), [Amos 8:4–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/AMO08.htm#V4), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2409](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Luther's Large Catechism, Seventh Commandment, 227](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/ten-commandments/), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html))

### The people who work for you

- `9` **Make sure no one works in slavery or forced labour for your business, or anywhere in the chains that supply it.** The Law punishes "Anyone who kidnaps someone and sells him", Paul names "slave-traders" among the lawless, and Revelation lists "people's bodies and souls" among the cargoes of Babylon's merchants. The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that the seventh commandment "forbids acts or enterprises that for any reason - selfish or ideological, commercial, or totalitarian - lead to the enslavement of human beings"; with other religious leaders in 2014, Pope Francis declared that "modern slavery — in the form of human trafficking, forced labour, prostitution or the trafficking of organs — is a crime 'against humanity'", and called "governments, businesses" and "all men and women of good will" to action against it. The United Methodist Church rejects "the practice of using enslaved, indentured, imprisoned or coerced laborers in extractive industries or in the production of commercial goods", and urges "socially responsible consumerism"; and the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "man-stealing" among the sins against the eighth commandment. ([Exodus 21:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO21.htm#V16), [1 Timothy 1:9–10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI01.htm#V9), [Revelation 18:11–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/REV18.htm#V11), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2414](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8B.HTM), [Pope Francis, Address at the signing of the Joint Declaration against Modern Slavery, 2014](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/speeches/2014/december/documents/papa-francesco_20141202_dichiarazione-schiavitu.html), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Economic Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-economic-community), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html))
- `8` **Keep the workplace safe, and give workers a voice, including the freedom to join a trade union and bargain together.** The Law told the builder of a house to put "a railing around your roof, so that you don't bring blood on your house if anyone falls from there", and Paul tells masters to give their servants "that which is just and equal" and to "give up threatening". John Paul II taught "the right to a working environment and to manufacturing processes which are not harmful to the workers' physical health or to their moral integrity", and "the right of association", whose associations "are called labour or trade unions"; the Compendium calls unions "an indispensable element of social life". The Evangelical Lutheran Church in America teaches that "People should be involved in decision making that directly affects their work", and that because employees "often are vulnerable and lack power", "they may need to organize in their quest for human dignity and justice"; and the United Methodist Church supports "the right to safe and secure working conditions, free from health and safety hazards", and workers' "rights to organize unions, to engage in collective bargaining". ([Deuteronomy 22:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU22.htm#V8), [Colossians 4:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V1), [Ephesians 6:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V9), [Laborem exercens, 19–20](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_jp-ii_enc_14091981_laborem-exercens.html), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 305](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2430](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Sufficient, Sustainable Livelihood for All](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Economic_LifeSS.pdf), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Economic Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-economic-community))
- `8` **Hire and promote people fairly, without unjust discrimination.** "You shall not oppress a hired servant who is poor and needy, whether he is one of your brothers or one of the foreigners who are in your land", and James warns: "don't hold the faith of our glorious Lord Jesus Christ with partiality". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "Access to employment and to professions must be open to all without unjust discrimination: men and women, healthy and disabled, natives and immigrants"; and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America commits itself as an employer to "hire without discriminating", and calls for "other employers to engage in similar practices". This applies `JUST-2` to work. ([Deuteronomy 24:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU24.htm#V14), [James 2:1–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS02.htm#V1), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2433](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Sufficient, Sustainable Livelihood for All](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Economic_LifeSS.pdf))

### Business, profit and investment

- `8` **Run your business for the common good, not for profit alone: weigh what it does to workers, customers, communities and creation.** Paul writes: "each of you not just looking to his own things, but each of you also to the things of others"; and the exiles were told to "Seek the peace of the city… for in its peace you will have peace". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "Those responsible for business enterprises are responsible to society for the economic and ecological effects of their operations", that "They have an obligation to consider the good of persons and not only the increase of profits", and that "Profits are necessary, however"; the Compendium warns that "a business may show a profit while not properly serving society". The Russian Orthodox Church teaches that labour "is not something pleasing to God if it is intended to serve the egoistic interests of individual or human communities"; and the United Methodist Church holds that "Rather than merely producing profits for their shareholders", businesses "have significant roles to play in promoting the social and economic health and welfare of society", and calls on them "to place people above profits". See also `CREAT-3`. ([Philippians 2:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP02.htm#V4), [Jeremiah 29:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JER29.htm#V7), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2432](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8D.HTM), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 338 and 340](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, VI.4](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/vi/), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Economic Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-economic-community))
- `7` **Invest responsibly: find out what your money is used for, and use your voice as an owner for good.** Paul asks the rich "that they do good, that they be rich in good works, that they be ready to distribute, willing to share", and Jesus says: "He who is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much." The Compendium teaches that "the decision to invest in one place rather than another, in one productive sector rather than another, is always a moral and cultural choice"; the Church of England's Church Commissioners follow an Ethical Investment Advisory Group that aims to enable the Church's investing bodies "to act as distinctly Christian – and Anglican – institutional investors", and they screen, engage with and vote in the companies they own; and the United Methodist Church commends "socially responsible investment as an important mechanism to hold corporations accountable for promoting the common good", and urges "individuals, families, congregations, and other church bodies to educate themselves about the use of their investment funds". See also `MONEY-6`. ([1 Timothy 6:17–19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI06.htm#V17), [Luke 16:10–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK16.htm#V10), [Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 358](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html), [Church Commissioners for England, Responsible and Ethical Investment Policy](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/ccfe_responsible_and_ethical_investment_policy_nov_2025.pdf), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Economic Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-economic-community))

## How to apply

- `7` [Modern Slavery and Human Trafficking](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/supporting-victims-and-survivors/modern-slavery-and-human) (Church of England, safeguarding guidance): what modern slavery is, the signs that someone may be exploited, and how to "refer any concerns about Modern Slavery and Human Trafficking through your safeguarding lead", with advice from the [Modern Slavery & Exploitation Helpline](https://www.modernslaveryhelpline.org/) in the United Kingdom. Supports `BIZ-3`.
- `7` [Responsible and Ethical Investment Policy](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/ccfe_responsible_and_ethical_investment_policy_nov_2025.pdf) (Church Commissioners for England, November 2025, PDF): one church body's investment policy, with screening, voting, and engagement with companies "rather than immediately divest if a potentially remediable issue exists or emerges". Supports `BIZ-7`.
- `7` [Sufficient, Sustainable Livelihood for All](https://cdn.elca.org/cdn/wp-content/uploads/Economic_LifeSS.pdf) (Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, 1999, PDF): a church's own commitments as an employer, which other employers can copy: to "compensate all people we call or employ at an amount sufficient for them to live in dignity", to provide "safe and healthy work conditions, sufficient periods of rest, vacation, and sabbatical, and family-friendly work schedules", and to "cultivate participatory workplaces". Supports `WORK-2`, `WORK-4`, `BIZ-4` and `BIZ-5`.
- `6` [What is the real Living Wage?](https://www.livingwage.org.uk/what-real-living-wage) (Living Wage Foundation): in the United Kingdom, a wage rate "independently calculated and rooted in the cost of living", which employers pay voluntarily and for which they can be accredited. Supports `WORK-2`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side. Whether Christians must keep Sunday, or a Sabbath, free from work, which bears on Sunday trading and working on Sundays, is set out in the [open questions of Worship and the sacraments](../../general/worship.md#open-questions). Whether a Christian may serve as a soldier, which bears on work and investment in the arms industry, is set out in the [open questions of Work and rest](../../general/work.md#open-questions).

- `7` [Responsible and Ethical Investment Policy](https://www.churchofengland.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/ccfe_responsible_and_ethical_investment_policy_nov_2025.pdf) (Church Commissioners for England, 2025, PDF): **which businesses may Christians work in or invest in, and when should they withdraw rather than engage?** The Church Commissioners exclude companies involved in "indiscriminate weaponry, firearms and defence, pornography, tobacco, gambling, high-interest rate lending, extraction of thermal coal, and exploration, production and refinement of oil and gas, subject to revenue thresholds", and otherwise prefer to engage with companies "rather than immediately divest". The Russian Orthodox Church sees "a whole industry intended to propagate vice and sin and satisfy such baneful passions and addictions as drinking, drug-addiction, fornication and adultery", and "testifies to the sin of being involved in such activities" ([Russian Orthodox Church, Bases of the Social Concept, VI.5](https://old.mospat.ru/en/documents/social-concepts/vi/)). The United Methodist Church urges the "redirection of their funds from industries that negatively impact people or the environment", with "boycott and divestment efforts" only "After all other avenues have been exhausted" ([United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Economic Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-economic-community)). The Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church calls every decision about where to invest "a moral and cultural choice" ([Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church, 358](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html)), and the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "unlawful callings" among the sins against the eighth commandment, without listing them ([Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 142](https://www.opc.org/lc.html)).
