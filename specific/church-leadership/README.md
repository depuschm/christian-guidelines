# Leading a church and pastoral care

"Shepherd the flock of God which is amongst you, exercising the oversight, not under compulsion, but voluntarily; not for dishonest gain, but willingly; not as lording it over those entrusted to you, but making yourselves examples to the flock" ([1 Peter 5:2–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE05.htm#V2)), and Jesus says: "whoever wants to become great amongst you shall be your servant" ([Mark 10:42–45](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK10.htm#V42)). This area is for ordained ministers (bishops, priests, pastors, ministers and deacons), and for elders, lay leaders, chaplains, pastoral visitors and everyone else who leads or cares for others in a church. It builds on [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md), whose rules apply here first: respect and pray for those who lead the church, and hold them to account (`CHURCH-4`), and protect children and vulnerable adults and report abuse (`CHURCH-6`), set out in detail in [Safeguarding](../safeguarding/README.md). Also relevant are the rules on keeping confidences unless keeping them would leave someone in danger (`SPEECH-6`), staying with people who suffer and caring for the sick and the dying (`HOPE-2`, `HOPE-3`), not overworking yourself or others (`WORK-4`) and honesty in all your dealings (`MONEY-1`). The churches differ on how the church is ordered and governed, on who may be ordained, on whether clergy must be celibate, on the seal of confession and on who may preside at the Eucharist; those are set out under "Open questions" without taking a side. This is moral and pastoral guidance, not legal or professional advice: the law on safeguarding, employment, charities and personal data differs from country to country. If someone is in danger, contact the emergency services first.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf) (World Council of Churches, Faith and Order Paper No. 111, 1982, PDF): the Ministry section is the churches' common text on ministry: the calling of the whole people of God and the gifts of every member (M 1–6), ordained ministry and its authority, which is "consecrated to service" (M 15–16), and the principle that "The ordained ministry should be exercised in a personal, collegial and communal way" (M 26). **Follow it as the baseline.**
- `8` [Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines) (Church of England): standards for deacons, priests and bishops on pastoral care and its boundaries, confidentiality, power and trust, money, well-being and accountability, written "to identify certain basic minimum standards of behaviour" and to encourage "the highest possible standards of conduct throughout a lifetime of ministry".
- `8` [Presbyterorum ordinis](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19651207_presbyterorum-ordinis_en.html) (Second Vatican Council, 1965): the Catholic decree on the ministry and life of priests: their relations with their bishop, with one another and with the lay faithful, the administration of the Church's goods, and their need for rest and a fair living.
- `8` [Pastores dabo vobis](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_jp-ii_exh_25031992_pastores-dabo-vobis.html) (John Paul II, 1992): the Catholic exhortation on the formation of priests, from the seminary to the "ongoing formation" that lasts through the whole of their ministry.
- `8` [Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf) (Orthodox Church in America, 2014, PDF): the Holy Synod's standards for clergy and lay workers, which hold that clergy, "by their position, have an inherent power over others" and set out the duties that follow from it.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1539–1553](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4T.HTM): Catholic teaching on holy orders, which says that the ordained priesthood "is ministerial" and that its authority "must therefore be measured against the model of Christ, who by love made himself the least and the servant of all".
- `7` [Holy Orders](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/holy-orders) (Thomas Hopko, *The Orthodox Faith*, Orthodox Church in America): Orthodox teaching on bishops, priests and deacons, through whom Christ, "the only priest, pastor and teacher of the Christian Church", acts; priests "head the local congregations of Christians" and "teach, preach, counsel and exercise the ministries of forgiveness and healing".
- `7` [Standards of Ethical Conduct](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf) (Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), 1998, PDF): the standards that ordained officers affirm, among them to "conduct my ministry so that nothing need be hidden from a governing body or colleagues in ministry".
- `7` [Augsburg Confession, Article XXVIII: Of Ecclesiastical Power](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-ecclesiastical-power/) (1530): the Lutheran teaching that the authority of bishops and pastors is to preach the gospel, forgive sins and administer the sacraments, and that they exercise it "without human force, simply by the Word"; [Article XIV](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-ecclesiastical-order/) adds that no one should "publicly teach in the Church or administer the Sacraments unless he be regularly called".
- `7` [Luther's Small Catechism, Table of Duties](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/table-of-duties/) (1529): the passages of Scripture Luther gives "For Bishops, Pastors, and Preachers", from 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1, and "What the Hearers Owe to Their Pastors".
- `7` [Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VI: The Church](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#vi) (Southern Baptist Convention): Baptist teaching on the local congregation, which "operates under the Lordship of Christ through democratic processes", and its "two scriptural offices", "pastor/elder/overseer and deacon"; its limiting of the office of pastor to men is one side of the open question on who may be ordained, below.

## Key rules

### Serving the people in your care

- `9` **Lead by serving, not by lording it over people.** Jesus tells his disciples that the rulers of the nations "lord it over them", "But it shall not be so amongst you", for "the Son of Man also came not to be served but to serve"; Peter asks elders to shepherd the flock "not as lording it over those entrusted to you, but making yourselves examples to the flock"; and Jesus washed his disciples' feet as "an example". The churches' common text on ministry teaches that "to be set apart means to be consecrated to service" and that "ordained ministers must not be autocrats or impersonal functionaries"; the Catechism of the Catholic Church that the authority of the ordained "must therefore be measured against the model of Christ, who by love made himself the least and the servant of all", and John Paul II asks for "an authority free from authoritarianism or demagoguery"; Orthodox teaching holds of bishops, priests and deacons that "All are servants of Christ and the Church"; the Augsburg Confession says bishops exercise their office "without human force, simply by the Word"; and the Church of England's guidelines for its clergy say that their power "needs to be used to sustain others and harness their strengths, and not to abuse, bully, manipulate or denigrate". ([Mark 10:42–45](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK10.htm#V42), [1 Peter 5:2–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE05.htm#V2), [John 13:14–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN13.htm#V14), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Ministry 15–16](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1551](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4T.HTM), [Pastores dabo vobis, 28](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_jp-ii_exh_25031992_pastores-dabo-vobis.html), [Orthodox Church in America, Holy Orders](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/holy-orders), [Augsburg Confession, XXVIII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-ecclesiastical-power/), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 12.2](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines))
- `9` **Never use a pastoral relationship for your own ends: no sexual, emotional or financial exploitation of anyone in your care.** Ezekiel speaks God's word against "the shepherds of Israel who feed themselves! Shouldn't the shepherds feed the sheep?", Peter asks elders to serve "not for dishonest gain", and an overseer must be "not greedy for money". The Orthodox Church in America holds that clergy, "by their position, have an inherent power over others", that "No member of the Clergy shall use or exploit his position in connection with his sexual or emotional needs or desires", and that "In a pastoral or counseling relationship, all sexual relations" between a clergyman and the person in his care "are deemed to be without consent"; Catholic canon law punishes a cleric who commits a sexual offence "by force, threats or abuse of his authority", and *Vos estis lux mundi* applies the Church's rules on reporting to sexual offences committed "through abuse of authority"; the Church of England's guidelines say that "The responsibility for maintaining appropriate boundaries always rests with the clergy", that "They should not seek sexual advantage, emotionally or physically, in the exercise of their ministry", and that they "should never seek any personal advantage or gain by virtue of their clerical position"; and ordained officers of the Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) promise to "Refrain from exploiting relationships within the community of faith for personal gain or gratification". Report any abuse as [Safeguarding](../safeguarding/README.md) sets out. ([Ezekiel 34:2–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EZK34.htm#V2), [1 Peter 5:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE05.htm#V2), [1 Timothy 3:2–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI03.htm#V2), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 2.11 and 5.03](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf), [Code of Canon Law, can. 1395 §3](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib6-cann1364-1399_en.html), [Vos estis lux mundi, art. 1](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 2.9, 10.8 and 12.4](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines), [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Standards of Ethical Conduct for Ordained Officers, II.5](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf))
- `8` **Keep confidences, and make clear from the start where confidentiality ends.** What people tell you in pastoral care stays with you unless they agree to share it, but if someone is at risk of serious harm you must act, and people should know that before they speak. "One who brings gossip betrays a confidence, but one who is of a trustworthy spirit is one who keeps a secret", and yet "Rescue those who are being led away to death!" The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that confidential information "must be kept, save in exceptional cases where keeping the secret is bound to cause very grave harm to the one who confided it, to the one who received it or to a third party"; the Church of England's guidelines say that a person seeking pastoral counsel "has the right to expect that the minister concerned will not pass on to a third party confidential information so obtained, without their consent or other lawful authority", with exceptions where there is a risk to children or vulnerable adults; the Methodist Church in Britain says that "The limits of confidentiality should be set by open and agreed policy rather than assumption" and "If you believe someone to be at risk disclose the information only to the relevant authorities"; and Presbyterian officers promise not to "divulge information obtained in confidence without express permission, unless an individual is a danger to self or others". What is said in sacramental confession falls under each church's own rules on the seal, an open question below. ([Proverbs 11:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO11.htm#V13), [Proverbs 24:11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO24.htm#V11), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2491](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8L.HTM), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 12.5](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines), [Methodist Church in Britain, Confidentiality guidelines](https://www.methodist.org.uk/for-churches/care-and-accountability/pastoral-care/confidentiality-guidelines/), [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Standards of Ethical Conduct for Ordained Officers, II.6](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf))

### Working with others

- `8` **Be accountable: welcome oversight, review and the discipline of your church.** Paul tells the elders of Ephesus: "Take heed, therefore, to yourselves and to all the flock", leaders "watch on behalf of your souls, as those who will give account", and 1 Timothy sets out how to weigh an accusation against an elder. The churches' common text on ministry teaches that "Only when they seek the response and acknowledgment of the community can their authority be protected from the distortions of isolation and domination", and that ordained ministry should be exercised "in a personal, collegial and communal way"; the Church of England requires its clergy to take part in "ministerial development review" and asks them to "encourage and support the development of a culture in which they and their colleagues can raise concerns openly and honestly"; the Orthodox Church in America requires clergy to "cooperate fully with all reviews and investigations" of misconduct; and Presbyterian officers promise to conduct their ministry "so that nothing need be hidden from a governing body or colleagues in ministry" and to "Accept the discipline of the church and the appropriate guidance of those to whom I am accountable for my ministry". ([Acts 20:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT20.htm#V28), [Hebrews 13:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB13.htm#V17), [1 Timothy 5:19–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI05.htm#V19), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Ministry 16 and 26](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 2.10 and 13.4](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 5.06](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf), [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Standards of Ethical Conduct for Ordained Officers, II and II.12](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf))
- `8` **Handle the church's money openly, and keep it apart from your own.** Paul arranged the collection so that no one could blame him "concerning this abundance which is administered by us", "Having regard for honourable things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men". John Paul II asks every priest to give "the witness of a total "honesty" in the administration of the goods of the community", which he holds as "something for which he will be held accountable by God and his brothers and sisters, especially the poor"; the Second Vatican Council asks that the Church's goods be "administered by priests with the help of capable laymen as far as possible", and Catholic canon law requires administrators "to render an account to the faithful" of what the faithful give; the Church of England's guidelines say that "There must be strict boundaries between church finance and personal moneys in order to avoid the possibility of suspicion or impropriety"; and Presbyterian officers promise to "Be a faithful steward of and fully account for funds and property entrusted to me". ([2 Corinthians 8:20–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO08.htm#V20), [1 Peter 5:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE05.htm#V2), [Pastores dabo vobis, 30](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_jp-ii_exh_25031992_pastores-dabo-vobis.html), [Presbyterorum ordinis, 17](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19651207_presbyterorum-ordinis_en.html), [Code of Canon Law, can. 1287](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib5-cann1254-1310_en.html#TITLE_II.), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 10.7](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines), [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Standards of Ethical Conduct for Ordained Officers, II.10](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf))
- `8` **Share the work: draw on the gifts of others, train new leaders, and refer people to those who can help them better.** Moses' father-in-law told him: "You are not able to perform it yourself alone", and advised him to appoint others, "and they shall share the load with you"; Christ gave shepherds and teachers "for the perfecting of the saints, to the work of serving", and Paul tells Timothy to "commit the same things to faithful men who will be able to teach others also". The churches' common text on ministry teaches that "All members are called to discover, with the help of the community, the gifts they have received and to use them"; the Second Vatican Council asks priests to "willingly listen to the laity" and "recognize their experience and competence in the different areas of human activity"; the Church of England's guidelines ask clergy to "recognize and affirm lay ministry that already exists and encourage new ministries", and to "discern and acknowledge their own limitations of time, competence and skill" and, "on occasion, to refer to specialist agencies"; and Presbyterian officers promise to "Recognize the limits of my own gifts and training, and refer persons and tasks to others as appropriate". ([Exodus 18:17–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO18.htm#V17), [Ephesians 4:11–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V11), [2 Timothy 2:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TI02.htm#V2), [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Ministry 5](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf), [Presbyterorum ordinis, 9](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19651207_presbyterorum-ordinis_en.html), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 2.3 and 6.3](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines), [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Standards of Ethical Conduct for Ordained Officers, II.7](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf))

### Caring for yourself

- `7` **Look after your own body and soul: pray, rest, and have someone to turn to.** Jesus told his tired disciples: "Come away into a deserted place, and rest awhile", and Paul told Timothy: "Pay attention to yourself and to your teaching". The Second Vatican Council asks priests to "gather together for recreation, remembering Christ's invitation to the weary apostles", and asks that their pay "permit priests each year to take a suitable and sufficient vacation"; John Paul II teaches that "it is the priest himself, the individual priest, who is the person primarily responsible in the Church for ongoing formation"; the Orthodox Church in America requires a clergyman at risk of misconduct to "immediately seek counsel and pastoral guidance from an individual trained and experienced in the field"; the Church of England's guidelines say that "A minister should have someone outside the work situation to whom to turn for help", that "Those who find themselves in difficulty with addictions of any kind should seek appropriate help", and that clergy should be encouraged to take "days off and their full holidays"; and Presbyterian officers promise to "Maintain a healthy balance among the responsibilities of my office of ministry, my commitments to family and other primary relationships, and my need for spiritual, physical, emotional, and intellectual renewal". ([Mark 6:31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK06.htm#V31), [1 Timothy 4:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI04.htm#V16), [Presbyterorum ordinis, 8 and 20](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19651207_presbyterorum-ordinis_en.html), [Pastores dabo vobis, 79](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_jp-ii_exh_25031992_pastores-dabo-vobis.html), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 5.05](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf), [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 10.9, 13.3 and 14.9](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines), [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Standards of Ethical Conduct for Ordained Officers, I.5](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf))

## How to apply

- `8` [Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 13.3–13.5 and 14.6](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines): regular ministerial development review, continuing ministerial education, peer groups for "mutual support and pastoral care", and "a spiritual director, soul friend or confessor to support their spiritual life". Supports `LEAD-4` and `LEAD-7`.
- `8` [Pastores dabo vobis, 70–81](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_exhortations/documents/hf_jp-ii_exh_25031992_pastores-dabo-vobis.html): the ongoing formation of priests through every stage of their ministry, which John Paul II calls "necessary to ensure that the priest can properly respond to this right of the People of God". Supports `LEAD-4` and `LEAD-7`.
- `7` [Methodist Church in Britain, Confidentiality guidelines](https://www.methodist.org.uk/for-churches/care-and-accountability/pastoral-care/confidentiality-guidelines/): a short checklist for "Anyone, lay or ordained, who is involved in pastoral work", from "Don't share information without express permission, even in open prayer or intercessions" to "Every pastoral worker should be supported by a supervisor", with the Methodist Conference's full guidelines to download. Supports `LEAD-3`, `LEAD-7` and `SPEECH-6`.
- `7` [Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), Standards of Ethical Conduct](https://pcusa.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/standards-of-ethical-conduct.pdf): short lists of standards for members, for employees and volunteers, and for ordained officers, who "commit myself to the following standards of ethical conduct"; churches of other traditions can use them as a checklist for their own leaders. Supports `LEAD-1` to `LEAD-7`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `9` [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Ministry 14 and commentary](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf) (World Council of Churches): **who may preside at the Eucharist?** The churches' common text notes that "In most churches this presidency is signified and represented by an ordained minister", and that the New Testament "says very little about the ordering of the eucharist". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "Only validly ordained priests can preside at the Eucharist" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1411](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P44.HTM)), and in Orthodox teaching priests "preside at the celebration of the liturgy" ([Orthodox Church in America, Holy Orders](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/holy-orders)). The Augsburg Confession teaches that no one should "administer the Sacraments unless he be regularly called" ([Augsburg Confession, XIV](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-ecclesiastical-order/)). In the Methodist Church in Britain "presidency at Holy Communion is normally exercised by presbyters, and any lay presidency requires specific authorisation by the Conference", a rule that rests on its Deed of Union: presbyters are ordained "For the sake of church order and not because of any priestly virtue inherent in the office" ([Methodist Church in Britain, Review of Criteria for Authorisations to Preside at the Lord's Supper, 2023](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/conf-23-pc-29-authorisations-to-preside-at-the-lords-supper.pdf)). The Anglican Diocese of Sydney's synod has resolved that lay and diaconal administration of Holy Communion "is consistent with the teaching of Scripture", although "Lay presidency has never been authorized in the Diocese of Sydney" ([Sydney Anglicans, Sydney resolute on deacons celebrating Lord's Supper, 2010](https://sydneyanglicans.net/news/deacons)).
- `9` [The Church: Towards a Common Vision, 52 and notes](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/The_Church_Towards_a_common_vision.pdf) (World Council of Churches): **how should the church be ordered and governed, and who oversees its leaders?** Episcopal, presbyteral and congregational church order, and the churches' positions, are set out in the open questions of [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1577–1580](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4X.HTM): **who may be ordained, and must clergy be celibate?** Whether women may be ordained is set out in the open questions of [1 Timothy](../../bible/new-testament/1-timothy.md#open-questions), and whether clergy must be celibate in those of [Marriage, family and singleness](../../general/family.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2490](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8L.HTM): **what may a priest reveal of what he hears in confession?** The Catechism teaches that "The secret of the sacrament of reconciliation is sacred, and cannot be violated under any pretext"; the churches' positions when abuse is disclosed in confession are set out in the open questions of [Safeguarding](../safeguarding/README.md#open-questions).

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Razvan Porumb, Orthodox Pastoral Theology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/OrthodoxPastoralTheology) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): how the Orthodox churches understand pastoral theology, "the education of the Church's ordinands" and the study of "the activities of the clergy", with examples of pastors from the twentieth century.
- `7` [Gregory the Great, Pastoral Rule, Book I](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/36011.htm) (New Advent): the classic Western guide for pastors, written about the "burdens of pastoral care", which holds that "the government of souls is the art of arts"; its introduction notes that the Emperor Maurice had it translated into Greek by Anastasius, patriarch of Antioch.
- `7` [John Chrysostom, On the Priesthood](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1922.htm) (New Advent): John Chrysostom's six books on the priesthood and the responsibilities of those who lead the church, in the translation of the *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*.
- `6` [Richard Baxter, The Reformed Pastor](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/baxter/pastor.html) (Christian Classics Ethereal Library): the seventeenth-century nonconformist pastor's book for other pastors, on "the pastoral responsibilities to God, to congregation, and to self".
