# Parents and children

"You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way" ([Deuteronomy 6:6–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU06.htm#V6)), and Jesus says: "Allow the little children to come to me! Don't forbid them" ([Mark 10:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK10.htm#V14)). This area is for parents, step-parents, foster and adoptive parents, grandparents and everyone else who brings up children, for grown children with ageing parents, and for churches that support families. It builds on [Marriage, family and singleness](../../general/family.md), whose rules apply here first: honour your parents and care for them when they are old (`FAM-1`), love your children, bring them up in the faith and don't provoke them (`FAM-3`), and never abuse or neglect anyone in your family (`FAM-6`). Also relevant are the rules on daily prayer (`PRAY-1`), reading Scripture (`SCRIP-1`) and protecting children in the church (`CHURCH-6`, and [Safeguarding](../safeguarding/README.md)). The churches differ on infant baptism, on when children may receive communion, on physical punishment and on how parents from different churches bring up their children; those are set out under "Open questions" without taking a side, and questions about marriage itself are in [Marriage, family and singleness](../../general/family.md#open-questions). This is moral and pastoral guidance, not legal, medical or psychological advice. If a child is in danger, contact the emergency services or your country's child protection service first.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Westminster Larger Catechism](https://www.opc.org/lc.html) (1648, on the site of the Orthodox Presbyterian Church): Q. 129 asks those responsible for others "to love, pray for, and bless" them, "to instruct, counsel, and admonish them", and to provide "all things necessary for soul and body"; Q. 130 lists the sins of those in authority, among them "correcting them unduly" and "provoking them to wrath".
- `8` [Amoris laetitia](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf) (Pope Francis, 2016, PDF): chapter 7, "Towards a better education of children" (paragraphs 259–290), on knowing where children are in their lives, moral formation, discipline and handing on the faith at home; chapter 5 on adoption and on grandparents and the elderly.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2214–2231](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM): Catholic teaching on the duties of parents, who "have the first responsibility for the education of their children", and of children towards their parents; it continues in [paragraphs 2232–2233](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7V.HTM) on respecting a child's vocation.
- `7` [Family](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sexuality-marriage-and-family/family) (Thomas Hopko, *The Orthodox Faith*, Orthodox Church in America): Orthodox teaching on the family as a "small church" in the home, on children's honour for their parents and on fathers and mothers, whose love "is expressed in loving discipline without hypocrisy".
- `7` [Luther's Large Catechism, Short Preface](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/) (1529): Luther's call for parents to teach the catechism at home, "the duty of every father of a family to question and examine his children and servants at least once a week".
- `7` [Baptist Faith and Message, XVIII: The Family](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xviii) (Southern Baptist Convention, 2000): children as "a blessing and heritage from the Lord", and parents' duty to teach them "spiritual and moral values".
- `7` [Social Principles: The Social Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-social-community) (United Methodist Church): "The Nurturing Community" on the family, which calls children "a gift from God to be welcomed and received", and on foster and adoptive parents.

## Key rules

### Handing on the faith

- `8` **Teach your children the faith at home, by your words and above all by your example.** The Law asks parents to talk of God's words "when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way", a psalm promises to tell "the generation to come the praises of the LORD", and Paul recalls the faith that "lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "Parents have a grave responsibility to give good example to their children" and that "Family catechesis precedes, accompanies, and enriches other forms of instruction in the faith"; Orthodox teaching holds that "The best teacher is one's own example"; Luther's Large Catechism makes it "the duty of every father of a family to question and examine his children and servants at least once a week"; and the Baptist Faith and Message asks parents "to teach their children spiritual and moral values and to lead them, through consistent lifestyle example and loving discipline". ([Deuteronomy 6:6–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU06.htm#V6), [Psalm 78:4–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA078.htm#V4), [2 Timothy 1:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TI01.htm#V5), [2 Timothy 3:14–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TI03.htm#V14), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2223 and 2226](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM), [Orthodox Church in America, Family](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sexuality-marriage-and-family/family), [Luther's Large Catechism, Short Preface, 4](https://bookofconcord.org/large-catechism/), [Baptist Faith and Message, XVIII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xviii))
- `8` **Pray with your children, and bring them into the worship and life of the church.** People brought little children to Jesus, and he said: "Allow the little children to come to me! Don't forbid them"; Jesus' own parents "went every year to Jerusalem at the feast of the Passover". The Catechism of the Catholic Church says that parents "have the mission of teaching their children to pray" and "should associate them from their tenderest years with the life of the Church", and Pope Francis writes that "moments of family prayer and acts of devotion can be more powerful for evangelization than any catechism class or sermon"; Luther's Small Catechism shows "How the head of the family should teach his household to pray morning and evening"; and in the Church of England's baptism service parents and godparents promise to "help them to take their place within the life and worship of Christ's Church". Whether infants should be baptised, and when children may receive communion, are open questions below. ([Mark 10:13–16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK10.htm#V13), [Luke 2:41–42](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK02.htm#V41), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2225–2226](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM), [Amoris laetitia, 288](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf), [Luther's Small Catechism, Daily Prayers](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/daily-prayers/), [Church of England, Common Worship: Holy Baptism](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/common-worship/baptism-and-confirmation/holy-baptism/holy-baptism-liturgy))

### Bringing up children

- `8` **Correct your children patiently and lovingly: never in anger, and never to humiliate or harm them.** "You fathers, don't provoke your children to wrath, but nurture them in the discipline and instruction of the Lord"; "Fathers, don't provoke your children, so that they won't be discouraged". Pope Francis writes that "A child who does something wrong must be corrected, but never treated as an enemy or an object on which to take out one's own frustrations", and the Catechism of the Catholic Church that "By knowing how to acknowledge their own failings to their children, parents will be better able to guide and correct them"; Orthodox teaching describes a father's love as "loving discipline without hypocrisy" and asks fathers to be "not violent but gentle"; the Westminster Larger Catechism counts "correcting them unduly" and "provoking them to wrath" among the sins of those in authority; and the Baptist Faith and Message speaks of "loving discipline". Whether physical punishment has any place is an open question below; abuse is never acceptable (`FAM-6`). ([Ephesians 6:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V4), [Colossians 3:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL03.htm#V21), [Amoris laetitia, 269](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2223](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM), [Orthodox Church in America, Family](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/spirituality/sexuality-marriage-and-family/family), [Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 130](https://www.opc.org/lc.html), [Baptist Faith and Message, XVIII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xviii))
- `7` **Respect your children as persons, and help them to grow in freedom.** Jesus says: "See that you don't despise one of these little ones". The Catechism of the Catholic Church teaches that "Parents must regard their children as children of God and respect them as human persons", that as their children grow up parents should "educate them in the right use of their reason and freedom", and, when a child's "unique vocation which comes from God asserts itself", that "Parents should respect this call and encourage their children to follow it"; Pope Francis warns that "Obsession, however, is not education", asks parents "lovingly to help them grow in freedom, maturity, overall discipline and real autonomy", and reminds them that "spiritual experience is not imposed but freely proposed"; and the United Methodist Social Principles urge "all family members to treat one another with love and respect" and encourage parents to raise "their children in ways that promote their physical, spiritual, emotional, and moral growth". ([Matthew 18:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V10), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2222 and 2228](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7U.HTM), [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2232](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7V.HTM), [Amoris laetitia, 261 and 288](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Social Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-social-community))

### Children in need of a family

- `7` **Open your home and your church to children who need a family, and support those who foster and adopt.** God is "A father of the fatherless" who "sets the lonely in families"; "Pure religion" includes visiting "the fatherless and widows in their affliction"; and Jesus says: "Whoever receives one such little child in my name receives me". Pope Francis calls adoption "a very generous way to become parents" and, quoting the 2015 Synod of Bishops, insists that "The best interests of the child should always underlie any decision in adoption and foster care"; the Southern Baptist Convention called on "each Southern Baptist family to pray for guidance as to whether God is calling them to adopt or foster a child or children"; and the United Methodist Social Principles "applaud the willingness of extended families and foster and adoptive parents to step in as primary nurturers". ([Psalm 68:5–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA068.htm#V5), [James 1:27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V27), [Mark 9:36–37](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MRK09.htm#V36), [Amoris laetitia, 179–180](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf), [Southern Baptist Convention, On Adoption and Orphan Care](https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/on-adoption-and-orphan-care/), [United Methodist Church, Social Principles: The Social Community](https://www.umc.org/en/content/social-principles-the-social-community))

## How to apply

- `8` [Amoris laetitia, 287–289](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf): handing on the faith at home, where "we learn to appreciate the meaning and beauty of the faith, to pray and to serve our neighbour"; faith education "has to adapt to each child", and "Children need symbols, actions and stories". Supports `PAR-1`, `PAR-2` and `PAR-4`.
- `8` [Amoris laetitia, 191–192](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf): grandparents and the elderly. "Very often it is grandparents who ensure that the most important values are passed down to their grandchildren", and Pope Francis hears in the psalmist's prayer, "Don't reject me in my old age" ([Psalm 71:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA071.htm#V9)), "the plea of the elderly, who fear being forgotten and rejected". Supports `FAM-1` and `PAR-1`.
- `8` [Amoris laetitia, 260–261](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/francesco/pdf/apost_exhortations/documents/papa-francesco_esortazione-ap_20160319_amoris-laetitia_en.pdf): keeping watch over "who is entering their rooms through television and electronic devices, and with whom they are spending their free time", since "neglect is never beneficial", without trying to control every movement. For church activities with children, see [Safeguarding](../safeguarding/README.md). Supports `PAR-4`, `FAM-6` and `CHURCH-6`.
- `7` [Church of England, Common Worship: Holy Baptism](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/common-worship/baptism-and-confirmation/holy-baptism/holy-baptism-liturgy): godparents promise with the parents to "pray for them, draw them by your example into the community of faith and walk with them in the way of Christ"; godparents and sponsors who keep that promise share the work of handing on the faith. Supports `PAR-1` and `PAR-2`.
- `7` [Luther's Small Catechism, Daily Prayers](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/daily-prayers/): a simple pattern of morning and evening prayer and grace at meals for the household. Supports `PAR-2`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1244](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3J.HTM): **when should children first receive communion?** The Catechism notes that "the Eastern Churches maintain a lively awareness of the unity of Christian initiation by giving Holy Communion to all the newly baptized and confirmed, even little children", while "The Latin Church... reserves admission to Holy Communion to those who have attained the age of reason". Orthodox teaching holds that "all who are members of the Church through the sacraments of baptism and chrismation, including small children and infants, may partake of Holy Communion" ([Orthodox Church in America, Communion](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-divine-liturgy/communion)). In the Church of England, regulations of 2006 allow "Children who have been baptised but who have not yet been confirmed" to be admitted to communion where the bishop has given permission ([Admission of Baptised Children to Holy Communion Regulations 2006, copy published by the Diocese of Leeds](https://leeds.anglican.org/content/pages/documents/admission-of-baptised-children-to-holy-communion-regulations.pdf)). The United Methodist Church "places no age restrictions on participation in the Lord's Supper" ([United Methodist Church, May children receive communion?](https://www.umc.org/content/ask-the-umc-may-children-receive-communion)), while the Westminster Larger Catechism teaches that the Lord's supper is to be given "only to such as are of years and ability to examine themselves" ([Westminster Larger Catechism, Q. 177](https://www.opc.org/lc.html)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1635–1636](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P53.HTM): **how should parents who belong to different churches bring up their children?** In the Latin Catholic Church, permission for a mixed marriage presupposes "the obligations assumed by the Catholic party concerning the baptism and education of the children in the Catholic Church", and the Catechism encourages "a common pastoral practice for mixed marriages" where the churches have agreed one. The guidelines of an Orthodox diocese in the United States require that "The parties must promise solemnly and in writing that any children born of the marriage will be reared and raised exclusively in the Orthodox Church" ([American Carpatho-Russian Orthodox Diocese, Spiritual/Sacramental Guidelines](https://www.acrod.org/metropolitan/sguidelines/)). The Lambeth Conference of 1978 welcomed "a growing Roman Catholic understanding that a decision as to the baptism and upbringing of any children should be made within the unity of the marriage, in which the Christian conscience of both partners must be respected", while holding that "the general principles underlying the Roman Catholic position are unacceptable to Anglicans" ([Lambeth Conference 1978, Resolution 34](https://iarccum.org/doc/1594)).
- `7` [Dagmar Heller, Baptism, 4.2: Infant baptism versus adult baptism](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/Baptism#section4.2) (St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology): **should parents have their babies baptised, or wait until their children can confess the faith for themselves?** The churches' positions are set out in the open questions of [Worship and the sacraments](../../general/worship.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Onyekachi G. Chukwuma, Reading selected biblical proverbs in the light of corporal punishment in child upbringing](https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/article/view/10895) (HTS Teologiese Studies/Theological Studies, 2025): **may parents use physical punishment?** The readings of the "rod" proverbs, and church statements against corporal punishment, are set out in the open questions of [Proverbs](../../bible/old-testament/proverbs.md#open-questions).
