# Safeguarding children and vulnerable adults

Jesus warned that it would be better to be thrown into the sea with a millstone around one's neck than to cause "one of these little ones who believe in me to stumble" ([Matthew 18:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT18.htm#V6)), and the psalm commands: "Defend the weak, the poor, and the fatherless" ([Psalm 82:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA082.htm#V3)). This area applies to everyone who serves in a church or a Christian organisation with children, young people or adults at risk, whether ordained or lay, paid or voluntary, and to every church as an institution, including how it responds to people who disclose abuse, recent or long past. It builds on the general rules `CHURCH-6` (protect children and vulnerable adults, and report abuse to the church and to the authorities), `CHURCH-4` (hold leaders to account), `FAM-6` (abuse in the family) and `SPEECH-6` (keep confidences, unless keeping them would leave someone in danger) in [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md), [Marriage, family and singleness](../../general/family.md) and [Honest and kind speech](../../general/speech.md). This is moral and pastoral guidance, not legal advice. Safeguarding law and statutory guidance differ from country to country and apply alongside every church's own policy: the Catholic Church's norms apply "without prejudice to the rights and obligations established in each place by state laws" ([Vos estis lux mundi, 2023, art. 20](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html)), and the Church of England notes that safeguarding work "is undertaken within a legislative framework supported by government guidance" ([Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Foundations](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/foundations)). If a child or adult is in immediate danger, contact the emergency services or the relevant statutory service at once ([Church of England, Responding to and reporting safeguarding concerns and allegations: Quick reference](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/reporting-and-managing-concerns-and-allegations/reporting/responding)).

## Adopted references

- `8` [Vos estis lux mundi](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html) (Pope Francis, apostolic letter motu proprio, definitive text 2023): the Catholic Church's universal law on reporting sexual abuse and its cover-up: who must report, the protection of those who report, care for those who have been harmed, and how reports about bishops and other leaders are investigated. **The baseline for Catholic communities.**
- `8` [Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People (Revised June 2026)](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf) (United States Conference of Catholic Bishops, PDF): the US Catholic bishops' commitments on outreach to victims and survivors, reporting to civil authorities, removing offenders from ministry, safe environment training and background checks.
- `8` [Promoting a safer church](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0) (Church of England, policy statement, 2026): the Church of England's safeguarding policy, with its foundations and six policy commitments, informed by a joint safeguarding statement with the Methodist Church. **The baseline for Church of England parishes.**
- `8` [Safeguarding Codes of Practice](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/codes-practice) (Church of England): the mandatory codes on safer recruitment and people management, reporting and managing concerns and allegations, and learning and development, with key guidance such as *Responding Well to Victims and Survivors of Abuse*.
- `8` [Safeguarding Policy, Procedures and Guidance](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf) (Methodist Church in Britain, 2023, PDF): the Methodist policy, with codes of safer working practice with children and adults, how to respond to and refer concerns, care for survivors, and safeguarding contracts for those who may pose a risk.
- `8` [Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf) (Orthodox Church in America, 2014, PDF): the Orthodox Church in America's policies on reporting and responding to sexual misconduct, screening and training, the supervision of church workers and children, and registered sex offenders in parishes.
- `7` [Policies and procedures](https://www.baptist.org.uk/Articles/558239/Policies_and_procedures.aspx) (Baptist Union of Great Britain): the Baptist Union's model safeguarding policy and procedures for local churches, guidance on making referrals to statutory authorities, and an audit tool.

## Key rules

### Responding to abuse

- `9` **Report every concern or disclosure of abuse at once to your church's safeguarding lead and to the statutory authorities, and never investigate it yourself.** Proverbs tells us to "Open your mouth for the mute", and Paul tells Christians to have no fellowship with "the unfruitful deeds of darkness, but rather even reprove them". The Church of England asks anyone who hears a concern to take it seriously "without investigating or asking leading questions", not to contact "the person about whom allegations have been made", and to report to the statutory authorities every concern that reaches the threshold, "irrespective of the status of the person". The Methodist Church in Britain asks for a referral to the statutory agencies "within one working day", and says the person accused must not be told "until after the allegations have been discussed and agreement reached with the statutory authorities". The Catholic Church obliges clergy and religious who learn of abuse "to report it promptly", and the US Catholic bishops' Charter requires every allegation of abuse of a minor to be reported "to law enforcement or the designated civil authorities", "regardless of when the alleged abuse occurred". The Orthodox Church in America will "report allegations in accordance with the civil laws" and cooperate "in any investigation by civil authorities". ([Proverbs 31:8–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO31.htm#V8), [Ephesians 5:11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH05.htm#V11), [Church of England, Quick reference](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/reporting-and-managing-concerns-and-allegations/reporting/responding), [Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Policy commitments, 3](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/policy-commitments), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 4.1 and 4.1.5](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [Vos estis lux mundi, 2023, art. 3](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html), [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 4](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 3.02](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf))
- `9` **Listen to anyone who discloses abuse, take them seriously, and put their welfare first.** James asks every believer to be "swift to hear, slow to speak", and the psalm commands "Rescue the weak and needy". The Church of England holds that "The welfare of the child, young person and vulnerable adult is paramount", and that those who have suffered abuse within the Church "will receive a compassionate response, be listened to and be taken seriously"; it tells those who receive a disclosure not to "Promise the child or adult that you can keep a secret", but to explain "that information will be shared if children or any adult is at potential risk". The Methodist Church in Britain asks listeners not to "pass judgement, minimise or express shock or disbelief", and "Do not promise confidentiality but explain what you will do with the information". The Catholic Church commits to treating those who say they have been harmed "with dignity and respect", so that they are "welcomed, listened to and supported", and forbids imposing "An obligation to keep silent" on them; the US Catholic bishops ask each bishop "to listen with humility and compassion". The Orthodox Church in America "will take all allegations of Sexual Misconduct seriously". ([James 1:19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JAS01.htm#V19), [Psalm 82:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA082.htm#V3), [Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Foundations](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/foundations), [Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Policy commitments, 4](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/policy-commitments), [Church of England, Quick reference](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/reporting-and-managing-concerns-and-allegations/reporting/responding), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 4.1.1](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [Vos estis lux mundi, 2023, art. 4–5](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html), [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 1](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 3.02](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf))
- `9` **Treat concerns about leaders like any others: report them whoever is involved, and never cover abuse up or quietly move someone on.** Paul tells Timothy to deal with an elder who sins "without prejudice, doing nothing by partiality", and Ezekiel condemns shepherds who "haven't bound up that which was broken" but "have ruled over them with force and with rigor". The Church of England and the Methodist Church in Britain both report concerns that reach the threshold "irrespective of the status of the person", and the Church of England's guidance on a healthy church culture asks that "senior clergy or staff are not reallocated different roles when safeguarding concerns are raised about them". The Catholic Church treats as a crime "actions or omissions intended to interfere with or avoid civil investigations or canonical investigations" by bishops and other leaders; the US Catholic bishops will not make settlements "that bind the parties to confidentiality" unless the victim or survivor "freely requests confidentiality", and commit to being "open and transparent" with parishes. The Orthodox Church in America prohibits "Retaliation against a Victim or anyone who, in good faith, has made a complaint". ([1 Timothy 5:19–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI05.htm#V19), [Ezekiel 34:2–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EZK34.htm#V2), [Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Policy commitments, 3](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/policy-commitments), [Church of England, Responding Well to Victims and Survivors of Abuse, Section 1](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/supporting-victims-and-survivors/responding-well-victims-and/section), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 4](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [Vos estis lux mundi, 2023, art. 1](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html), [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 3 and 7](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 8.10](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf))

### Preventing abuse

- `8` **Recruit safely: check, train and supervise everyone who works with children or adults at risk before they start, and keep their checks and training up to date.** "Lay hands hastily on no one", Paul tells Timothy, and those who serve are to "first be tested". The Church of England will "select and vet" everyone with responsibility for children, young people and vulnerable adults and "train and equip" them, and the Methodist Church in Britain will "carefully select and train all those with responsibility within the Church in line with safer recruitment principles". The US Catholic bishops require dioceses to evaluate the backgrounds of all paid staff and volunteers who work with minors, using "the resources of law enforcement and other community agencies", and to renew and verify safe environment training. The Orthodox Church in America requires every parish to screen those with more than incidental contact with minors, with "a criminal records check authorization form". ([1 Timothy 5:22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI05.htm#V22), [1 Timothy 3:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI03.htm#V10), [Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Policy commitments, 2](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/policy-commitments), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 3](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 12–13](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 11.02–11.03](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf))
- `8` **Work in the open: have two adults present, keep doors open, and don't contact children privately or in secret.** Paul takes care for "honourable things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men". The Orthodox Church in America asks that "Two or more unrelated adults should be present" at any church activity with children, and that doors be "left at least partially open". The Methodist Church in Britain asks those who work with children to "always aim to work with or within sight and hearing of another adult", and not to "provide personal contact details to a child or young person such as mobile number, email or social media contact". The US Catholic bishops require "clear and well-publicized" standards of behaviour and "appropriate boundaries" for everyone in contact with minors, which all clergy, staff and volunteers must "acknowledge and abide by". ([2 Corinthians 8:21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO08.htm#V21), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 13.01 and 13.03](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 3.1.1](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 6](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf))

### Caring and managing risk

- `8` **Care for survivors of abuse for as long as they need it, whenever and wherever the abuse happened.** God "heals the broken in heart, and binds up their wounds", and the Samaritan in Jesus' parable "bound up his wounds" and paid for the injured man's care until his return. The Church of England will offer care and support to all who have been abused, "regardless of the type of abuse, when or where it occurred", and the Methodist Church in Britain offers "appropriate pastoral care, counselling and support, according to their expressed and agreed need, as they seek to rebuild their lives". The Catholic Church commits to offering those harmed "spiritual assistance" and "medical assistance, including therapeutic and psychological assistance", and the US Catholic bishops keep reaching out to survivors "whether the abuse was recent or occurred many years in the past". The Orthodox Church in America "will reach out to the Victims of Sexual Misconduct and their families to provide for their spiritual well-being and healing". ([Psalm 147:3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA147.htm#V3), [Luke 10:33–35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK10.htm#V33), [Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Policy commitments, 4](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/policy-commitments), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 4.3](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [Vos estis lux mundi, 2023, art. 5](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html), [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 1](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 3.02](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf))
- `8` **Welcome someone who may pose a risk to others into the church's worship only under a written agreement that sets limits, and keep it under review.** Jesus sends his disciples out "as sheep amongst wolves" and asks them to be "wise as serpents and harmless as doves". The Church of England "opens its doors to all", and will "ensure that any risk has been assessed and is being managed in a safeguarding agreement". The Methodist Church in Britain puts a safeguarding contract in place "following risk assessment activity" to set the terms of someone's "involvement in roles, responsibilities or activities in the life of the Church or attendance at worship", with a monitoring and support group. The Orthodox Church in America asks registered sex offenders to tell the priest before attending, requires "a plan of accountability specific to the offender", signed by all involved, and ends the person's participation if the plan is broken. ([Matthew 10:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT10.htm#V16), [Church of England, Promoting a safer church: Policy commitments, 6](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/promoting-safer-church-0/policy-commitments), [Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 4.7](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf), [Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 15.01–15.03](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf))

## How to apply

- `7` [Responding to and reporting safeguarding concerns and allegations: Quick reference](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/reporting-and-managing-concerns-and-allegations/reporting/responding) (Church of England): four steps, "Recognise, Respond, Record, Refer", with a list of what to do and not to do when someone tells you about abuse: remain calm, use only open questions, write down the actual words used, never promise to keep a secret, and report to your safeguarding officer within one working day unless someone is in immediate danger. Supports `SAFE-1` and `SAFE-2`.
- `7` [Responding Well to Victims and Survivors of Abuse, Section 1](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/supporting-victims-and-survivors/responding-well-victims-and/section) (Church of England): why disclosing abuse is hard, and the marks of a healthy and safe church culture in which people can disclose it: no "coercive and/or controlling behaviour", power that "is shared and distributed", and concerns that can be raised without fear. Supports `SAFE-2` and `SAFE-3`.
- `7` [Safeguarding Learning and Development Framework](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/safeguarding-learning-and-development-framework) (Church of England): the mandatory code of practice that sets out the safeguarding training the Church of England requires of those who serve in different roles, with its training portal. Supports `SAFE-4`.
- `7` [Prevention of Sexual Misconduct](https://www.oca.org/about/sexual-misconduct) (Orthodox Church in America): background checks for those who work in the church, a child safety training programme, and a confidential line for reporting misconduct. Supports `SAFE-1` and `SAFE-4`.
- `7` [Policies and procedures](https://www.baptist.org.uk/Articles/558239/Policies_and_procedures.aspx) (Baptist Union of Great Britain): "Every church needs to have a safeguarding policy in place", with model policies and procedures for local churches and an audit tool to review and improve a church's safeguarding over time. Supports `SAFE-4` and `SAFE-5`.
- `7` [Support organisations for victims and survivors](https://www.churchofengland.org/safeguarding/find-support/support-organisations-victims-and-survivors) (Church of England): organisations that support survivors of church-related abuse, among them Safe Spaces, a free and independent service for anyone who has experienced abuse in relation to the Church of England, the Church in Wales or the Catholic Church in England and Wales ([Safe Spaces](https://safespacesenglandandwales.org.uk/)), and national helplines such as the NSPCC helpline for anyone worried about a child. These are examples from England and Wales; look for the equivalent services where you live. Supports `SAFE-6`.
- `6` [Safeguarding Helpline](https://thirtyoneeight.org/help-and-resources/safeguarding-helpline/) (thirtyone:eight): a UK Christian safeguarding charity whose helpline is "staffed by trained professionals who provide safeguarding advice and guidance" to churches and anyone with a concern, together with [ten safeguarding standards](https://thirtyoneeight.org/about-us/who-we-are/our-ten-standards/) that a church can use to check its practice. Supports `SAFE-1`, `SAFE-4` and `SAFE-5`.
- `6` [Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline](https://childhelphotline.org/) (United States): a helpline for "anyone concerned about or affected by child abuse"; it says that it "is not a reporting line", and lists the child protection reporting numbers of each state. Supports `SAFE-1` and `SAFE-6`.

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 5](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf): **can someone who has abused ever return to ministry, and how far may they share in church life?** The churches agree that a person who may pose a risk can be welcomed to worship under an agreement (`SAFE-7`), but differ on roles and ministry. The US Catholic bishops hold that a priest or deacon who has committed "even one act of sexual abuse of a minor shall not continue in ministry", while offering him therapeutic help. The Orthodox Church in America says that a registered sex offender "will never be allowed to work with children or youth in the church" ([Orthodox Church in America, Policies, Standards and Procedures on Sexual Misconduct, 15.03](https://www.oca.org/files/PDF/sexual-misconduct/2014-04-PSP-Sexual-Misconduct.pdf)). The Methodist Church in Britain sets the "roles, responsibilities or activities" of each person in a safeguarding contract after a risk assessment ([Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 4.7](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf)), and the Church of England provides for risk assessments of clergy under its canons ([Church of England, Safeguarding (Clergy Risk Assessments) Regulations 2024](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/safeguarding-gateway/roles-working-practices-and-safer-recruitment/safeguarding-clergy)). How repentance and forgiveness bear on trust and restoration is discussed in [Repentance and forgiveness](../../general/repentance.md).
- `7` [Apostolic Penitentiary, Note on the importance of the internal forum and the inviolability of the sacramental seal](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/tribunals/apost_penit/documents/rc_trib_appen_pro_20190629_forointerno_en.html) (2019): **what should a priest do when abuse is disclosed in confession?** The Catholic Church teaches that the secrecy of confession admits no exception, "not admitting any exception in the ecclesial sphere, nor, least of all, in the civil one"; its law on reporting abuse excludes what a cleric learns "in the internal forum", and the US Catholic bishops' Charter says reporting must respect "the seal of the Sacrament of Penance, which is always inviolable" ([Vos estis lux mundi, 2023, art. 3](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/motu_proprio/documents/20230325-motu-proprio-vos-estis-lux-mundi-aggiornato.html), [USCCB, Charter for the Protection of Children and Young People, art. 4](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026.07%20Charter-rev%20-%20for%20web.pdf)). The Church of England's guidelines for its clergy say that a priest is forbidden "by the unrepealed Proviso to Canon 113 of the Code of 1603" to reveal a confession made to receive absolution, but that if the penitent discloses the abuse of children or vulnerable adults "the priest must require the penitent to report his or her conduct to the police or other statutory authority" and should withhold absolution if they refuse; the Archbishops' Council commissioned work in 2014 to review that proviso ([Church of England, Guidelines for the Professional Conduct of the Clergy, 3.5–3.7](https://www.churchofengland.org/resources/clergy/guidelines-professional-conduct-clergy/guidelines)). The Methodist Church in Britain, which "does not have authorised liturgies for the sacraments of individual confession", holds that a minister "is not prevented from disclosing details of any crime or offence" revealed in a pastoral conversation or a confession ([Methodist Church in Britain, Safeguarding Policy, 7.1.2](https://media.methodist.org.uk/media/documents/safeguarding_policy_and_procedures_for_the_methodist_church_in-britain_july_20_FdKXANA.pdf)).
