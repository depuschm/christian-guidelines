# Write rules only where the churches broadly agree, and leave contested questions open

## Context and Problem Statement

Christians belong to many churches: the Catholic Church, the Eastern and Oriental Orthodox churches, the Anglican, Lutheran, Reformed, Methodist, Baptist, Pentecostal and Anabaptist churches, and many independent churches. They share the Bible, the faith of the early creeds and most of their teaching on how to live. They also differ, on questions such as which books belong in the Bible, baptism and the Lord's Supper, the authority of the church, who may be ordained, and some questions of marriage and sexuality. Should these guidelines take a side, or adopt one church's teaching as the standard?

## Decision Drivers

* Trust across the churches: Christians of every church should be able to use the guidelines, and so should people of other faiths or none who want to understand Christianity.
* Usefulness: guidance must still be concrete, not a survey that never says what to do.
* Honesty: the guidelines should not pretend there is agreement where there isn't, or disagreement where there isn't.

## Considered Options

* Adopt one church's teaching as the standard, and derive every rule from it
* Decide each contested question by our own judgement
* Write rules only where the churches broadly agree, and present each church's position on contested questions without taking a side

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: "Write rules only where the churches broadly agree". A key rule needs two things:

1. **A basis in Scripture**: at least one passage that teaches it.
2. **Evidence that the churches agree**: either an ecumenical text, such as a creed of the early church, a text of the World Council of Churches' Faith and Order Commission or an agreement between churches, or the teaching documents of at least two church families, one of them Catholic or Orthodox and one Protestant. No major church family may teach the opposite.

Agreement turns out to be wide. The two great commandments, the Ten Commandments, prayer, forgiveness, care for the poor and the stranger, honesty, faithfulness and the protection of the vulnerable are taught by every church.

Where the churches disagree, the file lists the question under **Open questions**, with sources that set out each church's position and a neutral summary of them. The [church pages](../../traditions/README.md) describe each church's own teaching in full ([ADR-0007](0007-describe-churches-on-their-own-terms.md)). Within an area of life, a rule may follow guidance that churches have adopted for that area, such as safeguarding standards, as long as the rule says whose guidance it is.

The shared faith of the creeds is described, not turned into rules: a planned `general/beliefs.md` sets out what the churches confess together, in the creeds' own words, and the rules are about how Christians live.

### Consequences

* Good, because the rules carry the weight of what the churches teach together, not one church's view.
* Good, because readers see openly where agreement ends, and can read each church's position in its own words.
* Bad, because some readers will want answers to contested questions that the guidelines won't give. They are pointed to the church pages and to their own church, and a planned discernment skill will help them reason through such questions with Scripture, tradition, reason and experience.
* Bad, because "broad agreement" is a judgement. The sourcing requirement above, the agreement criterion in [RANKING.md](../../RANKING.md) and review of each new rule keep it honest.

### Confirmation

[CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) sets out the sourcing requirement, and the pull request template asks contributors to confirm that each new rule cites Scripture and at least two church families or an ecumenical text, and that contested questions are listed as open questions.
