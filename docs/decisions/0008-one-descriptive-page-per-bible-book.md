# Give every book of the Bible a descriptive page, with the deuterocanon in its own section

## Context and Problem Statement

The guidelines should cover the whole Bible, every book of the Old and New Testaments. Two questions follow. First, how do book pages relate to the rules: should each book have its own rules, or should the books be described and the rules be gathered by topic? Second, which books count: the churches' Bibles differ, because the Catholic and Orthodox churches read books in their Old Testament that most Protestant churches don't count as Scripture.

## Decision Drivers

* Faithfulness to the text: a book should be read as a whole, in its own setting and genre, not mined for single verses.
* No duplication: the same teaching, such as the love of neighbour, appears in many books and should be one rule, not twenty.
* No ranking of Scripture: the churches don't rank the books of the Bible, and the guidelines shouldn't either.
* Ecumenical scope: every church should find its whole Bible covered ([ADR-0006](0006-write-rules-only-where-the-churches-agree.md)).

## Considered Options

* Rules per book, scored like other rules
* Topics only, with no book pages
* One descriptive page per book, without rules or scores, that links the book's teaching to the rules gathered by topic

And for the books:

* The 66 books of the Protestant canon only
* One church's canon
* The 66 books, plus a deuterocanon section with the further books that the Catholic and Orthodox churches read

## Decision Outcome

Chosen options: "One descriptive page per book" and "the 66 books, plus a deuterocanon section".

**Book pages describe.** Each page in `bible/` gives the book's genre, setting and structure, what the church has traditionally held and what scholars hold about its author and date, its themes, how the churches read it and where they differ. Every theme links to the rule IDs in `general/` and `specific/` that it matches. Book pages have no key rules, and neither books nor passages are scored: every link to a passage counts as a source with the same score ([ADR-0009](0009-quote-and-link-one-public-domain-translation.md)).

**Books are grouped as the churches group them**: the Old Testament (39 books, in every church's Bible), the deuterocanon and the New Testament (27 books, in every church's Bible). The deuterocanon section covers the books that the Catholic and Orthodox churches read in their Old Testament and most Protestant churches don't count as Scripture, including the Greek forms of Esther and Daniel, plus the books that appear in appendices of some Bibles. The table in [bible/README.md](../../bible/README.md) says which churches read each book. The book list follows the World English Bible British Edition with Deuterocanon, the edition that all Bible references link to. Books that some churches read but that edition doesn't include, such as those of the wider canon of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, are described in the canon section of [bible/README.md](../../bible/README.md) and have no pages yet.

### Consequences

* Good, because each book is presented as a whole, and its teaching is connected to the rules without being duplicated.
* Good, because every church finds its whole Bible covered, and readers can see openly where the churches' Bibles differ.
* Bad, because a page can't do justice to a book. Pages link to fuller introductions and commentaries.
* Bad, because there are about 80 pages to write and keep current. The table of books in `bible/README.md` is generated by `scripts/rank.py fix` from `scripts/bible_refs.py`, so it shows at a glance which pages are written.

### Confirmation

`scripts/rank.py check` fails if a book page has key rules or names a rule ID that doesn't exist, and if the table of books in `bible/README.md` is out of date.
