# 1 Esdras

1 Esdras retells the history of Judah from King Josiah's great Passover to Ezra's public reading of the law. Most of it runs parallel to books that every church reads: the end of 2 Chronicles, the whole of Ezra, and the reading of the law in Nehemiah ([2 Chronicles 35:1–36:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CH35.htm#V1), [Ezra 1:1–10:44](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EZR01.htm#V1), [Nehemiah 7:73–8:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NEH07.htm#V73); see the [2 Chronicles](../old-testament/2-chronicles.md), [Ezra](../old-testament/ezra.md) and [Nehemiah](../old-testament/nehemiah.md) pages). The NETS introduction sets out the parallels section by section, and notes that "in addition, in 1 Esdras there is a story of three youths who served as bodyguards for King Darius of Persia" ([NETS, 1 Esdras, translated by R. Glenn Wooden](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/15-1esdras-nets.pdf)). In that story, found nowhere else, three young men of the king's guard argue in turn that wine, the king, and women are strongest, and the third, Zerubbabel, adds that "above all things Truth is the victor" ([1 Esdras 3:10–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1ES03.htm#V10), [1 Esdras 4:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1ES04.htm#V13)). As his reward he asks Darius to keep his vow to rebuild Jerusalem and its temple. The Jewish Encyclopedia judges that the compiler "chose as a center the tale, commonly called the 'Dispute of the Courtiers,'" and grouped extracts from other books around it, so that the restoration of the temple, the worship and the community is "the leading thought of the entire work" ([Jewish Encyclopedia, Esdras, Books of](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/5852-esdras-books-of)). NETS notes that in this book Zerubbabel "plays a more prominent role" than in Ezra, and that "Another emphasis is the temple". The book ends in mid-sentence: the NRSVUE notes at its last verse that "The Greek text ends abruptly" ([NRSVUE, 1 Esdras 1–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Esdras%201-9&version=NRSVUE)).

The book has many names. NETS explains that it is "Esdras A" in the Greek tradition and "1 Esdras" in English translations, while the Greek "Esdras B" is Ezra and Nehemiah. The Catholic Encyclopedia explains that "Esdras A of the Septuagint is III Esdras of St. Jerome", the name it has in the Latin Bible ([Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), Esdras](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05535a.htm)), and the Jewish Encyclopedia notes that "In the English Bible it is again entitled I Esdras"; the [Ezra](../old-testament/ezra.md) page sets out the confusion in full. Nobody knows who compiled it. The Catholic Encyclopedia holds that "Unquestionably our book cannot claim to be Esdras's work", and that scholars believe it was "compiled, probably in Lower Egypt, during the second century B.C." The Jewish Encyclopedia reasons that "Josephus (c. 100 C.E.) made use of I Esdras", and dates it to "the last century before, or the first century of, the common era". NETS finds it "unclear whether this book is strictly the translation of a non-MT Hebrew-Aramaic text or loosely a composition in Greek that made use of 2 Chronicles, Ezra and Nehemiah", and notes that "the Greek of this book is of a good quality".

1 Esdras is not part of the Jewish Bible, and the churches disagree about its place in theirs ([which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)). The **Orthodox** churches read it among the *anaginoskómena*, the "books to be read" ([Munteanu, Canoniques, non-canoniques ou bons à lire ?](https://www.diacronia.ro/ro/indexing/details/A25808/pdf)). The WEBBE's own heading notes that it "is recognised as Deuterocanonical Scripture by the Greek Orthodox and Russian Orthodox Churches. It is not recognised by the Roman Catholic Church, but 1 Esdras is placed as an appendix to the Latin Vulgate Bible" ([1 Esdras](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1ES.htm)). The **Catholic Church** doesn't count it among the 46 books of its Old Testament, which name "Ezra and Nehemiah" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 120](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PR.HTM)); the "first book of Esdras, and the second which is entitled Nehemias" in the Council of Trent's list of 1546 are Ezra and Nehemiah ([Council of Trent, Session IV](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct04.html)). The **Church of England** names "The Third Book of Esdras" among the books that "the Church doth read for example of life and instruction of manners; but yet doth it not apply them to establish any doctrine" ([Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#VI)). **Luther** didn't take it into his Bible ([Rösel, Luther und die Apokryphen](https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen)). The **Reformed** Westminster Confession holds that the books called Apocrypha "are no part of the canon of the Scripture" and are not to be used otherwise "than other human writings" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). The **Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church**, which has its own canon, lists "Ezra (2nd) and Ezra Sutuel" among its Old Testament books, and says that its Bible "includes the book of Enoch, Baruch, and the third and fourth Esdras" ([Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, The Canonical Books](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `7` [New Advent, Augustine, City of God, Book XVIII](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120118.htm): **the contest of truth as a figure of Christ.** In chapter 36, Augustine counts Esdras as "historical rather than prophetical", "unless, perhaps, Esdras is to be understood as prophesying of Christ in that passage where, on a question having arisen among certain young men as to what is the strongest thing", the third youth "demonstrated that the truth is victorious over all. For by consulting the Gospel we learn that Christ is the Truth."
- `7` [New Advent, Cyprian of Carthage, Epistle 73](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050673.htm): **truth against custom.** Writing to Pompey in the controversy over the baptism of heretics, Cyprian argues that custom must not "prevent the truth from prevailing and conquering; for custom without truth is the antiquity of error", "knowing that in Esdras also the truth conquers, as it is written: Truth endures and grows strong to eternity, and lives and prevails for ever and ever". He goes on: "This truth Christ showed to us in His Gospel, and said, I am the truth."
- `7` [Munteanu, Canoniques, non-canoniques ou bons à lire ?](https://www.diacronia.ro/ro/indexing/details/A25808/pdf) (in French): **in the Orthodox Bible.** Munteanu lists 1 Esdras, which he calls 3 Esdras after the Latin, among the books of the Septuagint that the Orthodox read as *anaginoskómena*. He notes that the two books of Esdras (*deux livres d'Esdras*) named in the lists of the Apostolic Constitutions and of the councils of Laodicea and Carthage, as in the Fathers, are Esdras A and B of the Septuagint, and that the first Romanian Bible, of 1688, printed it with the other such books at the end of the Old Testament, as Protestant Bibles did. The **Orthodox Church in America** writes that 1 and 2 Esdras, with Esther, Tobit, Judith and the books of Maccabees, "are included in the Bible in the Orthodox Church" ([Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Old Testament, History](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/history)).
- `7` [Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#VI): **among the books read for example of life.** The **Church of England** lists "The Third Book of Esdras" first among the books that it reads "for example of life and instruction of manners" but not "to establish any doctrine", followed by "The Fourth Book of Esdras". It keeps the Latin numbering, in which the First and Second Books of Esdras are Ezra and Nehemiah, which the same article counts among the canonical books.
- `7` [Rösel, Luther und die Apokryphen](https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen) (Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, in German): **left out of the Luther Bible.** Martin Rösel writes that Luther took some of the Apocrypha into his Bible, but not others, such as the third and fourth books of Esdras (*Andere Bücher, etwa das 3. und 4. Buch Esra wurden nicht aufgenommen*). The Zurich Bible did print the third and fourth books of Esdras and 3 Maccabees, and Rösel notes that this led, already in the sixteenth century, to expanded Luther Bibles and separate editions of these three books.
- `6` [Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), Esdras](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05535a.htm) (New Advent): **outside the canon, kept in the Latin Bible.** For the **Catholic** Church, "Although not belonging to the Canon of the Sacred Scriptures, this book is usually found, *ne prorsus intereat*, in an appendix to the editions of the Vulgate", that is, so that it should not be lost entirely. The encyclopedia notes that "up to St. Jerome, some hesitation" surrounded the book, that "it was freely quoted by the early Fathers, and included in Origen's 'Hexapla'", and explains this by the fact that it "may be considered as another recension of canonical Scriptures". The Jewish Encyclopedia adds that it was counted as III Esdras "after Jerome, with his strong preference for the books preserved in Hebrew, had rejected it from the canon" ([Jewish Encyclopedia, Esdras, Books of](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/5852-esdras-books-of)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree, for example about the book's authorship or the meaning of a passage. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [NETS, 1 Esdras, translated by R. Glenn Wooden](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/15-1esdras-nets.pdf): **which came first, 1 Esdras or Ezra–Nehemiah?** NETS finds the book's relationship to the Hebrew and Aramaic books "unclear", and sets out "two main positions": that the book "represents an earlier form of the biblical accounts", of which the present book is only a part, or that it "is a composition subsequent to, and depending upon, the biblical books". The Catholic Encyclopedia reports both the view that it is "a history of the Temple from the time of Josias down to Nehemias" and the view that it is "an early translation of the chronicler's work, made at a time when Paralipomenon, Esdras, and Neh. still formed one continuous volume" ([Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), Esdras](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05535a.htm)). The Jewish Encyclopedia judges that the book "has no historical value, because it bears every mark of a true midrash", but that its extracts "are valuable as witnesses of an old Greek translation of the Hebrew text, made probably before the Septuagint" ([Jewish Encyclopedia, Esdras, Books of](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/5852-esdras-books-of)).
- `7` [Munteanu, Canoniques, non-canoniques ou bons à lire ?](https://www.diacronia.ro/ro/indexing/details/A25808/pdf) (in French): **does it belong in the Bible?** The churches' answers differ, as set out above and in the table of [which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads): the Orthodox read it among the *anaginoskómena*, and Munteanu notes that the early lists of two books of Esdras counted it; the Catholic canon names only Ezra and Nehemiah ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 120](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PR.HTM)), and the Catholic Encyclopedia notes the "hesitation" before Jerome ([Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), Esdras](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05535a.htm)); the Church of England reads it "for example of life" but not for doctrine ([Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#VI)); and the Westminster Confession counts the Apocrypha "no part of the canon" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `6` [Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), Esdras](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05535a.htm) (New Advent): **why is the story out of order?** The encyclopedia finds "A very strange feature in the work is its absolute disregard of chronological order; the history, indeed, runs directly backwards, mentioning first Artaxerxes (ii, 16-31), then Darius (iii-v, 6), finally Cyrus (v, 7-73)". In the book as it stands, Zerubbabel wins his reward from Darius ([1 Esdras 4:42–57](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1ES04.htm#V42)), yet the list that follows tells of the return under Cyrus, and the work is stopped "all the time that King Cyrus lived… until the reign of Darius" ([1 Esdras 5:70–73](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1ES05.htm#V70)). The Jewish Encyclopedia notes that the winner is called "Zerubbabel" in 4:13 but "Joakim the son of Zerubbabel" in 5:5 ([1 Esdras 5:5–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1ES05.htm#V5)), and concludes that "several individuals must have worked over the book before it received its final shape" ([Jewish Encyclopedia, Esdras, Books of](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/5852-esdras-books-of)).
- `6` [Jewish Encyclopedia, Esdras, Books of](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/5852-esdras-books-of) (1906): **is the story of the three guards older than the book, and was "truth" added to it?** The encyclopedia holds that of the names given to the winner, "Joakim the son of Zerubbabel", "seems to be the original", and that "the second part of his answer, 'truth,' seems to be an addition to the original story". NETS points instead to how the book as it stands makes Zerubbabel, "the winner of the wisdom contest", more prominent than he is in Ezra ([NETS, 1 Esdras](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/15-1esdras-nets.pdf)), and Augustine and Cyprian both quote the speech on truth ([New Advent, Augustine, City of God, Book XVIII](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120118.htm); [New Advent, Cyprian of Carthage, Epistle 73](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050673.htm)).
