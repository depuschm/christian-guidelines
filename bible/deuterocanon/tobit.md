# Tobit

The Book of Tobit is a story set among the Israelites of the northern tribes deported to Nineveh. It opens with its hero's own words: "I, Tobit walked in the ways of truth and righteousness all the days of my life, and I did many alms deeds to my kindred and my nation, who went with me into the land of the Assyrians, to Nineveh" ([Tobit 1:3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB01.htm#V3)). The USCCB introduction describes a book that "combines Jewish piety and morality with folklore in a fascinating story": Tobit is blinded and prays for death; in Media, Sarah, whose seven husbands have each been killed on the wedding night by the demon Asmodeus, prays for death too; "God hears the prayers of Tobit and Sarah and sends the angel Raphael in human form to aid them both", and Raphael guides Tobit's son Tobias to Media, to Sarah, and home again to heal his father ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Tobit](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/tobit/0)). The New American Bible calls its form a "religious novel (as in Esther and Judith) for the purpose of instruction and edification", and holds that the book stands "midway" between the historical books and the wisdom literature, with "standard wisdom themes: fidelity to the law, intercessory function of angels, piety toward parents, purity of marriage, reverence for the dead, and the value of almsgiving, prayer, and fasting". The HarperCollins Bible Dictionary sums up its message: "God will protect and heal those who are pious and compassionate" ([Bible Odyssey, Tobit (HarperCollins Bible Dictionary)](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/dictionary/tobit/)), and Michael Gilmour calls the book "perhaps the closest thing to a fairytale in the Bible" ([Michael Gilmour, Tobit's Dog, Bible Odyssey](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/tobits-dog/)).

The book names no author. The USCCB introduction holds that it "was probably written early in the second century B.C.", "most likely in Aramaic", and that fragments "of four Aramaic texts and of one Hebrew text were discovered in Qumran Cave 4 in 1952" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Tobit](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/tobit/0)); the German Bible Society's scholarly encyclopedia dates it to around 200 BC, possibly later, and judges an Aramaic original more likely than a Hebrew one ([Tobias Nicklas, Tobit / Tobitbuch, WiBiLex, Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft](https://www.die-bibel.de/ressourcen/wibilex/altes-testament/tobit-tobitbuch)). The book has come down in two Greek forms, a shorter one in most manuscripts and a longer one in Codex Sinaiticus. Alexander Di Lella, who translated both for the New English Translation of the Septuagint, notes that the King James Version was translated from the shorter form, but that "In the opinion of the vast majority of scholars today, however, GII with its highly Semitic coloring represents more accurately the original form of the book", and that recent translations, such as the New American Bible and the New Revised Standard Version, follow the longer form ([NETS, Tobit, translated by Alexander A. Di Lella](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/19-tobit-nets.pdf)). The WEBBE, which this page quotes, translates the shorter form: at [Tobit 14:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB14.htm#V4), for example, Tobit trusts "the things which Jonah the prophet spoke of Nineveh", where the longer form, and the NRSVUE, have Nahum ([NETS, Tobit, translated by Alexander A. Di Lella](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/19-tobit-nets.pdf); [Bible Gateway, Tobit 1–14 (NRSVUE)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Tobit%201-14&version=NRSVUE)).

Tobit is not in the Jewish Bible. Jerome, who says he turned it from "the Chaldean language" into Latin in "the work of one day", called it "the book of Tobias, which the Hebrews exclude from the catalogue of Divine Scriptures" ([Fourth Century Christianity, Jerome's Preface to the Book of Tobit](https://www.fourthcentury.com/jeromes-preface-to-the-book-of-tobias-c-a-d-407/)), and Athanasius counted it among the books "not indeed included in the Canon, but appointed by the Fathers to be read by those who newly join us" ([Athanasius, Letter 39](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2806039.htm)). Nicklas notes that early Christian writers in the East, such as Melito, Athanasius and Cyril of Jerusalem, denied it canonical status, while in the West Cyprian, Ambrose and Augustine accepted it, and the councils of Hippo (393), Carthage (397), Florence (1442) and Trent (1546) counted it as canonical ([Tobias Nicklas, Tobit / Tobitbuch, WiBiLex](https://www.die-bibel.de/ressourcen/wibilex/altes-testament/tobit-tobitbuch)). Today the churches still differ, as the [table of which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads) shows: Tobit is in the **Catholic** canon of the Old Testament ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 120](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PR.HTM); [Council of Trent, Session IV](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct04.html)); the **Orthodox** churches count it among the books "considered by the Orthodox as genuine parts of the Bible" ([Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Old Testament, History](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/history)); Article VI of the **Church of England** names "The Book of Tobias" among the books the church reads "for example of life and instruction of manners" but not "to establish any doctrine" ([Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#VI)); **Luther** kept it in his Bible among the Apocrypha, which he judged useful and good to read (*nützlich und gut zu lesen*) ([Martin Rösel, Luther und die Apokryphen, Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft](https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen)); and the **Reformed** Westminster Confession holds that the books called Apocrypha "are no part of the canon of the Scripture" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2360–2379](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P86.HTM): **Tobias and Sarah, and marriage.** In its teaching on "The Love of Husband and Wife", the **Catholic** Catechism quotes the prayer of Tobias and Sarah on their wedding night in full, beginning "Tobias got out of bed and said to Sarah, 'Sister, get up, and let us pray and implore our Lord that he grant us mercy and safety'" ([Tobit 8:4–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB08.htm#V4)), and among the Old Testament readings for a Catholic wedding are Tobit 7:6–14, "May the Lord of heaven prosper you both", and Tobit 8:4b–8, "Allow us to live together to a happy old age" ([USCCB, For Your Marriage, Old Testament Readings](https://www.foryourmarriage.org/old-testament-readings/)). The first **Anglican** Book of Common Prayer, of 1549, asked God at a wedding to bless the couple "as thou diddest sende thy Aungell Raphaell to Thobie", that is Tobit, "and Sara, the daughter of Raguel, to their great comfort" ([Society of Archbishop Justus, The 1549 Book of Common Prayer: Marriage](https://justus.anglican.org/resources/bcp/1549/Marriage_1549.htm)); the Book of Common Prayer of 1662 has in its place "as thou didst send thy blessing upon Abraham and Sarah, to their great comfort" ([Church of England, Book of Common Prayer: The Form of Solemnization of Matrimony](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/form-solemnization-matrimony)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2443–2449](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P8F.HTM): **almsgiving.** The **Catholic** Catechism counts "burying the dead" among the corporal works of mercy, and teaches that "giving alms to the poor is one of the chief witnesses to fraternal charity: it is also a work of justice pleasing to God", with a reference to Tobit 4:5–11 ([Tobit 4:5–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB04.htm#V5)); it names "fasting, prayer, and almsgiving" as the three forms of penance on which "Scripture and the Fathers insist above all", with a reference to Tobit 12:8 ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1434–1439](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4B.HTM)). Polycarp of Smyrna urged the Philippians, "When you can do good, defer it not, because alms delivers from death" ([Polycarp, Letter to the Philippians, 10](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0136.htm); [Tobit 4:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB04.htm#V10)). The **Anglican** Book of Common Prayer of 1662 includes two sentences from Tobit 4 among the sentences read at Holy Communion while the alms are collected, among them "Give alms of thy goods, and never turn thy face from any poor man; and then the face of the Lord shall not be turned away from thee" ([Church of England, Book of Common Prayer, The Lord's Supper or Holy Communion](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/lords-supper-or-holy-communion)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 325–354](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1A.HTM): **Raphael and the angels.** The **Catholic** Catechism teaches that "From infancy to death human life is surrounded by their watchful care and intercession", with a reference among others to Tobit 12:12 ([Tobit 12:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB12.htm#V12)), and notes that the Byzantine liturgy remembers "St. Michael, St. Gabriel, St. Raphael, and the guardian angels". At the **Orthodox** feast of the Archangel Michael and the bodiless powers on 8 November, Raphael is remembered as "the healing of God, the curer of human infirmities (Tobit 3:16, 12:15)" ([Orthodox Church in America, Lives of all saints commemorated on November 8: Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and the Other Bodiless Powers](https://www.oca.org/saints/all-lives/2026/11/08)). The **Reformed** reformer John Calvin, warning against speculation about the ranks of angels, mentions "the two names, Michael and Gabriel, mentioned in Scripture, or a third, if you choose to add it from the history of Tobit" ([John Calvin, Institutes of the Christian Religion, Book I, Chapter 14](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.iii.xv.html)).
- `7` [John Paul II, General Audience of 25 July 2001: Canticle of Tobit](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/audiences/2001/documents/hf_jp-ii_aud_20010725.html): **Tobit's song in daily prayer.** Part of Tobit's song ([Tobit 13:1–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB13.htm#V1)) is sung at Morning Prayer on Tuesday of the first week in the **Catholic** Liturgy of the Hours. John Paul II drew from the story that "Those who do good, above all, by opening their hearts to the needs of their neighbours, are pleasing to the Lord, even if they are tried; in the end, they will experience his goodness", and read the song's call to return to God as a "'reciprocal' conversion of God and man". The **Church of England** includes "A Song of Tobit", from Tobit 13, among the canticles of Common Worship ([Church of England, Common Worship: Daily Prayer, Canticle 44: A Song of Tobit](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/common-worship/daily-prayer/canticles-daily-prayer/44-song-tobit)).
- `7` [Martin Rösel, Luther und die Apokryphen](https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen) (Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft): **Tobit in the Luther Bible.** Rösel notes that Luther accepted Tobit among the Apocrypha he translated because they are useful and good to read (*nützlich und gut zu lesen*), that he judged the books by their content, and placed them between the Testaments. In a recent revision of the Luther Bible, the Council of the Evangelical Church in Germany had Tobit, with Judith, Sirach and others, newly translated from the Greek of the Göttingen Septuagint. How the **Lutheran** confessions read Tobit's teaching on alms is set out in the open questions below.
- `6` [USCCB, Readings for 5 June 2023 (Memorial of Saint Boniface)](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/060523.cfm): **Tobit at Mass.** The **Catholic** weekday lectionary reads Tobit's story at Mass: on 5 June 2023, for example, the first reading was Tobit 1:3 and 2:1–8, Tobit's charity and his burial of the dead ([Tobit 1:3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB01.htm#V3), [Tobit 2:1–8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB02.htm#V1)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Tobias Nicklas, Tobit / Tobitbuch](https://www.die-bibel.de/ressourcen/wibilex/altes-testament/tobit-tobitbuch) (WiBiLex, Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft): **history or story?** Nicklas assigns the book to a fictional genre, a novel-like teaching story, and finds that its many chronological details already show that it is a fictional story which should not be judged by the standard of history (in German: "eine fiktive Erzählung vorliegt, die nicht mit dem Maßstab der Historizität bewertet werden sollte"). The New American Bible notes that Sennacherib was "the son of Sargon II" and that neither was descended from Shalmaneser, whom [Tobit 1:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB01.htm#V15) makes his father (the WEBBE's "Enemessar") ([USCCB, Tobit, chapter 1, with notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/tobit/1)), and that the Tigris is "actually west of Nineveh, so they would not have come to it on their way to Media" ([USCCB, Tobit, chapter 6, with notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/tobit/6)), and it calls the book a "religious novel" whose "seemingly historical data" are "vivid details" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Tobit](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/tobit/0)). The Catholic Encyclopedia of 1912, by contrast, reported that "Until recently there never was question among Catholics in regard to the historicity of Tobias", and that, with a few exceptions, "Catholic exegetes are unanimous in clearly defending the historicity of Tobias", in line with a decision of the Pontifical Biblical Commission of 1905 ([Catholic Encyclopedia (1912), Tobias](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14749c.htm)).
- `7` [Apology of the Augsburg Confession, Article IV-b: Of Love and the Fulfilling of the Law](https://bookofconcord.org/defense/of-love-and-fulfilling-the-law/) (BookOfConcord.org): **do alms deliver from sin and death?** Tobit says that "alms-giving delivers from death" and that alms "purges away all sin" ([Tobit 4:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB04.htm#V10), [Tobit 12:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB12.htm#V9)). The **Catholic** Catechism lists, among the ways the Fathers name of obtaining forgiveness of sins, "the practice of charity 'which covers a multitude of sins'" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1434–1439](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4B.HTM)). The **Lutheran** Apology answers that "alms do not justify us without faith, ex opere operato", that freeing from sin and death is the prerogative of Christ, and that "those alms please God which follow reconciliation or justification, and not those which precede", adding that "the address of Tobias, regarded as a whole, shows that faith is required before alms". The **Anglican** Articles do not use these books "to establish any doctrine" ([Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#VI)), and the **Reformed** Westminster Confession holds that they are not "to be any otherwise approved, or made use of, than other human writings" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 325–354](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1A.HTM): **do angels present our prayers to God?** Raphael says that he "brought the memorial of your prayer before the Holy One" and calls himself one of the angels "which present the prayers of the saints" ([Tobit 12:12–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TOB12.htm#V12)). The **Catholic** Catechism, citing this verse, teaches that human life is surrounded by the angels' "watchful care and intercession", and the **Orthodox** Church honours Raphael as "the curer of human infirmities" ([Orthodox Church in America, Lives of all saints commemorated on November 8: Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and the Other Bodiless Powers](https://www.oca.org/saints/all-lives/2026/11/08)). Calvin grants that Scripture gives angels the work of "guiding us in all our ways, of encompassing, admonishing, and comforting us, of keeping watch over us", but insists on "that office of sole intercession which we have above claimed for Christ" ([John Calvin, Institutes of the Christian Religion, Book III, Chapter 20](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/institutes.v.xxi.html)), and the Westminster Confession teaches that religious worship is to be given to God "and to him alone; not to angels, saints, or any other creature", and "not without a Mediator; nor in the mediation of any other but of Christ alone" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 21.2](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [NETS, Tobit, translated by Alexander A. Di Lella](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/19-tobit-nets.pdf): **which form of the text is the original?** Di Lella reports that earlier scholars "up to the nineteenth century preferred GI", the shorter form, which the King James Version translated, while "the vast majority of scholars today" hold that the longer form "represents more accurately the original form of the book", supported by the Qumran fragments; he notes that some scholars "continue to defend the priority of GI over GII". Nicklas holds that the manuscripts differ too much to reconstruct an original text with any certainty ([Tobias Nicklas, Tobit / Tobitbuch, WiBiLex](https://www.die-bibel.de/ressourcen/wibilex/altes-testament/tobit-tobitbuch)). The WEBBE translates the shorter form, the NRSVUE and the New American Bible the longer ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Tobit](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/tobit/0)).

## Tutorials and further reading

- `7` [Tobias Nicklas, Tobit / Tobitbuch](https://www.die-bibel.de/ressourcen/wibilex/altes-testament/tobit-tobitbuch) (WiBiLex, Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft, in German): a scholarly article on the story, the Greek, Latin and Qumran texts, the genre and its irony, the book's date and place, its links with the New Testament, and its place in the canon.
- `6` [Michael Gilmour, Tobit's Dog](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/tobits-dog/) (Bible Odyssey): why a dog walks into the story twice, and what it may mean.
- `6` [Catholic Encyclopedia (1912), Tobias](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/14749c.htm) (New Advent): an older Catholic introduction to the book, its texts, its use by the Fathers and the debate about its history.
- `6` [Bible Odyssey, Tobit (HarperCollins Bible Dictionary)](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/dictionary/tobit/): a short entry on the book and its place among the Apocrypha and the deuterocanonical books.
- `6` [BibleProject, Tobit (overview video)](https://bibleproject.com/videos/tobit/): an overview video of the story and its major themes, and of how its authors drew on the Hebrew Scriptures.
