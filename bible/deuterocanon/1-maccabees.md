# 1 Maccabees

The First Book of Maccabees is a history of the Jewish revolt against the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes and of the family of the priest Mattathias, whose sons Judas Maccabaeus, Jonathan and Simon led the revolt and then the nation. It begins with the death of Alexander the Great and the rise of "a sinful root, Antiochus Epiphanes" ([1 Maccabees 1:1–10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA01.htm#V1)), and it ends with the murder of Simon and the succession of his son John ([1 Maccabees 16:14–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA16.htm#V14)). The USCCB introduction explains that "The name Maccabee, probably meaning 'hammer,' is actually applied in the Books of Maccabees to only one man, Judas", and that the book records "the deliverance of Israel that God worked through the family of Mattathias", covering the years "from 175 to 134 B.C." ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Book of Maccabees](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1maccabees/0)). Jewish sources call Mattathias and his descendants the "Sons [or House] of Hashmonay", and so they are also known as the Hasmoneans ([Bible Odyssey, Chris Seeman, Maccabees](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/maccabees/)). The Second Book of Maccabees is a separate work: the two books "contain independent accounts of events (in part identical)", the USCCB introduction notes.

The USCCB introduction holds that "First Maccabees was written about 100 B.C., in Hebrew, but the original has not come down to us. Instead, we have an early, pre-Christian, Greek translation full of Hebrew idioms", and that its author, "probably a Palestinian Jew, is unknown" but "had access to much reliable information" ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Book of Maccabees](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1maccabees/0)). The NETS translator George Themelis Zervos reports that modern scholars "generally confirm the ancient viewpoint that our Greek text of 1 Makkabees is a translation of a long lost Hebrew Vorlage", that "no fragments of the Vorlage are known to have survived, even among the Dead Sea Scrolls", and that its author wrote in a style "modeled purposely after the historical books of the Bible—Judges, Samuel, Kings and Chronicles" ([NETS, 1 Makkabees, translated by George Themelis Zervos](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/20-1makk-nets.pdf)). The book's account of the temple's rededication, which Judas and the people ordained to be kept "for eight days, from the twenty-fifth day of the month Chislev, with gladness and joy" ([1 Maccabees 4:59](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA04.htm#V59)), "is the origin of the Jewish feast of Hanukkah", the USCCB introduction notes. John's Gospel sets a dispute between Jesus and his hearers in the temple at "the Feast of the Dedication" ([John 10:22–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN10.htm#V22); see the [John](../new-testament/john.md) page), which the New American Bible explains as "an eight-day festival of lights (Hebrew, Hanukkah)" that celebrates "the Maccabees' rededication of the altar and reconsecration of the temple in 164 B.C." ([USCCB, John, chapter 10, with notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/10)).

1 Maccabees is not part of the Jewish Bible. Hanukkah "is one of the few Jewish holidays not mentioned in the Bible", and its story "is contained in the books of 1 and 2 Maccabees, which are not part of the Jewish canon of the Hebrew Bible" ([My Jewish Learning, The History of Hanukkah](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/hanukkah-history/)). The Talmud tells the story differently: when the Hasmoneans defeated the Greeks, "they searched and found only one cruse of oil", enough for one day, and "A miracle occurred and they lit the candelabrum from it eight days" ([Sefaria, Shabbat 21b:10](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.21b.10)). Chris Seeman writes that "in rabbinic Judaism the Maccabean military victory was gradually eclipsed by a focus on piety and miracle" ([Bible Odyssey, Chris Seeman, Hanukkah](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/hanukkah/)).

The churches disagree about the book's place in their Bibles ([which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)). The **Catholic Church** counts "1 and 2 Maccabees" among the 46 books of its Old Testament ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 120](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PR.HTM)), and the Council of Trent's list of sacred and canonical books in 1546 named "two books of the Machabees, the first and the second" ([Council of Trent, Session IV](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct04.html)). The **Orthodox** Church in America counts "1 and 2 Maccabees" among the historical books that "are considered by the Orthodox as genuine parts of the Bible" ([Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Old Testament, History](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/history)), and the Orthodox churches read these books as *anaginoskómena*, "books to be read" ([Munteanu, Canoniques, non-canoniques ou bons à lire ?](https://www.diacronia.ro/ro/indexing/details/A25808/pdf)). The **Church of England** names "The First Book of Maccabees" among the books that "the Church doth read for example of life and instruction of manners; but yet doth it not apply them to establish any doctrine" ([Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#VI)). **Luther**'s Bible printed the two books of Maccabees among the Apocrypha, books "useful and good to read" ([Rösel, Luther und die Apokryphen](https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen)). **Reformed** confessions hold that the books called Apocrypha "are no part of the canon of the Scripture" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)); the Belgic Confession names "the two books of Maccabees" among them, and adds that "The church may certainly read these books and learn from them as far as they agree with the canonical books" ([Christian Reformed Church in North America, Belgic Confession, Article 6](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/belgic-confession)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `8` [Christian Reformed Church in North America, Belgic Confession](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/belgic-confession): **a book to read, not to found doctrine on.** The **Reformed** Belgic Confession, whose chief author, Guido de Brès, "prepared this confession in the year 1561", names "the two books of Maccabees" among the apocryphal books, which the church "may certainly read" and "learn from them as far as they agree with the canonical books", but which do not have "such power and virtue that one could confirm from their testimony any point of faith" (Article 6). The **Church of England** lists "The First Book of Maccabees" among the books read "for example of life and instruction of manners" ([Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#VI)), and the German Bible Society notes that Luther took "das 1. und 2. Makkabäerbuch" into his Bible, and that for the Luther Bible of 2017 1 Maccabees was among the books "neu übersetzt", newly translated from the Greek ([Rösel, Luther und die Apokryphen](https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen)).
- `7` [Augustine, City of God, Book XVIII](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120118.htm) (New Advent): **the books in the early church.** In chapter 36 the church father Augustine says that the books of the Maccabees "are held as canonical, not by the Jews, but by the Church, on account of the extreme and wonderful sufferings of certain martyrs, who, before Christ had come in the flesh, contended for the law of God even unto death". Jerome, translating the Bible into Latin, wrote that "The first book of Maccabees I have found to be Hebrew, the second is Greek" ([Christian Classics Ethereal Library, Jerome, Prefaces to the Vulgate Old Testament: Samuel and Kings](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf206.vii.iii.iv.html)), and that "the Church reads Judith, Tobit, and the books of Maccabees, but does not admit them among the canonical Scriptures" ([Christian Classics Ethereal Library, Jerome, Prefaces to the Vulgate Old Testament: Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of Songs](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf206.vii.iii.x.html)). Athanasius's festal letter for 367, which lists the books of the canon and the books "to be read", names neither book of the Maccabees ([Athanasius, Letter 39](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2806039.htm)).
- `7` [Orthodox Church in America, Lives of all saints commemorated on August 1 (Holy Maccabee Martyrs)](https://www.oca.org/saints/all-lives/2026/08/01): **the Maccabee martyrs and Judas.** The **Orthodox** Church commemorates the seven Maccabee martyrs, their mother and their teacher Eleazar on 1 August. Their story is told in the Second Book of Maccabees, and the Orthodox Church in America adds that "The martyric death of the Maccabee brothers inspired Judas Maccabeus, and he led a revolt against Antiochus Epiphanes. With God's help, he gained the victory, and then purified the Temple at Jerusalem." It reads the books of Maccabees as part of the history that brings "the history of Israel down to New Testament times" ([Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Old Testament, History](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/history)).
- `6` [USCCB, Readings for 21 November 2025](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/112125.cfm): **the rededication and Jesus in the temple.** The **Catholic** Church read 1 Maccabees at weekday Masses in November 2025: on 17 November the first reading was the decree of Antiochus and the faithful who "chose to die" ([USCCB, Readings for 17 November 2025](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/111725.cfm); [1 Maccabees 1:10–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA01.htm#V10), [1 Maccabees 1:41–43](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA01.htm#V41), [1 Maccabees 1:54–57](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA01.htm#V54), [1 Maccabees 1:62–63](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA01.htm#V62)), and on 21 November the rededication of the temple ([1 Maccabees 4:36–37](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA04.htm#V36), [1 Maccabees 4:52–59](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA04.htm#V52)) was read with the Gospel of Jesus driving the traders out of the temple ([Luke 19:45–48](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK19.htm#V45)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree, for example about the book's authorship or the meaning of a passage. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Bible Odyssey, Chris Seeman, Maccabees](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/maccabees/): **how should readers judge the Maccabees' violence?** The book praises Mattathias's killing at the altar of Modin as zeal like that of Phinehas ([1 Maccabees 2:23–26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA02.htm#V23)), and records that his army "forcibly circumcised the boys who were uncircumcised" ([1 Maccabees 2:46](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA02.htm#V46)). Seeman describes "their militant defense of religious freedom" against a persecuting king. The USCCB introduction reads the conflict as "a struggle, not simply between Jew and Gentile, but between those who would uphold the law and those, Jews or Gentiles, who would destroy it" ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Book of Maccabees](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1maccabees/0)). Michele Alperin notes that 1 Maccabees portrays them "as religious zealots, murdering coreligionists who had chosen the path of Hellenism", and that "later rabbinic commentators frowned upon such zealotry"; she concludes that "the Maccabees must be judged to be both liberators and zealots" ([My Jewish Learning, Michele Alperin, The Maccabees: Heroes or Fanatics?](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/the-maccabees-heroes-or-fanatics/)). Augustine valued the books for "the extreme and wonderful sufferings of certain martyrs" rather than for the wars ([Augustine, City of God, Book XVIII](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120118.htm)). The churches' teaching on when armed resistance is justified is set out in [Public life](../../general/public-life.md#open-questions).
- `7` [New Advent, Thomas Aquinas, Summa Theologiae, II-II, Question 40, Article 4: Whether it is lawful to fight on holy days](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3040.htm#article4): **was it right to fight on the Sabbath?** Some of the faithful died rather than defend themselves on the Sabbath ([1 Maccabees 2:32–38](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA02.htm#V32)); Mattathias and his friends decided to fight ([1 Maccabees 2:39–41](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA02.htm#V39)). Thomas Aquinas quotes the decision of [1 Maccabees 2:41](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA02.htm#V41), "The Jews rightly determined", and argues, citing Jesus' healing on the Sabbath ([John 7:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN07.htm#V23)), that "for the purpose of safeguarding the common weal of the faithful, it is lawful to carry on a war on holy days, provided there be need for doing so". Michele Alperin reads the decision as the Maccabees "interpreting the law on their own authority", "setting themselves up as an opposition government" ([My Jewish Learning, Michele Alperin, The Maccabees: Heroes or Fanatics?](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/the-maccabees-heroes-or-fanatics/)).
- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the First Book of Maccabees](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1maccabees/0): **how reliable is the book as history?** The USCCB introduction says the author "had access to much reliable information" and "may well have played some part in it himself in his youth". The 1910 Catholic Encyclopedia reported that "Modern scholars of all schools… admit that the book is a historical document of the highest value", while noting that its accuracy on "a few minor details concerning foreign nations", such as the Romans and the Spartans, had been denied ([New Advent, Catholic Encyclopedia (1910), The Books of Machabees](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/09495a.htm)). Chris Seeman points out that although Simon "touted this undoing of Antiochus's legacy as Israel's liberation from 'the yoke of the gentiles,' Seleucid hegemony remained a reality" ([Bible Odyssey, Chris Seeman, Maccabees](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/maccabees/); compare [1 Maccabees 13:41](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA13.htm#V41)), and Michele Alperin that "The historical reality is murky, refracted as it is through the political and religious agendas of First and Second Maccabees" ([My Jewish Learning, Michele Alperin, The Maccabees: Heroes or Fanatics?](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/the-maccabees-heroes-or-fanatics/)).
- `7` [USCCB, 1 Maccabees, chapter 1, with notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1maccabees/1): **how does the book relate to Daniel?** 1 Maccabees calls the altar set up in the temple "an abomination of desolation" ([1 Maccabees 1:54](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA01.htm#V54)), and the New American Bible cross-references Daniel's "abomination that makes desolate" ([Daniel 11:31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DAN11.htm#V31)). Mattathias's farewell already names Hananiah, Azariah, Mishael and Daniel as examples of faith ([1 Maccabees 2:59–60](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1MA02.htm#V59)). The USCCB introduction contrasts the two books: 1 Maccabees expresses "the customary belief of Israel, without the new developments which appear in 2 Maccabees and Daniel" ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Book of Maccabees](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1maccabees/0)). Whether Daniel's visions foretold these events centuries before or were written during the persecution under Antiochus is set out among the open questions on the [Daniel](../old-testament/daniel.md#open-questions) page.
