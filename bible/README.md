# The Bible

A guide to every book of the Bible: the 39 books of the Old Testament, the 27 of the New Testament, and the deuterocanon, the further books that the Catholic and Orthodox churches read in their Old Testament. Each book page describes the book, its structure and themes, and how the churches read it, and links its teaching to the [general rules](../general/README.md). Book pages have no rules of their own, and neither books nor passages are scored ([ADR-0008](../docs/decisions/0008-one-descriptive-page-per-bible-book.md)).

Every Bible reference in these guidelines links to the passage in the World English Bible British Edition with Deuterocanon (WEBBE), a public domain translation, so it can be quoted freely ([ADR-0009](../docs/decisions/0009-quote-and-link-one-public-domain-translation.md)). For study, read the scholarly translations listed under "Adopted references" too.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition (NRSVUE)](https://www.biblegateway.com/versions/New-Revised-Standard-Version-Updated-Edition-NRSVUE-Bible/) (Bible Gateway): an ecumenical scholarly translation, revised under the Society of Biblical Literature, with the books that all the churches read, including the deuterocanon of the Catholic and Orthodox churches. The best single translation for study across the churches. Quoting more than 500 verses needs the publisher's written approval, which is one reason these guidelines quote the WEBBE ([ADR-0009](../docs/decisions/0009-quote-and-link-one-public-domain-translation.md)).
- `8` [New American Bible, Revised Edition (NABRE)](https://bible.usccb.org/bible) (United States Conference of Catholic Bishops): the Catholic translation used in the liturgy in the United States, with the 73 books of the Catholic canon and a scholarly introduction to each book.
- `7` [World English Bible British Edition with Deuterocanon (WEBBE)](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/) (eBible.org): the public domain translation that every Bible reference in these guidelines links to and quotes. It includes the deuterocanon and the further books of the Orthodox churches.
- `7` [New International Version (NIV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/versions/New-International-Version-NIV-Bible/) (Bible Gateway): the most widely used translation among evangelical Protestants, with the 66 books of the Protestant canon.
- `7` [A New English Translation of the Septuagint (NETS)](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/) (International Organization for Septuagint and Cognate Studies): a scholarly translation of the Greek Old Testament, the text the Orthodox churches read, book by book.
- `7` [Tanakh](https://www.sefaria.org/texts/Tanakh) (Sefaria): the Hebrew Bible in Hebrew, with English translations, book by book.
- `7` [SBL Greek New Testament (SBLGNT)](https://www.biblegateway.com/versions/SBL-Greek-New-Testament-SBLGNT/) (Society of Biblical Literature, on Bible Gateway): a free critical edition of the New Testament in Greek.

## Which books each church reads

All the churches read the same 39 books of the Old Testament, which are also the books of the Jewish Bible, and the same 27 books of the New Testament. They differ on a further group of books, most of which are found in the Greek translation of the Old Testament, the Septuagint: Catholics call them deuterocanonical, the Orthodox churches call them *anaginoskómena*, "books to be read", and many Protestants call them the Apocrypha. These guidelines call them the deuterocanon, and give them their own section ([ADR-0008](../docs/decisions/0008-one-descriptive-page-per-bible-book.md)).

| Church | Old Testament | The further books | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Catholic Church | 46 books: the 39, with Tobit, Judith, 1 and 2 Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach and Baruch, and Esther and Daniel with their Greek parts | Part of the canon of Scripture, as the Council of Trent defined in 1546 | [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 120](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__PR.HTM), [Council of Trent, Session IV](https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/ct04.html) |
| Eastern Orthodox churches | The 39, the Catholic books above, and also 1 Esdras, the Prayer of Manasseh, Psalm 151 and 3 Maccabees. The Greek synodal edition of 1937 prints 4 Maccabees in an appendix, and 2 Esdras appears only in Slavonic Bibles | Read as part of the Bible and called *anaginoskómena*. There is no single list decided by all the Orthodox churches together | [Munteanu, Canoniques, non-canoniques ou bons à lire ?](https://www.diacronia.ro/ro/indexing/details/A25808/pdf), [Orthodox Church in America, Old Testament: History](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/history), [Orthodox Church in America, Old Testament: Wisdom](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/wisdom) |
| Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church | By the church's own count, 46 books, including Jubilees and Enoch | The church counts 81 books in its Bible, 46 in the Old Testament and 35 in the New | [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, The Canonical Books](https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html) |
| Anglican churches | The 39 books are canonical | Article VI of the Church of England names the other books, including the Prayer of Manasseh and the books now called 1 and 2 Esdras (which it calls the Third and Fourth Books of Esdras), which the church reads "for example of life and instruction of manners", but not "to establish any doctrine" | [Church of England, Articles of Religion, VI](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion) |
| Lutheran churches | The 39 books | Luther's Bible printed them between the Testaments as books "not held equal to holy Scripture, but useful and good to read" | [Rösel, Luther und die Apokryphen](https://www.die-bibel.de/luther-und-die-apokryphen) |
| Reformed and Presbyterian churches | The 39 books | "No part of the canon of the Scripture", to be used only like "other human writings" | [Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.2–1.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html) |

The New Testament has 27 books in every church's Bible, although some of them, such as Hebrews, James, 2 Peter, 2 and 3 John, Jude and Revelation, were recognised more slowly than others ([Bible Odyssey, The New Testament Canon](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-new-testament-canon/)). The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church counts 35 books in its New Testament, adding books of church order to the 27.

## Open questions

- `7` [Three Biblical Canons](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/three-biblical-canons/) (Society of Biblical Literature, Bible Odyssey): are the deuterocanonical books Scripture? The Catholic Church counts them in its canon, the Orthodox churches read them as part of the Bible, the Anglican and Lutheran traditions read them for instruction but not to establish doctrine, and the Reformed confessions hold that they are not Scripture. The table above gives each church's own words.
