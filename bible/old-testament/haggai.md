# Haggai

Haggai is a short book of prophecy from the year 520 BC, after the return from exile in Babylon: "The phrase “LORD of hosts” occurs 14 times in the 38 verses of this short book" ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Haggai](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-haggai/)). The New American Bible explains that "Haggai’s words concern conditions in the Persian province of Judah at the beginning of the postexilic period during the reign of the Persian king Darius I (522–486 B.C.)", when "The community in Judah is struggling with its identity in light of the loss of its statehood through the demise of the monarchy and the destruction of the Temple" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Haggai](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/0)). The book gathers four messages, dated to the day in the second year of King Darius, to the governor Zerubbabel, the high priest Joshua and the people ([Haggai 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG01.htm#V1), [Haggai 2:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V1), [Haggai 2:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V10), [Haggai 2:20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V20)). Work on the temple in Jerusalem had stopped ([Ezra 4:24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EZR04.htm#V24)), and Haggai urges the people to rebuild it: "The brief period of Haggai’s ministry (August to December 520 B.C.) marks the resumption of work on the Temple, the symbol of divine presence among the people" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Haggai](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/0)). The book of Ezra names him with Zechariah: "The elders of the Jews built and prospered, through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah the son of Iddo" ([Ezra 5:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EZR05.htm#V1), [Ezra 6:14–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EZR06.htm#V14); [Ezra](ezra.md)), and the New American Bible calls Zechariah "a prophetic book connected in literary and thematic ways to Haggai" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Haggai](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/0)).

The ESV Global Study Bible holds that "it is reasonable to consider Haggai its author. Nothing is known of his genealogy" ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Haggai](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-haggai/)). His name means "festal", "of a festival" ([Bible Odyssey, Haggai (HarperCollins Bible Dictionary)](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/dictionary/haggai/)); the Orthodox life of the prophet holds that he "was of the Tribe of Levi" ([Orthodox Church in America, Prophet Haggai](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2026/12/16/103556-prophet-haggai)). The Talmud holds that "The members of the Great Assembly wrote" the book of "the Twelve Prophets" ([Sefaria, Bava Batra 15a:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.15a.2)), and Matthew Henry reports that "The Jews ascribe to these two prophets the honour of being members of the great synagogue", Haggai and Zechariah ([Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Haggai: Introduction](https://www.ccel.org/h/henry/mhc2/MHC37000.HTM)). Haggai, Zechariah and Malachi are the prophets after the return from exile: "The Lord raised up three witnesses—Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi", wrote Calvin ([John Calvin, Commentary on Haggai, Preface](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom29.v.i.html)), and the Talmud calls the three "the last of the prophets" ([Sefaria, Bava Batra 14b:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.14b.9)).

Haggai is Jewish Scripture: "Haggai is one of 12 books of Minor Prophets (“Trei Asar”), marked by their shortness", in which the prophet "criticizes those who have settled in Israel but not rebuilt the Temple and urges them to begin building" ([Sefaria, Haggai](https://www.sefaria.org/Haggai)). The Talmud counts the twelve as "one book of the Twelve Prophets" ([Sefaria, Bava Batra 14b:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.14b.9)), and the Orthodox Church calls Haggai "the tenth of the Twelve Minor Prophets" ([Orthodox Church in America, Prophet Haggai](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2026/12/16/103556-prophet-haggai)). In the Greek Septuagint, the Old Testament of the Orthodox churches, he is called Haggaios, and the translation is "reasonably close" to the Hebrew ([NETS, The Twelve Prophets, translated by George E. Howard](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/32-twelve-nets.pdf)). The Letter to the Hebrews quotes Haggai's promise, "Yet once more I will shake not only the earth, but also the heavens" ([Hebrews 12:26–28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB12.htm#V26); [Hebrews](../new-testament/hebrews.md)).

## Outline

The outline follows the book's four dated messages. The New American Bible divides the book into "The Restoration of the Temple" (chapter 1) and "Oracles of Encouragement" (chapter 2) ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Haggai](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/0)). The WEBBE and the New American Bible number the verses the same way. The ESV Global Study Bible and the Revised Common Lectionary divide verse 1:15 in two and join its date to the message of chapter 2: "The Former and Latter Glory of This House (1:15b–2:9)" ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Haggai](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-haggai/)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `7` [Augustine, City of God, Book XVIII, 35](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120118.htm) (New Advent): **"the desired of all nations".** Augustine, a teacher of the **Catholic** and the whole **Western** church, holds that "Haggai more openly prophesies of Christ and the Church", reading his Latin text of the promise, "and the desired of all nations shall come" ([Haggai 2:6–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V6)). "He moved the heaven by the testimony of the angels and the stars, when Christ became incarnate. He moved the earth by the great miracle of His birth of the virgin", and "the fulfillment of what follows, And the desired of all nations shall come, is looked for at His last coming."
- `7` [Orthodox Church in America, Prophet Haggai](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2026/12/16/103556-prophet-haggai): **the Orthodox commemoration.** The **Orthodox** Church commemorates the prophet on 16 December. Its life holds that "he persuaded the people to build the Second Temple at Jerusalem, and he proclaimed that the Messiah would appear in this Temple in the last times" ([Haggai 2:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V9)), and that "It is believed that Haggai was buried with the priests at Jerusalem, since he was descended from Aaron."
- `6` [John Calvin, Commentary on Haggai 2:6–9](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom29.v.iii.iv.html) (Christian Classics Ethereal Library): **the law and the gospel.** The **Reformed** reformer follows the Letter to the Hebrews ([Hebrews 12:26–28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB12.htm#V26)): "the gospel is here set in contrast with the law; for God exhibited his wonderful power, when the law was promulgated on mount Sinai; but a fuller power shone forth at the coming of Christ". On the promise to Zerubbabel he writes that "though Zerubbabel never had a kingdom, nor ever wore a crown, he was yet of the tribe of Judah", and that "Zerubbabel was at that time a type of Christ" ([John Calvin, Commentary on Haggai 2:20–23](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom29.v.iii.xi.html); [Haggai 2:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V23)).
- `6` [Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Haggai 2](https://www.ccel.org/h/henry/mhc2/MHC37002.HTM) (Christian Classics Ethereal Library): **Christ the glory of the latter house.** The **Presbyterian** commentator holds that the new temple would "in spiritual respects, though not in outward, exceed that of Solomon's temple, in which he has an eye to the coming of Christ". Christ comes "as the desire of all nations --desirable to all nations, for in him shall all the families of the earth be blessed with the best of blessings", and Zerubbabel would be "one of the ancestors of Messiah the Prince" ([Haggai 2:7–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V7), [Haggai 2:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V23)).
- `6` [Enter the Bible, Bible in the World – Haggai](https://enterthebible.org/courses/haggai/lessons/bible-in-the-world-haggai/) (Luther Seminary): **the Word of God beyond the temple.** In his lectures on the minor prophets, quoted here, the **Lutheran** reformer Martin Luther cautioned: "If we should consider the subject matter unsympathetically, the prophet will seem quite trivial on the surface, especially in our day. Everything about which he prophesies, especially about rebuilding the temple, has ceased. As a consequence, we must consider the subject matter correctly, so that we look not so much at it as at the Word of God."
- `6` [Hymnary.org, Come, Thou long expected Jesus](https://hymnary.org/text/come_thou_long_expected_jesus_born_to): **an Advent hymn.** "Charles Wesley… wrote this Advent hymn and printed it in his Hymns for the Nativity of our Lord (1744)". It calls Christ "dear desire of every nation", and hymnals such as the African Methodist Episcopal Church Hymnal and *Glory to God*, the Presbyterian hymnal, list [Haggai 2:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V7) among its sources.
- `6` [USCCB, Readings for Friday of the Twenty-fifth Week in Ordinary Time (26 September 2025)](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/092625.cfm): **in the lectionaries.** The **Catholic** weekday lectionary reads Haggai in the 25th week in Ordinary Time, as in 2025: Haggai 1:1–8 on the Thursday, with Herod's question about Jesus ([USCCB, Readings for Thursday of the Twenty-fifth Week in Ordinary Time (25 September 2025)](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/092525.cfm); [Luke 9:7–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK09.htm#V7)), and Haggai 2:1–9 on the Friday, with Peter's confession of Christ ([Luke 9:18–22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/LUK09.htm#V18)). The Revised Common Lectionary reads Haggai 1:15b–2:9 in Year C on Proper 27 ([Vanderbilt Divinity Library, Revised Common Lectionary: Sunday citations](https://lectionary.library.vanderbilt.edu/sunday-citations/)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Haggai, chapter 2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/2) (New American Bible, Revised Edition): **"the desire of all nations", or "the treasure of all nations"?** The New American Bible translates [Haggai 2:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V7) "so that the treasures of all the nations will come in", and notes that "The bringing of treasures of all the nations (v. 7) to Jerusalem recalls the visionary passages of Isaiah of the pilgrimage of all nations to Jerusalem" ([Isaiah 2:2–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ISA02.htm#V2), [Isaiah 60:6–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ISA60.htm#V6)). The WEBBE and the NRSVUE also read "The treasure of all nations will come" ([NRSVUE, Haggai 1–2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%201-2&version=NRSVUE)), and the Greek Septuagint reads "the choice things of all the nations shall come" ([NETS, The Twelve Prophets, translated by George E. Howard](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/nets/edition/32-twelve-nets.pdf)). The King James Version reads "the desire of all nations shall come" ([Bible Gateway, Haggai 2:7 (KJV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Haggai%202%3A7&version=KJV)), and Augustine's text "the desired of all nations" ([Augustine, City of God, Book XVIII, 35](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120118.htm)); both Augustine and Matthew Henry read it of Christ ([Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Haggai 2](https://www.ccel.org/h/henry/mhc2/MHC37002.HTM)). Calvin explains that "the Hebrews call whatever is valuable a desire", allows that "we may understand what he says of Christ", but holds that "the more simple meaning is that which I first stated—that the nations would come, bringing with them all their riches, that they might offer themselves and all their possessions as a sacrifice to God" ([John Calvin, Commentary on Haggai 2:6–9](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom29.v.iii.iv.html)).
- `7` [Christine Hayes, Introduction to the Old Testament, Lecture 23, Open Yale Courses](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-145/lecture-23): **did Haggai expect Zerubbabel to be the coming king?** Hayes holds that Haggai looked for "the re-establishment of Judah's independence under a Davidic King", and that "He hoped that he would serve as God's messiah, or appointed king"; "That hope is even stronger in the work of Haggai's contemporary, the prophet, Zechariah" ([Zechariah 4:6–10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ZEC04.htm#V6)). The New American Bible holds that in the book "the possibility for restoration of Davidic rule is not relinquished but rather is shifted to the eschatological future" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Haggai](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/0)), and that Zerubbabel, "as a Davidic descendant, will have a servant role in God’s future Israelite kingdom" ([USCCB, Haggai, chapter 2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/2)). The ESV Global Study Bible reads the signet ring as the promise that the Lord "will restore David’s house. The Messiah will come" ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Haggai](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-haggai/)), and Calvin calls Zerubbabel "a type of Christ" ([John Calvin, Commentary on Haggai 2:20–23](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom29.v.iii.xi.html)).
- `6` [Enter the Bible, Theological Themes in Haggai](https://enterthebible.org/courses/haggai/lessons/theological-themes-in-haggai/) (Luther Seminary): **how should the promise of prosperity be read?** Haggai ties the people's failed harvests to the ruined temple, and their blessing to its rebuilding ([Haggai 1:6–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG01.htm#V6), [Haggai 2:15–19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V15)). The New American Bible notes that "This link between temple and prosperity is part of the ancient Near Eastern temple ideology that underlies Haggai and Zec 1 – 8" ([USCCB, Haggai, chapter 2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/2)). Enter the Bible holds that the promise "should not be seen as proposing a bargain with God that will ensure immediate divine favor, since that is explicitly rejected by biblical theology" ([Deuteronomy 10:17–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/DEU10.htm#V17), [Psalm 50:12–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PSA050.htm#V12)), and that it "rejoices in God’s good gifts in the present, but also looks to the future". It also reports that the Lutheran scholar Paul Hanson "accused Haggai (and Zechariah) of excessive involvement in the political program of temple rebuilding, squandering the moral authority of the prophet" ([Enter the Bible, Bible in the World – Haggai](https://enterthebible.org/courses/haggai/lessons/bible-in-the-world-haggai/)).
- `6` [Enter the Bible, Bible in the World – Haggai](https://enterthebible.org/courses/haggai/lessons/bible-in-the-world-haggai/) (Luther Seminary): **does Haggai point to a future temple in Jerusalem?** Enter the Bible notes that many Christians hold that Jesus' words to the Samaritan woman ended the need for a temple, and that the Letter to the Hebrews ends the sacrificial system ([John 4:21–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN04.htm#V21), [Hebrews 8:1–10:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HEB08.htm#V1)): "Most Catholic, Orthodox and mainline Protestant Christians do not expect a physical rebuilding of another temple with attendant sacrificial systems in Jerusalem." "Yet other Christians read passages from Daniel, Haggai, Ezekiel and elsewhere" as showing "that another (third) temple must be built before the coming of the eschaton"; "This view is particularly prominent among dispensationalists and evangelical Christians" ([Matthew 24:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT24.htm#V15), [2 Thessalonians 2:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH02.htm#V3); [Ezekiel](ezekiel.md#open-questions)). Rashi reads "The latter glory of this house will be greater" of the Second Temple itself, reporting "a controversy between Rav and Samuel. One said: In the building; and one said: In the years, that the years of the First Temple were four hundred and ten, and those of the Second Temple were four hundred and twenty" ([Sefaria, Rashi on Haggai 2:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Haggai.2.9); [Haggai 2:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V9)).
- `6` [Enter the Bible, Introductory Issues in Haggai](https://enterthebible.org/courses/haggai/lessons/introductory-issues-in-haggai/) (Luther Seminary): **who is "this people" that makes the offerings unclean?** After the priests' ruling, Haggai says "So is this people, and so is this nation before me" ([Haggai 2:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/HAG02.htm#V14)). "Biblical scholars disagree as to what is meant by the analogy. Some argue that the people who are referenced as “unclean” in verse 14 are the “people of the land;” others assert that it refers to the Jews." The New American Bible's note reads: "the restored sacrifices were not acceptable because the people’s behavior was tainted" ([USCCB, Haggai, chapter 2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/haggai/2)).
