# Proverbs

Proverbs is "an anthology of collections of sayings and instructions", and "The primary purpose of the book is to teach wisdom". Wisdom here "was not theoretical knowledge but practical expertise", and "Ultimately wisdom … aims at the formation of character" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Proverbs](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/0)). The book's motto comes at the start: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge, but the foolish despise wisdom and instruction" ([Proverbs 1:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO01.htm#V7)). Chapters 1–9 are a father's instructions to his son, with poems in which wisdom speaks as a woman; most of the rest are short sayings of two lines. The USCCB introduction notes that Proverbs differs from other wisdom books "in concentrating on wisdom itself, treating it as a virtually independent entity and personifying it as an attractive woman", and Christine Roy Yoder that "Two portraits of women frame the book of Proverbs": wisdom in chapters 1–9 and the "woman of substance" of 31:10–31 ([Bible Odyssey, Christine Roy Yoder, Woman Wisdom and the Woman of Substance](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/woman-wisdom-and-the-woman-of-substance/)).

The book's headings name several authors: "The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, king of Israel" ([Proverbs 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO01.htm#V1), [Proverbs 10:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO10.htm#V1)), proverbs of Solomon "which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out" ([Proverbs 25:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO25.htm#V1)), "the words of the wise" and "sayings of the wise" ([Proverbs 22:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO22.htm#V17), [Proverbs 24:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO24.htm#V23)), "The words of Agur the son of Jakeh" ([Proverbs 30:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO30.htm#V1)), and the words of King Lemuel, "the revelation which his mother taught him" ([Proverbs 31:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO31.htm#V1)). The Babylonian Talmud says that "Hezekiah and his colleagues wrote" Proverbs, with Isaiah, the Song of Songs and Ecclesiastes ([Sefaria, Bava Batra 15a](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.15a.2)). The ESV Global Study Bible holds that "Although Proverbs was begun in the time of Solomon, it probably was not in its present form until the time of Hezekiah" ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Proverbs](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-proverbs/)); the USCCB introduction, that "Many of the sayings and perhaps some instructions were composed in the monarchic period", and that "Editing of the whole book was done in the early postexilic period, in the view of most scholars" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Proverbs](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/0)). Who wrote the book, and what it owes to Egyptian wisdom, are among the open questions below.

Proverbs is one of the wisdom books of the Old Testament ([Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Old Testament, Wisdom](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/wisdom)), and every church reads it ([which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)). It is also Jewish Scripture, *Mishlei*, "the second book of the section in the Hebrew Bible called Writings" ([Sefaria, Proverbs](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs)). The USCCB introduction notes that "The genres and themes of Proverbs continued on in Sirach, Wisdom of Solomon, and the later Pirqe Abot", two of them books of the [deuterocanon](../README.md#deuterocanon), and that "The Letter of James is an instruction resembling those in Proverbs" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Proverbs](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/0); see [James](../new-testament/james.md)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `7` [Athanasius, Discourse II Against the Arians](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/28162.htm) (New Advent): **Wisdom in chapter 8 and Christ.** Paul calls Christ "the power of God and the wisdom of God" ([1 Corinthians 1:24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO01.htm#V24)), and the USCCB introduction notes that "Christians will see in personified Wisdom aspects of Jesus Christ, who they believe is divine wisdom sent to give human beings true and full life" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Proverbs](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/0)). In the fourth century, Arius and his followers read the Greek text of [Proverbs 8:22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO08.htm#V22), "The Lord created me a beginning of His ways", as proof that the Son is a creature. The church father Athanasius of Alexandria answered that "these are proverbs", so that "we must not expound them nakedly in their first sense" (44), and that "the Word of God is not creature but Creator; and says in the manner of proverbs, 'He created me' when He put on created flesh" (50). Geoffrey Boyle, who teaches at a **Lutheran** seminary, notes that Athanasius and the Arians both took Wisdom to be Christ, and that they "struggled over a Greek term that could mean anything from 'create' to 'possess'", whereas "the Hebrew is much clearer and is rightly translated, 'possess'" ([The Lutheran Witness, Geoffrey R. Boyle, Parsing the Proverbs with St. Athanasius](https://witness.lcms.org/2021/parsing-the-proverbs-with-st-athanasius/)). The **Reformed** commentator Matthew Henry held that the person who speaks in verses 22–31 "can be no other than the Son of God himself" ([Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Proverbs 8](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc3.Prov.ix.html)). Christine Roy Yoder notes that wisdom was also identified "in Judaism with Torah", and that "some theologians evoke her to describe the Holy Spirit" ([Bible Odyssey, Christine Roy Yoder, Woman Wisdom and the Woman of Substance](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/woman-wisdom-and-the-woman-of-substance/)).
- `7` [Hippolytus, Exegetical Fragments](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0502.htm) (New Advent): **"Wisdom has built her house."** Fragments of a commentary on Proverbs attributed to the early church writer Hippolytus read [Proverbs 9:1–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO09.htm#V1) of Christ: "Christ, he means, the wisdom and power of God the Father, has built His house, i.e., His nature in the flesh derived from the Virgin", and Wisdom's table "refers to His honoured and undefiled body and blood, which day by day are administered and offered sacrificially at the spiritual divine table". In the **Orthodox** Church, [Proverbs 9:1–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO09.htm#V1) is among the readings appointed for the feasts of the Nativity of the Theotokos and of her Dormition ([Orthodox Church in America, Scripture Readings for 8 September 2026](https://www.oca.org/readings/daily/2026/09/08); [Orthodox Church in America, Scripture Readings for 15 August 2026](https://www.oca.org/readings/daily/2026/08/15)). The **Catholic** Church reads [Proverbs 9:1–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO09.htm#V1) at Mass with Jesus' words "the bread which I will give for the life of the world is my flesh" ([USCCB, Readings for the Twentieth Sunday in Ordinary Time, 18 August 2024](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/081824.cfm); [John 6:51–58](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN06.htm#V51)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1804–1811](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P65.HTM): **practical wisdom.** The **Catholic** Catechism defines prudence, the first of the cardinal virtues, as "the virtue that disposes practical reason to discern our true good in every circumstance and to choose the right means of achieving it", quoting Proverbs: "the prudent man looks where he is going" (paragraph 1806; [Proverbs 14:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO14.htm#V15)). The **Reformed** commentator Matthew Henry wrote that "as we have no book so useful to us in our devotions as David's psalms, so have we none so serviceable to us, for the right ordering of our conversations, as Solomon's proverbs", which contain "in a little compass, a complete body of divine ethics, politics, and economics" ([Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Proverbs: Introduction](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc3.Prov.i.html)).
- `6` [Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Old Testament, Wisdom](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/wisdom): **Proverbs in worship.** In the **Orthodox** Church the proverbs "are read in their entirety at the weekday Vesper services of the Church during Great Lent. Selections from the Proverbs are also read at the vigils of a number of feasts of the Church since for Christians the Wisdom of God is personified and embodied in Christ." The **Catholic** Church reads Wisdom's account of the creation, [Proverbs 8:22–31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO08.htm#V22), at Mass on Trinity Sunday, as on 15 June 2025 ([USCCB, Readings for the Solemnity of the Most Holy Trinity, 15 June 2025](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/061525.cfm)), and the Revised Common Lectionary, used by many Protestant churches, reads it on Trinity Sunday in Year C. It also reads [Proverbs 9:1–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO09.htm#V1), sayings from chapter 22 ([Proverbs 22:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO22.htm#V1)), [Proverbs 1:20–33](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO01.htm#V20) and the poem of the worthy woman, [Proverbs 31:10–31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO31.htm#V10), on Sundays after Pentecost in Year B ([Vanderbilt Divinity Library, Revised Common Lectionary: Sunday citations](https://lectionary.library.vanderbilt.edu/sunday-citations/)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Proverbs](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/0) (New American Bible, Revised Edition): **who wrote Proverbs, and when?** Matthew Henry held that the proverbs "were not merely a collection of the wise sayings that had been formerly delivered, as some have imagined, but were the dictates of the Spirit of God in Solomon" ([Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Proverbs: Introduction](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc3.Prov.i.html)), and the ESV Global Study Bible that the book "was begun in the time of Solomon" and reached its present form under Hezekiah ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Proverbs](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-proverbs/)). The Orthodox Church in America holds that it "undoubtedly comes from Solomon's time, although scholars place some of the proverbs at a much later date and tell us that the book was put in its present form only after the Babylonian exile" ([Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Old Testament, Wisdom](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/old-testament/wisdom)); the USCCB introduction dates many sayings to the monarchy and the editing to the time after the exile, "in the view of most scholars". The New American Bible notes that when the Egyptian Instructions of Amenemope were found in 1923, "Scholars immediately recognized it as a source of Prv 22:17–23:11", and that "The Egyptian work has thirty chapters", while Proverbs speaks of "thirty excellent things" ([USCCB, Proverbs, chapter 22, with notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/22); [Proverbs 22:20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO22.htm#V20)). Tremper Longman III concludes that the sages who made the book "learned from sources like the Instructions of Amenemope", while they "differ on the ultimate source of their wisdom", the God of Israel ([Bible Odyssey, Tremper Longman III, Proverbs and Ancient Near Eastern Literature](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/proverbs/)).
- `7` [Christine Hayes, Responses to Suffering and Evil: Lamentations and Wisdom Literature](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-145/lecture-20) (Open Yale Courses, Introduction to the Old Testament, lecture 20): **are the proverbs promises?** Hayes finds in Proverbs an "almost smug certainty that the righteous and the wicked of the world receive what they deserve in this life", a view that Job and Ecclesiastes challenge (see [Job](job.md) and [Ecclesiastes](ecclesiastes.md)). Many readers say instead that the proverbs state what usually happens. On [Proverbs 22:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO22.htm#V6), "Train up a child in the way he should go", W. Robert Godfrey of Ligonier Ministries holds that "the Proverbs are talking about what is normally true and what we can ordinarily expect, not that there is never an exception to these things" ([Ligonier Ministries, W. Robert Godfrey, What is your view of Proverbs 22:6?](https://learn.ligonier.org/qas/what-is-your-view-of-proverbs-22-6)), and Matthew Henry wrote that "it is to be hoped" the child will not depart from it, though "Many indeed have departed from the good way in which they were trained up; Solomon himself did so" ([Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Proverbs 22](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc3.Prov.xxiii.html)). The New American Bible's note reads the verse another way, as "ironic advice": "yes, go ahead and let the young do exactly what they want; they will become self-willed adults" ([USCCB, Proverbs, chapter 22, with notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/22)).
- `7` [Onyekachi G. Chukwuma, Reading selected biblical proverbs in the light of corporal punishment in child upbringing](https://hts.org.za/index.php/hts/article/view/10895) (HTS Teologiese Studies/Theological Studies, 2025): **do the "rod" proverbs commend physical punishment?** Several proverbs speak of the rod in bringing up children: "One who spares the rod hates his son, but one who loves him is careful to discipline him" ([Proverbs 13:24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO13.htm#V24), [Proverbs 22:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO22.htm#V15), [Proverbs 23:13–14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PRO23.htm#V13)). Chukwuma notes that "some studies equally maintain that the selected texts support the physical discipline of children. In contrast, others argue that 'rod' is a metaphor for discipline, encompassing teaching, guidance and correction", and the article itself urges "positive discipline". Matthew Henry read the rod as physical correction, "the rod of a parent, directed by wisdom and love", using "severe ones when gentle ones will not serve" ([Matthew Henry, Commentary on the Whole Bible, Proverbs 13](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/henry/mhc3.Prov.xiv.html)). The New American Bible's note on 22:15 takes the "rod" there to be "the flail", so that "Discipline is the process of winnowing away the folly" ([USCCB, Proverbs, chapter 22, with notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/proverbs/22)). In 2012 the General Assembly of the **Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.)** "passed a resolution encouraging the use of discipline methods that do not include corporal punishment of children", after a debate on the floor ([Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.), General Assembly adopts wide range of social justice issues](https://old.pcusa.org/news/2012/7/6/general-assembly-adopts-wide-range-social-justice-/)). The churches agree that no one should abuse a child (`FAM-6`, `CHURCH-6`).

## Tutorials and further reading

- `7` [Christine Hayes, Responses to Suffering and Evil: Lamentations and Wisdom Literature](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-145/lecture-20) (Open Yale Courses, Introduction to the Old Testament, lecture 20): a university lecture, with a transcript, whose third chapter reads Proverbs: its parallels with Egyptian wisdom, wisdom personified as a woman, and the forms of parallelism in its sayings.
- `6` [Christine Roy Yoder, Woman Wisdom and the Woman of Substance](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/woman-wisdom-and-the-woman-of-substance/) (Bible Odyssey): how the portraits of Woman Wisdom in chapters 1–9 and of the worthy woman in chapter 31 frame the book, and how later Jews and Christians read the figure of wisdom.
- `6` [Sean Burt, Proverbs 31 as an Acrostic Poem](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/proverbs-31-as-an-acrostic-poem/) (Bible Odyssey): how the poem of the worthy woman runs through the Hebrew alphabet, and why it portrays wisdom "from A to Z".
- `6` [BibleProject, Proverbs](https://bibleproject.com/videos/proverbs/): an animated overview of the book and its "short, clever sayings designed to teach us how to live and act wisely".
