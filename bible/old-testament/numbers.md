# Numbers

Numbers is the fourth of the five books of the Law. It continues the story of [Exodus](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EXO.htm) and follows Israel for some thirty-eight years, "from the end of their encampment at Sinai to their arrival at the border of the promised land". The introduction to the New American Bible calls it "a combination of law and history": laws are set among the stories of the journey ([USCCB, Introduction to the Book of Numbers](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/numbers/0)). Its English name comes from the two censuses that frame it ([Numbers 1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM01.htm), [Numbers 26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM26.htm)). The generation counted at Sinai refuses to enter the land and dies in the wilderness, and a new generation is counted on the plains of Moab, ready to cross the Jordan. In BibleProject's summary, the book tells of Israel's repeated rebellion in the wilderness and how God meets it with justice and mercy ([BibleProject, Numbers](https://bibleproject.com/videos/numbers/)).

Numbers is also Jewish Scripture. In Hebrew it is called *Bamidbar*, and Sefaria describes it as "the fourth book of the Torah, Judaism's foundational text" ([Sefaria, Numbers](https://www.sefaria.org/Numbers)). The priestly blessing it records ([Numbers 6:22–27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM06.htm#V22)) is still recited in synagogues by the *kohanim*, the descendants of Aaron ([My Jewish Learning, The Priestly Blessing](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/the-priestly-blessing/)).

The book names no author, though it says that Moses wrote down the stages of the journey ([Numbers 33:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM33.htm#V2)). Jewish and Christian tradition held that Moses wrote the five books of the Law, and the ESV Global Study Bible still calls him "the source and primary author" of Numbers ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Numbers](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-numbers/)). Christophe Nihan and Julia Rhyder, writing for the Society of Biblical Literature, describe Genesis to Deuteronomy instead as "the result of a long compositional and editorial process, stretching over several centuries", from the Neo-Assyrian to the Persian period (roughly 912–323 BC) ([Bible Odyssey, The Torah/The Pentateuch](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-torah-the-pentateuch/)). The Jewish Tanakh and the Protestant and Catholic Old Testaments all place it fourth among those five books ([Bible Odyssey, Three Biblical Canons](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/three-biblical-canons/)).

## Outline

The USCCB introduction divides the book into three parts: the census and preparations to leave Sinai ([Numbers 1:1–10:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM01.htm#V1)), the departure, rebellion and forty years in the wilderness ([Numbers 10:11–25:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM10.htm#V11)), and the second census of a new generation and its preparations to enter the land ([Numbers 26:1–36:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM26.htm#V1)). ## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2129–2132](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P7F.HTM): the **Catholic Church** teaches that already in the Old Testament God "ordained or permitted the making of images that pointed symbolically toward salvation by the incarnate Word: so it was with the bronze serpent", citing [Numbers 21:4–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM21.htm#V4) and [John 3:14–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN03.htm#V14). It draws on this in explaining why the veneration of icons does not break the first commandment.
- `7` [USCCB, Readings for the Solemnity of Mary, the Holy Mother of God](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/readings/010126.cfm): in the **Catholic Church**, the priestly blessing ([Numbers 6:22–27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM06.htm#V22)) is the first reading at Mass on 1 January. In **Lutheran** worship, "The Aaronic Benediction" is spoken over the congregation "at the close of almost every worship service" ([Harry C. Henneman, Whose Blessing is it?, Michigan District of the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod](https://www.michigandistrict.org/resources/blog/whose-blessing)).
- `6` [Second Book of Homilies, Against the Peril of Idolatry, Part 2](https://northamanglican.com/against-the-peril-of-idolatry-part-ii/) (The North American Anglican): the **Church of England**'s homily against images in churches, one of those that [Article 35 of the Articles of Religion](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion) judges should be read in churches, praises the bishop Epiphanius for tearing apart a painted image he found in a church, "following herein the example of the good King Ezechias who brake the brazen serpent to pieces and burned it to ashes, for that idolatry was committed to it" ([2 Kings 18:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2KI18.htm#V4)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree, for example about the book's authorship or the meaning of a passage. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Bible Odyssey, Christophe Nihan and Julia Rhyder, The Torah/The Pentateuch](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-torah-the-pentateuch/): who wrote Numbers, and when. Jewish and Christian tradition held that Moses wrote the five books of the Law, and the ESV Global Study Bible still calls him "the source and primary author" of Numbers ([ESV Global Study Bible, Introduction to Numbers](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/introduction-to-numbers/)). Nihan and Rhyder describe the books instead as the result of several centuries of writing and editing.
- `6` [Philip Ryken, How Many Israelites Exited Egypt?](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/how-many-israelites-exited-egypt/) (The Gospel Coalition, 2023): how to read the large numbers in the censuses, 603,550 and 601,730 men of fighting age ([Numbers 1:46](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM01.htm#V46), [Numbers 26:51](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/NUM26.htm#V51)), which would mean about two million people in all. Ryken sets out three answers: many scholars have concluded that the numbers were inflated; others take them at face value; others read the Hebrew word *eleph*, "thousand", as a clan or a military unit, which would give tens of thousands of people rather than millions.
