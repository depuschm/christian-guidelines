# Colossians

The Letter to the Colossians is written in the names of Paul and Timothy "to the saints and faithful brothers in Christ at Colossae" ([Colossians 1:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL01.htm#V1)), a town in the Lycus Valley in Asia Minor, east of Ephesus. The letter says that Paul had not visited the church there ([Colossians 1:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL01.htm#V4), [Colossians 2:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL02.htm#V1)), which Epaphras had apparently founded ([Colossians 1:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL01.htm#V7), [Colossians 4:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V12)). The introduction to the New American Bible describes the problem the letter answers: teachers who stressed angels and cosmic powers, together with rules about food and drink and ascetic practices. Against them the letter sets out who Christ is, above all in the hymn of [Colossians 1:15–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL01.htm#V15): these teachings are "shadows", and Christ is the "reality" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Colossians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/colossians/0)).

The letter is closely linked with Ephesians, whose thoughts it often parallels, and with the Letter to Philemon: it names Onesimus, "the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you", the man Paul sends back to his master in that letter ([Colossians 4:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V9), [Philemon 10–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHM01.htm#V10)). The letter was written from prison ([Colossians 4:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V10), [Colossians 4:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V18)), but it doesn't say where. Tradition places it in Rome; others suggest Caesarea or Ephesus; and still others regard it as the work of a pupil or follower of Paul, writing in his name ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Colossians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/colossians/0)). Colossians is in the New Testament of every church ([The Bible: which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `9` [Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 74](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism) (1563): on **baptism and circumcision** ([Colossians 2:11–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL02.htm#V11)), the **Reformed** catechism cites this passage to teach that infants too should be baptised, because baptism, "the sign of the covenant", replaced circumcision. The **Baptist** Faith and Message, citing [Colossians 2:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL02.htm#V12), teaches that baptism is "the immersion of a believer", symbolising "the burial of the old life" ([Baptist Faith and Message, VII: Baptism and the Lord's Supper](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#vii)), and the Seventh Day Baptists cite the same verse for "baptism by immersion as a symbol of death to sin" ([Seventh Day Baptist General Conference, Statement of Belief](https://www.seventhdaybaptist.org/statement-of-belief/)). Whether infants should be baptised is an open question in [Worship and the sacraments](../../general/worship.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 291](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P19.HTM): on **Christ and creation** ([Colossians 1:16–17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL01.htm#V16)), Catholic teaching quotes the hymn to show that "God created everything by the eternal Word, his beloved Son", and the Baptist Faith and Message, citing [Colossians 1:13–22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL01.htm#V13) among other passages, confesses Christ as "the eternal Son of God" and "fully God, fully man" ([Baptist Faith and Message, II: God](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#ii)). What the churches confess together about Christ is in [What the churches confess together](../../general/beliefs.md).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Colossians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/colossians/0): who wrote Colossians, and where? The letter names Paul and was written from prison, which tradition places in Rome; others suggest Caesarea or Ephesus, and still others regard it as the work of a pupil or follower of Paul, writing in his name. Bible Odyssey lists Colossians among the letters whose authorship scholars still debate ([Bible Odyssey, Paul](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/paul/), [Bible Odyssey, Pseudepigraphy](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/pseudepigraphy/)).
