# John

The Gospel according to John is the fourth of the four Gospels. The introduction to the New American Bible describes it as "quite different in character from the three synoptic gospels", Matthew, Mark and Luke: "highly literary and symbolic", following its own order and stories, and the product of "a developed theological reflection" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Gospel according to John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/0)). It opens with a prologue on the Word who "became flesh" ([John 1:1–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN01.htm#V1)), tells of a series of "signs", probably seven, from the wedding at Cana to the raising of Lazarus, with discourses that interpret them, and gives five chapters to Jesus' last evening with his disciples. It states its own purpose: "these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name" ([John 20:30–31](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN20.htm#V30)).

The Gospel names no author. It closes by tracing its witness to "the disciple whom Jesus loved", who leaned against Jesus at the supper and stood by the cross ([John 13:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN13.htm#V23), [John 19:26–27](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN19.htm#V26), [John 21:20–24](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN21.htm#V20)). In the second century, Irenaeus wrote that "John, the disciple of the Lord, who also reclined on his bosom, published his Gospel, while staying at Ephesus in Asia" ([Eusebius, Church History 5.8.4](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250105.htm)), and Eusebius records the tradition that John wrote last, after reading the other three ([Eusebius, Church History 3.24](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm)). Many modern scholars hold otherwise. The New American Bible's introduction says that, "although tradition identified this person as John, the son of Zebedee, most modern scholars find that the evidence does not support this", while the accuracy of much of its detail argues that the tradition "rests upon the testimony of an eyewitness"; it dates the final editing to between AD 90 and 100, at Ephesus or perhaps in Syria ([USCCB, Introduction to the Gospel according to John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/0)). Many scholars connect it with a network of churches in Asia Minor that also produced 1, 2 and 3 John ([Bible Odyssey, The Johannine Community](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-johannine-community/)).

Eusebius already called it the Gospel "known to all the churches under heaven" ([Eusebius, Church History 3.24](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm)), and every church reads it as the fourth Gospel of the New Testament.

## Outline

The New American Bible's introduction divides the Gospel into a prologue ([John 1:1–18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN01.htm#V1)), the Book of Signs (1:19–12:50), the Book of Glory (13:1–20:31) and an epilogue ([John 21:1–25](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN21.htm#V1)). The table follows the Gospel's smaller units.

| Passage | Contents |
| --- | --- |

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `8` [Second Vatican Council, Unitatis redintegratio, 2](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_unitatis-redintegratio_en.html): Jesus' prayer "that they may all be one" ([John 17:20–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN17.htm#V20)) is quoted at the start of the **Catholic Church's** principles on ecumenism, the work for unity between the churches. The churches' shared commitment to unity is `CHURCH-2` in [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md).
- `8` [Second Vatican Council, Nostra aetate, 4](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651028_nostra-aetate_en.html): John often calls Jesus' opponents "the Jews", and in one dispute has Jesus say that their father is the devil ([John 8:44](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN08.htm#V44)). The New American Bible's introduction reads this "bitter and harsh invective" as coming from the conflict between church and synagogue in the evangelist's own day ([USCCB, Introduction to the Gospel according to John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/0)). The **Catholic Church** teaches that Christ's passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today", and that the Jews should not be presented "as rejected or accursed by God".
- `7` [Meredith J. C. Warren, Eating Jesus's Flesh (John 6)](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/eating-jesuss-flesh-john-6/) (Bible Odyssey): is the bread of life discourse about the Lord's Supper ([John 6:51–58](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN06.htm#V51))? Warren notes that the passage "has long been debated among scholars, with no overwhelming consensus": many read it as eucharistic, while others read it as a statement about who Jesus is. The **Catholic** catechism quotes it in its teaching on the Eucharist ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1406](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P44.HTM)), and the New American Bible's introduction reads the discourse first as revelation and then as the Eucharist. How Christ is present in the Supper is an open question in [Worship and the sacraments](../../general/worship.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1257](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3M.HTM): on being "born of water and Spirit" ([John 3:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN03.htm#V5)), the **Catholic Church** teaches, citing this verse, that "the Lord himself affirms that Baptism is necessary for salvation", while "God has bound salvation to the sacrament of Baptism, but he himself is not bound by his sacraments"; it teaches that baptism "actually brings about the birth of water and the Spirit" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1215](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3H.HTM)). The **Westminster Confession** holds that it is "a great sin to contemn or neglect" baptism, "yet grace and salvation are not so inseparably annexed unto it, as that no person can be regenerated, or saved, without it" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 28.5](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 246–248](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P17.HTM): Jesus speaks of "the Spirit of truth, who proceeds from the Father" ([John 15:26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN15.htm#V26)), and the Nicene Creed confesses the Spirit "who proceeds from the Father". The **Western churches** added that the Spirit proceeds "from the Father and the Son" (*filioque*), which the Catechism calls "a point of disagreement with the Orthodox Churches"; the **Eastern tradition** confesses the Spirit as he "who proceeds from the Father". See "Where the churches differ" in [What the churches confess together](../../general/beliefs.md#where-the-churches-differ).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1461](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4E.HTM): the risen Jesus breathes on the disciples and tells them, "If you forgive anyone's sins, they have been forgiven them" ([John 20:22–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN20.htm#V22)). The **Catholic Church** teaches, citing this passage, that bishops and priests continue the ministry of reconciliation Christ entrusted to the apostles, and the **Orthodox churches** celebrate "the sacrament of penance", with confession usually "in the presence of the pastor of the Church" ([Orthodox Church in America, Penance](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/penance)); Protestant churches differ on confession to a minister. See the open question in [Repentance and forgiveness](../../general/repentance.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 553](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1L.HTM): the **Catholic Church** reads Jesus' charge to Peter, "Feed my sheep" ([John 21:15–19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN21.htm#V15)), as Jesus confirming, after his resurrection, the authority to govern the Church that he had given to Peter. Other churches read it differently; see the open question on the Bishop of Rome in [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Confession of Faith in a Mennonite Perspective, Article 13: Foot Washing](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/foot-washing/) (Mennonite Church USA): Jesus' command to "wash one another's feet" ([John 13:1–17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN13.htm#V1)) is kept as a practice of the church by **Mennonites** and some other **Anabaptist** churches, and the **Church of God** (Cleveland, Tennessee) confesses belief "in the Lord's Supper and washing of the saints' feet" ([Church of God, Declaration of Faith](https://churchofgod.org/beliefs/declaration-of-faith/)). In the **Church of England's** liturgy for Maundy Thursday, after the Gospel of John 13, "the president may wash the feet of some members of the congregation" ([Common Worship, The Liturgy of Maundy Thursday](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/common-worship/seasonal-material/passiontide/liturgy-maundy-thursday)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Christopher W. Skinner, The Beloved Disciple](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-beloved-disciple/) (Bible Odyssey): who was "the disciple whom Jesus loved", and who wrote the Gospel? The early tradition identifies him as John, the son of Zebedee, and as the author, a view that, Skinner notes, "remains an important view among contemporary Christians"; Skinner himself finds "little evidence to support it", and other scholars have proposed Lazarus, Thomas or Mary Magdalene. The New American Bible's introduction holds that the Gospel rests on an eyewitness's testimony but was edited in stages, with its final form between AD 90 and 100 ([USCCB, Introduction to the Gospel according to John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/john/0)).
- `7` [Chris Keith, The Manuscript History of John 8](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-manuscript-history-of-john-8/) (Bible Odyssey): was the story of the woman caught in adultery ([John 7:53–8:11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN07.htm#V53)) part of the Gospel from the start? Keith notes that "the earliest manuscripts of the Gospel of John do not contain this beloved passage" and that the first manuscript to contain it is from around AD 400. This history "fuels debates about whether the story was originally in John's Gospel and, if so, where", and Keith reports that most scholars think a later scribe inserted it.
