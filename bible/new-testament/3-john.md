# 3 John

The Third Letter of John is a short personal letter from "the elder to Gaius the beloved" ([3 John 1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/3JN01.htm#V1)). The introduction to the New American Bible describes it as "a brief glimpse into the problems of missionary activity and local autonomy in the early church". Unlike the other two letters of John, it is addressed to an individual, and it is "less theological in content and purpose": "The author's goal was to secure hospitality and material support for his missionaries". The elder praises Gaius for welcoming travelling Christians, criticises Diotrephes, who "refuses to receive either letters or friends of the Presbyter", and recommends Demetrius ([USCCB, Introduction to the Third Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/3john/0)).

The introduction treats 2 and 3 John together: both come from a writer who calls himself "the Presbyter", "traditionally attributed to John the apostle", though it holds that "these letters were probably written by a disciple or scribe of an apostle" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Second Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2john/0)). See the pages on [2 John](2-john.md) and [John](john.md#open-questions) for the questions of authorship. It also sees in the letter "valuable evidence of the flexible and personal nature of authority in the early church" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Third Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/3john/0)). Like 2 John, the letter was among the books Eusebius counted as disputed but "recognized by many" ([New Advent, Eusebius, Church History 3.25](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm)), and it is in the New Testament of every church today ([The Bible: which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Third Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/3john/0): who was Diotrephes, and why did he refuse the elder's missionaries? The introduction suggests the division "may also rest on doctrinal disagreement", and lists three views: Diotrephes as "an overly ambitious local upstart trying to thwart the advance of orthodox Christianity", as "an orthodox church official suspicious of the teachings of the Presbyter", or as "a local leader anxious to keep the debates in the Johannine community out of his own congregation".
- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Second Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2john/0): who was "the elder"? The introduction calls his identity "problematic"; see [2 John](2-john.md#open-questions).
