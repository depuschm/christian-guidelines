# Ephesians

The Letter to the Ephesians is a letter in Paul's name, addressed "to the saints who are at Ephesus, and the faithful in Christ Jesus" ([Ephesians 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH01.htm#V1)). The introduction to the New American Bible calls it "the great Pauline letter about the church": it speaks less of one congregation than of the worldwide church, whose head is Christ, and of the unity in it of Jews and Gentiles in God's household. Its first half is full of praise and prayer; its second half applies this to everyday life and relationships ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Ephesians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/0)).

The writer is in prison ([Ephesians 3:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH03.htm#V1), [Ephesians 4:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V1), [Ephesians 6:20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V20)). Tradition dates the letter, with Colossians, Philippians and Philemon, to Paul's imprisonment in Rome, around AD 61–63. The same introduction notes that since the early nineteenth century much critical scholarship has doubted that Paul wrote it, because of its style, vocabulary and view of the church, and that it may be the work of a secretary writing at Paul's direction, or of a later disciple developing his ideas around AD 80–100 ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Ephesians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/0)). Bible Odyssey lists Ephesians among the New Testament letters in which scholars identify pseudepigraphy, writing in another's name, and notes that in the ancient world this was often a way of interpreting a famous figure for a new situation, not simply forgery ([Bible Odyssey, Pseudepigraphy](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/pseudepigraphy/)).

Although Paul had worked in Ephesus for over two years ([Acts 19:10](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT19.htm#V10)), the letter has an impersonal tone and no personal greetings, and important early manuscripts leave out the words "in Ephesus" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Ephesians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/0)). Every church reads it as part of the New Testament ([Which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `10` [Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification, 10](https://www.christianunity.va/content/dam/unitacristiani/Relazioni%20ecumeniche/Sezione%20occidentale/Dialogo%20multilaterale/Edizione_20_Anniversario_DCDJ/EN%2020th%20Anniversary%20Edition%20joint_declaration_2019.pdf) (Lutheran World Federation and Catholic Church, 1999, 20th anniversary edition): on **grace and faith** ([Ephesians 2:8–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH02.htm#V8)), Lutherans and Catholics quote this passage in their common account of the biblical message of justification. In the same edition, the World Communion of Reformed Churches welcomes the declaration's "clear presentation that sinners are justified by grace through faith (Eph. 2:8) and that faith is active in love", and grounds the Reformed teaching on election in God, who "chose us in Christ before the foundation of the world" ([Ephesians 1:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH01.htm#V4)). How the churches still differ on justification and election is set out on the [Romans](romans.md#how-the-churches-read-it) page.
- `9` [World Council of Churches, The Church: Towards a Common Vision, 18 and 26–27](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/The_Church_Towards_a_common_vision.pdf) (Faith and Order Paper No. 214, 2013): on **the church** ([Ephesians 2:14](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH02.htm#V14), [Ephesians 5:32](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH05.htm#V32)), the churches together say that Christ "broke down the dividing wall between Jew and Gentile", and that every Christian receives gifts of the Spirit "for the common good", citing [Ephesians 4:11–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH04.htm#V11) (18). They note that the New Testament uses the word "mystery" both for God's plan of salvation and for the relation between Christ and the church ([Ephesians 3:4–6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH03.htm#V4)). Some churches, such as the Catholic Church, therefore speak of the "Church as sacrament", a sign and instrument of communion with God, while others "do not normally use such language or reject it outright" (26–27). How the churches differ on church order and ministry is an open question in [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md#open-questions).
- `8` [John Paul II, Mulieris dignitatem, 24](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/apost_letters/1988/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_19880815_mulieris-dignitatem.html) (1988): on **husbands and wives** ([Ephesians 5:21–33](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH05.htm#V21)), the Catholic letter reads the call for wives to be subject to their husbands, a way of speaking "rooted in the customs and religious tradition of the time", as something to be carried out "in a new way: as a 'mutual subjection out of reverence for Christ'". The Baptist Faith and Message reads the passage as teaching that "a wife is to submit herself graciously to the servant leadership of her husband", while husband and wife "are of equal worth before God" ([Southern Baptist Convention, Baptist Faith and Message 2000, XVIII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#xviii)). The churches' positions on the roles of husbands and wives are an open question in [Marriage, family and singleness](../../general/family.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1602–1620](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P51.HTM): on **marriage and the "mystery" of Christ and the church** ([Ephesians 5:25–32](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH05.htm#V25)), the **Catholic Church** quotes this passage and teaches that marriage between baptised persons "is a true sacrament of the New Covenant" (1616–1617). **Orthodox** teaching likewise speaks of "the sacrament of Christian marriage", in which Christ gives human love the meaning of "his own love for the Church" ([Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith: Marriage](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/marriage)). **Anglican** teaching counts matrimony among the rites that "are not to be counted for Sacraments of the Gospel", being "states of life allowed in the Scriptures" ([Church of England, Articles of Religion, XXV](https://www.churchofengland.org/liturgy/book-of-common-prayer/articles-religion#XXV)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Ephesians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/0) (New American Bible, Revised Edition): who wrote the letter, and when? Tradition holds that Paul wrote it in prison in Rome around AD 61–63, or earlier in Caesarea. Since the early nineteenth century, much critical scholarship has doubted that Paul wrote it, because of its style, vocabulary and view of the church, and suggests a secretary writing at his direction or a later disciple around AD 80–100. Bible Odyssey lists it among the letters in which scholars identify pseudepigraphy ([Bible Odyssey, Pseudepigraphy](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/pseudepigraphy/)).
- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Ephesians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/ephesians/0) (New American Bible, Revised Edition): who was the letter written for? Because important early manuscripts leave out "in Ephesus" ([Ephesians 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH01.htm#V1)), and the letter has no personal greetings, many regard it as a circular letter to several churches in Asia Minor, carried by Tychicus ([Ephesians 6:21–22](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/EPH06.htm#V21)); others think it is the letter "from Laodicea" mentioned in [Colossians 4:16](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/COL04.htm#V16).
