# Titus

The Letter to Titus is written in the name of Paul to "Titus, my true child according to a common faith" ([Titus 1:1–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TIT01.htm#V1)), whom it says Paul left in Crete "that you would set in order the things that were lacking and appoint elders in every city" ([Titus 1:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TIT01.htm#V5)). Titus was a Greek who went up to Jerusalem with Paul and Barnabas ([Galatians 2:1–3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/GAL02.htm#V1)), and 2 Corinthians shows him bringing Paul news from Corinth and being sent back there to complete the collection for the church in Jerusalem ([2 Corinthians 7:6–7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO07.htm#V6), [2 Corinthians 8:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO08.htm#V6)). The introduction to the New American Bible notes that his name doesn't appear in Acts, that Crete is "a place Paul had never, according to the New Testament, visited", and that the letter has three topics: presbyter-bishops, the groups of people in the church, and how to live in response to "the grace and love of God that appeared in Jesus Christ" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to Titus](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/titus/0)).

The letter is one of the three Pastoral Epistles, with 1 and 2 Timothy, and the questions of who wrote them and when are discussed together: from the late second century to the nineteenth, Paul's authorship went unchallenged, but most scholars today doubt it ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Letter to Timothy](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1timothy/0)). The introduction to Titus notes that those who hold that Paul wrote it usually place it after 1 Timothy and before 2 Timothy, while others see it, like 1 Timothy, as later than Paul's own letters and 2 Timothy; it has also been suggested that if the three letters once circulated together, Titus was meant to be read first ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to Titus](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/titus/0)). Bible Odyssey lists 1 and 2 Timothy and Titus among the letters written in Paul's name in which scholars find pseudepigraphy, writing in the name of another ([Bible Odyssey, Pseudepigraphy](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/pseudepigraphy/)). The letter is in the New Testament of every church ([The Bible: which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)).

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1214–1216](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3H.HTM): on **"the washing of regeneration and renewing by the Holy Spirit"** ([Titus 3:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TIT03.htm#V5)), the **Catholic Church** teaches, citing this verse, that baptism "is also called 'the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit,' for it signifies and actually brings about the birth of water and the Spirit". **Luther's Small Catechism** answers the question "How can water do such great things?" with this verse: with the word of God, the water "is a baptism, that is, a gracious water of life and a washing of regeneration in the Holy Ghost, as St. Paul says, Titus, chapter three" ([Luther's Small Catechism: The Sacrament of Holy Baptism](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/the-sacrament-of-holy-baptism/)). The **Orthodox** Church in America calls baptism a "new birth by water and the Holy Spirit", citing [John 3:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/JHN03.htm#V5) ([Orthodox Church in America, Baptism](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-sacraments/baptism)). The **Southern Baptist Convention** holds that baptism is "an act of obedience symbolizing the believer's faith in a crucified, buried, and risen Saviour" ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VII](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#vii)). Whether baptism is a sacrament or an ordinance, and who should be baptised, are open questions in [Worship and the sacraments](../../general/worship.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1577–1580](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4X.HTM): on the **elders and overseers** Titus is to appoint ([Titus 1:5–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TIT01.htm#V5)), the **Catholic Church** teaches that the apostles chose collaborators "to succeed them in their ministry", citing this passage with [1 Timothy 3:1–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1TI03.htm#V1), and that the ministry is exercised by "bishops, priests, and deacons" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1554](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4U.HTM)). The **Presbyterian Church in America** holds that the titles bishop, pastor, presbyter and elder "do not indicate different grades of office, but all describe one and the same office", and examines its elders and deacons on "the qualifications set out in 1 Timothy 3:1-7 and Titus 1:6-9" ([PCA Book of Church Order, 8-1 and 24-1](https://www.pcaac.org/book-of-church-order/part-1-the-form-of-government/)). **Luther's Small Catechism** gives the qualities of a bishop from this passage and from 1 Timothy 3 in its Table of Duties ([Luther's Small Catechism: Table of Duties](https://bookofconcord.org/small-catechism/table-of-duties/)). How the church should be ordered is an open question in [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md#open-questions).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to Titus](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/titus/0) (New American Bible, Revised Edition): did Paul write the letter, and when? Those who hold that Paul wrote it usually place it between 1 and 2 Timothy; others see it, with 1 Timothy, as later than Paul's own letters, with a growing emphasis on church structure. The introduction to 1 Timothy sets out the four main views on who wrote the Pastoral Epistles ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Letter to Timothy](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1timothy/0)), and Bible Odyssey lists them among the letters in which scholars find pseudepigraphy ([Bible Odyssey, Pseudepigraphy](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/pseudepigraphy/)). The letter itself names Paul as its sender ([Titus 1:1–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/TIT01.htm#V1)).
