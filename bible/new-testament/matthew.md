# Matthew

The Gospel according to Matthew tells the story of Jesus' life, teaching, death and resurrection. It opens with "the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham" ([Matthew 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT01.htm#V1)), names him "Immanuel", "God with us" ([Matthew 1:23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT01.htm#V23)), and ends with his promise to his disciples: "I am with you always, even to the end of the age" ([Matthew 28:20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT28.htm#V20)). The introduction to the New American Bible notes that it stands first among the gospels because it was long thought to be the first written, and because no other gospel was quoted so often in the earliest Christian writings outside the New Testament. It gathers Jesus' teaching into five great discourses, each ending with words such as "When Jesus had finished these words" ([Matthew 7:28](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT07.htm#V28), [Matthew 11:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT11.htm#V1), [Matthew 13:53](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT13.htm#V53), [Matthew 19:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT19.htm#V1), [Matthew 26:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT26.htm#V1)), and no other evangelist, in its words, "gives the teaching of Jesus with such elegance and order" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Gospel according to Matthew](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/0)).

Christian tradition names the author as Matthew, the tax collector whom Jesus called to follow him ([Matthew 9:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT09.htm#V9), [Matthew 10:3](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT10.htm#V3)), and in the second century the apostle was identified with the evangelist ([Bible Odyssey, The Apostle Matthew](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-apostle-matthew/)). Papias, as Eusebius reports him, wrote that "Matthew wrote the oracles in the Hebrew language, and every one interpreted them as he was able", and Irenaeus that "Matthew published his Gospel among the Hebrews in their own language" ([Eusebius, Church History 3.39.16](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm), [Church History 5.8.2](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250105.htm)). The New American Bible's introduction reports that most scholars now hold a different view: an unknown author used the Gospel of Mark and a collection of Jesus' sayings that Luke also used, and wrote after the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70, probably at least a decade later, perhaps in Antioch in Syria ([USCCB, Introduction to the Gospel according to Matthew](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/0), [Bible Odyssey, Antioch and the Gospel of Matthew](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/antioch-and-the-gospel-of-matthew/)).

Every church reads Matthew as part of the New Testament, where it stands first among the four gospels.

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `9` [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Eucharist](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf) (World Council of Churches, 1982): every church celebrates the Lord's Supper that Jesus gave at the Last Supper ([Matthew 26:26–29](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT26.htm#V26)), and the Faith and Order text records how far they agree on it. They differ on how Christ is present in it and whether it is a sacrifice; [Worship and the sacraments](../../general/worship.md) sets out each church's position under its open questions.
- `9` [Westminster Confession of Faith, 24.5–24.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html): on divorce ([Matthew 19:3–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT19.htm#V3)), the **Westminster Confession** reads Jesus' exception "except for sexual immorality" ([Matthew 19:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT19.htm#V9)) as allowing the innocent party to divorce after adultery and marry another, and names adultery and wilful desertion as the only causes for dissolving a marriage. The **Catholic Church** teaches that a ratified and consummated marriage between baptised people "cannot be dissolved by any human power or for any reason other than death" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 2382](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P87.HTM)). [Marriage, family and singleness](../../general/family.md) sets out the other churches' positions under its open questions.
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 552–553](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1L.HTM): on Peter and the rock ([Matthew 16:18–19](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT16.htm#V18)), **Catholic teaching** holds that Jesus gave Peter "the first place in the college of the Twelve" and the power of the keys, and that this pastoral office "is continued by the bishops under the primacy of the Pope" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 881](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2A.HTM)). The joint Catholic–Orthodox commission records that the West came to understand the primacy of the bishop of Rome as Peter's successor, and that "this understanding was not adopted in the East, which had a different interpretation of the Scriptures and the Fathers on this point" ([Chieti Document, 16](https://www.christianunity.va/content/unitacristiani/en/dialoghi/sezione-orientale/chiese-ortodosse-di-tradizione-bizantina/commissione-mista-internazionale-per-il-dialogo-teologico-tra-la/documenti-di-dialogo/testo-in-inglese1.html)). The **Lutheran** Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope (1537) reads Peter as speaking for all the apostles, and the rock as "the ministry of the confession which Peter made" ([Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope, 23–29](https://bookofconcord.org/power-and-primacy/treatise-compiled-at-smalcald/)), and the **Westminster Confession** holds that "there is no other head of the church but the Lord Jesus Christ" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 25.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1965–1974](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P6W.HTM): the **Catholic Church** teaches that the New Law of the Gospel "is expressed particularly in the Sermon on the Mount" ([Matthew 5:1–7:29](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V1)), and that besides its precepts it includes the evangelical counsels, which "point out the more direct ways" to the perfection of love. The **Augsburg Confession** rejects the view that monastic life is a state of perfection, and holds that "Christian perfection is to fear God from the heart" and to trust him, and to serve in one's calling ([Augsburg Confession, XXVII, 49–50](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-monastic-vows/)). The **Mennonite** Confession of Faith takes Jesus' teaching on loving enemies as binding on every disciple: "As disciples of Christ, we do not prepare for war, or participate in war or military service" ([Mennonite Confession of Faith, Article 22](https://www.mennoniteusa.org/who-are-mennonites/what-we-believe/confession-of-faith/peace-justice-and-nonresistance/)). [Love of God and neighbour](../../general/love.md) sets out the churches' positions on the use of force under its open questions, and [Honest and kind speech](../../general/speech.md) those on oaths ([Matthew 5:33–37](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/MAT05.htm#V33)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree, for example about the book's authorship or the meaning of a passage. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Gospel according to Matthew](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/0): who wrote the gospel, and when? The tradition handed down by Papias and Irenaeus names the apostle Matthew, writing in Hebrew ([Eusebius, Church History 3.39.16](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm), [Church History 5.8.2](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250105.htm)). The New American Bible's introduction reports that most scholars now hold that an unknown author wrote after AD 70, using Mark, and that the attribution to Matthew "may have been due to his having been responsible for some of the traditions found in it".
- `7` [Did the Authors of the Canonical Gospels Know Each Other?](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/did-the-authors-of-the-canonical-gospels-know-each-other/) (Society of Biblical Literature, Bible Odyssey): how are Matthew, Mark and Luke related? Most scholars hold that Mark was a source for Matthew and Luke. Many add a second shared source of sayings, called Q (the Two-Source Hypothesis), while others hold that one of the later evangelists used the other's gospel as well as Mark's, so that no Q is needed (the Farrer Hypothesis).
