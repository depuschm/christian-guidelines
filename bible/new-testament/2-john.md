# 2 John

The Second Letter of John is a short letter from "the elder, to the chosen lady and her children" ([2 John 1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2JN01.htm#V1)). The introduction to the New American Bible reads "the chosen Lady" and "her children" as a literary image of a particular Christian community, and describes the letter as "a reply to problems within the church": it urges the readers to hold to "the great commandment of mutual love" and "the historical truth about Jesus", against teachers who "don't confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh" ([2 John 7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2JN01.htm#V7)), and forbids welcoming them ([USCCB, Introduction to the Second Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2john/0)).

The writer calls himself only "the elder" ("the Presbyter" in the New American Bible). Its introduction notes that 2 and 3 John are of the same length, "perhaps determined by the practical consideration of the writing space on one piece of papyrus", share their style and wording, and are linked with 1 John and the Gospel of John. "Although traditionally attributed to John the apostle, these letters were probably written by a disciple or scribe of an apostle", and the traditional setting, Ephesus at the end of the first century, is "plausible" ([USCCB, Introduction to the Second Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2john/0)). The questions of authorship are set out on the pages on [1 John](1-john.md#open-questions) and [John](john.md#open-questions). Eusebius counted 2 and 3 John among the "disputed writings, which are nevertheless recognized by many", "whether they belong to the evangelist or to another person of the same name" ([New Advent, Eusebius, Church History 3.25](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm)); today the letter is in the New Testament of every church ([The Bible: which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Second Letter of John](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2john/0): who was "the elder", and who was "the chosen lady"? The introduction calls the elder's identity "problematic": tradition attributed the letter to John the apostle, but the introduction holds that it was "probably written by a disciple or scribe of an apostle". It reads "the chosen Lady" and her children as "a literary image of a particular Christian community".
