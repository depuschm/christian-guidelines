# 2 Thessalonians

The Second Letter to the Thessalonians is a short letter to the same church as 1 Thessalonians, in the names of the same three senders: "Paul, Silvanus, and Timothy, to the assembly of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ" ([2 Thessalonians 1:1–2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH01.htm#V1)). It encourages a church under persecution ([2 Thessalonians 1:4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH01.htm#V4)), corrects the claim, spread "by spirit or by word or by letter as if from us", that "the day of Christ has already come" ([2 Thessalonians 2:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH02.htm#V2)), and tells those who have stopped working to work quietly and earn their own bread ([2 Thessalonians 3:6–15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH03.htm#V6)).

The letter closes with a greeting that it says Paul wrote "with my own hand, which is the sign in every letter" ([2 Thessalonians 3:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH03.htm#V17)). Whether Paul wrote it is debated. Bible Odyssey lists 2 Thessalonians among the letters still under discussion, not among those with "some consensus" as Paul's ([Bible Odyssey, Paul](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/paul/)), and notes that it "is viewed by many New Testament scholars as Pauline pseudepigraphy", a letter written in Paul's name by a later writer ([Bible Odyssey, Thessalonica](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/thessalonica/)). It is in the New Testament of every church.

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `9` [Second Vatican Council, Dei Verbum, 8](https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_const_19651118_dei-verbum_en.html) (1965): on **holding the traditions** taught "by word or by letter" ([2 Thessalonians 2:15](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH02.htm#V15)), the **Catholic Church** teaches that the apostles, handing on what they received, "warn the faithful to hold fast to the traditions which they have learned either by word of mouth or by letter", citing this verse, and that this tradition "develops in the Church with the help of the Holy Spirit". The **Orthodox** Longer Catechism cites the same verse to teach that Christians must follow "that tradition which agrees with the divine revelation and with holy Scripture" ([Longer Catechism of the Eastern Church, Q. 23](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2.vi.iii.i.html)). The **Westminster Confession** holds that the whole counsel of God is set down in Scripture or deduced from it, "unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). The open question on Scripture and Tradition is in [Reading Scripture](../../general/scripture.md).
- `7` [Jamie Davies, Antichrist](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/antichrist/) (Bible Odyssey): on **"the man of sin"** ([2 Thessalonians 2:3–12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH02.htm#V3)), Davies notes that the idea of the antichrist "may also lie behind" this figure, though the word itself appears only in the letters of John ([1 John 2:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1JN02.htm#V18)), and that there is "a long tradition of attempts to identify this individual: popes, reformers, political leaders ancient and modern". The **Catholic** Catechism teaches that before Christ's return the Church will pass through a final trial, in which "the supreme religious deception is that of the Antichrist, a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 675](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1V.HTM)). The **Westminster Confession** of 1647 called the pope "that Antichrist, that man of sin and son of perdition", citing this passage ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1647, 25.6](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds3.iv.xvii.ii.html)); the text the Orthodox Presbyterian Church uses today keeps only that the pope cannot "in any sense, be head" of the church ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 25.6](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Olivia Stewart Lester, Thessalonica](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/thessalonica/) (Bible Odyssey): did Paul write the letter? Many New Testament scholars regard it as written in Paul's name by a later writer, and Bible Odyssey places it among the letters still under discussion ([Bible Odyssey, Paul](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/paul/)). The letter itself names Paul as its sender, warns against a letter falsely "as if from us", and ends with a greeting it says is in Paul's own hand ([2 Thessalonians 2:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH02.htm#V2), [2 Thessalonians 3:17](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2TH03.htm#V17)).
