# 1 Corinthians

Paul's First Letter to the Corinthians was written to the church he had founded in Corinth, a busy port city of Greece. The introduction to the New American Bible says that it gives "a fuller insight into the life of an early Christian community of the first generation than any other book of the New Testament". Paul came to Corinth around AD 51, on his second missionary journey, and stayed a year and a half ([Acts 18:1–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT18.htm#V1)). He wrote this letter from Ephesus, about the year 56, in answer to reports he had received and to questions the Corinthians had sent him ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Letter to the Corinthians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1corinthians/0), [1 Corinthians 1:11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO01.htm#V11), [1 Corinthians 7:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO07.htm#V1), [1 Corinthians 16:8](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO16.htm#V8)).

The same introduction lists the troubles Paul addresses: factions around different leaders, a member living in an incestuous union, lawsuits between believers in pagan courts, divisions at the Lord's Supper, spiritual gifts prized above love and used in a disorderly way, and some who denied the resurrection of the dead. It notes that the letter is of the greatest importance for early Christian teaching on the Eucharist and on the resurrection of the body ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Letter to the Corinthians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1corinthians/0)). Paul wrote at least two other letters to Corinth that have not survived as separate letters ([1 Corinthians 5:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO05.htm#V9), [2 Corinthians 2:3–4](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/2CO02.htm#V3), [Bible Odyssey, The Corinthian Letters](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/the-corinthian-letters/)).

The USCCB introduction says that Paul's authorship, "apart from a few verses that some regard as later interpolations, has never been seriously questioned". Some scholars have proposed that the letter combines parts of more than one of Paul's letters, but most commentators read it as a single, coherent work ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Letter to the Corinthians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1corinthians/0)). Every church reads it as part of the New Testament.

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `9` [Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry, Eucharist 1](https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf) (World Council of Churches, Faith and Order Paper No. 111, 1982): on **the Lord's Supper** ([1 Corinthians 11:23–26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO11.htm#V23)), the churches' common text begins its account of the Eucharist with Paul's words "I have received from the Lord what I also delivered to you". Andrew McGowan notes that this is probably the oldest version of the Last Supper story ([Bible Odyssey, Eucharist](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/eucharist/)). How Christ is present in the Supper, and whether it is a sacrifice, are open questions in [Worship and the sacraments](../../general/worship.md#open-questions).
- `8` [Church of God (Cleveland, Tennessee), Declaration of Faith](https://churchofgod.org/beliefs/declaration-of-faith/): on **spiritual gifts** ([1 Corinthians 12:31–14:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO12.htm#V31)), this **Pentecostal** church believes "in speaking with other tongues as the Spirit gives utterance" as "the initial evidence of the baptism in the Holy Ghost", and Elim believes "in the present operation of the manifold Gifts of the Holy Spirit according to the New Testament" ([Elim's Foundational Truths](https://www.elim.org.uk/Articles/417857/Our_Beliefs.aspx)). The **Catholic** Catechism calls charisms "graces of the Holy Spirit" given for building up the Church, and teaches that "discernment of charisms is always necessary" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 799–801](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P28.HTM)). The **Westminster Confession** holds that "those former ways of God's revealing his will unto his people" have "now ceased" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 1.1](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)). Whether God still guides through prophecy today is an open question in [Discernment and decisions](../../general/discernment.md#open-questions).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1602–1620](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P51.HTM): on **singleness and celibacy** ([1 Corinthians 7:32–35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO07.htm#V32)), the **Catholic Church** teaches that from the beginning some have "renounced the great good of marriage" to be "intent on the things of the Lord", and that marriage and virginity for the kingdom "come from the Lord himself" and "reinforce each other" (1618–1620). The **Augsburg Confession** cites Paul's "To avoid fornication, let every man have his own wife" and "It is better to marry than to burn" ([1 Corinthians 7:2](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO07.htm#V2), [1 Corinthians 7:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO07.htm#V9)) for the right of priests to marry ([Augsburg Confession, XXIII](https://bookofconcord.org/augsburg-confession/of-marriage-of-priests/)). Whether clergy must be celibate is an open question in [Marriage, family and singleness](../../general/family.md#open-questions).
- `7` [USCCB, 1 Corinthians, chapter 15, notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1corinthians/15) (New American Bible, Revised Edition): on **an early creed** ([1 Corinthians 15:3–5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO15.htm#V3)), the notes say that the language in which Paul sums up the gospel "is not his own but is drawn from older credal formulas", highlighting Jesus' death for our sins and his resurrection. The churches confess the same in the creeds ([What the churches confess together](../../general/beliefs.md)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, 1 Corinthians, chapter 14, notes](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1corinthians/14) (New American Bible, Revised Edition): what does "Let the wives be quiet in the assemblies" ([1 Corinthians 14:34–35](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO14.htm#V34)) mean, when Paul has written of women praying and prophesying in worship ([1 Corinthians 11:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1CO11.htm#V5))? The notes say the two passages are difficult to harmonise, and that the verses "are often considered an interpolation", though all manuscripts contain them; the NRSVUE notes that some ancient authorities put them after verse 40 ([1 Corinthians 14 in the NRSVUE, with notes](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2014&version=NRSVUE)). Behind this lies a wider question on which the churches differ: who may be ordained. The **Catholic Church** teaches that "only a baptized man (vir) validly receives sacred ordination" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1577](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4X.HTM)), and an answer on the **Orthodox** Church in America's website says that "the Orthodox Church precludes the ordination of women to the priesthood and episcopacy", as "a matter of Holy Tradition" ([Orthodox Church in America, Ordination of Women](https://www.oca.org/questions/priesthoodmonasticism/ordination-of-women)). The **Southern Baptist Convention** holds that the office of pastor, elder or overseer "is limited to men as qualified by Scripture" ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, VI](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#vi)), and the **Presbyterian Church in America** that its offices are "open to men only" ([PCA Book of Church Order, 7-2](https://www.pcaac.org/book-of-church-order/part-1-the-form-of-government/)). The **Methodist Church in Britain** has ordained women as presbyters since 1974 ([Methodist Church in Britain, 50 years of women's ordination](https://www.methodist.org.uk/about/news/the-methodist-church-in-great-britain-celebrates-50-years-of-womens-ordination-as-ministers/)), predecessors of the **Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.)** have ordained women as ministers since 1956 ([Presbyterian Mission Agency, Women in the church](https://pma.pcusa.org/ministries/101/women/)), and the **Church of England** made it possible for women to be bishops in 2014 ([Church of England, Legislation on women bishops](https://www.churchofengland.org/news/legislation-women-bishops-becomes-law-general-synod)).
