# Philippians

Paul's Letter to the Philippians is a warm letter from prison. It opens with greetings from "Paul and Timothy, servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are at Philippi, with the overseers and servants" ([Philippians 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V1)). Philippi was a Roman colony in Macedonia, in northern Greece. According to Acts, Paul founded there the first Christian community in Europe, probably in AD 49 or 50 ([Acts 16:9–40](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/ACT16.htm#V9), [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Philippians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/philippians/0)). Paul writes in chains and in danger of death ([Philippians 1:7](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V7), [Philippians 1:20–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V20)). He thanks the Philippians for the gift they sent him through Epaphroditus, urges them to unity and humility, and warns against teachers who would impose circumcision on them. The introduction to the New American Bible notes that it has been called "the letter of joy" ([Philippians 4:10–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V10), [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Philippians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/philippians/0)).

There is some consensus among scholars that Philippians is one of the letters Paul himself wrote, with 1 Thessalonians, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Romans and Philemon ([Bible Odyssey, Paul](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/paul/)). Where and when he wrote it is less certain, because he was imprisoned more than once. The traditional view is Rome, around AD 59 to 63; others suggest Caesarea or Corinth, and much recent scholarship favours Ephesus, around AD 55. Some scholars also think the letter joins parts of three letters to the Philippians ([USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Philippians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/philippians/0)). Philippians is in the New Testament of every church ([The Bible: which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)).

## Outline

The USCCB introduction divides the letter into eight parts.

| Passage | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Philippians 1:1–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V1) | Greeting, thanksgiving, and Paul's prayer that their love may grow. |
| [Philippians 1:12–26](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V12) | The gospel advances through Paul's imprisonment; "to live is Christ, and to die is gain". |
| [Philippians 1:27–2:18](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V27) | Unity and humility, the hymn to Christ who emptied himself, and shining as lights in the world. |
| [Philippians 2:19–3:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP02.htm#V19) | Plans to send Timothy and Epaphroditus. |
| [Philippians 3:2–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP03.htm#V2) | Warning against those who insist on circumcision; Paul counts everything loss to gain Christ, and presses on towards the goal. |
| [Philippians 4:1–9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V1) | Stand firm and agree in the Lord; rejoice; don't be anxious; think about what is good. |
| [Philippians 4:10–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V10) | Thanks for the Philippians' gift, and Paul's contentment. |
| [Philippians 4:21–23](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V21) | Final greetings. |

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `10` [Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification, 12, and Annex, 2C](https://www.christianunity.va/content/dam/unitacristiani/Relazioni%20ecumeniche/Sezione%20occidentale/Dialogo%20multilaterale/Edizione_20_Anniversario_DCDJ/EN%2020th%20Anniversary%20Edition%20joint_declaration_2019.pdf) (Lutheran World Federation and Catholic Church, 1999, 20th anniversary edition): on **working out salvation** ([Philippians 2:12–13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP02.htm#V12)), Lutherans and Catholics quote this passage together as the apostle's word "to the justified", and agree that "the working of God's grace does not exclude human action: God effects everything, the willing and the achievement, therefore, we are called to strive". In the same volume the World Methodist Council quotes John Wesley's sermon on the verse: "God works, therefore you can work. God works, therefore you must work". The **Catholic** Council of Trent cites it on the gift of perseverance: the justified should "with fear and trembling work out their salvation" ([Council of Trent, Session VI, chapter 13](https://www.papalencyclicals.net/councils/trent/sixth-session.htm)), and the **Reformed** Westminster Confession, in Paul's words, teaches that good works need "an actual influence of the same Holy Spirit, to work in them to will, and to do, of his good pleasure" ([Westminster Confession of Faith, 16.3](https://www.opc.org/wcf.html)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 461](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1J.HTM): on the **hymn to Christ** ([Philippians 2:5–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP02.htm#V5)), Catholic teaching names it as a hymn in which "the Church sings the mystery of the Incarnation". The **Lutheran** Formula of Concord reads it of Christ's "state of His humiliation", in which he held back from using his divine majesty, until after his resurrection he "laid aside the form of a servant, but not the [human] nature" ([Formula of Concord, Epitome, VIII: The Person of Christ, 11](https://bookofconcord.org/epitome/person-of-christ/)). What the churches confess together about Christ is in [What the churches confess together](../../general/beliefs.md).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1554](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4U.HTM): Paul greets the church "with the **overseers** and servants" ([Philippians 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V1)). Catholic teaching holds that the ministry is "exercised in different degrees by those who even from ancient times have been called bishops, priests, and deacons", while the Baptist Faith and Message, citing this verse, names two offices, "pastor/elder/overseer and deacon" ([Baptist Faith and Message, VI: The Church](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/#vi)). How churches are governed is an open question in [Belonging to a church](../../general/church.md#open-questions).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Philippians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/philippians/0): where was Paul imprisoned when he wrote, and when? The traditional view is Rome, between AD 59 and 63. Others suggest Caesarea in AD 57 or 58, or Corinth; much recent scholarship favours Ephesus around AD 55, close enough to Philippi for the journeys the letter mentions ([Philippians 1:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V13)).
- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the Letter to the Philippians](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/philippians/0): is Philippians one letter or several? Some scholars think an editor joined parts of three letters: a note of thanks ([Philippians 4:10–20](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP04.htm#V10)), a letter from prison ([Philippians 1:1–11](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP01.htm#V1) to [Philippians 2:19–3:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP02.htm#V19)), and a warning against false teachers ([Philippians 3:2–21](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/PHP03.htm#V2)). Others read it as one letter, with a break in writing between 3:1 and 3:2.
