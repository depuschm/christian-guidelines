# 1 Peter

The First Letter of Peter is written from "Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the chosen ones who are living as foreigners in the Dispersion in Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia" ([1 Peter 1:1](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE01.htm#V1)), Christian communities in five provinces of Asia Minor, apparently made up mostly of Gentiles. The introduction to the New American Bible describes its purpose: to encourage these "chosen sojourners", estranged both from their former religion and from the society around them, to stay faithful in spite of threats of persecution. It mixes moral exhortation with summaries of what God has given in Christ, and draws on the new birth of baptism ([USCCB, Introduction to the First Letter of Peter](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1peter/0)). Joel B. Green describes the letter's main concern as "Christian identity and behavior in a non-Christian world": its readers had become "the victims of slander, animosity, and scorn" because their faith had changed how they lived ([Bible Odyssey, 1 Peter](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/1-peter/)).

From Irenaeus in the late second century until modern times, Christian tradition named the apostle Peter as the author, writing from Rome, here called "Babylon" ([1 Peter 5:13](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE05.htm#V13)), before his martyrdom under Nero. Some modern scholars think it the work of a later writer; others hold that Peter wrote it through a secretary, Silvanus ([1 Peter 5:12](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE05.htm#V12)) (see [Open questions](#open-questions)). Eusebius of Caesarea, in the fourth century, wrote that the first letter of Peter "is acknowledged as genuine", and that the ancient elders used it "as an undisputed work" ([Eusebius, Church History 3.3.1](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250103.htm)). 1 Peter is in the New Testament of every church ([The Bible: which books each church reads](../README.md#which-books-each-church-reads)).

## Outline

## How the churches read it

Where the churches, or their interpreters, read the book differently, or where it has played a part in their history. Each entry starts with its source's score.

- `9` [The Apostles' Creed](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-apostles-creed) (English Language Liturgical Consultation): the agreed ecumenical text says that Christ "descended to the dead", and its notes give four ways the line has been read. One of them, "following 1 Peter 3:19", sees in it "our Lord proclaiming his victory to the souls of the departed"; the notes add that "Some Churches have officially adopted one of the foregoing interpretations". The **Catholic Church** teaches that Christ "descended there as Saviour, proclaiming the Good News to the spirits imprisoned there", and quotes [1 Peter 4:6](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE04.htm#V6): "The gospel was preached even to the dead" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 632–635](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P1R.HTM)). The **Orthodox** Matins of Holy Saturday sing: "When Thou didst descend to death O Life Immortal, Thou didst slay hell with the splendor of Thy Godhead!" ([Orthodox Church in America, Holy Saturday](https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/worship/the-church-year/holy-saturday)). The **Lutheran** Formula of Concord confesses that Christ "descended into hell, destroyed hell for all believers", and leaves how this happened to be revealed in the world to come ([Formula of Concord, Epitome, IX](https://bookofconcord.org/epitome/descent-of-christ-into-hell/)). The **Reformed** Heidelberg Catechism understands the creed's line instead as Christ's "unspeakable anguish, pain, and terror of soul, on the cross but also earlier", by which he delivers believers from hellish anguish ([Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 44](https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/confessions/heidelberg-catechism)).
- `7` [Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1262–1274](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P3N.HTM): on the royal priesthood ([1 Peter 2:5](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE02.htm#V5), [1 Peter 2:9](https://ebible.org/eng-webbe/1PE02.htm#V9)), the **Catholic Church** teaches that the baptised are "living stones" built into "a holy priesthood", and that "Baptism gives a share in the common priesthood of all believers". This common priesthood and the ministerial priesthood of bishops and priests are "ordered one to another", yet "they differ essentially" ([Catechism of the Catholic Church, 1546–1547](https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P4T.HTM)). The **Lutheran** Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope quotes "Ye are a royal priesthood", and holds that the words "pertain to the true Church, which certainly has the right to elect and ordain ministers since it alone has the priesthood" ([Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope, 69](https://bookofconcord.org/power-and-primacy/power-and-jurisdiction-of-bishops/)). **Baptist** confessions honour "the priesthood of believers" ([Baptist Faith and Message 2000, Preamble](https://bfm.sbc.net/preamble/)), and the Baptist World Alliance describes God's people as "called to be priests to one another" ([Baptist World Alliance, Beliefs](https://baptistworld.org/beliefs/)).

## Open questions

Questions on which the churches or scholars disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [USCCB, Introduction to the First Letter of Peter](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1peter/0): who wrote the letter, and when? Tradition names the apostle Peter, writing from Rome shortly before his death, between AD 64 and 67. Some modern scholars regard it as the work of a later writer, of the end of the first century or the beginning of the second, pointing to its cultivated Greek, its use of the Septuagint, its closeness to Paul's letters and its allusions to widespread persecution. Others answer that a secretary, Silvanus, may have written Peter's thoughts in his own style, and that the persecution may have been local harassment, so that nothing rules out Peter writing in the 60s. A middle view dates it to AD 70–90, by a disciple of Peter in Rome. Joel B. Green notes that whichever view is right, the problem the letter answers is the same ([Bible Odyssey, 1 Peter](https://www.bibleodyssey.org/articles/1-peter/)).
